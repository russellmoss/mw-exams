# Decision matrix — 2026 Paper 3 Question 2

## Question (verbatim)
```
Wines 5-6, 7-8, 9-10, and 11-12 are pairs. Within each pair, the wines are from the same country and region.

For each pair:

a) Identify the origin as closely as possible. (4 x 8 marks)

b) Comment on the methods of production. (4 x 14 marks)

c) Compare the quality in the context of the region of origin. (4 x 16 marks)

For each wine:

a) State the alcohol level. (8 x 3 marks)

b) State the level of residual sugar. (8 x 3 marks)
```

## Paper context
Paper 3 = the mixed/special paper: sparkling, fortified, sweet, rosé, oxidative, occasionally orange or unusual styles. This question is 8 of the paper's 12 wines and 200 of the likely ~300 marks on offer for the paper — the largest single flight in the corpus (previous max was six wines in one sub-question: 2013 P3 Q3). Q1 of this paper has already placed four traditional-method sparkling wines from four different countries, so this question exists to cover everything else the "special wines" universe has to offer.

## Stem signals
- **Four pairs, each pair sharing country AND region** — not producer (cf. 2018 P3 Q1, same-producer verticals) and not "different region per pair" (cf. 2021 P3 Q3). This is within-appellation contrast, testing whether the candidate knows a single region can produce genuinely different wines.
- The lettering **restarts** from pair-level (a,b,c) to wine-level (a,b) — a structural signal the examiner is switching scope, not a numbering error.
- **48 of 200 marks (24%) are for bald ABV/RS numbers** — the heaviest numeric-fact weighting in the corpus for this section. That is only a fair test if the eight wines span a genuinely wide, tasteable range of both variables — i.e., NOT eight similar dry table wines.
- **(b) methods of production = 14 marks/pair**, the single heaviest production-method allocation seen in any P3 question. This all but requires each pair to contrast in HOW it was made (biological vs oxidative ageing, cask vs bottle ageing, botrytis vs non-botrytis, fortification vs not) even though origin is held constant.
- **(c) quality-in-context = 16 marks/pair**, the largest component — rewards knowing the internal quality hierarchy of a single appellation (village/cru, vintage/non-vintage, entry/prestige, young/old).
- Sparkling is already spoken for by Q1, so this flight should draw from: fortified (sherry, port, madeira), botrytis/late-harvest sweet, ice wine, rosé, and oxidative/flor styles.

Textbook "same DO, opposite production philosophy" pair: biological (flor) vs oxidative ageing, huge RS spread (near-zero to 300+ g/l), moderate ABV spread (15% vs 17–20%). Tawny/Vintage contrasts oxidative cask ageing vs reductive bottle ageing at similar ABV (~19–20%) but different RS profile and colour — good for the quality/maturity-in-context prompt.

## Rule-outs
- **A second flight of sparkling wine** — ruled out; Q1 already owns that territory and the examiner rarely duplicates style-category across questions in the same paper.
- **Eight dry, still, unfortified wines** — inconsistent with the 48 marks dedicated to ABV/RS as legible, discriminable facts; dry table wines cluster too tightly (11.5–14.5% ABV, <5 g/l RS) to justify that mark allocation.
- **Four pairs from four totally unrelated countries with no fortified/botrytis representation** — unlikely; P3's identity depends on showcasing fortified and sweet styles, and this is the biggest flight of the year to do it in.

## The IMW's likely intent
This question is testing (1) precise origin ID within specialty categories, (2) the ability to explain **contrasting production methods that co-exist inside a single appellation** (the money skill — flor vs oxidative, cask vs bottle, botrytis vs late-harvest/dry), (3) numeric fluency translating aroma/palate impression into concrete ABV%/RS g/l estimates, and (4) quality/hierarchy literacy within one region rather than across regions.

## What to focus on in the glass
- Colour depth/hue first: pale straw/green = young & fresh; deep amber/mahogany = oxidative ageing or high RS/botrytis.
- Rancio, nutty, caramelised, or volatile-acidity-forward aromatics = oxidative/solera ageing (sherry, tawny, madeira) vs fresh fruit = reductive/bottle-aged (vintage port, young Sauternes).
- Viscosity/"legs" and perceived sweetness on entry to anchor the RS estimate before defaulting to a guess.
- Heat/warmth and finish length to anchor ABV — distinguish ~15% (sherry) from ~19–20% (port/madeira) from ~7–9% (German Prädikat).
- Botrytis markers (honeyed, saffron, dried apricot, marmalade) vs raisining/passerillage (fig, date) vs flor character (green olive, salted almond, chalky) to separate the pairs' production stories.
- Acid backbone relative to sweetness — a key differentiator for quality-in-context (balance vs cloying) within each region's hierarchy.
