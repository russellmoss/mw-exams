## Scope: which behaviours count as pandemic effects

I take this narrowly and deliberately. A great deal has changed in wine since March 2020, but much of it — the drift towards lower alcohol, the long premiumisation trend, the rise of organic claims — was already in motion and would have happened anyway. I confine myself to purchasing behaviours the pandemic either triggered outright or measurably accelerated. I also confine myself to consumers: the container shortages, dry-goods inflation and freight disruption of the last two years have been severe, but they are a supply-side story and not what is asked. My three markets are the United States, the United Kingdom and China, chosen because they behaved very differently and the differences are the argument.

## The channel shift, and how far it went

The first and largest effect was compulsory: on-trade closure moved a large block of consumption home. The striking global finding is how little drinking was lost — the OIV put the 2020 decline in world consumption at around 3% — so the volume did not disappear, it changed channel.

In the UK the off-trade absorbed it. Off-trade wine volumes rose by roughly a fifth in 2020 on Nielsen data reported by the WSTA, while hospitality lost most of its trading year. Supermarket wine became the default purchase, and the online specialists — Naked Wines, Laithwaites, the grocers' own delivery slots — took a step change in customer numbers that no marketing budget could have bought.

In the US the shift went further, because the pandemic forced a regulatory change consumers immediately used: a large majority of states temporarily permitted to-go and delivered alcohol. Direct-to-consumer winery shipments, already the fastest-growing route to market, grew of the order of a quarter by value in 2020 on the Sovos ShipCompliant and Wines Vines Analytics report. Uber's acquisition of the delivery platform Drizly in 2021 is the clearest signal that the trade read this as structural rather than temporary.

## What consumers bought once they were buying at home

Three behaviours here are specifically pandemic-shaped rather than pre-existing.

First, occasion substitution lifted the off-trade price point. A consumer who could not spend £40 in a restaurant spent £15 in a supermarket instead of their usual £8. This resembles premiumisation and I want to separate it from that trend: it is not consumers becoming steadily wealthier and more curious, it is a fixed budget displaced from one channel into another, and it should be expected to unwind as restaurants reopen.

Second, stockpiling and format. Early lockdown produced pantry-loading, a shift to larger pack sizes, and a real if temporary rehabilitation of bag-in-box in markets that had written it off.

Third, occasion loss at the top. Champagne is the cleanest evidence: CIVC shipments fell to around 245 million bottles in 2020, then rebounded above 320 million in 2021. Celebration wine depends on gatherings, and the rebound shows that demand was deferred rather than destroyed.

## China: the counter-case

China matters because it shows the channel shift was not universal. Chinese wine consumption is heavily on-trade, banqueting and gifting-led; there is no established habit of everyday domestic wine drinking to absorb displaced volume. When restaurants and banquets closed, the volume was simply lost. Bottled wine imports, already in decline, fell by roughly a quarter by volume in 2020 on China Customs figures. What did accelerate was e-commerce and livestream selling through Tmall, JD and Douyin, now a serious route to the Chinese wine consumer rather than a curiosity. And China is not past this: the Shanghai lockdown this spring has repeated the 2020 shock in the country's single most important wine city.

## What has survived reopening

This is where the answer has to commit.

- Durable: online and direct-to-consumer purchasing. Once a consumer has completed the first transaction, surrendered a card and had wine arrive, the habit holds; US DtC has kept growing after reopening.
- Durable: the premium at-home occasion. "Restaurant at home" has proved to be a genuine new occasion rather than a substitute one.
- Not durable: stockpiling, large formats and the extreme off-trade skew. These tracked closure and have unwound with it.
- Uncertain: the traded-up off-trade price point. It survived 2021, but inflation is now the stronger force acting on it, and I expect trading down through the mainstream with the premium tier holding.

## Conclusion

The pandemic did not change what consumers want from wine; it changed where and how they buy it, and it did so in two years rather than the decade it would otherwise have taken. The lasting consequences are a materially larger digital and direct channel in the US and UK, a rebuilt commercial relationship between producer and drinker wherever DtC took hold, and — in a market like China, where wine consumption is social and public rather than domestic — a cohort of consumers that reopening alone has not brought back.
