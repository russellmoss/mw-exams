## The stance I am taking

I am auditing this supplier, not running it. My output is an audit report with graded non-conformances and corrective-action deadlines, so every area below is framed as a question I put to the supplier, the documentary evidence I require in answer, and the verification I run on that evidence. The method throughout is the same three steps: show me the procedure, show me the record that proves it was followed on a date I choose, then let me test it live.

Scope matters and I set it at the outset. This is a QC and bottling audit, so it runs from goods-in — bulk wine, dry goods, additives — to despatch of finished own-label product. Viticulture, vinification and bulk transport sit in the technical manager's supplier-approval file and I touch them only where a record I need is missing at goods-in. The reason for that focus is that own-label puts our name on the label: under EU food law we are a food business operator carrying the legal and reputational liability, so what I must be able to prove is that this site's *systems* are capable, not that its winemaking is good.

## 1. Management system, HACCP and legality

I ask first for the current certification — BRCGS Global Standard for Food Safety, or IFS Food or FSSC 22000 — with the grade, the announced or unannounced status, and every open non-conformance from the last audit with evidence of closure. Certification proves a system exists; it does not prove they understand their own risks, so I go to the HACCP study next. Is it wine-specific and current? Is the team multi-disciplinary? Are the critical control points justified by hazard analysis rather than copied? On a bottling line I expect CCPs around filtration integrity and foreign-body control, with everything else managed by prerequisite programmes; a study listing fifteen CCPs tells me they cannot prioritise.

Legality is checked as a compliance file: SO2 within EU maxima — broadly 150 mg/L total for a dry red and 200 mg/L for a dry white — the 10 mg/L declaration threshold for sulphites, allergen declaration where egg or milk fining agents are used, ingredient and nutrition declaration, artwork version control against approved label copy, and certificates supporting any organic or vegan claim.

## 2. Supplier validation, packaging specification and dry goods

Our supplier must show documented validation procedures assessing safety, regulatory and quality criteria for everything it in turn buys: glass, closures, capsules, cartons, labels, adhesives, and all additives and processing aids. I want an approved-supplier list with review dates, agreed specifications, certificates of conformity per delivery, food-contact declarations and allergen status, plus retained samples of dry goods.

Packaging specification is verified physically, not on paper: glass dimensions and annealing, closure torque and extraction values, liner type against the oxygen-transmission specification we bought. And I require live registers for glass and hard plastics on site, because these are the highest-consequence foreign bodies and their control is a documented register or it does not exist.

## 3. Product specification and the analytical record

The specification is the contract, so I audit whether it is being verified. I require full records of analyses and of treatment decisions, batch by batch: alcohol, free and total SO2, volatile acidity, residual sugar, pH and TA, dissolved oxygen and total package oxygen at fill, dissolved CO2, microbial counts, protein and cold stability certificates, and contaminant testing such as ochratoxin A against the EU maximum of 2 micrograms per kilogram. Tolerance on declared alcohol is 0.5% abv for most still wine and I check results against label, not against the tank sheet.

I then audit the laboratory itself — methods, calibration records, reference standards, proficiency-scheme participation — and correlate three of their certificates against our own retained-sample results. If their lab and mine disagree, every other record is unverifiable. Sampling protocol is audited in the same spirit: how many bottles, from where in the run, retained for how long. Organoleptic release needs a documented panel, defined defect thresholds and a signed release, not a winemaker's opinion.

## 4. The line: records, not a walkthrough

At the line I ask for evidence of specific past runs. Line-clearance records between products. Filter integrity: bubble-point results before and after each run. CIP validation with swab or ATP results. Filler dissolved-oxygen trend data. Fill volume against average-quantity control. Rejection-device challenge tests at start, middle and end of run for the empty-bottle inspector, fill-level detector and metal detector.

Breakages are a discrete focus. I want a written procedure — immediate line stop, defined quarantine radius, two-person sign-off, disposal of exposed product — and then the breakage log, and I read the last three entries and follow one through to the disposal record.

## 5. Traceability and lot coding

I run a vertical challenge: pick one pallet at despatch, read its lot code, and require the supplier to trace back through the bottling record to tank, bulk delivery and every dry-goods batch, with a mass balance, within four hours. I check the lot code is legible and identical on bottle, case and pallet label, and I ask for the last mock recall — within twelve months, with results and corrective actions.

## 6. People, policies and environment

Training records mapped to the roles that carry risk: filter operator, taster, breakage sign-off. Hygiene, jewellery, glass and contractor policies with evidence of briefing. Finally the environmental policy — water and effluent, energy, packaging weight and recycled content — and the ethical audit, SMETA or equivalent, both of which are conditions of listing for us.

## Conclusion

My focus is not the wine but the evidence. I grade findings critical, major or minor, require corrective actions with owners and dates, and set the re-audit interval accordingly, because the risk I am managing is an own-label recall carrying our name.
