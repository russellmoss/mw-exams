## What a specification is for

A technical specification is the contractual definition of the product a buyer has agreed to receive: the analytical, microbiological, stability, contaminant, sensory and packaging parameters, each with a target and a tolerance, plus the method by which each is measured and the remedy if it is missed. Its objective is single — that the wine reaches the importer in good quality and in a chemically and microbiologically stable condition. What "stable" has to mean differs entirely between the two wines. The sweet white is finished goods: it must survive shipping and shelf and be sold to the consumer as it stands. The bulk red is a raw material: it must survive a container voyage in a condition that allows final treatment and bottling at destination.

## What any specification covers

Both documents share a skeleton. Analytical: alcohol, pH, titratable acidity, volatile acidity, free and total SO2, glucose plus fructose, malic acid, CO2, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, colour measures and metals. Microbiological: viable yeast and bacteria counts. Stability: protein, tartrate, colour and microbial. Contaminant and residue limits for the destination market. Allergen and labelling data. A sensory clause referencing an approved sample. And a governance section: sampling protocol, methods and laboratories, retained samples, arrival tolerances, rejection and arbitration.

## a. White dessert wine, 150 g/l residual sugar, bottled at source

**The spoilage risk.** With 150 g/l of sugar the wine is a fermentation medium. The named threat is *Zygosaccharomyces bailii*, which is both osmotolerant and unusually SO2-tolerant, so the specification must call for sterile filtration and a sterility certificate, not merely a free SO2 figure. A single viable cell per bottle can restart fermentation and blow corks in a warm market.

**Sulphur dioxide.** Sugar and, in a botrytised wine, carbonyl compounds bind SO2 heavily, so total will be high and free hard to hold. The specification must set free SO2 and the pH together, since molecular SO2 is what protects, and must state the total against the legal ceiling of the destination — the EU ceiling for the sweetest white categories runs to 300-400 mg/L total depending on the category, and a wine legal at origin can be illegal at destination.

**Style-specific tolerances.** A botrytised wine legitimately carries higher volatile acidity and higher gluconic acid than a dry white; set the VA limit at a level appropriate to the style or the specification will reject the wine for being what it is. Laccase activity must be nil, or the wine will brown oxidatively in bottle. Specify colour at bottling and an acceptable drift, since these wines darken.

**Packaging.** Being bottled at source, the specification also covers the finished pack: format, closure grade and TCA limit, fill height, capsule, label compliance for the destination, case and pallet configuration, and the documentation travelling with it.

**A judgement on proportionality.** This is a premium wine likely shipped in small volume, and many buyers would not demand a formal specification at all for a few hundred cases bought on reputation. I would still insist on the sterility and free SO2 clauses, because they are the ones that fail catastrophically.

## b. Entry-level red, 4 g/l residual sugar, imported in bulk

**The spoilage risk.** Four grams reads dry on the palate but residual sugar above roughly 2 g/L is fermentable, so the yeast risk is real, and an entry-level red will sit at a pH where SO2 is weak. The classic bulk failure is an incomplete malolactic conversion restarting in a warm container, so I would specify malic acid below 0.2 g/L at loading, with microbiological counts to match. Acetic acid bacteria feeding on oxygen picked up during transfer are the second risk.

**Transport clauses do the heavy lifting.** Flexitank or ISO tank specified, single-use food-grade liner with a declaration of compliance, full fill with inert headspace, no recycled liner material, temperature monitoring in transit, and free SO2 loaded at 30-35 mg/L so that it arrives above 25 mg/L after the voyage. Dissolved oxygen at loading and on arrival both specified.

**Blending and bottling parameters.** Because the wine will be treated and bottled at destination, the specification behaves like a raw-material specification: alcohol tolerance of plus or minus 0.5% abv, because the figure must survive labelling and duty; colour density and phenolic measures for blending consistency; copper below 0.5 mg/L to avoid reductive and casse problems downstream; and tartrate and protein stability left to be achieved at destination but declared as not yet done.

**Compliance.** Residue and contaminant limits per the destination, including pesticide MRLs and the ochratoxin A limit of 2 micrograms per kilogram in the EU, egg and milk fining residues below 0.25 mg/L, and confirmation that no additive used at origin is prohibited in the market of sale. A pre-shipment sample approved analytically and organoleptically, and a sealed retained sample from the tank for arbitration, are non-negotiable at this volume.

## Finished goods versus raw material

The sweet white's specification defines a product that cannot be corrected; the red's defines an input whose remaining faults will be corrected, and therefore has to tolerance what will be adjusted and control what cannot be — sound condition, no spoilage, and honest paperwork.
