## Tradeoffs & risks

- **Latency cuts the other way.** Sonnet was *slower* than Opus on the two user-facing generative
  tasks: model_answer (87s vs 46s) and full_debrief (54s vs 34s). Cheaper ≠ snappier here — a Sonnet
  shift on real-time paths trades dollars for wait time. Watch this, not just cost.
- **Quality is unproven, not proven-equal.** "0 invalid in both arms" with N=2–5 is the absence of
  evidence, not evidence of absence. A single bad Opus-vs-Sonnet divergence on a high-tariff question
  could erase a month of savings in candidate trust. Hence: keep experimenting, don't switch blind.
- **feedback_analysis / question_generation are input-token heavy.** Their cost is mostly the large
  static system context, so a model swap saves less than the per-call ratio suggests once caching is
  in play — and conversely, caching saves a lot without touching the model.

## Open questions / what to measure next

1. **Gather N on question_generation** — the #1 spend and the only task with a clean quality signal.
   Need ~30/arm + ~10 feedback events/arm to call it.
2. **Prompt caching on feedback_analysis (~39k in) and question_generation (~53k in)** — likely the
   single biggest, zero-risk cost win. Measure cache hit rate and $/call before/after; this may beat
   the Opus→Sonnet decision outright.
3. **Track latency per arm as a first-class metric** for model_answer + full_debrief — the Sonnet
   slowness needs to be weighed against the savings on user-facing paths.
4. **Image-pipeline cost (Tavily) is currently trivial** ($0.49 / 61 searches) thanks to the
   media_cache; the relevance-ranking change (8 candidates vs 5) didn't move it materially. No action.
