# Mock answer — 2024 Paper 2 Question 1

## a) Identify the grape variety (15 marks)

Syrah/Shiraz. All three wines share a core of blue-black fruit — blueberry, black plum, cassis — layered with cracked black pepper, violet and a savoury edge (olive on Wine 1, smoked meat and licorice on Wine 2, charcuterie and coffee on Wine 3) sitting over firm, fine-grained tannin and structuring acidity. Grenache was considered for Wine 1's brighter red-fruit lift, but its paler colour and softer, more granular tannin do not match the depth and grip common to all three glasses. Mourvèdre was weighed against Wine 3's gamey register, but its blood-and-iron signature is absent here; the violet-and-pepper thread, present at every alcohol level from 12.5% to 14.5%, is the clearer marker of Syrah/Shiraz expressed across a spread of climates.

## b) Compare and contrast the styles and consumer appeal of the wines (30 marks)

Wine 2 is the leanest and most structured: 12.5% alcohol, high, taut acidity, firm but fine tannin, and a smoky, granite-inflected reduction of olive and cracked pepper with no discernible new-oak signature — a savoury, food-driven style built for the sommelier by-the-glass list and the terroir-curious specialist consumer rather than mass appeal. Wine 1 sits at the opposite pole of texture despite a similar alcohol band to Wine 3: silky, almost weightless tannin, vivid acidity and a red-and-black fruited profile with a peppery lift, its neutral, well-worn oak keeping fruit rather than wood in front — an approachable, easy-drinking style suited to premium-by-the-glass and everyday fine-dining consumers who want Syrah's spice without heavy tannin. Wine 3 is the most overtly commercial: dense dark fruit, charred coffee and vanilla from prominent new French oak, and firm, ripe tannin built for immediate generosity — the archetypal confident, fruit-driven Shiraz positioned for premium retail and steakhouse-style on-trade, its structure a world away from Wine 2's austerity.

## c) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1** — Aconcagua Valley, Chile. The bright red-and-black fruit, peppery lift and moderate 14% alcohol could initially suggest a cool Northern Rhône site, and that possibility genuinely needs testing against Wine 2; but the riper core of black cherry and cassis, the more polished, silkier tannin, and a faintly saline, maritime freshness in place of granite-derived austerity point instead to a cool, coastal New World site. Moderate alcohol paired with New World fruit purity rather than Old World reduction narrows the call to a cool, coastal Chilean sub-region such as Aconcagua rather than the warmer Colchagua heartland.

**Wine 2** — Cornas, Northern Rhône. The unusually low 12.5% alcohol together with high acidity, firm tannin and a granite-driven note of crushed stone, olive and cracked pepper is the clearest possible Northern Rhône signature; the absence of overt fruit sweetness and the reductive, savoury edge rule out a New World reading. Within the Northern Rhône, the depth of colour and structural power — rather than the more perfumed, floral register typical of Côte-Rôtie — point specifically to Cornas' granite slopes.

**Wine 3** — Barossa Valley, Australia. The highest alcohol of the flight, a dense core of ripe dark fruit, and confident new French oak toast and vanilla are consistent with a warm, dry, old-vine Australian site; the generous, ripe tannin and absence of the herbal, eucalyptus lift found in cooler Australian regions such as Yarra Valley point specifically to Barossa Valley.
