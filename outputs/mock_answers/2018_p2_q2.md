# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 2 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (15 marks)

Pinot Noir. All three wines share a pale-to-medium ruby colour with a garnet-tinged rim, red and black cherry fruit lifted by fine baking spice, and a savoury, faintly earthy undertow beneath the fruit. Tannin is fine-grained and silky rather than grippy, and acidity remains bright relative to alcohols of 13.5–14.1%, giving lightness of touch despite real fruit concentration. Grenache was considered given the ripe red-fruit register, but its typically softer acidity, higher natural alcohol and lack of the savoury, forest-floor lift seen here rule it out. Nebbiolo was also weighed for its high acid and pale colour, but its assertive, grainy tannin and tar-and-rose profile are absent; the finer tannin and more generous mid-palate fruit here point firmly to Pinot Noir.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 6 — Russian River Valley, Sonoma, California, USA.** The darkest fruit of the three, plush black cherry and plum with a cola and baking-spice edge, softer acidity and the highest alcohol of the flight (14.1%) point to a New World site with warm afternoon ripening beneath morning fog — the Russian River signature.

**Wine 7 — Bannockburn, Central Otago, New Zealand.** The most vividly aromatic wine, with wild-thyme and rose lift over red cherry, firmer tannin and the freshest acidity of the trio despite full ripeness, reflects the huge diurnal swing and intense UV of the world's southernmost major wine region; the schistous, savoury edge points specifically to Bannockburn.

**Wine 8 — Willamette Valley, Oregon, USA.** Sitting between the other two, with red cherry and cranberry fruit, a cooler, higher-toned acid line than the Californian wine and an earthy, forest-floor savouriness, this is the most Burgundian in feel — consistent with Willamette's cooler, maritime-influenced climate and volcanic Jory soils.

## c) Discuss quality with reference to winemaking techniques used (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 6** — very good quality, comparable to a premium single-vineyard Russian River bottling retailing around $55–70. The generous, sweet-fruited oak framing and plush texture suggest a meaningful proportion of new French barriques, full malolactic and ripe, extended maceration; the fruit is concentrated enough to absorb the oak without being dominated, though it carries slightly less precision than Wine 7.

**Wine 7** — outstanding quality, equivalent to a top single-vineyard Central Otago wine at $50–65. The aromatic lift and fine, structured tannin suggest a notable whole-cluster component alongside careful, gentler extraction, with French oak used for framing rather than sweetness — the purity of fruit and tension show the most complete integration of ripeness and structure in the flight.

**Wine 8** — very good quality, reserve-tier Willamette Pinot at $40–55. Moderate, well-judged French oak and balanced extraction give polish without masking the red-fruited, savoury core; full malolactic softens the acid line. It shows less concentration than Wine 6 and less aromatic complexity than Wine 7, consistent with a very good rather than outstanding wine.
