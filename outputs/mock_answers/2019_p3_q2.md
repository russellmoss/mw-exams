# Mock answer — 2019 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the region (10 marks)

Both wines show a fine, persistent perlage without the toasty, bready autolytic depth of a traditional-method wine, pointing to secondary fermentation in tank rather than bottle. Pale straw colour, moderate alcohol of 11–11.5%, and pronounced orchard-fruit and blossom aromatics — pear, apple, white flowers — with an off-dry finish are consistent with Glera vinified in the Charmat style. This combination of light body, low alcohol and fresh primary fruit, together with the shared aromatic and structural profile across both glasses, points to the same source region: the Veneto in north-east Italy, home to Prosecco production.

## b) Compare and contrast style and quality (30 marks)

Wine 3 shows greater aromatic definition — layered pear, white peach and acacia blossom rather than simple orchard fruit — with a finer, creamier mousse and more textural grip on the finish, giving genuine length for the category. This precision and structure suggest fruit from the steep, low-yielding hillsides between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, with a slower, cooler tank fermentation and longer lees contact building the extra depth; the vintage date reinforces a single-harvest, higher-tier wine. This is very good quality, equivalent to a Valdobbiadene Prosecco Superiore DOCG at rive or single-vineyard level, retailing around £15–20.

Wine 4 is simpler and more overtly fruity — pear and apple with little secondary complexity — with a coarser bubble, softer acidity and a shorter finish. The perceptible sweetness reads more forward here despite both sitting in the Extra Dry band (worth noting Extra Dry is sweeter than Brut, the reverse of intuition); high-volume, flatland fruit and a shorter tank cycle explain the lighter texture. This is an acceptable-to-good, entry-level Prosecco DOC, correctly positioned as commercial rather than terroir-driven, retailing around £7–9.

## c) Discuss the commercial appeal (10 marks)

Wine 3's precision and finer mousse suit Independent Specialists and premium by-the-glass listings, targeting consumers trading up from generic Prosecco toward Champagne-alternative sparkling; export potential in the UK and US premium off-trade. Wine 4's broad, accessible sweetness and low price point suit supermarket own-label space and high-volume US on-trade pours, competing directly with Cava and basic Crémant for the casual aperitivo and brunch occasion, and driving Prosecco's continued volume growth in export markets.
