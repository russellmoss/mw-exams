# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 2 Question 4

## a) Identify the grape variety and the region as closely as possible (20 marks)

Malbec. Both wines share a deep, dense purple-black colour, ripe blue-black fruit and violet aromatics, carried on a plush, rounded tannin that lacks the fine, high grip of Cabernet Sauvignon and the leafier, red-fruited profile of Cabernet Franc — both eliminated. The high alcohols (13.9% and 14.5%) sit alongside surprisingly fresh, lifted acidity, a combination only altitude readily explains; at low elevation this ripeness would normally flatten the acid line. That freshness-at-high-ripeness signature, together with riper, rounder fruit than the leaner, more rustic tannin of Cahors Malbec, points to Argentina over France. The precision within concentrated fruit narrows this further to high-altitude Uco Valley, Mendoza, rather than the lower, warmer sites of Luján de Cuyo or Maipú.

## b) Compare and contrast style and quality (20 marks)

Wine 9 is the fresher, brighter, less extracted of the pair. The lower 13.9% alcohol, livelier acid line and more restrained, integrated oak point to earlier picking, gentler extraction and a shorter, part-neutral oak regime — a modern, fruit-forward Uco Valley style built for immediate drinking appeal. Quality is very good: pure, balanced and true to place, but moderate concentration and a lighter tannic frame place it at estate/premium-varietal level rather than top tier.

Wine 10 is denser and more structured: firmer, grippier tannin, greater fruit concentration and a more pronounced oak signature of mocha and spice suggest riper old-vine fruit, longer maceration and a more assertive, likely majority-new French oak programme. The higher 14.5% alcohol is still carried by real freshness, again pointing to high-altitude fruit despite the extra ripeness. This is the higher-quality wine: greater depth, complexity and structural grip place it at reserve/old-vine level, with clearer ageing potential, though it currently reads as a touch oak-driven and would benefit from further integration.

## c) Compare and contrast market potential (10 marks)

Wine 9's fresher, approachable style and 13.9% alcohol suit broad on-trade listings and premium supermarket placement, retailing around $15–18; it competes with other polished modern Argentine and Chilean varietal Malbec for consumers wanting accessible, food-friendly reds, with strong potential across UK, US and Canadian off-trade. Wine 10's concentration, oak and old-vine reserve positioning justify a higher price, around $30–40, through independent specialists and by-the-glass listings; it competes with single-site Mendoza bottlings for consumers seeking more serious, age-worthy Malbec, though its lower volumes limit supermarket reach.
