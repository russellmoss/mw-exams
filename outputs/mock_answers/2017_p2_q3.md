# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 2 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety (4 x 8 marks)

**Wine 9** — Cabernet Franc. Medium ruby with a violet edge, light-to-medium body and high acidity frame redcurrant, raspberry, pencil shavings and green pepper with a leafy, herbaceous lift — the pyrazine-and-graphite combination is Cabernet Franc's aromatic signature. Gamay was considered but lacks the capsicum lift and fine, silky tannin shown here; a Right Bank Bordeaux blend is eliminated by the absence of Merlot's fleshier mid-palate and by the restrained 12.5% alcohol.

**Wine 10** — Pinot Noir. Pale-medium ruby, red cherry, strawberry and rose petal with subtle oak spice, fine silky tannin and fresh acidity point unmistakably to Pinot Noir's aromatic and textural blueprint. Gamay is ruled out by the finer tannin architecture and integrated oak; the riper fruit and firmer structure at 13.5% alcohol argue this is a warmer, more serious expression than basic Burgundy village wine.

**Wine 11** — Pinotage. Deep, opaque ruby-purple with ripe dark plum and blackberry, a distinctive smoky rubber-and-acetone note, mocha and coffee from heavy oak, and dense, grippy tannin at 14.5% alcohol. That smoky, sweet-tar volatile character is diagnostic and shared by no other major variety; Shiraz was briefly weighed for its own smoky register but lacks the acetone lift and shows coarser, peppery tannin. This calls for a direct, confident identification.

**Wine 12** — Lagrein. Deep, almost inky purple-black, medium-full body, firm chalky tannin and high, fresh acidity carry dark bramble and violet fruit into a bitter-chocolate, leathery finish at a restrained 13% alcohol. This combination of very deep colour with high acid and moderate alcohol points to Lagrein. Teroldego, its close relative, was considered but typically shows softer, rounder tannin; Syrah and Merlot are eliminated by the absence of black pepper or fleshy plum richness and by the more angular acid line here.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (4 x 8 marks)

**Wine 9** — Chinon, Touraine, Loire Valley, France. The high acidity, fine silky tannin and restrained 12.5% alcohol point to a cool-climate site rather than the riper Right Bank of Bordeaux. Red-fruit precision alongside graphite minerality suggests limestone-and-tufa soils typical of the mid-Loire, narrowing to Chinon or neighbouring Bourgueil; the particularly fine, sand-inflected tannin and violet lift point specifically to Chinon.

**Wine 10** — Baden, Germany. The riper red-fruit register and 13.5% alcohol — unusually high for cool-climate Pinot Noir — argue against Burgundy or the Ahr. The polished oak spice and fine tannin suit a serious German Pinot Noir project rather than basic Qualitätswein. Combined with the ripeness and structure, this points to Baden, Germany's warmest Spätburgunder region, ahead of cooler Pfalz.

**Wine 11** — Stellenbosch, Western Cape, South Africa. The variety itself is confirmatory: Pinotage is an exclusively South African crossing of Pinot Noir and Cinsaut, so identification narrows the country immediately. The depth of colour, generosity of fruit and 14.5% alcohol point to a warm inland site rather than a cooler coastal ward; the concentrated tannin and heavy new-oak treatment suit premium Stellenbosch rather than the leaner style typical of Swartland.

**Wine 12** — Südtirol/Alto Adige, Trentino-Alto Adige, Italy. The Alpine combination of very deep colour with high acidity and firm tannin at a restrained 13% alcohol points to a cool, high-diurnal-range site rather than a warm Mediterranean one, consistent with northern Italy rather than Tuscany or Puglia. The bitter-chocolate, mineral-fresh finish suits Lagrein from the valley-floor gravels around Bolzano rather than Trentino further south.

## c) Comment on quality and commercial potential, within the context of the region of origin (4 x 9 marks)

**Wine 9** — Very good quality, comparable to a well-sited single-vineyard Chinon rather than a basic village blend; the concentration and fine tannin lift it above entry Chinon retailing around $12-15. Realistic positioning here is $18-22 through Independent Specialists and Loire-focused importers. Rising interest in cool-climate Cabernet Franc supports export into the UK, Belgium and USA, competing with village Bourgueil and light Beaujolais Cru.

**Wine 10** — Outstanding quality, equivalent to VDP Erste Lage or GG level — fine tannin, integrated oak and concentration place it well above generic Qualitätswein Spätburgunder. Realistic retail is $45-60. Germany's growing Pinot Noir reputation supports placement through Independent Specialists and fine-wine merchants targeting Burgundy-curious consumers; export interest in the UK, USA and Scandinavia, competing with premium Oregon Pinot Noir and entry-level Burgundy Premier Cru.

**Wine 11** — Very good to excellent quality, equivalent to a premium single-estate Stellenbosch bottling rather than a commercial branded Pinotage retailing under $12. Bush-vine concentration and judicious new-oak integration lift it into the $22-28 bracket. Good commercial prospects among drinkers seeking South African identity wines — UK premium multiple tiers, USA, the Netherlands — positioned as an alternative to New World Shiraz, though the variety's rustic reputation remains a challenge to overcome.

**Wine 12** — Good to very good quality, equivalent to a serious DOC estate bottling above basic Alto Adige Lagrein but below the region's top single-vineyard riserva — firm tannin and fresh acid give real structure without the concentration a flagship cuvée would show. Retail around $20-25 through Independent Specialists and Italian-focused restaurant lists. Strong domestic on-trade demand plus a developing export niche in the UK and Germany among drinkers seeking structured alternatives to Valpolicella or northern Rhône Syrah.
