# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the region of origin (4 x 5 marks)

**Wine 5** — Deep garnet with only a hint of brick at the rim, sweet dark fruit still knitted to gripping tannin and a warming 20% alcohol place this as a fortified red matured reductively in bottle rather than oxidatively in cask — the classic Vintage Port profile. Douro, Portugal.

**Wine 6** — Near-opaque purple-black colour and primary, peppery blackberry fruit immediately mark a much younger wine than Wine 5, and the riper, more chocolatey register lacks the savoury tobacco-iron edge of a Douro field blend, pointing instead to a warm New World Shiraz base. Barossa Valley, South Australia — since "Port" is a protected term, this is an Australian fortified wine in the vintage-Port idiom rather than true Port.

**Wine 7** — Amber-mahogany, viscous, with walnut and caramelised notes rather than fresh fruit signal oxidative ageing without flor protection — an Oloroso base, subsequently sweetened. The high 20.5% alcohol and rancio lift confirm Jerez, Andalucía, Spain.

**Wine 8** — A sharper, more acid-driven amber-mahogany with a burnt-sugar, volatile lift distinct from Wine 7's smoother rancio signals heat-driven maderisation rather than simple oxidative ageing. Madeira, Portugal.

Two of the four — Wines 5 and 8 — share a country, yet Douro and Madeira are entirely different fortified traditions: one reductive and bottle-matured, the other deliberately heated. The remaining two, Spanish Sherry and Australian fortified Shiraz, complete a flight that tests style recognition as much as geography.

## b) Comment on quality and maturity (4 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — Very good to outstanding quality, equivalent to a top single-quinta declaration, retailing around $80-100. Deep but brick-edged colour and fully integrated tertiary notes of dried fig, tobacco and cedar indicate a wine of some seventeen years, now in full maturity; the tannin, while softened, has not dried out, so it will hold at this plateau for a further 5-10 years without material further improvement.

**Wine 6** — Good to very good quality for the style, an approachable premium fortified retailing around $30-40. The saturated colour and primary fruit indicate a wine of only three years, still in its infancy; it has the concentration to develop for a further 5-8 years and hold thereafter, gaining dried-fruit and spice complexity but never approaching the savoury depth of Wine 5.

**Wine 7** — Outstanding quality, VORS-equivalent tier, priced well above $60 for a half-bottle. The deep colour, rancio intensity and layered dried-fruit, toffee and orange-peel complexity confirm a very old average-age blend; as a solera wine there is no single vintage to date, but the depth argues for a multi-decade average age, fully mature and stable, capable of holding for years once opened.

**Wine 8** — Very good quality, upper Colheita tier, around $40-50. The amber-orange hue and searing acid alongside marmalade and roasted-nut complexity indicate a wine of some nineteen years, fully developed; the structural acidity is Madeira's guarantee of longevity, so the wine will hold for decades yet, showing only gradual further concentration of nutty, caramelised notes.

## c) Discuss the method of production (4 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — The dense colour and firm tannin structure suggest vigorous extraction, most likely foot-treading or mechanical lagares, before fermentation was arrested with grape spirit at around 20% alcohol, leaving substantial residual sugar. The still-primary spirit lift and unresolved tannin indicate no more than two to three years in wood before bottling, with the bulk of ageing and softening happening reductively in glass over the subsequent decade and more.

**Wine 6** — The same principle applied in a hotter climate: fermentation was similarly halted with spirit while still sweet, but the riper, more opulent fruit and softer tannin point to a warmer growing season and probably a shorter, more commercially-driven post-fortification maturation before release, consistent with the wine's youth.

**Wine 7** — The absence of any fresh or biologically-aged (flor) character shows this base wine was deliberately fermented fully dry and fortified above the flor threshold, forcing oxidative ageing from the outset. The concentrated sweetness with no fruity primary character indicates blending with a Pedro Ximénez or must-concentrate component after ageing, matured through an extended criadera y solera system that fractionally blends multiple vintages — hence no single vintage date.

**Wine 8** — Fermentation and fortification were followed by deliberate exposure to warmth; the smooth, rounded caramelised profile without any harsh baked quality argues against rapid estufagem and for the slower canteiro method, natural loft-warmth ageing over many years, which preserves finer aromatics while still delivering the maderised, high-acid signature that separates this wine from every other fortified style in the flight.
