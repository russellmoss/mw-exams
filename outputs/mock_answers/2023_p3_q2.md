# Mock answer — 2023 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the origin and grape variety/ies as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5.** Traditional method rosé from a cool northern French origin, a blend of Pinot Noir, Pinot Meunier and Chardonnay. The fine, persistent bead confirms second fermentation in bottle rather than tank or injected carbonation, and the pronounced brioche and biscuit autolytic character points to extended lees ageing consistent with a non-vintage cuvée of several years. The racy acidity at only 12% alcohol rules out a warmer regional sparkling and, combined with the depth of autolytic complexity, narrows this to Champagne rather than Crémant.

**Wine 6.** A barrel-influenced premium rosé from Côtes de Provence, Grenache-led with Cinsault, Syrah and Rolle. The onion-skin copper hue is deeper and the texture broader and creamier than the pale, neutral direct-press template of generic Provence pink, and the 14% alcohol with integrated toast and spice confirms oak handling rather than simple stainless-steel vinification. Bandol was considered given its similar barrel-aged tradition, but the softer tannic grip and lighter, Grenache-dominant fruit rule out Mourvèdre-driven Bandol in favour of Côtes de Provence.

**Wine 7.** An off-dry, lightly spritzy commercial rosé from Portugal, a blend of indigenous red varieties, Baga-led. The pale pink colour, gentle natural spritz, off-dry residual sugar and modest 11% alcohol, allied to simple candied strawberry and floral fruit with no lees or oak signature, point to a large-volume blended style rather than a single-appellation wine. Vinho Verde rosé was considered but eliminated by the riper red-fruit profile rather than Vinho Verde's characteristic green apple and citrus.

## b) Discuss quality and commercial potential (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5.** Very good quality, equivalent to a premium house-style NV Brut Rosé Champagne, above entry-level Brut NV on length and lees-derived complexity; retailing around £45-55. Sold through Independent Specialists, premium supermarkets and by-the-glass in on-trade bars; competes with grower Champagne and premium English sparkling rosé; strong in the UK, US and Japan.

**Wine 6.** Outstanding quality for Côtes de Provence, at the top of the appellation and closer to a prestige cuvée than the generic AOC template; the concentration and oak integration justify £45-70. Positioned in fine dining and premium on-trade, competing with top-end white Burgundy and luxury Provence rosé; appeals to affluent, food-focused consumers and performs strongly in US and UK summer/luxury channels.

**Wine 7.** A sound, fault-free entry-level branded style, retailing around £6-8; simple and short but commercially effective. Sold through supermarkets and casual by-the-glass off-trade, targeting occasional drinkers wanting an easy, off-dry pink; historically a high-volume export success in the UK, Germany and US.

## c) Identify the key production techniques that have influenced the style (3 x 7 marks)

**Wine 5.** Base wines from cool-climate Pinot Noir, Meunier and Chardonnay; colour likely achieved through blending a small proportion of still red wine into the white base, the exception uniquely permitted in Champagne, though a brief saignée cannot be ruled out given the fruit intensity. Second fermentation in bottle with tirage, several years on lees explaining the autolytic depth, and a Brut dosage rounding the racy acidity.

**Wine 6.** Early, hand-picked fruit, gently whole-bunch or direct pressed with minimal skin contact to retain the pale-to-copper hue; a portion barrel-fermented and aged on fine lees, explaining the creamy texture and integrated spice, then blended back with fresher stainless-steel lots to preserve aromatic lift alongside structure and length.

**Wine 7.** Fruit sourced and blended across regions for a consistent branded profile; fermentation stopped or sweetened via dosage to leave off-dry residual sugar; the light spritz from partial retention of fermentation CO2 or gentle tank carbonation rather than traditional method, built for immediate, chilled, large-scale consumption over complexity.
