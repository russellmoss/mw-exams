# Mock answer — 2023 Paper 3 Question 1

## a) Identify the origin and grape variety/ies as closely as possible (4 x 13 marks)

**Wine 1.** Pale lemon with a fine, persistent bead; apple, brioche and a waxy, faintly herbal, saline edge sit over a bone-dry, textural palate. That waxy phenolic weight, rather than the linear, chalky acid line a Chardonnay/Pinot Meunier base would give, points away from a Champagne-variety blend towards the traditional Iberian trio of Xarel·lo, Macabeo and Parellada — Xarel·lo supplying the waxy body, Macabeo the floral apple lift, Parellada the freshness. The deep, integrated toast against a completely dry finish argues for extended tirage well beyond the statutory minimum, consistent with Cava DO from Penedès rather than a generic Spanish sparkling wine.

**Wine 2.** A softer, more floral profile — white blossom, ripe pear, light brioche — with rounder, less searing acidity than either Wine 1 or Wine 3. This gentler aromatic register and creamier texture suggest a Pinot Blanc/Auxerrois-led blend rather than a pure Chardonnay base; the moderate rather than deep autolytic depth is consistent with lees ageing closer to the statutory nine-month minimum. The spicy, blossom-driven aromatics, distinct from Cava's waxier fruit and from English sparkling's citrus purity, point to Alsace — Crémant d'Alsace.

**Wine 3.** Pale lemon-gold, taut and linear, with pure lemon, green apple and white flowers over a pronounced flinty, chalky minerality and racy, high acidity; the single-note fruit purity, with no red-berry or spice component from Pinot, indicates a 100% Chardonnay base — Blanc de Blancs. Riper, more tropical fruit would suggest a warmer New World Blanc de Blancs, but the searing acid and green, understated ripeness argue for cool, marginal viticulture on chalk/greensand — English sparkling wine, West Sussex. The harmonious integration of citrus fruit with deep bready, biscuity notes, alongside a stated vintage, confirms an extended lees regime well beyond entry level.

**Wine 4.** Pale lemon with green tints, intensely varietal — lime, green apple, white flowers and a distinct petrol topnote over wet slate — is unmistakably Riesling rather than a Champagne-variety or Iberian blend, immediately marking this out as the odd one among the four. Structure and concentration at 13% alcohol, together with pronounced slate minerality, are riper and weightier than typical Mosel Sekt and argue for the Rheingau; the intensity and precision of fruit are consistent with a base wine sourced from a top Grosses Lage site such as Marcobrunn, unusually serious raw material for a sparkling cuvée.

## b) Comment on quality in the context of origin (4 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Outstanding quality for Cava — the depth of autolytic complexity, textural richness and length place this at Gran Reserva level (minimum 30 months on lees), well above standard Cava Reserva (18 months); retailing around $30-40, it rivals mid-tier Champagne on complexity while retaining a distinctly Iberian, indigenous-variety identity rather than leaning on an added Chardonnay component as many premium Cavas now do.

**Wine 2.** Very good quality, a leading commercial expression of Crémant d'Alsace, around $20-25 retail — clean, precise and true to its floral, Pinot-family typicity, but the moderate depth of autolysis and simpler length place it below the complexity of a prestige-tier Crémant or grower Champagne.

**Wine 3.** Outstanding quality, comparable to a serious vintage-dated Grande Marque Champagne — the concentration, chalky drive and seamless integration of primary and secondary characters justify a $45-60 price point and confirm England's top sparkling tier, not merely a promising cool-climate curiosity.

**Wine 4.** Very good to outstanding quality — sourcing from a Grosses-Lage-calibre site lifts this well above generic Deutscher Sekt; the precision and structure justify a premium position around $35-45, near the top of the still-developing German Sekt category.

## c) State the residual sugar (4 x 2 marks)

**Wine 1** — Bone-dry, no perceptible sweetness against the racy acid: Brut Nature, under 3 g/L.
**Wine 2** — Fruit-forward but balanced, without Brut Nature austerity: Brut-level dosage, around 8-10 g/L.
**Wine 3** — Taut finish with only a faint softening of high acid: Brut, roughly 6-9 g/L.
**Wine 4** — Piercing acid barely softened: Extra Brut, around 3-5 g/L.
