# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 1 Question 3

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible with reference to the grape variety used (2 x 12 marks)

**Wine 6.** Pale lemon with subtle, understated aromas of white blossom, pear skin and almond, over a saline, volcanic edge; the palate is dry, medium-bodied, gently phenolic and closes on a distinctive bitter-almond note, with moderate acidity and a restrained 12% alcohol. Verdicchio was considered for its own almond signature, but that variety usually carries higher, more citrus-driven acidity and less textural weight; Trebbiano was eliminated as too neutral to give this blossom-and-almond complexity. The bitter-almond finish is the killer marker for Garganega. The saline, volcanic character and the precision of the fruit, rather than a broad, commercial style, point to the basalt hillside sites of Soave Classico in the Veneto rather than the flatter, higher-volume Soave DOC plain.

**Wine 7.** Pale-to-medium lemon, more overtly floral with pear, white peach and a herbal, fennel lift; the palate is dry, softer and rounder in acidity than the first wine, fuller-bodied at 13.5% alcohol, with a gently bitter, almond-edged finish. Gavi was considered but Cortese typically shows crisper, more citrus-focused acidity without this pear and fennel aromatic profile; Fiano was also eliminated, being more honeyed and tropical in warmer climates. The rounder texture, riper alcohol and fennel-tinged finish point to Arneis, a variety nearly lost to extinction before its 1980s revival, grown on the sandy soils of Roero in Piedmont — its home DOCG.

## b) Discuss the wine's style and quality; in which area of the trade would this wine be most successful? (2 x 13 marks)

**Wine 6.** A dry, delicate, texture-led style built on saline minerality rather than overt fruit — restrained by design, not by dilution. The purity of fruit, chalky-saline length and site-driven character mark this as a very good quality Soave Classico, well above the generic, high-volume Soave that devalued the name; equivalent to a serious Classico bottling retailing around $18–25. It would find its best success with independent specialists and by-the-glass restaurant lists, where staff can tell the delicacy story against Soave's poor commercial reputation, and with export markets seeking a food-friendly alternative to Chablis or Muscadet.

**Wine 7.** A dry, fuller, more overtly aromatic and textural style than Wine 6, with softer acidity that invites earlier drinking. Good varietal purity, pear and white-peach fruit and a gently bitter finish give real personality, placing this as a very good quality Roero Arneis, around $20–28, just below the most concentrated old-vine examples. Its natural home is on-trade by-the-glass and premium supermarket ranges, appealing to consumers trading up from Pinot Grigio toward more textured Italian whites, and it competes well in North American independent channels as an alternative to Viognier or unoaked Chardonnay.
