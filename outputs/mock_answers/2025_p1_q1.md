# Mock answer — 2025 Paper 1 Question 1

## a) Identify the grape varieties (10 marks)

Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon in both wines. Wine 1 carries Sémillon's fingerprint — waxy honeysuckle and candied orange rind beneath racy acidity, giving real mid-palate weight and age-worthy structure — while Wine 2 is Sauvignon-led, its passionfruit, gooseberry and lemongrass lift dominant, with Sémillon showing only as a softening of texture. Muscadelle was considered given the Old World setting but the pronounced grassy, gooseberry-driven cut across both wines rules it out. That contrast — one Sémillon-weighted, one Sauvignon-weighted — is itself consistent with the same two-variety blend at differing ratios, exactly what the shared-variety stem implies.

## b) Identify the origin (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 1** — Pessac-Léognan, Bordeaux. The moderate 12.5% alcohol, taut mineral finish and barrel-fermented texture point to Bordeaux's dry white heartland; the Sémillon-forward weight and precise, savoury oak integration are textbook Pessac-Léognan rather than a simple Entre-Deux-Mers, which would show plainer fruit and little oak ambition.

**Wine 2** — Margaret River, Western Australia. The higher 13% alcohol, purer varietal fruit and mineral-tinged finish point instead to a New World maritime climate, ruling out cooler Loire Sauvignon. The gravelly-inflected finish and confident, judicious Sémillon component favour Margaret River over Marlborough, which rarely blends the two with such poise.

## c) Style, winemaking and quality (2 x 12 marks)

**Wine 1** is a serious, barrel-fermented dry white built for age: no malolactic keeps the piercing acid pure, while fermentation in a high proportion of new French oak followed by 16-18 months' lees ageing in barrel explains the toasty spice and creamy texture beneath the citrus and orange-rind fruit. Outstanding quality, equivalent to a leading unclassified Pessac-Léognan white produced to Cru Classé standards, retailing around $50-70 — markedly more structured than the second wine.

**Wine 2** is a fresher, fruit-driven modern style: cool stainless-steel fermentation with aromatic yeast strains preserves the primary passionfruit and lime, while a small barrel-fermented Sauvignon component and brief lees contact add subtle texture without masking freshness; mostly neutral oak and no malolactic keep it crisp rather than broad. Very good quality for premium Margaret River Sauvignon-Sémillon, a benchmark of the region's signature blend at around $20-25, offering strong value against Wine 1's structured ageability.
