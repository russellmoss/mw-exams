# Mock answer — 2024 Paper 3 Question 4

## a) State the level of residual sugar (5 x 2 marks)

Wine 8 is very sweet, with dense, syrupy sugar suggesting a level well above 200 g/L. Wine 9 is also very sweet, luscious and dessert-level though a touch less viscous than Wine 8. Wine 10 is very sweet, its sugar carried on a dense, oxidative frame. Wine 11 is very sweet, with sugar sitting alongside a warming, spirity finish. Wine 12 is only medium sweet — noticeably less than the other four, with sugar in the 15-25 g/L demi-sec band rather than dessert territory.

## b) State the alcohol level (5 x 2 marks)

Wine 8 is low, around 9.5%. Wine 9 is low-medium, around 11%. Wine 10 is medium-high, around 15.5%. Wine 11 is medium-high, around 15% with a distinct spirit note. Wine 12 is medium, around 12%. This spread is the single most useful clue in the flight: the two lowest-alcohol wines point away from fortification, the two mid-fifteens wines split into one clearly spirit-warmed and one clearly grape-derived, and Wine 12's combination of moderate alcohol with modest sweetness marks it as the arrested-fermentation outlier rather than a dessert wine proper.

## c) Identify the region and grape variety/ies (5 x 11 marks)

**Wine 8.** The very low 9.5% alcohol against very high sugar, allied to piercing, almost citric acidity and vivid, unbaked tropical and candied-citrus fruit with no honeyed or nutty lift, points to freeze concentration rather than botrytis or passerillage — noble rot was considered but rejected as there is no waxy, honeyed botrytis signature, and the acidity is racier than botrytis wines typically show since freezing concentrates sugar and acid in parallel rather than metabolising acid. This is Icewine. The variety shows the marmalade-and-mango register typical of Vidal rather than the more floral, petrol-edged profile of Riesling Eiswein, and commercial icewine at this scale is essentially confined to Canada, pointing to Niagara Peninsula, Ontario.

**Wine 9.** Bright gold colour with pronounced honeyed, waxy lift alongside stone fruit and orange blossom, at a modest 11% alcohol, indicates botrytis rather than frozen or dried fruit — the honeyed complexity rules out Icewine, and the clean, non-oxidative freshness rules out passerillage. This is Beerenauslese. The blend register — a pure, fruit-driven style without the varietal signature of Semillon-Sauvignon botrytis wines — together with Welschriesling's presence points to Central Europe rather than Sauternes, and the shallow-lake botrytis conditions and Pradikat framework confirm Burgenland, Austria.

**Wine 10.** Amber-gold colour with pronounced oxidative walnut, almond and caramelised citrus, at a comparatively high 15.5% alcohol for an unfortified wine, points to dried-grape passerillage followed by long oxidative ageing rather than botrytis or fortification — the nutty, developed character lacks any noble-rot honey note, and the alcohol is too high for a natural botrytis stop yet there is no spirit heat to suggest fortification. This is Vin Santo. The Malvasia-Trebbiano register and the DOC-specific caratello ageing style are unmistakably Tuscan, narrowing to Chianti Classico.

**Wine 11.** Pale gold colour with explosively grapey, floral Muscat aromatics preserved in pristine, primary condition, alongside 15% alcohol and a distinct spirit warmth on the finish, points to fortification arresting fermentation mid-ferment rather than natural concentration — the alcohol is too high to have arisen from unfortified fermentation at this sweetness, and the aromatic freshness rules out oxidative ageing. This is a Muscat vin doux naturel from Muscat à Petits Grains Blancs. The purity and elevation over Rivesaltes-style Muscat of Alexandria points south of the Rhône proper, to Beaumes-de-Venise.

**Wine 12.** Medium sweetness at a moderate 12% alcohol, with sugar balanced by the firm, phenolic-edged acidity and quince-apple register of Chenin Blanc, points to a natural, cool-fermented arrest rather than botrytis or fortification — the RS is far below dessert level and there is no honeyed or spirit character. This is demi-sec Chenin Blanc from the mid-Loire, and the chalky mineral cut and structured, ageworthy feel point specifically to Vouvray.

## d) Comment on the winemaking (5 x 10 marks)

**Wine 8.** Vidal was left on the vine into deep winter until natural frost, around -10C or colder, froze the water within each berry; pressing while still frozen extracts only the concentrated sugar-rich juice, with ice crystals left in the press cake, explaining the tiny yields and extreme concentration. Fermentation stalled naturally once rising alcohol and sugar overwhelmed the yeast, leaving very high RS; no oak character is apparent, consistent with stainless-steel handling to preserve pure fruit.

**Wine 9.** Selective hand-picking, likely over several passes, targeted botrytis-shrivelled berries once noble rot had concentrated sugars and added honeyed compounds; slow fermentation of this dense must stalled naturally near 11% alcohol, leaving high RS. The clean, non-oxidative freshness indicates stainless-steel fermentation and maturation rather than barrel ageing, preserving primary botrytis lift over wood influence.

**Wine 10.** Hand-harvested Malvasia and Trebbiano were dried for months on mats in ventilated lofts, dehydrating the berries and concentrating sugars through passerillage; the resulting must fermented and matured, undisturbed, for around six years in small sealed caratelli, where slow oxygen ingress through the wood produced the walnut and caramelised-citrus development. The higher 15.5% alcohol than the botrytis or Icewine examples reflects the greater sugar concentration achieved by drying before fermentation.

**Wine 11.** Fully ripe Muscat was hand-picked to maximise aromatic intensity, then fermentation was begun and deliberately arrested by mutage — the addition of neutral grape spirit partway through — which raised alcohol to around 15% and killed the yeast, locking in residual sugar and primary floral aromatics before any oxidative or botrytis-type development could occur. Minimal subsequent ageing in inert vessel preserves that freshness.

**Wine 12.** Chenin was picked with some extended hang time to build ripeness and modest botrytis-derived concentration, then fermented slowly in a cool cellar; fermentation was arrested, likely by chilling, racking and filtration, once around 20 g/L residual sugar remained, balanced against Chenin's naturally high acidity to give a demi-sec rather than fully sweet result. Ageing in neutral vessel preserves fruit purity and the wine's structured, ageworthy grip.
