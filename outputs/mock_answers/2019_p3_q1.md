# Mock answer — 2019 Paper 3 Question 1

## a) Identify the region (10 marks)

Both show fine, persistent mousse, high, precise acidity and pronounced chalky minerality alongside autolytic bread/brioche notes — classic traditional-method sparkling from a cool, chalk-soil origin. Wine 1's floral, apple-citrus fruit with soft, well-integrated dosage and Wine 2's deeper, toasted, nutty complexity with a specific single-village character both sit comfortably within Champagne rather than English sparkling or Crémant, which would show less depth of autolysis and less textural weight at this level. **Champagne.**

## b) Compare and contrast the method of production (15 marks)

Wine 1's consistent, polished fruit and moderate autolytic depth point to a multi-vintage assemblage of reserve wines drawn across several villages, built to hold a fixed house style; full malolactic softens the acidity, and the fresh, light finish with a generous but restrained dosage suggests the AOC-minimum 15 months' lees ageing typical of large-house NV. Wine 2 shows far greater depth, texture and savoury, oxidative complexity, indicating a single vintage from Grand Cru sites, likely Pinot Noir-dominant; the rich, creamy mousse and toasted-hazelnut layering suggest partial barrel fermentation and several years' extended lees ageing with late disgorgement, while the bone-dry, taut finish points to very low dosage rather than standard Brut.

## c) Compare and contrast the style, quality and commercial appeal (25 marks)

Wine 1 is very good quality, equivalent to a well-made grande marque Brut NV: fresh, consistent, immediately approachable, drinking now with little further development expected. It retails around £35 through premium supermarkets, on-trade by-the-glass and international duty-free, aimed at the mainstream celebratory consumer, competing directly with other house-style NV Champagnes. Wine 2 is outstanding quality — a Grand Cru, single-village classification confirms fruit sourced only from top-rated communes, and the fact a vintage was declared at all signals a year judged good enough to stand alone; twelve years on disgorgement shows exceptional development, still fresh yet layered with dried-fruit and toasted complexity, drinking beautifully now with scope to hold further. It commands £90-plus through independent specialists and sommelier-led fine dining, targeting collectors and Champagne connoisseurs in markets such as the UK, US and Japan — the finer, more distinctive wine, but its scarcity and price mean it can never substitute commercially for Wine 1's scale and consistency.
