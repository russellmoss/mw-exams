# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 2 Question 4

## a) Country, region and predominant grape varieties (20 marks)

Both wines share deep ruby-purple colour, ripe black-fruit concentration and confident new-oak sweetness pointing away from Europe toward a warm New World origin; the ripe tannin and 14-14.5% alcohol rule out cooler regions such as Bordeaux or coastal Chile. The plush, high-extract fruit and assured oak handling are more consistent with Napa Valley than a leaner Coonawarra Cabernet or rustic Mendoza red.

Wine 11's soft, plump core of plum, black cherry and cassis, rounder tannin and vanilla-mocha sweetness from largely American oak is generous rather than structured, pointing to Merlot and ruling out a Cabernet-led wine. Wine 12's firmer, linear tannic spine, blackcurrant-cedar-graphite profile and French rather than American oak point to a Cabernet Sauvignon-dominant Bordeaux-style blend with Merlot and minor varieties in support; the tighter structure rules out a varietal Merlot call. Both sit toward the lower end of Napa's alcohol range rather than 15%+, consistent with a cooler, later-picked 2011 vintage.

## b) Method of production (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 11.** The plush style and soft tannin suggest gentle cap management, pump-overs rather than punch-downs, with an extended maceration to build colour and mid-palate weight. The vanilla-mocha character points to American oak forming a significant share of a 16-18 month barrel regime, a mix of new and once-used rather than all-new wood.

**Wine 12.** The firmer tannic spine and cedar-graphite complexity point to separate-lot fermentation and barrel-ageing by variety ahead of final assembly. The overt toast and fine-grained tannin indicate predominantly new French oak over a longer élevage of around 18-20 months, with the blending decision itself — the proportion of Cabernet Sauvignon to Merlot and minor varieties — the key choice shaping structure.

## c) Quality within the context of the region of origin (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 11.** A generous, well-made Napa Merlot with good concentration and a long, oak-sweetened finish — very good quality, equivalent to a premium commercial Napa Valley bottling retailing around $25-30, below single-vineyard or reserve level. Given 2011's cool, difficult growing season, the ripeness and balance shown are commendable rather than the dilution or green tannin that marked lesser Napa reds that vintage; judged against 2011 rather than a benchmark warm Napa year, this is a successful, well-judged wine.

**Wine 12.** A more structured, complex wine with greater depth and ageing potential than Wine 11 — outstanding quality, equivalent to a flagship Napa Bordeaux-blend retailing around $45, above Wine 11's tier though below cult-Cabernet pricing. The firmer tannin and restrained fruit again reflect 2011's cooler character, but here lend welcome structure rather than austerity — a more Bordeaux-leaning expression of the vintage. Still primary, it has the tannic backbone to develop further over the medium term.
