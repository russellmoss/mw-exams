# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 2 Question 3

## a) Identify the sub-region as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

Both wines show the pale-to-medium ruby colour, red-cherry and raspberry fruit, dried-herb and earthy undertones, and racy acidity typical of Pinot Noir, so variety is not in question here — the exercise is placing each within Burgundy's hierarchy of sub-regions.

**Wine 7** is light-to-medium in body, with fresh red fruit, gentle spice and only moderate tannin at 12.5% alcohol. This lack of concentration and the comparatively low alcohol point away from a warm, mid-slope Côte de Nuits site and toward one of the cooler, higher-elevation zones set back from the main slope — the Hautes Côtes. The finish is pleasant but short, consistent with a regional or sub-regional appellation rather than a village or Cru site.

**Wine 8** shows noticeably darker fruit, rose-petal and forest-floor complexity, broader and finer-grained tannin, and a full 14% alcohol with real depth and persistence on the finish. That combination of ripeness, extract and length is well beyond village level and points to a mid-slope Côte de Nuits Grand Cru site — the concentration argues specifically for a large, historic climat such as Clos de Vougeot rather than a smaller, more marginal Grand Cru.

## b) Comment on the key winemaking techniques used (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 7's** pure, primary red fruit and light tannic grip suggest a largely destemmed ferment with restrained extraction and ageing in mostly neutral or older oak, protecting freshness over structure — consistent with a wine built for early drinking. **Wine 8's** riper, sweeter tannin and spice/toast lift point to a proportion of whole-bunch inclusion, longer cuvaison and maturation in a meaningful share of new French oak, giving the density and structural backbone this style demands.

## c) Comment on the style and quality (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7** is a good-quality, correctly made regional Bourgogne — retailing around £18–22 — offering typicity and drinkability rather than depth; drink now to 2–3 years. **Wine 8** is outstanding, Grand Cru quality — commanding £150 and above — with the concentration, structural complexity and long finish expected at this level; it will improve for 8–10 years and hold well beyond. The gap between them is one of site and hierarchy rather than a warm 2019 against an easier 2017, since both vintages were generous — the alcohol and structural step is the terroir speaking.
