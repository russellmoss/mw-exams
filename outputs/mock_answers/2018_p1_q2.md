# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (6 x 7 marks)

**Wine 5** — Semillon. The strikingly low 10% alcohol against bone-dry, high, racy acidity and toasted, honeyed, lanolin-and-lime-curd complexity with no trace of oak is the classic unwooded Hunter signature; this low-alcohol ripeness pattern is variety-driven, from grapes picked well ahead of full physiological ripeness. Aged Riesling was considered for the toast and lime, but the waxy lanolin texture and broader mid-palate weight, without any petrol or floral lift, rule it out.

**Wine 6** — Viura. A neutral, textured base grape is needed to survive this level of deliberate oxidation without collapsing, and the nutty, waxy, almost rancio character over a dry, moderate-acid frame is consistent with Viura rather than a more aromatic Spanish white. Fino Sherry was considered given the sherried, nutty profile, but the 12.5% alcohol confirms an unfortified table wine, not a flor-aged fortified style.

**Wine 7** — Grüner Veltliner. The white pepper and green lentil note alongside crisp, precise acidity and dry, medium-plus body is the variety's unmistakable calling card. Riesling was briefly considered for its Austrian pedigree, but the savoury, peppery register in place of floral lime and petrol settles the call.

**Wine 8** — Garganega. Almond, chamomile and white blossom over a saline, understated citrus core, with textural weight and a bitter-almond finish, point to Garganega. A generic Pinot Grigio or Trebbiano blend was considered given the pale colour, but neither shows this fruit concentration or the distinctive almond-mineral finish.

**Wine 9** — Chenin Blanc. Ripe quince, guava and waxy honeysuckle on a dry, full, textured palate are hallmark Chenin, tipped toward a warmer, riper expression than Loire examples. Viognier was considered for the textural richness, but higher acidity and the absence of overt floral pungency argue against it.

**Wine 10** — Torrontés. An explosively floral, lychee-and-rose-petal nose initially suggests Muscat or Gewürztraminer, but the dry, fresh, firm palate with no spice weight or grapey sweetness is the classic Torrontés aroma-palate mismatch — heady on the nose, lean and dry on the palate.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (6 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5** — Hunter Valley, New South Wales, Australia. Toast and honey with no oak input is only explained by bottle development in a warm, humid site that forces very early picking to retain acid; this low-alcohol, unwooded, developed style is essentially unique to the Hunter.

**Wine 6** — Rioja Alta, Rioja DOCa, Spain. The deep amber colour and pronounced oxidative, nutty, waxy character sit well outside modern, pale, fresh white Rioja, pointing instead to the traditional long-aged style historically associated with Rioja Alta's older bodegas rather than a contemporary cooler-fermented Rioja Blanco.

**Wine 7** — Kamptal, Lower Austria. Concentration and precision at a ripe but not over-ripe 13.5%, with fine mineral definition, suggest a premium single hillside site on the Danube's loess-and-rock terraces rather than a simple varietal bottling — consistent with a top Kamptal vineyard.

**Wine 8** — Soave Classico, Veneto, Italy. The saline, volcanic mineral edge and generous mid-palate weight, beyond basic valley-floor Soave, point to the basaltic hillside sites of the historic Classico zone rather than the flatter, higher-yielding plain.

**Wine 9** — Stellenbosch, Western Cape, South Africa. The dry, full, tropically inflected fruit profile with genuine old-vine concentration is distinctly Southern Hemisphere rather than Loire, and Stellenbosch's granite-and-sandstone old bush-vine sites are the Cape's benchmark source for this style.

**Wine 10** — Valle de Uco, Mendoza, Argentina. Firm acid retention alongside intense floral ripeness at 13% points to a high-altitude site with wide diurnal swing rather than the lower, warmer Mendoza plain, where the same variety would show flabbier acidity and less lift.

## c) Discuss quality with reference to winemaking techniques used (6 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — Outstanding quality, equivalent to a benchmark single-vineyard Hunter Semillon retailing around AUD 40–60. The early, low-sugar harvest explains the low alcohol; whole-bunch pressing and cool stainless-steel fermentation with no malolactic and no oak preserve the racy acid line, leaving development entirely to seven years in bottle — the toast and lanolin are secondary, not oak-derived, confirming a wine built for decades-long ageing.

**Wine 6** — Excellent, atypical quality for the category, equivalent to an extended-ageing Gran Reserva Blanco tier, priced around USD 35–50 for a specialist market. Fermentation and years of maturation in large old American oak vats rather than small new barriques explain the textural, non-toasty oak influence; deliberate exposure to slow, controlled oxidation before an unusually late release accounts for the sherry-like, rancio complexity — a traditional style now rare even within Rioja Alta.

**Wine 7** — Outstanding quality, Erste Lage (Premier Cru-equivalent) level, retailing around USD 30–45. Fully ripe fruit, gentle whole-bunch pressing and cool stainless fermentation with no oak or overt lees handling preserve the pure white-pepper fruit and taut acid structure; the concentration and precision on the finish confirm the top-tier vineyard designation rather than a village-level bottling.

**Wine 8** — Very good quality, single-vineyard Soave Classico level, above basic Soave DOC, around USD 25–40. The riper 2015 vintage explains the generous texture; fermentation and ageing in large old Slavonian oak or stainless, with no new wood, keep the fruit and mineral saline edge intact — the bitter-almond finish and textural depth separate it from commodity-tier Soave.

**Wine 9** — Outstanding quality, benchmark Old Vine tier, around USD 25–40. Low-yielding, drought-stressed old bush vines concentrate fruit; partial barrel fermentation and maturation with lees contact explain the creamy, waxy mid-palate texture and moderate acid retention despite a hot, dry vintage — a considerably more serious style than commercial Cape Chenin.

**Wine 10** — Good quality, premium varietal tier above bulk supermarket Torrontés, around USD 15–22. Cool stainless-steel fermentation with no oak preserves the intense floral aromatics, and early bottling protects freshness; minimal skin contact avoids the phenolic bitterness that can mar the variety, though the finish is relatively short, marking this as a wine for immediate drinking rather than cellaring.
