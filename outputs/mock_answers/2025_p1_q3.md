# Mock answer — 2025 Paper 1 Question 3

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible, making reference to the grape variety (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7.** Pale gold, with orange marmalade, roasted white peach and honeysuckle over a smoky, gunflint mineral edge; a fleshy, creamy-textured palate with saline minerality on a long finish, at a restrained 12% alcohol. The low alcohol with this saline, lees-derived texture is killer evidence for Melon de Bourgogne; Chardonnay was considered for the creamy weight but ruled out by the more neutral aromatics and an alcohol level well below unoaked Chardonnay of this richness. Points to Muscadet Sèvre-et-Maine sur lie, Pays Nantais, Loire Valley — depth and gunflint intensity beyond standard sur lie level suggest a Cru Communal bottling from a named commune such as Monnières-Saint-Fiacre.

**Wine 8.** Golden yellow with citrus, spice and dried-fruit aromatics tinged with a hint of botrytis, a racy acid line carrying noticeable sweetness to a long, limestone-driven finish at 13.5% alcohol. This precision of acidity against ripe fruit and a petrol-adjacent spice note is diagnostic of Riesling; Pinot Gris was eliminated by its broader, oilier texture and lack of tension. The dry-leaning acid/sugar integration and limestone rather than slate minerality point to Alsace over Germany. Origin: Alsace, limestone soils — the individual character and absence of botrytis-driven concentration point to a named lieu-dit rather than a generic blend or Grand Cru.

**Wine 9.** Medium gold, with quince, pear, honeycomb and white-flower aromatics, chalky minerality and a full palate where roughly 18 g/l residual sugar sits balanced against zesty acidity, finishing on bitter almond and crushed flint. This waxy, honeyed fruit with high natural acidity and clear off-dry sweetness is characteristic of Chenin Blanc; Riesling was considered but ruled out by the lanolin-quince register in place of Riesling's floral, petrol-tinged profile. The chalky, flinty finish and demi-sec sweetness point to Vouvray, Touraine, Loire Valley.

## b) Comment on the method of production (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 7.** The creamy texture and saline lift suggest extended sur lie ageing with bâtonnage beyond the appellation's 24-month minimum — the added depth over standard Muscadet points to several years on lees in neutral vessels, no oak evident.

**Wine 8.** The taut acid line with no buttery secondary character indicates malolactic fermentation was avoided; the slow-building aromatic intensity and gentle textural roundness suggest a long, cool, indigenous-yeast fermentation finished in large old oak rather than steel alone.

**Wine 9.** The coexistence of ripe fruit sweetness with a moderate 13% alcohol points to arrested fermentation, halted once the desired sugar/acid balance was reached; successive harvest passes are suggested by the evenly concentrated ripeness running through the wine.

## c) Comment on the style, quality, and maturity (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7.** A bone-dry, saline, lees-textured style, not fruit-driven. Very good quality, Cru Communal level — above standard sur lie Muscadet, retailing around $35-40. Developed marmalade and gunflint notes suggest a wine already 5-7 years old, drinking well now and holding for a further 3-4 years without further gain.

**Wine 8.** An off-dry, textured style with racy underlying tension, not overtly sweet. Very good to outstanding quality, lieu-dit level below Grand Cru, retailing around $30-35. Bright primary fruit and youthful reflections suggest a wine of only 2-3 years; it has the acid backbone to improve for a further 5 years and hold a decade beyond that.

**Wine 9.** A clearly demi-sec style — residual sugar deliberately prominent, balanced by acidity rather than masked, not a dry wine. Outstanding quality, top single-vineyard Vouvray level, retailing around $45-55. Still primary and tightly wound at 2-3 years, with the concentration and acidity to improve for a decade or more and hold two decades beyond that, gaining honeyed, waxy complexity.
