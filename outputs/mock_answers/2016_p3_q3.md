# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 3 Question 3

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible, with particular reference to the grape varieties used (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 9.** The deep gold colour, botrytis aromas of apricot, orange marmalade, saffron and acacia honey, and a palate that is very sweet yet carried by a searing, tart acid spine point to a botrytised wine built on Furmint. The strikingly low 9% alcohol for such concentration argues against fermentation through to dryness and instead suggests must enriched with individually selected shrivelled berries — the hallmark of Tokaji Aszú — while the marmalade-and-saffron register rules out the broader Semillon profile of Sauternes. This points to Tokaji Aszú, Tokaj, Hungary.

**Wine 10.** Wine 10 shows a fuller, unctuous texture, richer amber-gold colour and a broader sweetness of barley sugar, crème brûlée, dried apricot and toasted nut, lifted by vanilla from evident oak. At 13.5% — some 4.5 points above wine 9 — a fully-fermented rather than arrested style is indicated, and the lanolin weight and nutty oak point to Semillon, likely with a splash of Sauvignon Blanc. The rounder acidity confirms Sauternes, Bordeaux, France.

## b) Discuss the key winemaking techniques used to arrive at this style (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 9.** The botrytis concentration, low alcohol and high extract suggest hand-selection of individual aszú berries, macerated for a day or two in a dry base wine or must ahead of pressing — extracting sugar and colour without triggering a full ferment. Fermentation then proceeds slowly and stalls naturally against the sugar load, explaining the arrested 9% alcohol; ageing in old, neutral oak and further bottle age account for the developed marmalade and saffron notes without imparting oak spice.

**Wine 10.** The higher alcohol and evident oak here point instead to successive selective pickings through the vineyard for whole botrytised bunches, direct pressing, and an unhindered fermentation carried through to 13.5%. The vanilla and toast suggest maturation in a significant proportion of new French oak barriques, rounding the texture and adding the nutty, spiced complexity that separates this fuller style from wine 9's more fruit- and acid-driven profile.

## c) Comment on quality and maturity (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 9.** Outstanding quality, at the top of the 6 Puttonyos tier, comparable to the finest examples retailing around $60–80 for a 50cl bottle. Still youthful at five years old, the vivid acid line suggests further development over the next decade, holding a further 15 years as marmalade and dried-fruit notes deepen.

**Wine 10.** Equally outstanding, at the top of premier cru classé level from a benchmark vintage, retailing around $150–200 for a 75cl bottle. The deep amber colour and developed barley-sugar, crème brûlée character show a wine at its peak fifteen years after harvest, mature but with enough acid and botrytis concentration to hold without decline for a further decade.
