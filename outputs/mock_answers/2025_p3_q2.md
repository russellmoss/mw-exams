# Mock answer — 2025 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 4** — The fine, persistent bead and pronounced brioche and biscuit character point to traditional-method rather than tank-method sparkling — a Charmat wine such as Prosecco would show a coarser, faster-fading mousse and purely primary fruit. The riper apple and pineapple fruit and moderate 12% alcohol, warmer than the citrus drive typical of Champagne, indicate a warmer traditional-method zone; the floral lift alongside apple and pineapple fits the classic Macabeo-Xarel·lo-Parellada blend, pointing to Cava, and specifically to Sant Sadurní d'Anoia in Penedès.

**Wine 5** — Pale, translucent ruby colour and a perfumed nose of red berries, dried herbs and white pepper first suggest Pinot Noir, but the peppery, garrigue-like spice and 14% alcohol are unusual for that variety and point instead to Garnacha. A saline, granite-derived minerality on the finish confirms a cool, high-altitude rather than Mediterranean site, ruling out riper Priorat or Campo de Borja Garnacha, and places the wine in Sierra de Gredos — Valle del Tiétar, Vinos de Madrid DO — where old vines on granite above 800m preserve this freshness.

**Wine 6** — Deep mahogany-amber colour, an intensely oxidative nose of walnut, dried fig and leather, and a bone-dry, full, viscous palate at 20% alcohol identify this as Oloroso rather than the sweetened Oloroso Dulce style. The absence of any flor-derived saline freshness rules out Amontillado, which begins its life under flor before turning oxidative, and Palo Cortado, which retains some of that early lift; here the depth is purely oxidative throughout. The Palomino-derived nutty richness and solera-blended complexity place this in Jerez de la Frontera.

## b) Comment on the winemaking techniques (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 4** — Each variety was fermented separately in stainless steel with full malolactic evident in the creamy, softened acidity, then blended and given a secondary fermentation in bottle. The depth of biscuit autolysis indicates extended lees ageing, consistent with the legally defined minimum of 18 months required for Reserva.

**Wine 5** — Hand-harvested and fermented with wild yeast, a deliberately short maceration explains the pale colour and fine, silky tannin. Ageing in large, neutral old oak vessels for around nine months adds structure and texture without any detectable vanilla or toast.

**Wine 6** — Fortification to 20% after fermentation halted any flor development, directing ageing along a purely oxidative path in a solera system of old American oak butts, where fractional blending across decades has built this depth and consistency.

## c) Comment on the quality and commercial position (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 4** — The depth of autolysis and textural complexity here sit above entry-level Brut, at the top of the Reserva tier — outstanding quality for Cava Reserva, retailing around $18-22, edging toward Gran Reserva character. This is a strong off-trade proposition for premium supermarkets and independent specialists, competing with Crémant and entry-level Champagne, with good prospects in the UK, Germany and US where value traditional-method fizz is in demand.

**Wine 5** — The purity of fruit, precision of tannin and mineral lift mark this as outstanding quality at village level within Gredos's emerging Burgundian-style hierarchy — comparable in ambition, if not price, to village Volnay — retailing around $35-45. It suits fine-dining and independent on-trade lists chasing alternatives to village Burgundy or cru Beaujolais, with strong momentum in the US, UK and Scandinavian markets for elegant, terroir-driven Garnacha.

**Wine 6** — VORS is a legally verified, carbon-dated designation guaranteeing a minimum 30-year average age, the summit of the Sherry hierarchy above VOS; this wine shows outstanding quality, its concentration and length consistent with genuinely venerable solera stock, retailing around $80-120 for a half bottle. It belongs in specialist on-trade by-the-glass programmes and fine-wine independents rather than multiple grocery, appealing to a connoisseur audience in the UK, US and Scandinavia.
