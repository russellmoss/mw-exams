# Mock answer — 2021 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Compare and contrast quality and maturity (36 marks)

**Wine 5** is pale-medium lemon with fresh green apple, pear and citrus zest on a simple, unoaked nose; the medium body, clean high acidity and short-medium finish give a good quality, entry-level style, equivalent to a commercial branded Anjou AOC retailing around $12-15. Pure primary fruit puts it at 1-2 years old; it is ready now, will not improve further, and should be drunk over the next 1-2 years before its freshness fades. **Wine 6** shows deep gold with amber, and a complex nose of beeswax, quince, dried apricot, roasted hazelnut and lanolin that signals full tertiary development; remarkably the acidity remains taut beneath this weight, driving a very long, saline, mineral finish. This is outstanding quality, on a par with a top cru-level Savennières retailing $60-80. The secondary/tertiary spectrum puts the wine at around 13-14 years old; it is fully mature and at or just past its peak, with little further improvement likely, though it should hold a further 3-5 years on this acid spine before oxidative characters dominate. **Wine 7** has a brighter medium gold hue with pure pear, lemon curd and a saline mineral core lifted by subtle brioche and oak spice; concentrated fruit and a long finish mark this as very good to excellent quality, equivalent to a premium single-vineyard old-vine bottling at $25-35. Still largely primary with emerging development, it is around 3 years old, drinking well now but with the structure to improve over 3-5 years and hold a further 5-8. Quality rises from commercial (Wine 5) through premium New World (Wine 7) to grand terroir (Wine 6), while maturity runs from drink-now, through developing, to fully mature — illustrating Chenin's rare ability to age on acid alone, without recourse to new oak.

## b) Identify the variety (15 marks)

Chenin Blanc. The unifying thread across the three wines is a very high, persistent acid line that survives increasing ripeness (12.5% to 14.5% alcohol) and, in Wine 6, over a decade of bottle age — a structural hallmark of the variety. The honeyed, waxy, quince and lanolin register that Wine 6 has developed, and the saline-mineral drive common to all three, are also distinctively Chenin rather than incidental winemaking effects. Riesling was considered given the racy acidity and age-worthiness, but the tertiary profile here is honeyed and waxy rather than the petrol/kerosene character typical of mature Riesling, with more textural weight than Riesling's usual linear drive. Chardonnay was also considered for Wine 7's touch of barrel spice, but the acidity is too persistently high for Chardonnay's softer malic profile, and the pear/quince fruit sits closer to Chenin's typical spectrum than Chardonnay's citrus-stone-fruit default.

## c) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5** — Anjou, Loire Valley, France. The fresh, uncomplicated green apple and pear fruit, moderate 12.5% alcohol and absence of oak point to a cool, high-yielding, commercially driven site; the lack of concentration or textural depth rules out a named cru, pointing instead to broad varietal Anjou AOC.

**Wine 6** — Savennières, Anjou, Loire Valley, France. The extraordinary acid retention after well over a decade, the saline-mineral tension and the honeyed complexity built on a schist-derived backbone point to the grand terroir end of Anjou. This combination of power, precision and longevity is the calling card of Savennières, the appellation where dry Chenin reaches its most ageworthy expression.

**Wine 7** — Citrusdal Mountains, South Africa. The riper 13% alcohol, pure pear and lemon-curd fruit, subtle toasty barrel spice and a saline-mineral drive more typical of a cooler, high-altitude site than the Loire together suggest a New World, elevated vineyard rather than a European origin. The precision and old-vine concentration point specifically to the cool mountain sites of the Western Cape's Citrusdal Mountains.
