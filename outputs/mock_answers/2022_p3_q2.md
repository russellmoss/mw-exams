# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety/ies and origin as closely as possible (5 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5.** The pale gold colour with a coppery flush and the pungently perfumed lychee, rose-petal, Turkish delight and ginger-spice aromatics point unmistakably to Gewürztraminer — Muscat and Viognier were both considered and dismissed on the absence of, respectively, grapey and apricot-blossom character. The oily, viscous texture at 14% alcohol with only a whisper of sweetness indicates a warm, New World expression rather than Alsace, where the variety typically shows lower alcohol and firmer phenolic grip; the riper, more exuberant fruit profile points to Marlborough, New Zealand.

**Wine 6.** The pale straw-green colour, racing acidity and, above all, the strikingly low 7% alcohol are the loudest clue in the flight — this alcohol level against clear residual sweetness is only realistically reached by arresting fermentation at natural Prädikat ripeness, immediately signalling German Riesling. Pure lime and stone-fruit aromatics with an emerging petrol note and slate-inflected minerality confirm Riesling over Müller-Thurgau, and locate the wine in the Rheingau at Spätlese level, given moderate rather than intense Auslese-style concentration.

**Wine 7.** Deep gold colour with honeyed apricot-jam, marmalade richness and a waxy beeswax edge point to a botrytis-affected sweet wine; retained natural acidity beneath the lusciousness, with a slightly nutty, quince-like fruit signature, suggests Chenin Blanc rather than Sémillon, whose botrytis wines typically show more lanolin weight. The vanilla-laced new-oak overlay and generous, sunny fruit profile point away from the Loire towards a New World botrytis region — consistent with Stellenbosch, South Africa.

**Wine 8.** A similarly deep gold hue but with a more phenolic, faintly grippy finish and a distinctive dried-mango, orange-blossom lift suggests a variety other than Chenin or Sémillon — high natural acidity, honeyed concentration and a saline, mineral edge are consistent with Furmint, likely blended with Hárslevelű. Intensity of fruit relative to a moderate 11.5% alcohol, together with an old-oak, slightly oxidative barrel character, points to Tokaj rather than a New World botrytis style.

**Wine 9.** Medium-to-deep gold with lifted citrus-blossom and lime-marmalade aromatics layered over honeyed botrytis richness suggests a Sémillon-based blend rather than a single variety — the zesty, floral top note is consistent with a meaningful proportion of Sauvignon Blanc. The elegant, lighter-textured concentration, rather than the weightier unctuousness of Sauternes proper, at 13% alcohol points to Barsac within the Sauternes-Barsac appellation, Bordeaux.

## b) Comment on the key winemaking techniques, with specific reference to the level of residual sugar (5 x 7 marks)

**Wine 5.** The near-absence of pronounced sweetness against 14% alcohol shows this Gewürztraminer was picked ripe but fermented almost to dryness in stainless steel, with only a small deliberate stop or back-blend leaving an off-dry finish; cool, protective handling and early bottling preserve the pristine aromatics rather than any oak influence.

**Wine 6.** The 7% alcohol against evident concentration confirms fermentation was arrested naturally by chilling and racking off the yeast once sugar levels fell, leaving a substantial residual sugar in the 50-80 g/L range typical of Spätlese; neutral vessels and minimal intervention let acidity, not oak, carry the structure.

**Wine 7.** Hand-selected botrytised bunches were pressed gently and fermented slowly in cold conditions, again halted well short of dryness to preserve an estimated 150-200+ g/L RS; the vanilla and toast overlay indicates maturation in a high proportion of new French oak, adding structure to match the wine's concentration.

**Wine 8.** The paste-like richness points to the traditional aszú method: botrytised berries macerated as a dough in base wine or must for a day or more before pressing, extracting concentrated sugar and phenolics. The puttonyos designation confirms a legal minimum RS, around 120 g/L for 5 puttonyos, with the oxidative, old-oak character indicating extended ageing in small, used Hungarian barrels.

**Wine 9.** Multiple selective picking passes through the vineyard concentrated botrytis-affected berries, whole-cluster pressed and fermented in barrel; toasty, spicy oak integration suggests fermentation and ageing in a meaningful proportion of new French barriques, with fermentation stopped naturally by rising alcohol and falling temperature to leave an estimated 120-150 g/L RS.

## c) Comment on the quality and state of maturity (5 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5.** Pure varietal expression, textural weight and a clean, persistent finish indicate well above-average, single-vineyard quality for Marlborough Gewürztraminer, ahead of generic regional bottlings retailing around $15-18. The bright, primary fruit and youthful copper flush point to a wine of only around 2 years old with little more complexity to gain — best drunk over the next 1-2 years before its aromatic lift fades.

**Wine 6.** Precision of fruit, a racy acid line and an already-emerging petrol note indicate excellent quality for Spätlese, comparable to a top Rheingau estate rather than a generic cooperative bottling. At around 7 years old the wine is developing well, showing early tertiary complexity while retaining freshness; it will continue to gain honeyed, petrol-inflected depth for a further 5-8 years and hold for a decade beyond that.

**Wine 7.** Density of botrytis concentration, layered complexity and well-judged oak integration point to outstanding quality within South Africa's small Noble Late Harvest category, comparable to the country's very best rather than a simple late-picked style. At around 3 years old, still showing predominantly primary marmalade and honey fruit, the wine has ample concentration to develop for a further 5-7 years and will hold for a decade beyond that.

**Wine 8.** Extraordinary concentration allied to Tokaj's hallmark high acidity and a long, saline-edged finish confirm outstanding quality, at least equivalent to a well-made 5 Puttonyos and arguably approaching 6 Puttonyos intensity. At around 5 years old the wine already shows the beginnings of nutty, oxidative development from barrel ageing; it will continue to gain complexity for a further decade and, given Tokaji's legendary longevity, will hold for several decades more.

**Wine 9.** Elegant concentration, lifted citrus freshness and a long, complex finish indicate outstanding quality, consistent with Premier Cru Classé-level Barsac rather than a generic Sauternes-Barsac bottling. At around 6 years old the wine is still primary-dominant with only the first hints of dried-apricot development; it has the acid backbone and concentration to improve for a further 10-15 years and will hold for two to three decades given the vintage's noted freshness.
