# Mock answer — 2024 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the region as closely as possible (15 marks)

Both wines point to the Jura. Wine 3 shows pale gold colour, a taut, high-acid citrus and green-apple profile with a smoky, reductive edge and a saline, chalky finish — a lean, mineral white that could suggest cool-climate Burgundy, but the reductive popcorn note and racy, almost austere acidity at only 12.5% alcohol sit outside typical Chardonnay expression. Wine 4 is deeper gold, broader and nuttier, with walnut, dried apricot and a distinct salty-oxidative character alongside 14.5% alcohol — this rules out simple lees-aged Chardonnay and instead recalls the curry-spice, apple-skin, walnut signature of wines aged under a yeast veil. Taken together, the pairing of a fresh, topped-up style with a markedly oxidative, higher-alcohol style from the same region is the defining stylistic fork of the Jura, narrowing the call precisely: Wine 3 to Arbois AOC, Wine 4 to the smaller, chalk-and-limestone L'Étoile AOC.

## b) Compare and contrast the winemaking (15 marks)

Wine 3's precise citrus purity and lack of any nutty or acetaldehyde note point to ouillé ageing — barrels kept topped up throughout maturation, protecting the wine reductively and preserving primary fruit; the light, savoury depth suggests extended fine-lees contact in older barrels. Wine 4's walnut, apple-skin and salinity indicate the opposite regime — barrels left ungauged (non-ouillé), allowing a partial yeast veil to develop and drive gentle, controlled oxidation without reaching the intensity of a full sous voile Vin Jaune. The riper, higher-alcohol base wine in Wine 4 is consistent with this maturation surviving without spoilage.

## c) Quality and commercial potential (20 marks)

Wine 3 is very good quality, comparable to a serious single-vineyard Arbois bottling, its precision and length justifying $30–40 retail through independent specialists targeting drinkers of unoaked Burgundy or Chablis. Wine 4 is outstanding, showing the complexity and typicity of a benchmark L'Étoile, commanding $45–60 and appealing to adventurous sommeliers and by-the-glass lists seeking an alternative to Vin Jaune or fino Sherry styles, though its niche oxidative character limits mainstream appeal.
