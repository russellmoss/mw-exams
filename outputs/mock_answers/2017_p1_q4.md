# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 1 Question 4

## a) Identify the grape variety (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 8** — Muscat. The pungent, unmistakably grapey nose of fresh grape, elderflower and citrus blossom is a killer marker no other white variety mimics. The palate is light-medium, brisk and finishes bone dry, ruling out a sweet style. Gewürztraminer was considered for its aromatic intensity, but its lychee, rose-petal and spice profile with oilier texture is absent; the higher acid, more literally grapey character confirms Muscat.

**Wine 9** — Albariño. Pale lemon colour with peach, citrus zest, white blossom and a distinct sea-spray, saline lift, allied to zesty, medium-light acidity and a faintly phenolic finish, is diagnostic. Riesling was considered for the citrus and mid-weight acid, but the absence of petrol or lime-cordial character, and the phenolic grip, point away from it. The maritime salinity and stone-fruit register confirm Albariño.

**Wine 10** — Viognier. Pronounced apricot, honeysuckle and tangerine-oil aromatics, a broad, oily texture and notably low, soft acidity at 14% alcohol are classic for the variety. Muscat was the alternative given the shared floral intensity, but Muscat's fruit reads as literally grapey with markedly higher, more linear acid; this wine's stone-fruit register, fuller body and low acid confirm Viognier.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 8** — Alsace, France. Dry Muscat with this aromatic purity and a herbal, fennel-edged lift points strongly to Alsace, the only region making serious dry varietal Muscat at commercial volume. The moderate 12.5% alcohol and taut acid line are consistent with a cooler vintage preserving lift over ripeness.

**Wine 9** — Rías Baixas, Spain. The saline, maritime edge with peach and citrus fruit signals Atlantic Galicia rather than inland Spain; Rueda Verdejo was considered but lacks this salinity and shows more fennel-like herbal notes. The coastal, granite-and-sand character narrows the call to Rías Baixas.

**Wine 10** — Northern Rhône, France (Condrieu). This level of aromatic concentration, textural richness and 14% alcohol, with a faint toasted note suggesting barrel use, points to a single steep granite site rather than a generic Vin de Pays Viognier. The fruit purity and fine texture are too layered for simpler varietal bottlings from the Midi or New World, confirming Condrieu.

## c) Consider how the winemaker has sought to retain the wine's sense of place (3 x 9 marks)

**Wine 8.** The absence of oak or buttery malolactic signature, and the fully dry finish despite intensely perfumed fruit, are deliberate: fermentation in neutral, likely stainless steel, vessels at cool temperature protects the primary grape-and-blossom aromatics, while blocking malolactic and avoiding wood preserves the herbal lift that defines dry Alsace Muscat. Restraint, not intervention, keeps the wine tasting of variety and site.

**Wine 9.** The winemaking is again essentially subtractive: stainless steel, low temperature, no oak and, on the evidence of the clean, unbuttered fruit, malolactic likely blocked or only partial. A short period on lees may explain the slightly phenolic grip on the finish, used sparingly so as not to blur the saline, granite-inflected purity that defines Rías Baixas Albariño — any oxidative or oaky handling would erase the coastal sense of place.

**Wine 10.** The more delicate balancing act. Viognier must be picked very ripe for apricot and honeysuckle character, explaining the 14% alcohol and naturally low acidity; fermenting and ageing only in stainless steel would risk a flat, blowsy result once that acid softens further. The faint toast and expansive, oily texture suggest partial fermentation and ageing in older barrels with some lees stirring, adding weight without imposing new-oak flavour — full or partial malolactic is plausible given the roundness. Keeping new oak minimal lets the site's aromatic signature, not the cooperage, dominate; this is single-site, low-yielding hillside fruit, and the price reflects it, retailing at a significant premium (around £55–65) against the more commercial Muscat (around £15) and Albariño (around £13–15) alongside it.
