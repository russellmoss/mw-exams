# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 1 Question 1

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible, with reference to the grape variety(ies) used (3 x 11 marks)

**Wine 1.** Pale lemon, restrained on the nose beyond green apple, citrus peel and a distinct saline, wet-stone edge, with searing, high acidity and a light 12% alcohol carrying almost no phenolic weight. This lack of overt aromatic lift rules out Sauvignon Blanc, and the absence of Chenin's waxy grip rules out that variety too, leaving a neutral, high-acid grape consistent with Melon de Bourgogne. Given the flight's shared Loire origin, the low alcohol, saline drive and a faint textural prickle place this at the Atlantic mouth of the river — Muscadet Sèvre-et-Maine, the appellation built around exactly this profile.

**Wine 2.** Medium lemon with visible development, lanolin, quince and dried flowers on the nose and a firm, dry, textural palate with high but broader, rounder acidity and a fuller 13.5% alcohol. The waxy grip and structural weight point to Chenin Blanc, distinguishing it from Wine 1's neutrality and from Wine 3's aromatic lift. Within the Loire, a fully dry, austere, mineral-driven Chenin with real ageing potential rather than the softer fruit of generic Anjou Blanc or the honeyed sweetness of Vouvray moelleux points to Savennières, the schist-based appellation on the river's north bank at Anjou.

**Wine 3.** Pale lemon with a pungent, lifted nose of gooseberry, elderflower and citrus zest, underpinned by a distinct smoky, flinty edge; the palate is dry, linear and tense at 12.5% alcohol. This aromatic intensity and pyrazine-driven fruit is unmistakably Sauvignon Blanc, and the flint and gunflint character — more pronounced than the citrus-led austerity typical across the river — narrows this to the silex soils of the Central Vineyards: Pouilly-Fumé rather than Sancerre or generic Touraine Sauvignon.

## b) Discuss the winemaking techniques used to arrive at this style (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 1.** Grapes were likely picked at moderate ripeness to preserve acidity, then fermented cool in stainless steel with no malolactic fermentation, keeping the tart, appley freshness intact. The textural creaminess and faint spritz on the finish indicate extended ageing sur lie over winter, with the wine bottled directly off its lees without racking or fining — the technique that defines this style and appellation.

**Wine 2.** The riper fruit and fuller mid-palate suggest a slightly later harvest for phenolic ripeness. The absence of overt toast argues against new oak, but the waxy texture and structural weight point to fermentation and/or ageing in neutral cask or large foudre with extended lees contact. Malolactic fermentation appears blocked, preserving the searing acid line that gives this Chenin its ageing capacity.

**Wine 3.** Hand-picking and gentle, whole-bunch pressing would protect the thiol-driven gooseberry and elderflower aromatics from oxidation. Cool fermentation in stainless steel, with malolactic avoided, retains the linear acidity and pungent fruit; minimal lees contact and an absence of oak keep the flinty, mineral character to the fore, with early bottling locking in freshness.

## c) Discuss the commercial potential (3 x 6 marks)

**Wine 1** offers strong value, retailing around £9–12 through independents and supermarkets, and by-the-glass on seafood-led wine lists; it competes with Picpoul de Pinet and entry Chablis and benefits from the current trend toward sur lie, cru-communal Muscadet trading up.

**Wine 2** is a niche, ageworthy style at £18–25 through specialist independents and sommelier-led on-trade lists, appealing to Chenin enthusiasts and trade buyers; export interest centres on the UK and Benelux, where it competes with premium Vouvray and entry-level white Burgundy.

**Wine 3** sits in the premium Sauvignon bracket at £20–30, well placed on fine-dining and by-the-glass lists; it competes directly with Sancerre and premium Marlborough Sauvignon, with strongest export demand in the UK and US among drinkers seeking classic flinty typicity over riper New World fruit.
