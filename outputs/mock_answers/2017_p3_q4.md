# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 3 Question 4

## a) Name the dominant grape variety (10 marks)

Grenache. Both wines share a core of sweet, glycerol-rich red-fruit, alcohols of 14-14.5% and a soft, rounded tannin grain rather than the fine, gripping structure Syrah would give. Syrah and Mataro/Mourvèdre are evident as supporting partners — white pepper and iron lift in Wine 7, darker spice and leather in Wine 8 — but neither variety dominates the tannin profile or fruit register in either glass. The warm fruit sweetness and plush texture point to Grenache as the backbone of a classic GSM-style blend in both cases.

## b) Identify the origin(s) as closely as possible (20 marks)

**Wine 7** — ripe, jammy dark-fruit intensity, a plush, velvety tannin texture and a lifted vanilla/coconut oak note point to a warm New World origin. The sheer concentration and old-vine depth, dense fruit off naturally low yields, mark out heritage bush-vine material rather than young plantings, and the generous 14.5% alcohol with warm-climate spice is consistent with inland South Australia. McLaren Vale was considered, but the density and old-vine power here sit more convincingly with Barossa Valley, home to some of the oldest surviving Grenache in the world.

**Wine 8** — garrigue (thyme, rosemary, lavender), leather, iron and a savoury, dried-herb top note point unmistakably to the Southern Rhône. The firmer, more structured tannin and real power and concentration at 14% alcohol, rather than the lighter, more perfumed style of a village wine, point to Châteauneuf-du-Pape: its galets roulés and famously high minimum-alcohol requirement produce exactly this combination of warmth and structure. Gigondas was considered but lacks this level of richness and finish.

## c) Compare and contrast the styles with reference to likely market positioning (20 marks)

Wine 7 shows riper, sweeter fruit, a plusher, oak-inflected texture and softer tannin — an opulent, fruit-forward modern style for immediate drinking pleasure. Wine 8 is more savoury and structured, with firmer tannin and far more neutral oak handling — a traditional, food-oriented style built to hold and develop. Both sit at 14-14.5% alcohol, so alcohol offers no useful discrimination here; the fruit-sweetness-versus-savoury axis, tannin texture and oak treatment are the real points of difference. Commercially, Wine 7 is a specialist premium New World bottling at around £25, sold through independent specialists and by-the-glass lists to drinkers moving on from mainstream Barossa Shiraz, competing against the region's own GSM producers. Wine 8 is a benchmark fine-wine appellation with strong name recognition, retailing around £40 through independent merchants and traditional restaurant lists, and exported widely across the US, UK and Benelux on the strength of its appellation reputation.
