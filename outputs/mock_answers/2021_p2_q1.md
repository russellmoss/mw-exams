# Mock answer — 2021 Paper 2 Question 1

## a) Identify the region as closely as possible, referencing each wine (15 marks)

All three wines show a Cabernet Sauvignon-dominant blend with cassis, graphite and cedar aromatics over a firm, fine-grained tannic frame and pronounced new French oak, pointing immediately to the Left Bank of Bordeaux and specifically the Médoc's gravel communes. The persistence of this profile across three vintages of clearly differing ripeness rules out a blended regional wine and points instead to a single, well-drained gravel site farmed and vinified consistently. Pauillac was considered given the structural weight and cassis depth, but the tannin here is finer and more polished than Pauillac's typical density and power. Margaux was also considered, but its characteristic violet perfume and softer mid-palate are absent. The combination of real structure with genuine finesse across all three wines, sitting between these two neighbouring communes' extremes, is textbook Saint-Julien.

## b) Identify the vintage. Discuss quality in the context of the region and the vintage (3 x 20 marks)

**Wine 1.** The palest colour of the three, deep ruby without real density, and the leanest, greenest tannin with cool herbal edges rather than pure cassis richness. At 13.0% alcohol the fruit reads diluted rather than concentrated, with a noticeably shorter finish than its companions. This points to a difficult, cool and wet growing season with uneven flowering and rot pressure at harvest — 2013, one of the weakest recent Left Bank vintages. Saint-Julien's gravel drainage limited the damage relative to more marginal Médoc sites, but the wine is still only good rather than outstanding for the appellation, equivalent to a lesser classed-growth year retailing well below the property's normal price. It is fully ready now and has little to gain from further ageing; drink over the next 3-5 years before the fruit fades further.

**Wine 2.** The deepest, most saturated colour of the flight, with ripe cassis, violet and sweet spice over abundant but ripe, well-integrated tannin and a long, complete finish. The 13.5% alcohol, the highest of the three, together with this fruit density and structural completeness, points to a warm, successful growing season — 2015, a genuinely strong Left Bank vintage, if slightly behind the earlier-ripening right bank. The wine remains clearly primary and youthful, with little tertiary development yet showing. This is outstanding quality for Saint-Julien, equivalent to a top-tier classed-growth vintage commanding a significant premium. It is not yet ready to drink, needing a further 8-10 years to resolve its ripe tannin, and should then hold and improve for two decades or more, developing cedar and graphite complexity.

**Wine 3.** A garnet rim and genuinely evolved aromatics — cedar, cigar box, truffle and dried leaf — sit over a still-present but resolving tannin, with primary cassis fruit now receding into savoury secondary and tertiary notes. At 13.0% alcohol this is not the leanest wine of the flight but the most developed, indicating considerably more bottle age than Wine 1 despite the similar alcohol level, consistent with a warm, dry and even growing season some fifteen to sixteen years ago — 2005, a benchmark modern vintage across the Médoc. The tertiary complexity layered over still-firm structure confirms outstanding quality, equivalent to the top of the classed-growth hierarchy in a great year. It is at its peak now, drinking beautifully, with the structure to hold for a further 10-15 years without decline.
