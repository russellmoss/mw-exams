# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 3 Question 6

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 9 marks)

**Wine 11** — Amber-mahogany colour, a bone-dry palate and high 20% alcohol point to Sherry over Madeira or Marsala, neither of which gives this precise walnut, hazelnut and toffee rancio without heat-driven caramelisation or a rustic edge. An Amontillado's nutty delicacy layered over an Oloroso's weight is the calling-card of Palo Cortado, pointing firmly to Jerez-Xérès-Sherry DO, Andalucía.

**Wine 12** — Deep garnet colour, pronounced sweetness and rancio-tinged baked fruit at a markedly lower 15.5% rule out Sherry's dry, higher-strength style. Port was considered but the lighter fruit weight and tawny-rancio register point instead to a Vin Doux Naturel from Mediterranean France. Gingerbread, toffee and garrigue spice are typical of oxidatively-aged Grenache Noir, narrowing origin to Roussillon, most likely Maury.

## b) Comment on the method of production with reference to quality (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 11** — Palomino Fino was fermented fully dry, then fortified to around 15% to allow flor development, ageing biologically like a Fino. Classically, Palo Cortado is a wine that unpredictably loses its flor veil and continues ageing oxidatively; today the style is often achieved deliberately, fortifying higher once flor character is judged sufficient. The final 20% reflects that second, later fortification. Fractional ageing through a criadera y solera system averages age across many vintages — the seamless integration of walnut, toffee and rancio with no single vintage marker points to a mature, multi-decade solera. Quality rests on complexity and harmony across the blend, placing this well above entry-level Sherry, at an aged, "viejo" Palo Cortado level.

**Wine 12** — Fermentation was arrested early by mutage — addition of neutral grape spirit — retaining substantial natural sugar, unlike Sherry's fully-fermented base; this earlier stopping point also explains the lower 15.5%. Extended, multi-vessel oxidative ageing — old barrels, then glass demijohns exposed to heat and light, then further cask time — drives the rancio, dried-fruit and toasted-spice complexity; the depth and integration suggest years of ageing well beyond the appellation's regulatory minimum. Quality is judged on rancio depth and harmony rather than fruit primacy, placing this above a young, fruit-driven style of Maury.

## c) Who would buy this wine? (2 x 6 marks)

**Wine 11** — A connoisseur or sommelier seeking a rare, intellectually engaging dry Sherry, retailing around $30-50 — outstanding value against its age and complexity. Sold through fine-dining by-the-glass lists and independent specialists to drinkers exploring Jerez beyond Fino and Manzanilla.

**Wine 12** — An adventurous drinker or sommelier looking beyond Port and Sauternes for fortified sweet wine, retailing around $25-40 — again exceptional value for its extended ageing. Suits independent specialists and artisan-leaning by-the-glass dessert lists, paired with chocolate or blue cheese.
