# Mock answer — 2023 Paper 3 Question 3

## a) Identify the origin and grape variety/ies (14 marks)

Furmint. Wine 8 shows a pale lemon-gold colour, a waxy, lanolin texture and orchard fruit and citrus peel notes carried on searingly high, racy acidity — this rules out Grüner Veltliner, which would show more white pepper, and Riesling, which would not carry this waxy weight. Wine 9 is deep amber-gold with pronounced botrytis character — dried apricot, orange marmalade, saffron and singed toffee — set against very high residual sugar balanced by the same piercing acid spine. Sauternes was considered but the aromatic profile leans more toward candied orange than the honeyed pineapple typical of botrytised Sémillon, and the acidity is higher than Sémillon typically shows. The shared high-acid backbone across both wines despite the sweetness gap points to a common variety and origin: volcanic, high-acid Furmint from Tokaj-Hegyalja, Hungary.

## b) Compare the production methods (16 marks)

Wine 8 shows direct, unbotrytised fruit with no oak or overt lees character, indicating whole-bunch pressing, cool fermentation in inert vessels, and early bottling to preserve freshness and varietal purity — a modern, protective dry style. Wine 9's concentration and complexity point to a different, additive process: individually selected, shrivelled aszú berries picked across successive passes through the vineyard, then macerated in a base wine or must before slow, botrytis-inhibited fermentation that halts naturally at high sugar. The layered dried-fruit and toasted-nut depth, together with a residual sugar level in the 120 g/L range, is consistent with the 5 Puttonyos legal minimum, followed by extended ageing in small oak and further time in bottle. The decisive contrast is harvest and selection intensity — single-pick whole bunches versus multiple selective tries for botrytis — while shared Furmint acidity is what keeps Wine 9 vibrant rather than cloying.

## c) Comment on quality and maturity (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 8** — Good quality, a well-made commercial dry Furmint retailing around $20–25. The pale colour and primary citrus/orchard fruit suggest a young wine of 2–3 years, drinking well now with little further upside; it is built for freshness rather than development and should be enjoyed over the next 1–2 years before that vibrancy fades.

**Wine 9** — Very good quality, a well-judged 5 Puttonyos example at the benchmark commercial level for the category, retailing around $40–60 per half bottle. The developed amber colour and marmalade, dried-fig complexity suggest 8–10 years of age; it is drinking beautifully now but the concentrated sugar and high acidity give it real ageing capacity, with further development of nutty, toffee-like richness over the next 15–20 years.
