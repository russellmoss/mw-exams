# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 1 Question 2

With the country given as shared, the task is regional discrimination within it.

## Wine 3

### a) Origin and variety (10 marks)

Riesling. The pronounced lime and lime-cordial fruit, floral lift and racing, bone-dry acidity are immediately varietal; the tightly wound finish with no oak rules out an aromatic look-alike such as Assyrtiko, which would show more saline, olive-leaf austerity in place of this lime-blossom perfume. The riper citrus fruit and stony, slatey minerality point away from a cooler Germanic or Austrian style toward a warmer New World origin, and the textural generosity beneath the racy acid line funnels this to Clare Valley's Watervale sub-district rather than the more linear, austere Eden Valley style.

### b) Quality and maturity (10 marks)

Outstanding quality, equivalent to a top single-vineyard estate wine at the head of the Clare Valley tier, retailing around AUD 45–55: the concentration of lime fruit, chalky tension and persistent, mouth-watering finish show real depth rather than simple citrus freshness. The pale lemon colour and pure, still-primary lime character indicate a young wine of around a year old. It has ample acid backbone to improve for a further 8–10 years, developing toast and marmalade notes, before holding for a decade beyond that.

### c) Winemaking (5 marks)

Whole-bunch pressing preserves the pure citrus fruit; a cool, neutral-vessel fermentation with brief post-ferment lees contact builds texture without masking fruit; malolactic fermentation is evidently blocked to protect the racy acid line, and early screwcap bottling locks in the primary lime character.

## Wine 4

### a) Origin and variety (10 marks)

Chardonnay. Ripe white peach and grapefruit fruit, a creamy, cashew-nut texture and a flinty, reductive edge are classic varietal markers, while the fuller body and 13.5% alcohol rule out a cooler-climate look-alike such as Chablis or Yarra Valley, both leaner and more linear on the acid line. The generous fruit concentration alongside a firm, well-integrated spine and warm, maritime-inflected structure points to Western Australia, and specifically to Margaret River rather than Adelaide Hills or Tasmania.

### b) Quality and maturity (10 marks)

Very good to outstanding quality, equivalent to a flagship estate Chardonnay competing with Village to Premier Cru-level white Burgundy, retailing around AUD 70–90: the layered fruit, textural richness and long, saline finish show real complexity rather than simple oak flavour. The bright gold colour and integrated toasty, nutty complexity suggest a wine of around three years old, past its most youthful primary phase. It has the structure to improve for another 4–5 years and hold for 6–8 more, gaining honeyed, praline depth.

### c) Winemaking (5 marks)

Wild-yeast barrel fermentation in predominantly new French oak explains the toasty, spicy frame; full malolactic fermentation and lees-stirring account for the creamy, cashew texture; extended barrel ageing of around 10–11 months rounds and softens the palate.

## Wine 5

### a) Origin and variety (10 marks)

This is the flight's hard wine. The low aromatic intensity, waxy, honeysuckle and toasted-nut character with no oak signature suggest a variety that develops richly with bottle age rather than showing overt primary fruit. Viognier was considered, but its blossom and apricot lift and higher perceived alcohol are absent; the neutral, honeyed profile with a soft, broad acid line instead points to Marsanne. The moderate body and 13% alcohol are consistent with a warm Australian site, and the waxy, lanolin development without new oak funnels this toward Victoria's Nagambie Lakes, historically the country's key site for old-vine Marsanne.

### b) Quality and maturity (10 marks)

Very good quality, equivalent to a deliberate reserve-tier estate release retailing around AUD 35–45 given its extra bottle age before sale: the honeyed complexity and lingering, savoury finish show genuine development, not oxidative decline. The deep gold colour, toasted-nut and beeswax notes indicate a mature wine of around 7–8 years old, already on its drinking plateau. It is unlikely to gain further complexity but should hold comfortably for another 3–4 years before the fruit fades.

### c) Winemaking (5 marks)

Fermentation and maturation in neutral, likely older large-format vessels explain the absence of oak spice; deliberate extended bottle ageing before release, aided by screwcap closure, accounts for the toasty, honeyed complexity while retaining freshness on the finish.
