# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 1 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety (20 marks)

Pinot Gris. Both wines show the faintly coppery, almost onion-skin blush to a deep gold colour that betrays a pink-skinned mutation of Pinot Noir, alongside a smoky, waxy, bacon-fat register on the nose over ripe pear, quince and ginger-spice — a combination distinctive to this variety and not typical of any other white grape. Moderate-to-low acidity and real textural weight on both palates, even at the lighter 13.1% alcohol of Wine 6, confirm the naturally low-acid, broad-shouldered structure of Pinot Gris. Pinot Blanc was considered but rejected — it would show a leaner, more neutral, appley profile without this smoky-spicy lift. Viognier was also eliminated: it typically carries a more overt floral, blossom and apricot character with an oilier, less waxy texture. Chardonnay does not fit either, lacking the ginger-spice and showing a different, more citrus-to-stone-fruit register that would usually carry oak-derived toast rather than this variety's innate smokiness.

## b) Identify the region of origin (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5** — Alsace, France, from a walled single-vineyard (clos) site. The deep gold colour, unctuous, oily texture and 14.5% alcohol point to a warm, sheltered, low-yielding site consistent with Alsace's rain-shadow mesoclimate. A touch of perceptible sweetness alongside the high alcohol is typical of Alsace's variable-dosage approach to Pinot Gris, and the complexity and length suggest a single, prized parcel rather than a generic blend.

**Wine 6** — Willamette Valley, Oregon, USA. The paler gold hue, fully dry finish and markedly lower 13.1% alcohol point to a cooler climate and a leaner winemaking intent than Wine 5. The clean, fresh pear and light melon fruit, without residual sugar, is consistent with Oregon's deliberate stylistic alignment with Alsace rather than with Italy's lean, neutral Pinot Grigio idiom — Oregon is now the USA's leading producer of this variety.

## c) Discuss style and commercial appeal (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 5** is a rich, off-dry, full-bodied style built for slow enjoyment with food — foie gras, Munster cheese or Asian spice. Its complexity and single-site pedigree suit Independent Specialists and fine dining by-the-glass lists, retailing around £45, competing with top Alsace Grand Cru and premium Austrian Ruländer.

**Wine 6** is a dry, medium-weight, fresh style suited to broader by-the-glass and premium supermarket listings at around £18, appealing to consumers seeking an accessible, food-friendly alternative to Pinot Grigio, and competing well against New Zealand and other Oregon Pinot Gris in the US and UK on-trade.
