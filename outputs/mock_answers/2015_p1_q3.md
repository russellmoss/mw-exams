# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 1 Question 3

Tasted as a flight, the six wines run from more muted, texture-driven examples (Wines 7-9) toward increasingly bombastic, overtly aromatic examples (Wines 10-12) — a useful cross-reference when narrowing each individual call.

## Wine 7

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Pale lemon-green colour with fresh citrus, white peach and apricot aromatics, underpinned by a distinctive saline, almost oceanic edge. High, racy acidity, light-to-medium body and a dry, clean finish with no oak influence. Sauvignon Blanc was considered given the high acid and freshness, but the profile lacks pyrazine/gooseberry lift and instead carries a more textured stone-fruit and saline character. Assyrtiko was also considered for its saline minerality, but it lacks the peach and apricot fruit richness shown here. This combination points to Albariño.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
The oceanic salinity and bright, citrus-driven fruit indicate a cool, Atlantic-influenced coastal site rather than a Mediterranean one, narrowing to Rías Baixas, Galicia, Spain.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
A clean, unoaked, dry style built on primary fruit and briny freshness, equivalent to a good, well-made DO Rías Baixas retailing around $18-22 — solid mid-premium quality rather than a top single-vineyard example, which would show greater concentration and length. At two years old this is in its primary phase; it should be drunk over the next 1-2 years before the fresh citrus and saline lift begin to fade.

## Wine 8

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Pale straw to light gold, with lemon, green apple, fig and fresh herb aromatics and a subtle textural grip on the palate alongside lively, moderate-high acidity. No oak or buttery lees character is evident. Sauvignon Blanc was eliminated — the aromatics lack pungent tropical or blackcurrant-leaf lift, and the phenolic, slightly waxy grip is not typical of that variety. Unoaked Chardonnay was also considered but the linear, citrus-driven acid line and herbal green edge here are more precise than Chardonnay's rounder stone fruit. This points to Semillon.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
The purity of primary fruit with no botrytis or oxidative development rules out Sauternes, and the absence of overt French oak rules out a white Bordeaux blend. The freshness and precision, rather than the broader, lower-acid style typical of Hunter Valley Semillon, point to a cooler maritime New World site — Margaret River, Western Australia.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
A pure, unoaked varietal expression relying on fruit purity and natural grip rather than winemaking amplification — very good quality, estate-level Margaret River white retailing around $35-45. Still youthful at two years, it has the acid and phenolic structure to develop honeyed, waxy complexity over the next 5-8 years and will hold comfortably for a decade.

## Wine 9

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Deep gold colour with a powerful nose of white pepper, lentil, lemon oil, quince and ripe peach. Full-bodied and dense, with high acidity balancing a notably high 14.5% alcohol, and no discernible new oak. Chardonnay was considered given the weight and richness, but the absence of vanilla or toast and the presence of persistent white pepper spice rule it out. Viognier was also considered for its aromatic intensity, but the acid here is far more incisive and the spice profile is pepper-and-lentil rather than peach-blossom. This points to Grüner Veltliner.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
High natural acidity retained despite considerable ripeness, combined with pronounced spice and no new oak, indicates a cool-climate site capable of pushing sugar ripeness while preserving acid through marked diurnal shift — Austria's Wachau. The 14.5% alcohol places this at the top of the region's ripeness ladder, the Smaragd category.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
A powerful, dry, oak-free style built for long development — outstanding quality, benchmark single-vineyard Smaragd comparable in stature to a Grand Cru white Burgundy, retailing around $55-70. Still primary at three years old, its acid-alcohol balance gives real capacity to improve for a further 8-10 years and hold safely for 15-20, developing honeyed spice and waxy depth.

## Wine 10

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Deep gold with an intensely aromatic nose of lychee, rose petal, Turkish delight and ginger spice, full-bodied and broad on the palate with notably low acidity and a dry, oily finish. Muscat was considered for its floral lift but is typically more grapey and less spice-driven with a lighter frame. Viognier — the other floral, low-acid, full-bodied candidate in this flight — is ruled out by the specific lychee-rose-ginger signature here rather than the peach-apricot-violet profile of Wine 11. This is unmistakably Gewurztraminer.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
The deep colour from extended skin contact on a pink-berried variety, combined with a genuinely dry finish at high alcohol rather than the off-dry style seen in Germany or Alto Adige, points to a house committed to dry vinification — Alsace AOC, Haut-Rhin.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
A deliberately dry, full-bodied, aromatic style from a house whose approach favours structure over sweetness — outstanding quality for varietal AOC Alsace, retailing around $30-40. Developing at three years, its naturally low acidity means it will hold rather than dramatically improve; best drunk within the next 5-6 years before the spice character softens and the wine loses definition.

## Wine 11

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Deep gold with an intensely perfumed nose of white peach, apricot, violet and anise, full-bodied with a silky, almost unctuous texture and naturally low acidity. Set against Wine 10's Gewurztraminer, the fruit here is stone-fruit and violet rather than lychee and rose, with a more mineral, granite-inflected edge on the finish. Marsanne and Roussanne were considered for their similarly low-acid, full-bodied Rhône profile, but neither shows this degree of floral lift. This points to Viognier.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
The granite-mineral edge beneath the ripe fruit, together with restrained 14% alcohol rather than the riper, more tropical register of New World Viognier, indicates a cool site on decomposed granite — Condrieu, Northern Rhône.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
A dry, full, low-acid style made for early enjoyment — very good quality, a solid mid-tier Condrieu from outside the appellation's best-known names, retailing around $45-55. At two years old this sits right in its optimal drinking window; Viognier's floral lift fades quickly, so it should be consumed within the next 1-2 years rather than cellared.

## Wine 12

### a) Variety (10 marks).
Deep gold with amber hints, showing developed petrol/kerosene character alongside preserved lemon, beeswax, ginger and slate minerality. Medium-full bodied, with high, racy acidity cutting through the mature fruit and a very dry, long mineral finish. Aged Chenin Blanc was considered for its capacity to develop honeyed, waxy notes, but the distinctive petrol/TDN character is decisive for Riesling; aged white Burgundy was ruled out by the far higher, racier acid and complete absence of oak or buttery lees work. This is Riesling.

### b) Origin (5 marks).
The fuller body and dry, high-alcohol style (13%) alongside pronounced petrol development point to Alsace rather than Germany, which at this stage of maturity would more likely retain some residual sweetness and lower alcohol, or Austria, which would show more overt spice. The concentration and dry extract suggest fruit from a top, likely Grand Cru-designated, site — Alsace AOC, Haut-Rhin.

### c) Quality, style and ageing (10 marks).
The combination of racy, still-vibrant acidity against fully developed tertiary fruit is the tell here — it reveals a wine picked at genuine physiological ripeness on a premium site rather than one relying on residual sugar for balance, and confirms the ambition behind this cuvée. Outstanding quality, equivalent to Grand Cru-level Alsace Riesling, a prestige bottling retailing around $60-80. At eight years old it is entering a beautiful secondary phase; the acid backbone gives it a further 5-10 years of improvement and a total drinking window of 20-25 years, developing deeper honeyed, waxy and mineral complexity as the petrol notes intensify.
