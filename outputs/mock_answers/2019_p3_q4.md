# Mock answer — 2019 Paper 3 Question 4

## a) Comment on the method of production (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 9** — pale straw colour, pungent camomile and green-apple, briny nose, with a bone-dry, piercingly saline palate at 15% abv point to Palomino fermented fully dry then fortified precisely to the level that lets flor survive. It is then aged biologically under a living veil of yeast in a solera, young wine constantly refreshing the system; the flor's consumption of glycerol and oxygen and its production of acetaldehyde explain the green-apple pungency and the total absence of oxidative browning.

**Wine 10** — by contrast, deep gold-amber colour, walnut, curry spice, dried apricot and beeswax, with searing acidity and a lower 13% abv, point to an unfortified white matured in old, un-topped barrels under its own yeast film rather than flor. The nutty, oxidative intensity suggests several years' ageing sous voile, though the fresher fruit register alongside the nuttiness suggests something short of the six-year threshold defining formal vin jaune. Both wines share the same underlying principle — biological ageing beneath a living yeast film that excludes air yet drives an oxidative aromatic profile — one fortified and solera-blended, the other unfortified and single-vintage.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 9** — the salty, sea-breeze intensity and unusually fresh, delicate flor character point beyond generic Sherry to Sanlúcar de Barrameda rather than Jerez de la Frontera itself; the Atlantic-facing, humid microclimate sustains a thicker, more continuous flor blanket than the hotter inland town, giving the wine its Manzanilla identity within Jerez DO.

**Wine 10** — the oxidative, nutty, curry-spiced profile signals Jura over any other French white region. The elegant, saline-edged precision, rather than the fuller, more rustic weight of Château-Chalon, and the fine acid line point to the limestone-influenced L'Étoile appellation, whose blue lias soils give a lighter, more mineral sous voile style than its neighbours.

## c) Comment on the style, quality, and commercial positioning (2 x 9 marks)

**Wine 9** — bone dry, light-bodied, saline and precise; a benchmark Manzanilla of very good quality, equivalent to a well-aged, solera-blended commercial Manzanilla retailing around $15-20. It sits with Independent Specialists and tapas-led on-trade lists, competing with premium Fino for consumers seeking a savoury, low-alcohol aperitif style.

**Wine 10** — rich, complex, savoury-oxidative with real length; very good to outstanding quality within L'Étoile, comparable to a premium single-vineyard bottling retailing around $35-45. Sold almost exclusively through independent specialists and sommelier-led lists, it serves a narrow but committed audience of Jura enthusiasts. Both wines occupy connoisseur niches rather than mainstream retail — Wine 9 through solera consistency, Wine 10 through Jura's low-volume, cult specialist appeal.
