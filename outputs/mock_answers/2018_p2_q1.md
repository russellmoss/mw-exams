# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 2 Question 1

The stem signals five classic Western European benchmarks, so the expectation here is confident, specific appellation calls rather than hedged generic origins.

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible, making reference to the grape variety(ies) used (5 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Deep ruby moving to garnet, with sour cherry, dried rose and balsamic, leathery spice; high, bright acidity against firm, still-drying tannin and a warm 14.5% alcohol. This acid-tannin combination with garnet development points to Sangiovese rather than Nebbiolo — Nebbiolo would show a paler, more onion-skin rim and a tar-and-rose profile with even higher relative acidity against lower alcohol. The riper, warmer fruit and structured but rounder tannin here indicate central Italy, specifically Montalcino: Brunello di Montalcino.

**Wine 2.** Pale-to-medium, translucent ruby with red cherry, rose petal and fine earth; precise, silky texture, fine tannin, high polished acidity and a moderate 13% alcohol. The combination of pale colour, red-fruit purity and fine-grained tannin points to Pinot Noir over Gamay — the latter would show more confected, carbonic fruit and looser structure. The perfume-over-power profile and fine, transparent texture, rather than the broader, more muscular tannin of neighbouring communes, narrow this to Burgundy's Côte de Nuits, and specifically Chambolle-Musigny.

**Wine 3.** Deep ruby with smoked meat, violet, black olive, cassis and cracked pepper; concentrated, tightly structured, savoury and reductive, with a long, ferrous, mineral finish. Violet, pepper and olive point to Syrah over Cabernet Sauvignon, which would show blackcurrant leaf rather than olive and a firmer, less savoury tannin. The moderate 13.5% alcohol with pronounced pepper and violet (rather than riper New World fruit) suggests a cool northern climate; the schistous, ferrous minerality and single-site density point to Côte-Rôtie in the Northern Rhône.

**Wine 4.** Garnet-ruby with cedar, dried blackcurrant, tobacco, earth and truffle; medium-full body with resolved but present tannin and a savoury, mature finish. The softer, more plush dried-fruit register alongside cedar spice suggests a Merlot-led blend rather than Cabernet Sauvignon dominance, which would retain a firmer cassis-led backbone even at this age. The mature, garnet colour with fifteen-plus years of development and a Right Bank limestone-clay signature point to Bordeaux, Saint-Émilion.

**Wine 5.** Ruby with a garnet rim, dried red cherry, coconut, cedar, vanilla, leather and sweet spice; dry, medium-bodied, with smooth, polished tannin and a warm 14% alcohol. The coconut-vanilla oak signature layered over dried red fruit and leather is the textbook marker of Tempranillo raised in American oak rather than Grenache, which would show riper, more raisined fruit and looser structure. This oak-driven, traditionally aged profile places the wine unmistakably in Rioja.

## b) Discuss quality within the context of the region of origin (5 x 8 marks)

**Wine 1** — outstanding quality, at the top of the Brunello di Montalcino Riserva DOCG tier, retailing around $70-90; concentrated fruit, integrated tannin and a long finish show benchmark typicity without losing Sangiovese's acid spine.

**Wine 2** — very good to outstanding quality, at the top of the village tier and verging on Premier Cru depth, retailing around $90-120; the precision and persistence are unusual for straight village Chambolle-Musigny.

**Wine 3** — outstanding quality, comparable to a top named Côte-Rôtie site, retailing around $120-180; length and concentration sit well above generic Côte-Rôtie AOC.

**Wine 4** — very good quality, solid Saint-Émilion Grand Cru rather than Premier Grand Cru Classé depth, originally around $50-70 on release; the tannin has resolved gracefully but the wine lacks the extra layering of a classified growth.

**Wine 5** — good to very good quality, a classic commercial Gran Reserva retailing around $25-35; reliable regional typicity rather than singular complexity.

## c) What has the winemaker done to maximise quality and regional typicity during the winemaking process? (5 x 7 marks)

**Wine 1** — 100% Sangiovese, long maceration for structure, then extended ageing in large, neutral Slavonian botti rather than barrique, preserving the variety's sour-cherry fruit and acid line instead of masking it with new-oak vanillin; the additional cask and bottle time mandated for Riserva explains the leather and balsamic development.

**Wine 2** — likely partial whole-bunch inclusion and gentle extraction to protect the perfumed, silky texture, with a measured proportion of new French oak that supports rather than dominates the fruit — winemaking choices geared to transparency over power, as this commune demands.

**Wine 3** — Syrah from steep schist terraces, probably co-fermented with a portion of whole bunches/stems to lift the violet-pepper lift and add grip, matured in new French oak that integrates spice without smothering the olive and reduction that define the appellation.

**Wine 4** — traditional Merlot-led fermentation and maceration for structure, matured in new French oak barrique for the cedar spice now evident, with roughly eighteen years of bottle age resolving the once-firmer tannin into today's leather and truffle complexity.

**Wine 5** — extended maturation, predominantly in American oak, explains the pronounced coconut-vanilla signature; the long combined barrel-and-bottle ageing required for Gran Reserva accounts for the resolved tannin and leather development — a deliberate choice for traditional Rioja typicity over a modern, fruit-forward style.
