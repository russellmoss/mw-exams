# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (10 marks)

Chenin Blanc. Both wines carry a quince and honeyed lanolin register with a waxy, wet-wool edge, and both retain very high, persistent acidity even where ripeness and alcohol diverge sharply between them — a hallmark of Chenin's naturally high natural acid retention regardless of climate. Riesling was considered for the piercing acidity but ruled out by the honeyed, waxy texture rather than Riesling's more petrol-and-lime purity; Chardonnay was eliminated by the acid level, which is too searing and the fruit too orchard/quince-driven for Chardonnay's stone-fruit register; Sémillon was considered given the lanolin note but its lower acid and waxier, less taut structure do not match either wine.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — Piekenierskloof, South Africa. The 14% alcohol, deep gold colour and riper guava/pineapple fruit point to a warm New World origin; the textural richness and old-vine concentration, together with acidity still fresh rather than flabby, suggest high-altitude old bush-vine fruit rather than warm valley-floor Chenin, narrowing this to a cooler, elevated Western Cape ward such as Piekenierskloof rather than Swartland or Stellenbosch.

**Wine 6** — Vouvray, Loire Valley, France. The paler colour, lower 12.5% alcohol, honeysuckle-quince-apple fruit and taut, chalky acid line point to a cool Old World site; the soft, slightly wet-stone minerality is consistent with tuffeau limestone, narrowing this to Vouvray on the Loire's right bank near Tours.

## c) Discuss quality, with particular reference to winemaking (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** is very good quality, comparable to premium single-vineyard Western Cape Chenin retailing around $25–35. The generous, rounded texture and integrated warmth suggest wild-yeast fermentation partly in older barrels with some lees contact, restrained MLF preserving the underlying freshness, and a fully dry finish — old-vine concentration doing the work rather than new oak, none of which is evident.

**Wine 6** is good quality, Village-level Vouvray around $18–25. The neutral, mineral purity indicates stainless steel or large old wood with no MLF, protecting the piercing natural acidity. That acidity carries a distinct textural sweetness on the mid-palate rather than a bone-dry finish, pointing to a demi-sec style from selective harvesting in a cool, high-acid vintage rather than a fully dry sec.
