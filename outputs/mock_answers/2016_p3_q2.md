# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

All three wines carry an identical 20% alcohol, so strength gives no help in telling them apart — the discriminator has to come from colour, oxidative character, sweetness and texture.

**Wine 6** shows amber-to-mahogany colour with walnut, orange peel and saline notes, bone dry yet glyceral and expansive, combining the aromatic finesse of a wine that began under a yeast veil with the breadth of a fully oxidised style. This hybrid profile — Amontillado-like nose over Oloroso-like body — is the calling card of Palo Cortado, and its saline, almost chalky undertone points specifically to Jerez de la Frontera in Andalucía, southern Spain.

**Wine 7** is sweeter, with fig, toffee, orange peel and coffee over a soft tawny rim; the palate is rich but carried by spirit and acidity rather than tannin, red-grape structure having dissolved into cask-derived complexity. This retained sugar and nutty oxidative character, with no saline or acetaldehyde lift, points away from Jerez and toward the Douro Valley of northern Portugal, home to aged tawny wine of this style.

**Wine 8** is the driest and most linear of the three, pale amber-gold with a lifted volatile edge, citrus peel, roasted nuts and a smoky, saline finish; the acidity is searing rather than broad. Such racy acid allied to burnt-sugar oxidative character is not a feature of Jerez or the Douro and instead is the fingerprint of the island of Madeira, off the Portuguese coast.

## b) Discuss the key winemaking techniques used to arrive at this style (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 6** begins as a dry-fermented Palomino base wine given an initial fortification low enough, around 15%, to sit under flor like a Fino; when the veil breaks down unevenly the barrel is reclassified and pushed on to around 17-18%, letting air-influenced ageing take over and drive the aldehydic, nutty depth in the glass. Extended ageing through a fractional criadera y solera system then blends multiple vintages, so the wine's declared age is an average rather than a single harvest — an important point given the NV designation.

**Wine 7** is fortified with grape spirit part-way through fermentation, arresting the yeast before all the sugar converts and locking in the fig-and-toffee sweetness evident on the palate. It is then aged oxidatively in large, seasoned wooden casks that impart colour and nutty complexity without fresh oak flavour; as with Wine 6, the "20 Year" designation reflects an average age across a blended stock rather than a single vintage.

**Wine 8**'s Sercial base is fortified after a longer-than-usual fermentation for Madeira, preserving the variety's naturally high acidity while keeping the wine essentially dry. The defining step is thermal: rather than the accelerated estufagem tank-heating used for basic Madeira, a wine of this age and quality is matured slowly in warm lofts by the canteiro method, deliberately exposing it to heat and oxygen over years — the technique behind the burnt-sugar, smoky character and the near-indestructible stability that follows.

## c) Comment on quality and maturity (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 6** is very good to excellent quality, equivalent to a VOS-level Palo Cortado retailing around $40-60; the concentration and aldehydic complexity go well beyond a basic Amontillado. As a solera blend it has no single vintage to mature — it arrives fully resolved and will hold indefinitely once opened.

**Wine 7** is outstanding quality for its category, comparable to a premium 20 Year Old Tawny at $50-70; the balance of sweetness, spirit and length places it above a basic Reserve Tawny. The average-age blend is ready to drink on release and needs no further bottle ageing, though it holds its character for years once opened.

**Wine 8** is outstanding quality for a 10 Year Old varietal Madeira, priced around $35-50, its intense acid-driven length distinguishing it from an entry-level 3 Year Old style. Fully mature through its long canteiro ageing, it is ready now and, like all Madeira, essentially indestructible once opened.
