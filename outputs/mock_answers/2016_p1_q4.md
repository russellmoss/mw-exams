# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 1 Question 4

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7.** Deep gold colour with developed nutty, waxy, honeyed notes over toasted oak and dried straw point to a mature, deliberately oxidative white built for long ageing. The firm acid and grippy oak tannin under 13.5% alcohol argue against a Loire or Rhône white, where such an overt barrel imprint on a still-fresh acid line is unusual; the profile reads as old-vine Viura fattened with Garnacha Blanca and worked hard in barrel — the traditional style of white Rioja. The developed colour and complexity suggest Rioja Alavesa fruit from a serious, small-production estate rather than a large industrial bodega.

**Wine 8.** Pale lemon-gold colour, taut citrus and white peach fruit with a distinct saline, granite-like minerality and racy acidity at a modest 12.5% alcohol identify a cool, maritime, Atlantic-facing origin. The combination of stone-fruit intensity and salinity is the calling card of Albariño; Vinho Verde was considered but rejected on the riper fruit register and the absence of the spritz and leaner body typical there. The purity and precision point to Rías Baixas, most likely the Val do Salnés heartland rather than a warmer inland subzone.

## b) Comment on the winemaking (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 7.** The rich texture, integrated toasty oak and nutty, slightly oxidative development indicate barrel fermentation followed by extended ageing in old, seasoned barrels with periodic lees stirring — the traditional white Rioja method that predates stainless steel and deliberately courts slow oxidation over fresh fruit retention. Malolactic fermentation appears complete, softening the acid and adding to the waxy, buttery register.

**Wine 8.** The pristine citrus and orchard fruit with no oak or buttery weight point to cool stainless-steel fermentation with malolactic blocked to preserve acidity. Brief pre-fermentation skin contact or short lees contact without stirring would explain the textural roundness beneath the cut, but there is no evidence of barrel or extended lees work — every choice here protects primary fruit and freshness.

## c) Discuss style and quality (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 7.** A serious, age-worthy traditional white Rioja, very good quality equivalent to a top estate Reserva Blanco, retailing around £25. The balance of firm acid against developed complexity gives genuine typicity for the old style; it is drinking well now but has the structure to hold for several more years.

**Wine 8.** A well-made, classic varietal Albariño, good quality at Rías Baixas estate benchmark level, retailing around £15. Clean, precise and commercially reliable rather than profound, it is built for early drinking and shows little benefit from further ageing.
