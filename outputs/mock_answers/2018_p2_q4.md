# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 2 Question 4

## a) Identify the grape variety(ies) used (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 11.** Zinfandel. The deep, almost opaque ruby-purple colour, jammy blackberry and black cherry fruit shot through with black pepper and a raisiny, semi-dried edge, allied to broad, soft tannins and a warm 14.5% alcohol, point to Zinfandel's characteristic uneven ripening within the bunch, which produces this jammy-plus-fresh fruit spectrum. Grenache was considered for the pepper and warmth but lacks the density of fruit and the higher, grippier tannin shown here.

**Wine 12.** Cabernet Sauvignon. Cassis, graphite and mint aromatics with firm, fine-grained tannin and a savoury cedar edge are classic Cabernet markers; the moderate 13.5% alcohol and precise structural line rule out a Merlot-dominant blend, which would show softer, plummier fruit and less grip.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 11.** Lodi, California. The sheer ripeness and generosity of fruit, plus the plush, broad-shouldered tannin, point to a warm inland California site rather than a coastal one; the old-vine texture and jammy concentration are the signature of Lodi's Central Valley sand and warm diurnal range, the historic old-vine Zinfandel heartland of the state.

**Wine 12.** Puente Alto, Maipo, Chile. The cassis-graphite purity combined with a cool, fresh mineral edge running beneath ripe tannin suggests cold Andean night-time influence rather than a warmer, flatter site; this points to Upper Maipo's Puente Alto district, where alluvial gravels over the Maipo river and cool mountain air yield Chile's most structured, age-worthy Cabernet.

## c) Discuss quality in relation to the region of origin (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 11.** Good to very good quality for the Lodi Zinfandel style — an honest, well-made regional benchmark rather than a fine-wine appellation bottling, comparable to Lodi's better-known old-vine labels retailing around $18–22. The ripe, pleasing fruit and soft tannin are typical of the appellation's commercial mainstream; it lacks the layered complexity or structural precision that would push it toward a single-vineyard, boutique-scale wine.

**Wine 12.** Outstanding, icon-tier quality, on a par with Chile's very best and competing directly with Napa Cabernet and classed-growth Bordeaux at $150–250+. The restrained 13.5% alcohol, in a country capable of much riper wines, is the clearest evidence of deliberate site-driven balance rather than commercial ripeness-chasing; combined with fine tannin structure and aromatic precision, this reflects Puente Alto's status as Chile's single most prestigious Cabernet terroir. This should not be under-rated for being Chilean — it stands comparison with the world's finest Cabernet Sauvignon.
