# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 1 Question 1

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** The pale-to-medium lemon colour, ripe citrus and orchard fruit intertwined with hazelnut and a light buttery-oak polish over a stony, saline core point unmistakably to Chardonnay from Burgundy's Côte de Beaune. The moderate 13% alcohol and creamy, barrel-worked texture, generous but not grand in scale, argue against Puligny-Montrachet, whose more citrus-driven, flinty austerity does not match this broad, nutty roundness. That generous, savoury-nutty register with real textural weight funnels the call specifically to Meursault, at village level.

**Wine 2.** The same grape and broad origin recur, but at a different pitch of intensity: concentrated citrus and stone fruit, smoky reduction, toast and struck-flint sit over a far more vertical, driving mineral spine, with a denser palate and a markedly longer finish. This scale, allied to 13.5% alcohol, points beyond village or Premier Cru to Grand Cru white Burgundy. The specific combination of flinty reduction and architectural power narrows the origin to Corton-Charlemagne, the Côte de Beaune's principal white Grand Cru outside Montrachet.

## b) Compare the two wines: quality, maturity, oak, market position (30 marks)

With producer and vintage held constant, every difference between the two glasses is a function of site and appellation hierarchy rather than winemaking or seasonal variation — that is the entire point of this pairing. Wine 1 is very good to outstanding quality, equivalent to a top-flight village Meursault edging toward Premier Cru intensity: fine balance and real textural interest, but without the Grand Cru's layered complexity and persistence. Wine 2 is outstanding quality, a benchmark Grand Cru whose concentration, precision and extended finish justify its tier. Both show new oak, but proportionately rather than absolutely: Wine 1's lighter fruit concentration lets its buttered-toast character sit a little more forward, while Wine 2's greater fruit density absorbs a comparable or higher proportion of new wood without it dominating — the same cellar regime reading quite differently across two tiers of fruit. Both are youthful, primary-dominant wines of around 2-3 years of age with development ahead: Wine 1 should improve for a further 3-4 years and hold for 4-5 more; Wine 2's firmer structure and mineral drive give it 8-10 years of further improvement and a substantially longer window, developing honeyed, brioche and flint-driven complexity. Commercially, Wine 1 suits premium independent retailers and by-the-glass Burgundy lists at around £60-80, competing with other village Meursault and Puligny-Montrachet; Wine 2 belongs on fine-wine lists and in specialist and auction channels at £200 or more, competing with Grand Cru Chassagne and Puligny-Montrachet for the collector and top-restaurant market.
