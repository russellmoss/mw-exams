# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible, making reference to the grape variety(ies) used (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7.** The full body, 14% alcohol and layered toasted-hazelnut, lanolin and honeyed-grapefruit character point to a barrel-fermented Bordeaux blend rather than Burgundy: the boxwood-edged citrus cut is Sauvignon Blanc, the waxy, honeyed weight underneath is Sémillon. White Burgundy was considered but the pungent citrus-leaf lift and unusually high alcohol for Chardonnay rule it out. The creamy, biscuity texture confirms barrel fermentation with lees stirring, and the toasted, custardy secondary layer at this age is classic of a classed-growth Graves white. This is Pessac-Léognan, Bordeaux; the ripe 14% is consistent with the celebrated 2010 vintage.

**Wine 8.** Full-bodied but noticeably low in acidity, with an oily texture, white peach, apricot and herbal garrigue lift, this points south rather than to Bordeaux or Burgundy. The lack of Sauvignon-style citrus cut rules out a Bordeaux blend, and the broad, low-acid weight is well beyond anything Chardonnay shows. The Grenache Blanc-led richness, fennel-and-stone-fruit aromatics and minimal oak point to a southern Rhône white blend of Grenache Blanc, Clairette, Roussanne and Bourboulenc. This is Châteauneuf-du-Pape blanc, Southern Rhône — a rare style, representing only around 5% of the appellation's output.

**Wine 9.** Medium body, racier acidity, citrus blossom, green apple and a flinty mineral edge, with only subtle vanilla oak, point unmistakably to Chardonnay. The leaner frame and higher acid immediately rule out the riper, waxier Sémillon-based Wine 7 and the broad, low-acid Rhône blend of Wine 8. Within Burgundy, the structure is tighter and less generously fruited than a Meursault or Puligny-Montrachet village wine would show, suggesting a cooler, higher side-valley site on the Côte de Beaune. This is Saint-Romain, Côte de Beaune, Burgundy.

## b) Discuss the quality in the context of the region of origin (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 7.** Outstanding quality, equivalent to classed-growth (Cru Classé de Graves) level. The concentration, layered honeyed-nutty development, creamy texture and very long, savoury finish show the depth and ageing capacity that separates a classified Pessac-Léognan white from generic AOC examples; retailing around £70-90 against £25-35 for unclassified Pessac blanc.

**Wine 8.** Good to very good quality for Châteauneuf-du-Pape blanc, which has no internal cru ladder. The purity of fruit and textured, concentrated palate place it toward the top of estate-level white CdP production, though it lacks the extra layering and precision of the very finest examples; a realistic retail level of around £35-45.

**Wine 9.** Very good quality for village-level Saint-Romain, an appellation with no Premier or Grand Cru. The precision, freshness and clean varietal expression are typical of a well-made négociant village wine, though it does not have the concentration or depth of a village Meursault or Puligny; priced around £20-28.

## c) Comment on maturity (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 7.** At eight years from a landmark vintage, this is developing beautifully, with honeyed, toasted secondary notes emerging over the citrus core; it is drinking well now but has the structure to improve for a further 5-8 years and hold for a decade beyond that.

**Wine 8.** At four years, this sits within the typical 3-6 year drinking window for white CdP; it is ready to drink now and will hold for another 1-2 years before its naturally low acidity leaves it prone to oxidative fade.

**Wine 9.** At three years, this is moving from primary into early secondary character; it is best enjoyed now to 2-3 years, with village-level structure that will not reward extended cellaring.
