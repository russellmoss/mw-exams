# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 3 Question 2

## a) Highlight the key winemaking techniques used (15 marks)

The deep amber-orange colour and genuine, grippy tannin on a wine made from white grapes point directly to extended skin-contact fermentation rather than conventional pressed-juice white winemaking. The oxidative register of dried apricot, quince, walnut and tea leaf, with a broad textured palate and low-to-medium acidity, is consistent with ambient/wild-yeast fermentation and minimal sulphur use, allowing natural oxidative and phenolic development. Maturation appears to be in neutral vessels — old large-format oak or amphora — since there is no fresh oak spice or vanilla. The unfiltered-looking texture suggests little or no fining or filtration, leaving phenolics and solids intact. The 15% alcohol points to very ripe, late-picked fruit fermented fully to dryness, consistent with a warm, even growing season.

## b) Discuss its style, quality and possible market positioning (10 marks)

This sits firmly in the skin-contact "orange wine" category — deliberately divisive and textural rather than fault-driven. Judged within that category, the concentration, aromatic complexity and long finish indicate very good quality, comparable to leading examples of the genre. It is a low-volume, high-conviction wine suited to Independent Specialists and sommelier-curated by-the-glass lists rather than supermarkets, retailing around £30-40, targeting curious, experienced drinkers seeking alternatives to mainstream whites, with export potential into natural-wine-focused markets such as the UK, US and Scandinavia.
