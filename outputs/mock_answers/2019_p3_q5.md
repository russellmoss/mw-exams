# Mock answer — 2019 Paper 3 Question 5

## a) Compare and contrast the method of production (14 marks)

Both wines share an orange-blossom, dried-apricot lift pointing to Muscat, but reach concentration by different mechanisms. Wine 11's amber-gold colour, racy acidity and 14.5% alcohol with no spirit heat indicate appassimento: grapes dried on mats to concentrate sugars, fermented until alcohol and RS reach natural equilibrium, then held in stainless steel with no oak to preserve primary purity. Wine 12's deeper tawny-mahogany hue, rancio, toffee and coffee notes and a hot 17% finish point instead to fortification: semi-raisined fruit arrested early by grape spirit, then aged oxidatively in old wood through a fractional, solera-style system layering multiple vintages. Wine 11 relies on sun and natural balance for sweetness; Wine 12 achieves it chemically, building complexity through deliberate oxidative age rather than fruit freshness.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 11** — The perfumed orange-peel and jasmine character, alongside a saline, volcanic mineral edge, points beyond mainland Sicily to a wind-battered Mediterranean island. Combined with the Muscat of Alexandria (Zibibbo) variety, this identifies Pantelleria, the volcanic island DOC between Sicily and Tunisia.

**Wine 12** — The deep tawny colour, rancio and Christmas-cake concentration, allied to old-wood ageing and a warm, fortified style, is unmistakably Australian. The Brown Muscat grape and fractional blending tradition narrow this precisely to Rutherglen in north-east Victoria, the historic home of the style.

## c) Comment on quality and maturity (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 11** — Outstanding quality: the layered dried-fruit and floral complexity, unctuous yet lifted texture and long finish sit well above basic Passito di Pantelleria, at a benchmark DOC level retailing around $40–50 for the standard 50cl bottle. The amber-gold colour and vibrant primary fruit indicate a young wine of 2–3 years, delicious now with the freshness to hold and gain honeyed complexity for a further 5–8 years.

**Wine 12** — Very good to outstanding quality within the Rutherglen classification: the depth of rancio, coffee and toffee complexity suggests a Classic-tier blend above entry-level Rutherglen, retailing around $40 per 500ml. As a non-vintage fractional blend there is no single vintage to assess; the average blend age gives immediate developed complexity, it is fully ready to drink now, and the solera system means it will show consistent house style rather than evolve further in bottle.

## d) State the level of residual sugar (2 x 2 marks)

**Wine 11** — Approximately 150–180 g/L, consistent with concentrated but not cloying sweetness on the palate.

**Wine 12** — Approximately 220–260 g/L, reflecting greater raisining and a longer, more viscous finish.

## e) State the level of alcohol (2 x 2 marks)

**Wine 11** — 14.5%, entirely natural, reached when fermentation found its own equilibrium against the high sugar level.

**Wine 12** — 17.0%, boosted by the addition of grape spirit used to arrest fermentation and lock in sweetness.
