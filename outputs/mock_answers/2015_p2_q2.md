# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 2 Question 2

## Wine 4

**a) Origin.** Garnet colour with an orange rim, leather and dried strawberry fruit, and pronounced vanilla, coconut and dill from oak point to Tempranillo aged in a significant proportion of American oak — a marker no other Old World Tempranillo region shows to this degree. Ribera del Duero was considered but its typically darker fruit and firmer tannin don't fit the soft, integrated palate here. Rioja DOCa, Rioja Alta.

**b) Variety.** Tempranillo: red cherry and strawberry fruit, moderate acidity and soft, resolved tannin carrying the oak easily; a cedary, herbal lift suggests a small proportion of Graciano or Mazuelo in the blend.

**c) Method of production.** The harmonious, well-integrated oak — sweet American vanilla alongside more restrained French cedar — points to a long élevage, likely close to two years in barrel, followed by further bottle ageing before release: consistent with the Reserva minimum of one year in oak and three years total ageing. The pure, unbaked fruit beneath the oak implies careful, cool fermentation.

**d) Style, quality and improvement.** Traditional, oak-driven rather than fruit-forward. Outstanding for the Reserva tier — length and oak integration place it near the top of the category, around £15. At five years it is just entering its window; fine tannin and balanced acidity suggest 3-5 more years of improvement and a decade of holding, gaining leather and dried-fruit complexity.

## Wine 5

**a) Origin.** Deep, youthful purple-ruby colour, blackberry and cassis fruit, black pepper and smoked meat framed by vanilla and mocha oak point to warm-climate New World Shiraz. Plush tannin and 14.5% alcohol rule out cooler Australian sites like Yarra Valley; the density of fruit and old-vine character favour Barossa Valley over the more savoury, mineral McLaren Vale.

**b) Variety.** Shiraz: ripe blackberry and plum fruit, black pepper spice and soft, high-glycerol texture. Cabernet Sauvignon was considered but eliminated by the absence of firm, grippy tannin and cassis-dominant fruit.

**c) Method of production.** Dark fruit concentration with ripe rather than green tannin suggests fully phenolically ripe fruit, fermented in stainless steel with extended maceration and regular pump-overs. Vanilla-mocha rather than fresh toast oak points to a mix of American and predominantly older barrels, used around 12-18 months to round the wine without masking fruit.

**d) Style, quality and improvement.** A generous, commercial warm-climate style rather than reserve tier. Good quality, equivalent to a solid regional Barossa bottling around £15 — built for immediate pleasure over complexity. Already open at two years, with little to gain from cellaring; best drunk over the next 2-3 years.

## Wine 6

**a) Origin.** Pale-to-medium translucent ruby, lifted violet, iris and fresh cherry aromatics, silky near-imperceptible tannin and a short, delicate finish point to Gamay from Beaujolais. Burgundian Pinot Noir was considered given the pale colour but eliminated by the kirsch-like fruit and absence of any savoury undertow. The purity of floral intensity and light 12.5% alcohol point beyond generic Beaujolais-Villages to a granite cru — Fleurie.

**b) Variety.** Gamay: confected red-berry and floral character, negligible tannin and bright, moderate acidity. Pinot Noir ruled out for lacking structural weight or savoury complexity.

**c) Method of production.** Pure cherry-kirsch fruit with no green edge and near-total absence of tannin point to semi-carbonic maceration on whole berries rather than a destemmed vinification, with a short cuvaison preserving freshness. No oak is discernible, indicating stainless steel or neutral large wood, and the youthful, primary fruit suggests an early release.

**d) Style, quality and improvement.** Light, aromatic, early-drinking rather than age-worthy. Good quality, cru Beaujolais level — floral intensity lifts it above Beaujolais-Villages but the short finish keeps it below sturdier crus like Moulin-à-Vent; around £18 given the cru premium. Fully ready at two years, with no real capacity to improve; drink within 1-2 years while the aromatic lift remains.
