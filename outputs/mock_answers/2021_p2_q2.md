# Mock answer — 2021 Paper 2 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 4** — Pinot Noir. Medium ruby with lifted red cherry, raspberry and rose petal over a savoury, stemmy edge, and a dry, medium-bodied palate with fine, silky tannin and bright acidity. Nebbiolo is eliminated by the absence of tar, orange peel and grippy tannin; Grenache by the finer tannin grain and more precise, less soft acidity.

**Wine 5** — Nebbiolo. Pale-to-medium garnet with an orange-tinged rim, rose, dried cherry, tar and orange peel, backed by ferociously drying, fine-grained tannin and searing acidity that neither Pinot Noir nor Grenache shows at this intensity. Grenache was considered for the rose-adjacent perfume but its softer tannin and lower acid rule it out.

**Wine 6** — Grenache. Medium ruby, bright red plum and raspberry with dried herb, white pepper and warm spice; the palate is soft-tannined, only moderately acidic, and finishes with generous alcohol warmth. This lack of grip immediately excludes Nebbiolo, while the riper, sweeter fruit and rounder texture rule out Pinot Noir.

## b) Identify the country and region as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 4** — California, Sonoma Coast. The precise red fruit, savoury lift and only moderate 14.1% alcohol point to a maritime, fog-influenced New World site rather than warm inland California — the fine tannin and mineral edge argue against the riper, rounder style typical of Russian River Valley. The subtle, partly new French oak framing without overt sweetness is consistent with a serious, cool coastal Pinot Noir producer, narrowing this to the true Sonoma Coast rather than a broader Central Coast blend.

**Wine 5** — Piedmont, Italy, Barbaresco DOCG. The tar-rose-orange-peel signature and 14.5% alcohol confirm Nebbiolo from a warm Piedmontese vintage, but the tannin, while firm, is ripe and already somewhat open rather than the most powerful, structured expression — this points to Barbaresco rather than Barolo, whose Tortonian marl and lower hillside sites typically give a touch more perfume and earlier accessibility. The large old-cask ageing, evident in the lack of overt toast, supports a traditional Barbaresco house rather than a modernist barrique-driven producer.

**Wine 6** — South Australia, McLaren Vale. As the stem confirms each wine is from a different continent, this must sit outside Europe, ruling out southern Rhône Grenache despite the similar pepper and herb notes. The savoury, earthy spice, old-vine density of fruit and restrained old-oak handling point to McLaren Vale's Mediterranean climate and ancient soils rather than Barossa Valley's more opulent, riper, sometimes raisined Grenache style.

## c) Comment on the style and commercial appeal (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 4** — An elegant, cool-climate style: fine tannin, bright acidity and only moderate alcohol give freshness and length rather than power, with subtle oak supporting rather than dominating the fruit. This competes with premium Russian River and Oregon Pinot Noir at around $45-60 retail, sold through independent specialists and premium on-trade lists to sommelier-driven, Burgundy-curious consumers; strong appeal in the UK, Japan and Hong Kong fine-wine trade.

**Wine 5** — A traditional, structured style built for the table and for ageing, with savoury tannin and high acid rather than immediate fruit sweetness — old-cask rather than new-oak handling signals a classicist producer. Retailing around $40-55, it undercuts premium Barolo while offering comparable seriousness, positioning it well with independent Italian specialists and restaurant lists across the US, UK and Germany as an accessible entry into fine Nebbiolo.

**Wine 6** — A warm, generous, fruit-forward style with soft tannin and open red-fruit character, rustic rather than polished, showing restrained old oak that lets the variety's personality show. At a mid-premium $25-35, it competes with southern Rhône Grenache blends and rival McLaren Vale reds, appealing to consumers seeking Mediterranean warmth without Shiraz's weight; well suited to the growing old-vine Grenache trend in independent retail and export markets including the US and UK.
