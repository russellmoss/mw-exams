# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 2 Question 3

## a) Identify the specific origin, with particular reference to the dominant grape variety (2 x 9 marks)

**Wine 6.** Deep garnet with only moderate rim development, cassis, cedar and pencil-shavings on the nose, and a full-bodied, structured palate with firm, fine-grained tannin and a long, cool, graphite finish point to a Cabernet Sauvignon-dominant Bordeaux blend rather than a Merlot-led one — Merlot would give plusher, rounder fruit and softer tannin at this weight. The precision and restraint of the fruit, combined with 13.0% alcohol and classic gravel-derived minerality, place this on the Médoc's mid-gravel terraces rather than the more powerful, tannic wines of Pauillac to the north; the polish alongside the firm structure is typical of Saint-Julien, and the concentration and length argue for classed-growth status.

**Wine 7.** Against Wine 6 this shows deeper plum and black-fruit character, sweeter oak spice and a broader, more generously fleshed mid-palate with riper, less austere tannin — evidence of a Merlot-led blend with Cabernet Franc rather than Cabernet Sauvignon dominance. The rounder texture, earlier approachability, limestone-derived precision on the finish and 13.5% alcohol point to the Right Bank, specifically Saint-Émilion rather than Pomerol, which would show even plusher, more opulent fruit with less overt structure.

## b) Discuss the relative quality of both wines within the context of their specific region (18 marks)

Wine 6 shows outstanding concentration, a long, layered finish and fine tannin management consistent with classed-growth Saint-Julien — likely Second Growth level, retailing around $120-150 on release and considerably more with this bottle age. The balance of ripe fruit, freshness and structure reflects both a great vintage and top-flight winemaking. Wine 7, while well made with good fruit purity and integrated oak, shows less depth and grip, more typical of Saint-Émilion Grand Cru appellation level than Grand Cru Classé — a very good rather than outstanding wine, retailing around $40-60. Its softer structure and immediate generosity suit earlier drinking, whereas Wine 6's firmer tannin and greater complexity confirm its superior standing within the Bordeaux hierarchy.

## c) Identify the vintage (14 marks)

Both wines show tertiary development — cedar, tobacco and forest-floor notes alongside primary fruit — consistent with roughly a decade of bottle age, yet both retain substantial tannin and fresh acidity that argue against a fully mature or cooler, leaner vintage. This combination of concentrated fruit, ripe but firm tannin and evident further ageing potential across both banks points to 2005, Bordeaux's benchmark modern vintage: a long, even, dry growing season producing wines of exceptional structure and longevity on both Left and Right Banks. A cooler year such as 2004 or 2007 would show leaner fruit and less persistent structure at this age, while the near-identical ripeness levels across both wines despite their different banks reinforce a single, uniformly excellent vintage.
