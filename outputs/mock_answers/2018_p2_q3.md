# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 2 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety and origin, as closely as possible (20 marks)

Syrah. Both wines show the mid-ruby colour, black pepper and violet lift, together with red-to-black berry fruit and a savoury, olive-tinged edge, that are the varietal signature. The fine, moderate tannin and fresh acidity across both glasses confirm a cool-climate expression of the grape rather than a warm one.

The peppery, floral, red-fruited profile at only 12.5–13.5% alcohol initially suggests the Northern Rhône, and Crozes-Hermitage or St-Joseph were seriously considered. But the purer, more primary fruit, riper and rounder mid-palate, and more polished, sweeter tannin — without the Rhône's typical reductive, meaty, bacon-fat reduction — point instead to a New World cool-climate site. This, combined with the restrained alcohol for Syrah and a slightly riper fruit register than Rhône examples typically show, funnels toward New Zealand rather than Australia, where Shiraz at this alcohol and with this much floral lift is unusual. Hawke's Bay is the obvious fit: New Zealand's leading warm, free-draining Syrah region, and the stonier, riper concentration of Wine 10 is consistent with the alluvial Gimblett Gravels sub-zone within it.

## b) Discuss the wine's quality within the context of the region of origin (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 9** — Very good quality, benchmark Hawke's Bay Syrah, retailing around £20. Pure varietal fruit, well-judged oak integration and a persistent, peppery finish show good concentration and balance without the depth of the region's top tier.

**Wine 10** — Outstanding quality, equivalent to the flagship tier of Hawke's Bay Syrah, retailing around £50. Greater fruit concentration, a longer and more layered finish, and more thorough oak integration place it well above regional norms, showing the complexity and structure of a serious, age-worthy wine.

## c) Comment on the winemaking techniques used (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 9** — The pure, primary fruit with only moderate spice suggests fermentation in mostly inert vessels with limited new oak, giving a fresher, more approachable style built for earlier drinking.

**Wine 10** — The greater depth of colour, riper tannin and pronounced toasted, spicy oak point to a longer maceration, likely partial whole-bunch inclusion for aromatic lift, and maturation in a higher proportion of new French oak, reflecting stricter fruit selection for a flagship bottling.
