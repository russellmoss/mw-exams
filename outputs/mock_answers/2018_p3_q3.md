# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 3 Question 3

## a) Identify the predominant grape variety used (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 10.** A pale garnet-ruby colour, light body, low tannin and a lifted red cherry, raspberry and rosewater perfume at a restrained 12.5% alcohol point away from the deep-coloured, high-alcohol reds usually associated with California and toward a variety more commonly used for blending than bottled varietally. This softness and pale hue are consistent with Cinsault rather than Grenache, which even from cool sites shows riper, sweeter fruit and higher alcohol, or Pinot Noir, which would show more savoury complexity and a firmer acid line.

**Wine 11.** A medium ruby-crimson colour, soft fine tannin, sweet red cherry and raspberry fruit and moderate acidity on a warm 14% frame point to Garnacha. Cinsault is ruled out by the riper fruit weight and higher alcohol; Tempranillo by the absence of its more savoury, leathery, higher-acid signature.

**Wine 12.** Deep garnet colour, firm structured tannin, dark damson and blackberry fruit, garrigue herbs and a smoked, leathery savoury edge point to Mourvèdre. Syrah — the obvious Rhône default excluded by the stem — would show more overt black pepper and cassis purity; Grenache would be paler and softer. The tannic grip and length here are distinctly Mourvèdre.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 8 marks)

**Wine 10.** Californian sun-ripened fruit with an unusually restrained alcohol and soft, old-vine textural depth points to a warm interior site with old plantings rather than the coastal elegance of Sonoma or the power of Napa. This narrows to Lodi — a Central Valley delta region on sandy soils known for surviving, own-rooted historic vine stock producing exactly this precision without extraction.

**Wine 11.** Ripe fruit retaining real freshness and mineral lift at 14% alcohol suggests a high-altitude inland site rather than a coastal Mediterranean one. Not Priorat, which would show more schistous power and extraction, nor Rioja, with its overt American-oak vanilla and Tempranillo typicity. This points to inland eastern Spain, the Valencia/La Mancha border, a high-altitude single-estate site outside the classic Priorat/Rioja Garnacha framework, under a Vino de la Tierra designation.

**Wine 12.** Garrigue, structured tannin and evident extended oak maturation point to Mediterranean southern France. Southern Rhône is eliminated by a tannic backbone firmer than a Grenache-led Châteauneuf; Languedoc-Roussillon Mourvèdre blends show less concentration and shorter mandatory ageing. This points to coastal Provence, specifically Bandol AOC.

## c) Comment on the style, quality and commercial potential (3 x 12 marks)

**Wine 10.** A light-bodied, low-tannin, bone-dry style made with minimal oak for early drinking, though old-vine concentration lends unusual precision for its weight. Very good quality for the style — depth and purity place it above generic light Californian reds, comparable in stature to a well-made Cru Beaujolais, retailing around $30-35. Best suited to independent specialists and natural-leaning on-trade lists in the US and UK targeting chillable, low-intervention reds; competes with Beaujolais and light Loire reds. The old-vine story is the opportunity; the variety's unfamiliarity to mainstream consumers is the challenge.

**Wine 11.** Medium-bodied, fine-tannined and fresh rather than blockbuster, with judicious oak framing rather than dominating the fruit; drink now to 3-4 years. Very good quality, comparable to a well-regarded single-estate old-vine Garnacha from Aragón, outperforming its Vino de la Tierra classification, retailing around $30-40. Suits Independent Specialists and premium by-the-glass lists seeking alternatives to Rioja and Priorat; competes with southern Rhône Grenache; export potential across northern Europe and premium US retail, with the challenge being the unfamiliar VdlT designation needing on-list explanation.

**Wine 12.** Full-bodied and firmly structured, savoury and dry, built by its mandatory extended oak ageing for the long term and still tight at five years old. Outstanding quality, near the top of the Bandol appellation, its depth, structure and length above generic AOC level, retailing around $45-60. Suited to fine-wine independent merchants and serious restaurant lists in the US, UK and increasingly Asia; competes with premium Châteauneuf-du-Pape and top Priorat. Growing demand for savoury, food-focused reds is the opportunity; limited volume and tannin needing decanting is the challenge.
