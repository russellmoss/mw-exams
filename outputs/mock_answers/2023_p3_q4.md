# Mock answer — 2023 Paper 3 Question 4

## a) Identify the grape variety (18 marks)

Grenache. Across all three glasses the colour is notably paler than the level of extract and alcohol would suggest for the respective climates — pale tawny-mahogany in Wine 10 despite its evident concentration, and only medium ruby in Wines 11 and 12 despite ripe, high-toned fruit. This low natural pigment, combined with soft, broad tannins rather than a firm structural grip, and consistently high potential alcohol (16.5% once fortified in Wine 10, 15% in the dry Wine 11), points to Grenache rather than Mourvèdre or Syrah, both of which would show deeper colour and firmer, more angular tannin at these ripeness levels. The red cherry and strawberry fruit with a warm, spicy, slightly baked character running through all three, together with the rancio, dried-fig and coffee notes that oxidative ageing has drawn out in Wine 10, confirms a red grape rather than an aromatic white base for the fortified example, and is entirely consistent with Grenache's known behaviour under both oxidative and reductive handling.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 7 marks)

**Wine 10** — Roussillon, southern France; Vin Doux Naturel. The deep tawny-mahogany hue, rancio notes of walnut, coffee and dried fig, and the fortified sweetness alongside 16.5% alcohol point unmistakably to a mutage-arrested VDN. The structured, slightly tannic backbone under the oxidative complexity suggests an inland, schist-based site rather than the more saline, coastal character of Banyuls, pointing to Maury.

**Wine 11** — Montsant, Catalonia, Spain. Ripe dark fruit, dried Mediterranean herbs and a mineral, schist-derived edge alongside 15% alcohol point to old-vine Grenache from Catalonia's llicorella soils. The concentration and oak-derived spice suggest a premium bottling, but it lacks the extra layer of density and minerality typical of neighbouring Priorat, pointing to Montsant rather than the more prestigious DOQ next door.

**Wine 12** — Napa Valley, California. Pure red berry fruit and supple, New World-ripe tannin point to California, but the restrained 13% alcohol is markedly lower than the Spanish example and atypical for Napa's usual Cabernet-driven ripeness, suggesting a cooler pocket or deliberately early-picked, boutique Rhône-variety planting rather than the valley's mainstream style.

## c) Discuss the production methods (3 x 6 marks)

**Wine 10** — The residual sweetness alongside high alcohol indicates fermentation was arrested by mutage, adding neutral grape spirit partway through to halt yeast activity and lock in sugar. The rancio character and tawny colour point to prolonged oxidative ageing, likely in old wood or glass demijohns with deliberate air exposure, consistent with a wine of some 25 years.

**Wine 11** — Full dryness at 15% alcohol required late picking of low-yielding old vines; moderate extraction gives structured yet supple tannins, and integrated vanilla-spice notes suggest maturation in barrique, part new, for a premium "old vines" cuvée rather than a simple entry-level Montsant.

**Wine 12** — The freshness and restraint suggest an earlier harvest and cooler fermentation to protect primary fruit; soft, fine tannins hint at a whole-bunch component, while the lack of overt toast points to neutral or minimal new oak, prioritising fruit purity over power.

## d) Comment on quality and maturity (3 x 6 marks)

**Wine 10** — Outstanding quality, equivalent to a top cru-level Roussillon VDN retailing around $60–90; the rancio complexity shows a wine at its peak after 25 years, with the oxidative style meaning it will hold for decades more rather than decline.

**Wine 11** — Very good quality, comparable to a strong single-vineyard Montsant just below Priorat's Grand Cru tier, around $25–35; still youthful at five years, with ripe primary fruit that will gain savoury, dried-herb complexity over the next 3–5 years and hold for a further 5–8.

**Wine 12** — Good to very good quality, a boutique single-vineyard bottling around $35–45; the fresh, primary fruit profile indicates a wine of 3–4 years built for immediate drinking, with limited scope to improve further and best enjoyed over the next 2–3 years.
