# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 1 Question 1

## a) Identify the region of origin as closely as possible, comment with reference to the grape varieties used (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Pale gold with a green tinge and a taut, racy acid line. Gooseberry, grapefruit and boxwood point straight to Sauvignon Blanc, while a waxy, lanolin-toned weight on the mid-palate signals Sémillon alongside it — a classic dry white Bordeaux blend rather than a pure Loire or New World Sauvignon, which would show none of this honeyed richness. Toast, vanilla and a creamy texture confirm barrel fermentation. The precise fruit definition sitting comfortably under fine new oak, without the wine tipping into flab, places this in Pessac-Léognan rather than a simpler, unoaked Entre-Deux-Mers.

**Wine 2.** Deep gold, broad and oily in texture with low-to-moderate acidity, ripe white peach and apricot, waxy honeyed notes, fennel and garrigue herb on a warm finish. The low acid and rounded structure rule out a Marsanne-Viognier northern Rhône white; the layered stone fruit, floral lift and herbal top note instead suggest the full southern Rhône palette of Grenache Blanc, Clairette, Roussanne and Bourboulenc. Same country as Wine 1 confirms France; the warmer, broader, multi-variety profile places this firmly in Châteauneuf-du-Pape blanc.

## b) Comment on quality within the context of the region of origin (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Very good quality, comparable to an unclassified but serious Pessac-Léognan estate at around $35-45 retail — the fruit purity, judicious oak integration and length place it above generic Bordeaux Blanc but a notch below the precision and grip of a classified Cru Classé de Graves such as Domaine de Chevalier's white.

**Wine 2.** Very good, arguably outstanding quality for the appellation — white Châteauneuf-du-Pape is a small fraction of total output, and the concentration, waxy Roussanne texture and persistent length here sit at strong Village-equivalent level, around $45-60, just short of the very best cuvées that show additional precision and ageing potential.

## c) Discuss the key winemaking techniques used to arrive at this style (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 1.** The toast, vanilla and creamy texture indicate fermentation in French oak barriques, likely with a portion new, and lees stirring for added richness; the retained freshness suggests malolactic fermentation was partial or suppressed to preserve Sauvignon's zip.

**Wine 2.** The absence of overt toast despite full texture points to ageing in old demi-muids or foudres rather than new barrique; extended lees contact builds the oily mid-palate weight, while the broad, low-acid structure reflects grape ripeness at harvest rather than malolactic softening.
