# Mock answer — 2021 Paper 1 Question 4

## a) Identify the country (15 marks)

USA. Three very different styles — a low-alcohol frothy sweet wine, a rich barrel-fermented Chardonnay and a dry, fruit-driven Pinot Gris — share the same underlying signature: technically clean winemaking, confident varietal labelling and generous, forward fruit at every price point from commodity to icon. Australia was considered for the Chardonnay's richness, but its more restrained citrus-stonefruit register and the Pinot Gris's Alsace-facing rather than confected style argue against it. The vineyard-designate precision on the Chardonnay, the AVA framing of the Pinot Gris and the brand-led simplicity of the sweet wine funnel to the USA, spanning California's range from bulk to grand cru-equivalent single vineyard alongside Oregon's cooler, Burgundian ambition.

## b) Identify the origin and variety as closely as possible (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 10** — Muscat, California. The intensely perfumed orange blossom, peach, lychee and grape aromatics are the unmistakable calling card of Muscat; Torrontés was considered but would show higher acid and a drier finish. The 9% alcohol and pronounced sweetness confirm an early-harvested, arrested fermentation, and the simple, unoaked fruit with no site complexity points to a large-volume, warm interior source such as Central Valley rather than a coastal AVA.

**Wine 11** — Chardonnay, Carneros, Napa. The rich palate, creamy texture and toasted, vanilla-laden new oak point to Chardonnay in the New World idiom; Viognier's exotic top notes were briefly considered but the stone-fruit and citrus-curd core is more classically Chardonnay. The 14.5% alcohol and full malolactic roundness suggest fully ripe fruit, while the retained acid line beneath the richness points to a maritime-moderated site — Carneros, at Napa's cool, bay-facing southern edge.

**Wine 12** — Pinot Gris, Willamette Valley, Oregon. Restrained pear, green apple and citrus with bright acidity and a dry finish rule out the broader, phenolic Pinot Grigio idiom of northern Italy in favour of the Alsace-facing dry style. At 13.5% with no discernible oak, the fruit generosity suggests a warm, ripe season, while the retained acidity confirms a cool-climate, high-latitude US site — Willamette Valley.

## c) Discuss the key winemaking techniques used (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 10** — Obvious residual sugar alongside only 9% alcohol indicates fermentation was chilled and arrested early to preserve sweetness and delicate Muscat aromatics. The absence of oak or lees character and the fresh, lightly spritzy lift suggest tank fermentation and inert storage, with retained CO2 for liveliness — a technique chosen purely to protect primary fruit at scale.

**Wine 11** — Pronounced toast, vanilla and integrated spice point to fermentation and maturation in a high proportion of new French barriques, likely nine to twelve months. The creamy mid-palate weight indicates full malolactic fermentation, probably reinforced by lees stirring, explaining the seamless integration of oak and fruit.

**Wine 12** — Purity of primary pear and citrus fruit, absence of buttery or toasted notes and a crisp, unadorned finish point to fermentation and elevage entirely in stainless steel with no malolactic fermentation, preserving freshness and varietal typicity, and early bottling to protect the wine's clean, food-friendly style.

## d) Comment on the market position (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 10** is a mass-market, entry-level style, retailing under $10 through supermarkets and chain grocery, targeting casual and new drinkers who favour sweeter, lower-alcohol wines; it competes with other bulk moscato-style wines, with strong domestic volume and growing export interest in developing sweet-wine markets.

**Wine 11** sits at the premium end of California Chardonnay, a single-vineyard restaurant-list and independent-specialist wine retailing around $50-70, competing with top White Burgundy and benchmark Sonoma/Napa examples; its audience is the connoisseur seeking rich, oak-driven Chardonnay with vineyard pedigree, with export demand in the UK, Canada and Asian fine-wine markets.

**Wine 12** occupies the mid-premium, food-friendly bracket at around $18-25 through independent retailers and by-the-glass restaurant lists, appealing to drinkers seeking a drier alternative to Alsace or Italian Pinot Gris/Grigio; Oregon's small-producer, terroir-driven reputation supports steady export demand in the UK and Asian premium markets.
