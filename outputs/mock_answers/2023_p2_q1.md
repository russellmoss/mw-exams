# Mock answer — 2023 Paper 2 Question 1

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (4 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Pale-to-medium garnet with an orange rim, searingly high acidity and a firm, drying, almost numbing tannin frame the variety before the aromatics do — rose petal, tar, dried cherry and mint confirm Nebbiolo rather than Sangiovese, which would show similar acid and colour but a softer, less viscous tannin and more sour-cherry/leather fruit. The calcareous soil signature of a firm mineral spine, the fullish body at 14.5% alcohol and the depth of savoury development point to a serious Piedmont cru rather than a generic Langhe blend, giving Barolo DOCG, most likely a named commune such as Novello. This wine sets the country: an assertive, tannic, high-acid northern Italian red establishes Italy as the shared origin for the flight.

**Wine 2.** Bright, light-to-medium ruby, crisp acidity and soft, low-to-medium tannin with sour cherry, plum, dried herb and white pepper point unmistakably to Corvina, here in a fresh, unconcentrated register that rules out Ripasso or Amarone — there is none of the raisined, dried-fruit richness or 15%+ alcohol those styles would show at 13%. This is base Valpolicella DOC from the Classica zone in Veneto, not a Bardolino, whose typically paler colour and thinner mid-palate this wine's slightly firmer fruit concentration exceeds.

**Wine 3.** Deep ruby with real density, high tannin and high acidity, black cherry, plum, balsamic and tar notes at 14% alcohol point south to Aglianico rather than Nebbiolo, whose paler colour and rose-tar perfume this wine's weight and darker fruit rule out; Nero d'Avola is a plausible southern alternative but lacks this wine's tannic grip and volcanic-mineral tension. The firm structure layered with savoury, developed balsamic notes and extended tannin management point to Campania's Irpinia zone: Taurasi DOCG, and the depth of development for the vintage suggests the mandated Riserva category.

**Wine 4.** Pale ruby, aromatic red cherry, wild strawberry, dried herbs and a distinctive ashy, saline mineral note could initially be mistaken for Nebbiolo given the pale colour and perfume, but the tannin here is far finer-grained and the acidity, while bright, lacks Nebbiolo's searing intensity — this, combined with a smoky, volcanic ash character rather than tar and rose, points instead to Nerello Mascalese. At 13.5% alcohol with real aromatic delicacy this is Etna Rosso DOC from Sicily's northern slopes, a lighter-framed but genuinely serious volcanic red that closes the flight's north-to-south sweep.

## b) Quality, maturity and capacity to age (4 x 10 marks)

**Wine 1.** Outstanding quality, equivalent to a top named-cru Barolo retailing around $80-120. The persistent length, tar-and-rose complexity and still-tight tannic core show real intensity beyond simple Langhe DOC. At around eight years old the colour and fruit remain more youthful than expected for the vintage, consistent with 2015's ripe, earlier-drinking character; it can be enjoyed now but has the structure to improve for a further 8-10 years and hold for 15-20, developing dried rose, leather and truffle complexity.

**Wine 2.** Very good quality for the category, a polished example of everyday Valpolicella DOC around $15-20 — the freshness, precise fruit and clean finish are the point here, not concentration. At barely a year old this is a wine for immediate drinking; it has no meaningful capacity to improve and should be consumed within the next 1-2 years while the primary fruit is intact.

**Wine 3.** Outstanding quality, comparable to a benchmark Riserva-tier Taurasi retailing around $45-65. The combination of firm tannin, layered black fruit and emerging balsamic, tertiary notes shows genuine complexity and length. At roughly eight years old it shows early development but retains ample structure; it will improve for another 5-8 years and hold for 15+, gaining more dried fruit, leather and spice.

**Wine 4.** Very good to excellent quality, approaching cru-level Etna Rosso around $30-40, well above generic DOC. The precision of fruit, fine tannin and saline minerality give real typicity and length. At around three years old it is still primarily fruit-driven; it has the acid and tannic backbone to improve for a further 4-6 years and hold for 8-10 more, developing smokier, spicier tertiary character.

## c) Comment on the method of production (4 x 5 marks)

**Wine 1.** The firm but non-aggressive tannin and complete absence of vanilla or toast point to an extended post-fermentation maceration followed by ageing in large, neutral Slavonian oak rather than new barrique — a traditionalist choice that preserves fruit purity while building structure; further bottle age before release explains the current textural integration.

**Wine 2.** The pure, unadorned red-fruit character with no oak spice at all indicates fermentation and ageing entirely in stainless steel, with only brief bottle ageing before release — decisions that protect the freshness and immediacy this wine is built to deliver.

**Wine 3.** Ripe, well-managed tannin alongside a touch of sweet spice under the tarry black fruit suggests maturation split between barrique and larger cask, softening Aglianico's naturally rustic edge; the extended, DOCG-mandated bottle ageing accounts for the balsamic, developing layer over still-primary fruit.

**Wine 4.** The fine-grained, silky tannin texture and only a whisper of spice, rather than overt vanilla, point to brief ageing in older or larger-format oak rather than new wood; the soft, creamy integration on the mid-palate is consistent with malolactic fermentation having softened the variety's natural acidity.
