# Mock answer — 2018 Paper 1 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety (10 marks)

Riesling. Both wines share the same piercing, racy acidity and a floral lime-zest, white peach aromatic core that survives everything from bone-dry (Wine 11) to lusciously sweet (Wine 12), framed by a slate-driven mineral purity. Wine 12's emerging honeyed, petrol-edged development is Riesling's classic TDN ageing marker. Chenin Blanc was considered for Wine 11's dry, high-acid structure but lacks the waxy lanolin and quince typical of that variety; Furmint was considered for Wine 12 but its nuttier, more phenolic finish does not match the pure lime-honey fruit shown here.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 11** — Germany. The precise, site-specific labelling and bone-dry, 13% alcohol style point to the VDP's Grosses Gewächs tier — Germany's dry Grand Cru equivalent — rather than a traditional off-dry Prädikat wine. The broader fruit weight and firm but not searing acidity suggest the warmer, Rhine-facing Rheingau rather than the leaner Mosel, narrowing to a top Grosse Lage site such as Kiedrich.

**Wine 12** — Germany. The very low 9% alcohol carrying substantial residual sugar, a piercing acid line and a delicate, fine-boned mid-palate point away from the broader Rheingau toward the Mosel. The weightless texture beneath the concentration suggests the Ruwer tributary rather than the riper Middle Mosel, with the Auslese designation confirming a Prädikat-level selective harvest at a classified Grosse Lage site.

## c) Comment on winemaking, quality and state of maturity (2 x 12 marks)

**Wine 11** — The bone-dry finish at 13% alcohol shows all sugar fermented out, requiring a long, cool fermentation to protect the pure lime and floral aromatics rather than let oxidative characters develop — consistent with whole-bunch pressing and neutral, stainless or large old-wood vessels; no oak is evident. The layered, textural mid-palate suggests extended lees contact. This is outstanding quality, Grosses Gewächs level, retailing around $60-80. Still largely primary, the wine has the concentration to improve for 5-8 years and hold for a further 15-20.

**Wine 12** — The 9% alcohol beside pronounced sweetness confirms fermentation was deliberately arrested, most likely by chilling and racking off the yeast, following a hand-selected, late, botrytis-touched harvest as required for Auslese. No oak character is evident, pointing to stainless steel handling that preserves pure fruit and acid tension. This is outstanding quality, Auslese-level Prädikat wine, likely $50-70 per half-bottle. The pale gold colour and emerging honeyed, petrol notes show an eleven-year-old wine, from a healthy, well-ripened vintage, entering secondary development, with acidity to improve for a further decade and hold for 20-30 years beyond that.
