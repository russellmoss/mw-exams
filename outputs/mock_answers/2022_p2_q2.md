# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 2 Question 2

## a) Identify the sub-region as closely as possible (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — Chianti Classico. Medium ruby colour, sour cherry and dried herb aromatics, floral iris lift and savoury spice, with bright, racy acidity and only medium, fine tannin at 13.5% alcohol, point to Sangiovese from a cooler, higher-altitude site rather than a warm southern one. The touch of soft plum alongside the cherry fruit is consistent with the small proportion of Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon that Chianti Classico's DOCG rules permit as complementary varieties — something Brunello's regulations do not allow. That freshness and moderate weight point to the galestro-soil hill country of Chianti Classico rather than Montalcino.

**Wine 6** — Montalcino, from an elevated single-vineyard site within Brunello di Montalcino. Deeper ruby colour, riper black cherry and dark berry fruit, rose petal, balsam and spice, with a broader, denser tannic frame, longer finish and 14.5% alcohol all point to a warmer, drier, more southerly climate than Wine 5. The purity of fruit, with no secondary-variety softening, is consistent with the 100% Sangiovese Brunello demands. The added ripeness and site-driven concentration narrow this further to a specific cru rather than a broader Montalcino blend.

## b) Comment on the key winemaking techniques used (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 5** — pure red-fruit definition suggests stainless steel fermentation at controlled, moderate temperature; refined, medium tannin implies a restrained 12–16 day maceration. Subtle, integrated spice with no dominant vanilla or toast points to ageing in older, third- and second-passage tonneaux rather than new barriques, in line with DOCG's shorter minimum ageing and this style's emphasis on freshness over overt oak.

**Wine 6** — firmer, more structured tannin and darker fruit suggest a longer, warmer traditional fermentation with extended maceration. Well-integrated oak alongside early balsamic and dried-rose complexity points to extended cask ageing beyond Brunello's two-year minimum, plus further bottle age before release — the longer mandated ageing explaining the more advanced development relative to Wine 5.

## c) Comment on the style and quality (2 x 10 marks)

**Wine 5** — very good quality, upper Chianti Classico Annata level: balanced acid, fruit and tannin with clean varietal typicity, though lacking the depth of Gran Selezione. A commercial, food-friendly style retailing around $25–35 through independent and premium on-trade channels. Youthful and primary, ready to drink now, it will hold rather than develop over the next 3–5 years.

**Wine 6** — outstanding quality, at the top of the Brunello ladder: site-specific concentration, structural depth and length point to a prestige single-vineyard bottling rather than generic Brunello, retailing $70–100+ through fine-wine specialists. The 2015 vintage's ripeness combined with Brunello's four-year minimum ageing explains its gentle tertiary lift; real capacity remains to improve over the next decade and hold for a further 15–20 years.
