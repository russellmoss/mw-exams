# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 3 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety (2 x 8 marks)

**Wine 5** — Riesling. Pale lemon-green with a lifted nose of lime, green apple and lemon zest, and an emerging kerosene/TDN note already showing despite the wine's youth. Allied to a racing, almost searing acidity, this combination points firmly to Riesling; Sauvignon Blanc was briefly considered for the citrus lift but lacks both the petrol character and the touch of residual sweetness softening the mid-palate. The moderate fruit weight sits with cool South Island New Zealand fruit rather than the more austere, mineral-driven style typical of German Riesling.

**Wine 6** — Gewürztraminer. The deep golden colour, oily viscosity and an intensely perfumed nose of lychee, rose petal, Turkish delight and ginger are unmistakable varietal markers that no other aromatic white replicates at this volume. Muscat was considered for the floral lift but its aromas run more grapey and linear, without this exotic spice or the broad, lower-acid texture found here. A cool, fog-moderated coastal California AVA fits the hang-time needed to concentrate this aromatic intensity.

## b) State the level of residual sugar and alcohol (2 x 4 marks)

**Wine 5** — approximately 30-45 g/L RS at 10.0% alcohol. The low alcohol against a medium-dry impression indicates an early-arrested fermentation retaining sugar rather than any dilution.

**Wine 6** — approximately 80-120 g/L RS at 11.0% alcohol. The genuinely sweet, viscous palate against a still-moderate alcohol for such a high must weight again points to fermentation stopped well short of dryness.

## c) Discuss the quality and commercial potential (2 x 13 marks)

**Wine 5** — good to very good quality, equivalent to a well-made commercial varietal Riesling retailing around $12-16. The bright fruit purity and lively acid-sugar balance give real refreshment and drinkability, though the finish is straightforward rather than layered, and there is little of the textural complexity a premium site would add. Commercially this occupies a difficult niche: off-dry Riesling still fights consumer perception that sweetness equals low quality, but it offers good value by-the-glass and works well through independent retailers and Asian-restaurant wine lists targeting drinkers who enjoy an aromatic, low-alcohol style.

**Wine 6** — very good quality, comparable to an entry-to-mid-tier Late Harvest bottling at $20-30 for a half-bottle. The concentration of botrytis-adjacent honeyed fruit, ginger spice and a long floral finish shows genuine dessert-wine intensity, balanced by the moderate alcohol and enough acid to avoid cloying. It is a small-volume, niche proposition, sold mainly through cellar doors, fine-dining by-the-glass dessert lists and specialist retailers to consumers seeking an alternative to Sauternes or German Auslese at a lower price point.
