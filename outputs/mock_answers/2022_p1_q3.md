# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 1 Question 3

## a) Identify the origin and variety/ies as closely as possible (3 x 15 marks)

**Wine 6.** Pale lemon with a green tinge, delicate pear, citrus peel and white flower aromas, light body, moderate-plus acidity, dry and unoaked at 12.0% alcohol point to Pinot Grigio; Pinot Bianco was considered but its more neutral, less pear-and-blossom-driven profile does not fit as well. The clean, unadorned fruit and stainless-steel-fresh finish, with no textural weight from lees or barrel, are typical of the high-volume Delle Venezie zone rather than a single-vineyard Friuli or Alto Adige bottling; the wine's polish narrows this to Veneto rather than the leaner, more mineral Friulian style.

**Wine 7.** Intense rose petal, lychee, ginger and exotic spice on the nose, an oily, broad-shouldered palate and a notably high 14.0% alcohol with dry, savoury finish point unmistakably to Gewürztraminer; Muscat and Viognier were both considered but their more grapey/orange-blossom and apricot-musk registers respectively do not match this lychee-rose-ginger signature. The alcohol is high yet the wine stays structured and food-friendly rather than blowsy — evidence of rapid sugar ripening balanced by cool nights, the signature of an alpine Italian site. This funnels to Alto Adige/Südtirol, likely the Caldaro basin given the polished, varietally precise, cooperative-quality execution rather than a rustic single-estate cuvée.

**Wine 8.** Pale gold colour, saline, smoky and citrus-fennel aromatics, taut high acidity and a lean, tensile palate at 12.0% alcohol point to Carricante; Grillo and Catarratto were eliminated as both would show riper stone fruit and softer acid, while Verdicchio lacks this smoky-saline edge. The salinity and smoke are the calling card of volcanic soils, and combined with the herbal, fennel-inflected fruit and altitude-driven freshness this funnels directly to Etna on Sicily's slopes — the appellation most associated with Carricante grown on lava-derived terraces at altitude.

## b) Discuss the wine's style and quality; consider which markets this wine would be successful in (3 x 10 marks)

**Wine 6.** Light, dry, fruit-forward and entirely unoaked — a style built for immediate drinking rather than complexity. Clean and correctly made but simple; a commercial-DOC quality level rather than a site-specific example, equivalent to a solid entry-level Pinot Grigio retailing around £7–9. Best suited to high-volume off-trade: UK and US supermarket own-label and branded shelves, and by-the-glass restaurant pouring, competing directly with Friuli/Trentino Pinot Grigio and inexpensive New Zealand or Chilean whites; the target consumer is price-driven, seeking a recognisable varietal name over depth.

**Wine 7.** Fully dry, aromatically pronounced yet structured rather than overblown, with real palate weight from the alcohol — a food-oriented rather than sweet style. Very good quality at premium Alto Adige DOC level, more precise and better balanced than warmer-climate Gewürztraminer, retailing around £15–20. Suited to independent specialists and premium on-trade lists — Italian restaurants and spiced/Asian-fusion menus — across the UK, US, Germany and Austria; it appeals to adventurous consumers seeking an aromatic alternative to Alsace, though limited volumes restrict supermarket listing.

**Wine 8.** Taut, mineral-driven and structurally built for ageing rather than immediate fruit appeal, with restrained alcohol supporting the tension. Very good to outstanding for the appellation, showing genuine site expression; equivalent to Etna Bianco's top DOC tier, retailing around £25–35, reflecting the region's rising fine-wine reputation. This is a fine-wine independent and sommelier-list wine, well placed in allocation-driven export markets — the US, UK and Japan — among consumers chasing volcanic, mineral whites as alternatives to Chablis or Assyrtiko; small production supports premium, collector-oriented positioning rather than broad distribution.
