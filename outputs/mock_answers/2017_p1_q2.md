# Mock answer — 2017 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (16 marks)

Pinot Gris. Both wines share a deepening straw-to-gold colour with a faint copper tinge from the variety's pigmented skins, low-to-moderate acidity and a broad, oily texture at 14% on both. Wine 4's smoky, honeyed pear and quince and Wine 5's cleaner peach, blossom and ginger spice both carry the variety's signature spice-and-weight. Pinot Blanc is eliminated — it would show softer, more neutral fruit without this spice or fuller mouthfeel. Viognier was considered for the weight and stone fruit, but its blossom-apricot register normally pairs with a more overt floral lift. Chardonnay is ruled out by the absence of any buttery or toasty oak signature and the persistent spice.

## b) Identify the origin (2 x 6 marks)

**Wine 4** — Alsace, France. The deep gold colour, bottle development, glyceral texture and smoky, honeyed spice over ripe pear and quince point to a warm, dry, continental site with a tradition of leaving Pinot Gris to ripen fully. This weight without New World primary fruit character is consistent with Alsace.

**Wine 5** — Nelson, New Zealand. The cleaner, brighter fruit, gentler spice and more poised texture point to a cooler, maritime Southern Hemisphere site. The polish and controlled ripeness rule out warmer regions such as Central Otago, narrowing this to a cool, coastal Nelson.

## c) Quality and maturity (2 x 6 marks)

**Wine 4** — Very good quality for AOC Alsace Pinot Gris, comparable to a serious Réserve-style bottling around $30-35. The layered spice, honeyed weight and lingering finish show genuine concentration, not just ripeness. Roughly a decade old, it is fully developed with tertiary honey and smoke; it will hold rather than improve, best drunk over 2-3 years before the fruit fades.

**Wine 5** — Good, well-made commercial quality for Nelson Pinot Gris, around $20-25, clean and true to variety but without Wine 4's layered complexity. The pale colour and primary fruit indicate a wine of only 2-3 years; it has enough concentration to hold rather than improve, best drunk over the next 1-2 years.

## d) Key winemaking techniques (2 x 5 marks)

**Wine 4** — The broad, oily texture with no overt oak spice suggests fermentation in neutral steel or old, large-format wood. The rounded mid-palate and faint sweetness suggest malolactic was blocked but fermentation checked short of dryness, the traditional Alsace approach that preserves fruit weight and textural breadth.

**Wine 5** — The clean, precise fruit with no phenolic grip suggests gentle pressing and cool, neutral-vessel fermentation to protect aromatics. The fresh mid-palate and absence of buttery character point to blocked malolactic, while a touch of residual sweetness suggests fermentation stopped just short of dry, a common modern Nelson approach to texture without losing freshness.
