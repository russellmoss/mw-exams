# Mock answer — 2024 Paper 2 Question 3

## a) Identify the grape variety (25 marks)

Pinot Noir across all five glasses. Each shows a transparent, pale-to-medium ruby core fading to a watery rim rather than the saturated purple of a thicker-skinned variety, with a red-fruited aromatic core of cherry, raspberry and rose running through every wine even as ripeness and oak handling shift the register from savoury (Wines 8 and 9) to riper and spicier (Wines 10-12). The palates are consistently light-to-medium in body with fine, silky rather than chewy tannin, moderate 13-13.5% alcohol and a persistent line of acidity even in the ripest examples. Gamay was considered given the pale colour and red-fruit theme, but the more structured tannin, absence of banana/kirsch carbonic lift and the deliberate, often new, oak treatment across the flight rule it out. Nebbiolo was also weighed for the rose-petal aromatics, but the lighter colour, softer tannin and lower acidity here are inconsistent with that variety's characteristic tar, tar-rose intensity and grippy structure. The wine labelled Spätburgunder is simply the German synonym for the same variety.

## b) Identify the origin as closely as possible (5 x 5 marks)

**Wine 8** — Savigny-lès-Beaune, Côte de Beaune, Burgundy, France. Transparent ruby, red cherry and rose with a savoury, faintly earthy undertow and restrained oak point to a cool Old World Pinot origin, with the fine but not grand structure suggesting a Côte de Beaune village site.

**Wine 9** — Niagara Peninsula (Twenty Mile Bench), Canada. A racier, saline, faintly ferrous mineral edge alongside red cherry and herbs, on a cooler acid line than the Burgundy, points away from Europe to a cool continental New World Bench site.

**Wine 10** — Baden, Germany. Riper red-to-dark cherry and plum with a warmer, fuller mid-palate than the French or Canadian wines, yet still tightly structured, is consistent with a warm-site German Spätburgunder from a volcanic, south-facing origin.

**Wine 11** — Russian River Valley, Sonoma, USA. Darker bramble and plum fruit, riper silky tannin and pronounced French oak spice, carried on enough acid to stay fresh, point to a premium, cool-influenced Californian coastal AVA rather than a warmer inland site.

**Wine 12** — Central Otago, New Zealand. The most vividly fruited, dark-cherried and fragrant wine in the flight, with a lifted wild-herb note and polished, generous oak-derived texture, is consistent with the high-UV, diurnal-swing climate of Central Otago.

## c) Discuss how climate and winemaking techniques have influenced the quality and style of the wine (5 x 15 marks)

**Wine 8.** The cool, disease-pressured 2021 season in Burgundy — frost and mildew cutting yields — explains the wine's tension, red-fruited precision and moderate 13.5% alcohol rather than any solar richness. Hand-harvesting and careful sorting followed by open-vat fermentation with punch-downs point to gentle extraction, giving fine rather than dense tannin, while roughly fifteen months in barrel with only around 15% new oak accounts for the subtle vanilla and cedar rather than overt toast. This is very good quality at Village AOC level — elegant and precise but lacking the depth and structural intensity of a premier cru site — retailing around $45-55, the upper end of village Burgundy pricing.

**Wine 9.** A cool continental climate with lake-moderated diurnal swings and an even, slow-ripening season preserved acid and produced pristine, unblown fruit rather than density, explaining the wine's racy line and restrained 13.5% alcohol. The clean, saline, mineral-driven fruit with only light oak influence points to gentle handling prioritising site transparency over overt winemaking imprint. This single-vineyard Bench Pinot is very good quality, showing premier-cru ambition in its precision even if the market has not yet caught up with Burgundian pricing; it competes at $45-50 as a serious cool-climate New World alternative.

**Wine 10.** A warm, sunny, early-picked 2018 season gave riper fruit and a fuller body than the French or Canadian wines, while the volcanic Kaiserstuhl soils retain a mineral cut that keeps the wine from feeling heavy. Full malolactic fermentation on skins softens the acid, and extended maturation of around two years in barrique explains the more pronounced vanilla, spice and rounder tannin structure than Wine 8. The VDP.Erste Lage classification — an official second tier below Grosse Lage — supports a call of very good to excellent quality, comparable to a strong village-to-low-premier-cru Burgundy, and at $35-45 remains keen value against French equivalents.

**Wine 11.** An uneven, fast 2022 season — frost, drought and a late-season heat spike — forced early, decisive picking that preserved freshness despite riper black-cherry and plum fruit, explaining the combination of generosity and retained acidity. Hand-sorted fruit, gravity-fed small open-top fermenters, a five-day cold soak and punch-down extraction are classic quality-driven techniques that build structured, silky tannin, while ten months in French oak at 40% new explains the pronounced spice and toast. This is outstanding, premium Sonoma quality, polished and site-driven, retailing around $55-65 and competing with both top Russian River peers and premier cru Burgundy on serious wine lists.

**Wine 12.** High UV intensity, a wide diurnal range and dry, sunny continental conditions concentrate fruit and pigment while retaining acidity, giving the most vividly fruited and fragrant wine in the flight; a minimal-handling philosophy preserves that purity rather than masking it. Brief cold maceration, a week-long fermentation extended by ten to fourteen further days on skins, and ten months in French oak with a third new, shape a supple, generously fruited yet structured style. Blending two sub-zones captures both perfume and depth, giving very good to excellent, premium New Zealand quality, retailing $40-50 and standing confidently alongside premier-level Burgundy and top Russian River Pinot.
