# Mock answer — 2025 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety (16 marks)

Chardonnay. The common thread across all four wines is a core of citrus and orchard fruit — lemon, green apple, white peach and pear — ranging from taut and saline (Wine 3) to riper and more textural (Wines 4 and 6), a breadth that reflects a variety with genuine site transparency rather than a fixed aromatic signature. The buttery, creamy notes seen to varying degrees in Wines 4, 5 and 6 point to malolactic conversion, and the ease with which three of the four carry new oak without losing varietal definition is also typical. Chenin Blanc was considered given Wine 5's South African origin, but Chenin retains a waxier, quince-like character and higher residual grip even under oak, neither present here. Semillon was ruled out too — none shows the lanolin or waxy-lemon character expected, and the consistent malic-lactic softening is more consistent with Chardonnay's readiness for full or partial MLF.

## b) Comment on the style, quality and maturity (4 x 13 marks)

**Wine 3.** Deep gold with silver reflections signal considerable bottle age, and the salted lemon peel, wet stone, crushed oyster shell and blanched almond confirm a fully mature, tertiary-driven style. No oak character is present, and the bracing, still-vivid acidity against a textured, autolytic mid-palate points to a bone-dry, unoaked wine built on extended lees ageing rather than wood. Outstanding quality, equivalent to a serious Premier Cru retailing around £40-50 — length, saline persistence and layered complexity all sit well above village level. At an estimated 15 years old it is fully evolved and at its peak; it will not gain further complexity but has the acid spine to hold for a further 3-5 years before the fruit fades.

**Wine 4.** Medium gold with citrus, green apple, white peach and pear alongside subtle vanilla and butter place this as a younger, partially oaked wine with milder tertiary development than Wine 3. The creamy texture and moderate length suggest full MLF and mixed steel/oak ageing rather than the reductive, unoaked approach above. Good quality, more commercial than profound, consistent with a mid-market Californian appellation wine retailing around £18-22 — well made but lacking the depth of Wine 3. At around 6 years old it is drinking now with light early tertiary notes emerging; it may hold this plateau for a further 2-3 years but has limited scope to improve further.

**Wine 5.** Pale gold with a green tinge and a distinctly Burgundian nose — lemon cordial, white stone fruit, oyster-shell minerality and a touch of reductive struck-flint — points to careful, hands-on winemaking. The lean, shapely palate with marzipan and almond notes, bright acidity and well-integrated oak at just 12.5% achieves real weight without heaviness, a cool-climate signature. Very good quality, at the top end of its cool-climate New World peer group, likely retailing around £25-30. Still youthful with primary fruit dominant and only emerging secondary complexity, it has the structure to improve over the next 3-4 years and should hold for 5-7 years thereafter, gaining brioche and hazelnut depth.

**Wine 6.** Pale golden straw with ripe white peach, nectarine, lemon cheesecake and fresh vanilla, layered with nougat, toasted oats and cashew, shows a generously oaked, full-bodied style. Firm but well-integrated acidity beneath the richness — despite 14% alcohol and evident new oak — suggests a cool, extended ripening season rather than simple warm-climate power, a detail that elevates this beyond a straightforward rich New World Chardonnay. Outstanding quality, benchmark premium Margaret River estate level, likely retailing around AUD $55-60. At roughly 2 years old it is youthful with primary fruit still dominant but already showing early secondary nutty, toasty complexity; it will improve for a further 4-6 years and hold for 6-8 years beyond that, developing honeyed, hazelnut depth.

## c) Identify the origin as closely as possible (4 x 8 marks)

**Wine 3.** The unoaked, high-acid, saline-mineral profile at 13% alcohol is textbook cool-climate Burgundy; the absence of wood and pronounced oyster-shell character point specifically to Chablis. The concentration and length place it above village level, but it lacks the extra dimension and grand depth of a Grand Cru site — Chablis Premier Cru.

**Wine 4.** Riper stone-fruit and apple character with moderate oak and a creamy, MLF-softened texture point to a moderate-climate New World site rather than a cooler coastal one. The balance of ripeness against retained acidity is consistent with the fog-moderated conditions of California's central coast — Monterey.

**Wine 5.** The combination of low alcohol, bright acidity and a distinctly Burgundian, reductive character, allied to real concentration, rules out warmer Western Cape regions such as Stellenbosch. This points to a high-elevation, maritime-cooled site on South Africa's Cape South Coast — the Elgin Highlands.

**Wine 6.** Full body, generous new French oak and full malolactic point to a confident, warm-climate New World producer, yet the freshness retained beneath the richness rules out a hotter inland site. This combination of power and poise is characteristic of a maritime-tempered Western Australian GI — Margaret River.
