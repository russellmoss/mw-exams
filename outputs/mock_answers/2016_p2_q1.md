# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 2 Question 1

## a) Identify the grape variety and origin as closely as possible (18 marks)

Both wines share a pale-to-medium ruby hue, red-fruited floral aromatics, tannin light relative to colour depth, and moderate alcohol of 12-13%. Cherry, kirsch, floral lift, no oak spice and juicy acidity over limited tannin point to Gamay. Pinot Noir was considered given the pale colour but is ruled out by Wine 1's confected, almost banana-like top note and the absence of Pinot's savoury, earthy secondary layer.

The bright acidity and stony minerality point to Beaujolais rather than a lighter Loire Gamay; concentration and length on both place them above Beaujolais-Villages, at cru level. Wine 1's lifted, perfumed red-cherry fruit with softer tannin is consistent with Morgon. Wine 2's darker fruit, firmer tannic spine and higher 13% alcohol point to Moulin-à-Vent, the most structured and ageworthy of the ten crus, grown on manganese-rich granite.

## b) Compare the winemaking styles (18 marks)

Wine 1's transparent fruit, faint banana ester and soft, fine-grained tannin point to whole-bunch semi-carbonic maceration in a sealed vat, where intracellular fermentation inside the unbroken berry favours esters over extraction, followed by a short élevage in stainless steel or neutral wood that preserves transparency — the traditional Beaujolais approach prioritising immediacy over structure.

Wine 2's grainier tannin and more cellar-built, savoury shape argue against purely carbonic handling, instead suggesting destemming with a longer, warmer cuvaison and regular punch-downs to extract colour and structure, a Burgundian approach. Subtle spice and structural polish point to ageing in a proportion of French oak for longer than Wine 1, consistent with a single-clos bottling built for age rather than early charm. Wine 2 sits at the architectural, Burgundian-vinified end of Beaujolais; Wine 1 at the traditional, whole-cluster carbonic end — the two philosophies that genuinely coexist within this one region.

## c) Compare quality, with particular reference to both wines' potential evolution (14 marks)

Wine 1 is very good to outstanding quality, equivalent to a top-tier cru Beaujolais retailing around $30-35, its purity and length well above Beaujolais-Villages. At around two years old it already shows primary fruit and is approachable now; it will hold rather than improve dramatically, with a drinking window of the next 3-4 years before its lifted fruit fades.

Wine 2 is outstanding quality, equivalent to a top single-vineyard Moulin-à-Vent retailing around $40-45, a step above generic cru Beaujolais. Its firmer tannin gives genuine ageing potential: still youthful and slightly closed, it needs 2-3 years to integrate, will improve for a further 5-6 years developing savoury, gamey, dried-cherry complexity, and should then hold for 5 years beyond that — a longevity closer to red Burgundy than to Beaujolais' reputation for early drinking.
