# Mock answer — 2022 Paper 2 Question 5

## a) Identify the primary grape variety and the region as closely as possible (20 marks)

Both wines show the same core signature: brambly raspberry and dark berry fruit, white/cracked pepper, licorice and a jammy-plus-fresh fruit spectrum alongside notably high alcohol (15.5% and 14.6%) and American-oak vanilla seasoning. This, with alcohols pushing well past 14.5%, points to Zinfandel — its famously uneven ripening within a single bunch, raisined berries next to underripe ones, explains both the elevated alcohol and the mixed jammy/fresh fruit character. Grenache was considered for its peppery, high-alcohol profile but eliminated by the darker fruit and American rather than neutral oak seasoning; Primitivo, genetically identical, was eliminated by the more overt vanilla-oak and riper alcohol typical of California over Puglia's leaner expression.

The stem's wording — "same single grape variety or predominant grape variety" — flags that one wine is not pure varietal. Wine 12's deeper colour, firmer tannic grip and more savoury, earthy layer beyond Zinfandel's brambly lift suggest a thicker-skinned, more structured variety co-fermented in, most likely Petite Sirah with a touch of Carignane — the historic California field-blend construction.

The ripe fruit, oak handling and alcohol confirm a warm New World origin; within California, the combination of old-vine intensity with gravelly-soil grip and structure points specifically to Sonoma County's Dry Creek Valley, the benchmark old-vine Zinfandel AVA, rather than the softer, jammier styles typical of Lodi or Paso Robles.

## b) Compare and contrast style and quality (20 marks)

Wine 11: brambly red and black fruit, white pepper, licorice and vanilla oak over a broad, generous texture with medium-plus tannin and 15.5% alcohol — a classic, powerful varietal Zinfandel style. Very good to outstanding quality, equivalent to a premium single-vineyard AVA bottling retailing around $28–32; concentration and typicity are high, but the fruit is more overt and one-dimensional than Wine 12.

Wine 12: deeper colour, firmer tannic structure, darker damson fruit with herbal, earthy complexity from the co-fermented varieties, integrated American oak and a longer finish, at a marginally lower 14.6% alcohol giving better freshness and balance. Outstanding quality, equivalent to an icon field-blend bottling around $45–55 — the layered fruit, structural grip and ageing potential place it above most straight varietal Zinfandel from the same AVA.

## c) Compare and contrast market potential (10 marks)

Wine 11 suits mid-premium retail and by-the-glass listings around $25–30, through independent specialists and premium supermarkets domestically, with export interest in the UK and Canada where old-vine Zinfandel retains novelty appeal; it competes with other AVA-designated Sonoma and Lodi Zinfandels. Wine 12 commands allocation-list pricing above $45 through top independents and fine-dining by-the-bottle, competing with premium Sonoma Cabernet and other cult field blends; demand is led by US collectors, with limited, sought-after allocations into the UK and Japan.
