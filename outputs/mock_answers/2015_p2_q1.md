# Mock answer — 2015 Paper 2 Question 1

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (3 x 7 marks)

All three share the variety, so origin rests on style contrast. Wines 1 and 3 show a reticent, savoury nose of dried herbs, forest floor and dark spice, firm fine tannin, racy acidity and moderate alcohols of 13-13.5%, consistent with a cool, marginal European climate: Burgundy. Wine 2 is more open and fruit-forward, with riper dark cherry, softer tannin and a fuller 14% alcohol pointing to a sunnier New World site; Central Otago and Willamette Valley were considered, but the bright acid and precise fruit purity, without Central Otago's deeper colour or Willamette's riper grip, point instead to Marlborough. Within Burgundy, Wine 3's deeper colour, broader tannic frame and darker, smokier spice mark it Côte de Nuits — Gevrey-Chambertin Premier Cru — while Wine 1's paler, garnet-edged hue and lighter red-fruited profile point to a gentler Côte de Beaune village bordering Volnay: Monthelie Premier Cru.

## b) Comment on winemaking (3 x 4 marks)

**Wine 1** — Fine, integrated tannin and subtle oak spice suggest partial destemming, fermentation in open-top wooden cuves with gentle pigeage, and ageing in predominantly older French oak for around a year — a low-new-oak regime letting fruit and terroir show through.

**Wine 2** — Plush texture and a touch of vanilla point to a cold soak ahead of open-vat fermentation with pigeage, followed by ten to fourteen months in French oak carrying more new wood than the Burgundies, rounding the fruit without masking it.

**Wine 3** — Firmer grip and smoky, savoury lift indicate partial whole-bunch inclusion during an extended maceration, with maturation in barrique — mostly older wood, sixteen to eighteen months — consistent with a low-intervention, terroir-focused approach.

## c) Discuss quality and maturity, with particular reference to the wine's ability to improve (3 x 9 marks)

**Wine 1** — Very good quality, comparable to a well-made lesser Côte de Beaune Premier Cru rather than a grand Volnay site; around £35. The bright red fruit and still-primary character with emerging forest-floor notes put the wine at about five years old. The tense, structured style typical of the small 2010 crop means it is not yet at peak; it should improve for three to five years and hold five to six beyond, gaining savoury, gamey complexity as fruit recedes.

**Wine 2** — Very good quality, a strong benchmark for premium Marlborough Pinot Noir at around £30. The ripe, pure fruit and supple tannin show a wine already at ease at three years old, with little to gain from cellaring; it will hold its fruit-driven charm for three to four more years before primary fruit fades.

**Wine 3** — Outstanding quality, equivalent to a top-tier Côte de Nuits Premier Cru, around £90. The riper, more generous 2009 vintage gives immediate depth beneath Gevrey's inherent structure — accessibility and grip rarely found together. At six years old it already drinks well, but concentration and tannic frame suggest it will improve for six to eight years and hold a decade more, developing truffle, game and dried-fruit complexity.

## d) Comment on the commercial appeal (3 x 5 marks)

**Wine 1** — Suits independent specialists and Burgundy-focused by-the-glass lists at around £35, appealing to consumers exploring serious Burgundy before trading up to Volnay or Meursault; competes with value Premier Cru from Savigny-lès-Beaune. Export potential in the UK, Japan and US, though the lesser-known village is a recognition challenge.

**Wine 2** — Fits premium supermarkets and independents at £28-32, targeting drinkers who love New Zealand Sauvignon Blanc trading up to red; competes with Central Otago and premium Oregon Pinot. Strong demand in the UK, US and Australia; the opportunity is NZ's quality reputation, the challenge is justifying price against famous Old World names.

**Wine 3** — Belongs on fine-wine merchant lists and top on-trade programmes at around £85-90, targeting collectors and sommeliers; competes with Chambolle-Musigny and Vosne-Romanée Premier Crus. Strong demand from US and Asian markets; the opportunity lies in rising interest in grower Burgundy, the challenge is limited allocation.
