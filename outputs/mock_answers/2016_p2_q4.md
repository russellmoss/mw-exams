# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 2 Question 4

## a) Identify the origin as closely as possible (2 x 9 marks)

Both wines are dry, structured reds from 2011 sharing a single broad region, but the two-percentage-point gap in alcohol — 13% against 15% — is the loudest signal that this "same region" actually spans two very different Rhône worlds.

**Wine 8.** Deep ruby colour with violet, black olive, cured meat and white pepper over a granite-etched mineral core, medium-to-full body, fine ripe tannin and vivid acidity at only 13% is a classic Syrah profile; the perfume and freshness rule out warmer-climate Shiraz. Steep granite slopes, pepper-and-violet lift and moderate alcohol point to the Northern Rhône, and the site-driven concentration and length suggest a single-vineyard Côte-Rôtie rather than a generic regional Syrah.

**Wine 9.** Deeper, more saturated colour with ripe black cherry, kirsch, garrigue herbs, lavender and warm spice, full body and broad, ripe tannin at 15% is inconsistent with monovarietal Syrah and instead suggests a Grenache-led blend. That alcohol jump, combined with galets-roulés warmth and layered blend complexity, points to the Southern Rhône, and the depth and pedigree here are consistent with Châteauneuf-du-Pape rather than a village-level Côtes du Rhône Villages.

## b) Discuss quality within the context of the specific region (2 x 9 marks)

**Wine 8.** Very good to excellent quality for Côte-Rôtie: pure fruit concentration, fine tannin, granite-driven length and site transparency place it above generic Côte-Rôtie and in line with a single-vineyard bottling, retailing around £70. The moderate, earlier-drinking structure fits the softer 2011 vintage rather than a monumental year like 2010, but balance and savoury persistence still put it near the top of the appellation's pyramid.

**Wine 9.** Very good to excellent quality for Châteauneuf-du-Pape: the wine retains savoury freshness despite its high alcohol, rather than tipping into jamminess, showing genuine complexity and resolved, ripe tannin. This reads as benchmark, top-tier Châteauneuf-du-Pape, comparable to the appellation's leading traditional estates and priced accordingly around £60 — again reflecting the approachable 2011 vintage rather than a blockbuster.

## c) Discuss the key winemaking techniques used to arrive at this style (2 x 7 marks)

**Wine 8.** The pure varietal perfume alongside spice and firm structure suggests partial whole-bunch fermentation and ageing in mostly used with some new French oak, giving polish without masking site character. Côte-Rôtie also permits a small proportion of co-fermented Viognier, which would help explain the violet lift and softened tannin here.

**Wine 9.** The broad texture and layered fruit point to blending several varieties — principally Grenache with Syrah and Mourvèdre — vinified and matured separately, most likely in large old foudres or concrete rather than new barriques, preserving fruit purity and warmth rather than overt oak, consistent with a traditional, restrained élevage.
