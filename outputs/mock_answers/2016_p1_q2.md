# Mock answer — 2016 Paper 1 Question 2

## a) Identify the grape variety and origin(s) as closely as possible (16 marks)

Both wines share ripe orchard and stone fruit, a creamy, malolactic-softened mid-palate and generous new oak — pointing to Chardonnay. Viognier was briefly considered for Wine 4's richness but its firmer, more linear acidity and citrus-pith precision sit more with cool-influenced Chardonnay. Wine 3's moderate acidity, 13.5% alcohol, tropical pineapple/banana fruit and heavy vanilla oak indicate warm, high-yielding, valley-floor fruit sourced across multiple districts rather than one site — a broad California appellation wine, likely with a North Coast/Sonoma County core. Wine 4's deeper colour, greater concentration, mineral, citrus-edged finish and higher 14.1% alcohol despite a cooler-tasting profile point to a specific, elevated, low-yielding vineyard — an elevated Sonoma Mountain site where altitude moderates the county's underlying warmth.

## b) Discuss winemaking with particular reference to maturation post-fermentation (14 marks)

Wine 3's overt vanilla-coconut sweetness alongside only moderate fruit concentration suggests oak influence from staves or chips in tank as much as genuine barrel ageing, with full malolactic delivering its buttery, low-acid roundness; the wine's straightforward, ready-now character implies a short post-ferment hold with minimal lees contact before early fining, filtration and bottling for consistency at volume. Wine 4's finer-grained toast, hazelnut and integrated cream point to fermentation and ageing in genuine French oak barriques with a meaningful new-oak component; the textural weight and savoury complexity indicate extended lees contact with regular bâtonnage, and full malolactic is again evident from the creamy mid-palate. The purity and precision on the finish suggest minimal fining or filtration, consistent with a longer, unhurried maturation before bottling.

## c) Comment on quality within the context of region of origin (20 marks)

Wine 3 is an acceptable-to-good quality example at the entry level of the California AVA category — pleasant, fruit-forward and commercially reliable, but its oak and butter dominate rather than integrate, and it lacks the concentration or length for anything above a broad, retailing-around-$12 commercial tier. Wine 4 is outstanding quality, equivalent to a top single-vineyard bottling within Sonoma Mountain — the concentration, oak integration, mineral tension and persistent finish reflect low-yielding, site-specific fruit and meticulous handling, placing it at an allocation-list, $80-plus price point. Within the same country and variety, the pair illustrates the full quality spectrum California Chardonnay can reach: from generic, blended, technically sound wine to a benchmark, terroir-expressive example capable of extended ageing.
