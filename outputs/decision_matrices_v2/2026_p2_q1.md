### Country lock
The inverted mark order (style/commercial 11 > ID 8) is the single loudest signal in this question: the examiners are not chasing fine origin detective work, they are testing **market fluency and stylistic range within one country's red-wine landscape**. That reframes the country-selection logic — the IMW does not need the country to field three "trophy" appellations; it needs three varieties that occupy genuinely different commercial slots so there is something real to say about price/channel/positioning for each. This is the direct P2 mirror of 2025 P1 Q3 (French white showcase: Muscadet / Alsace Riesling / Loire Chenin). Per the tree's Branch 2.1b, France's bench is deep enough to run this exercise entirely off non-Bordeaux, non-Burgundy varieties — a genuinely light-bodied, cool-climate single-varietal tour (Loire Cabernet Franc, Beaujolais Gamay, Jura Trousseau/Poulsard) rather than the "big three" cast (Nebbiolo/Napa Cab/Barossa Shiraz) a blind read reaches for first.

### Mark-order signal
Style/commercial marks (11) reward reading each wine's fruit purity, tannin management, oak (or absence of it), and finish against a plausible retail/on-trade slot. Method (6) rewards one nameable technique per wine (co-operative tank vinification, semi-carbonic maceration, biodynamic low-intervention élevage). ID (8) is almost a confirmation step once style is read correctly — spend the least write-up time here relative to its mark share.

### Anti-collapse rule
Do not read this stem as "one classic-region tour" (Burgundy/Bordeaux/Rhône) by default — Branch 2.1b shows the IMW deliberately favours breadth across France's *lighter, cooler* red bench when the mark order signals market fluency over trophy-hunting. Country is the free variable already given; don't waste time re-litigating it, and don't assume "same country" implies grand/classified wines.

### One rule-out worth naming — the pale-red trap
Wine 3's pale ruby colour, high acid, low-to-moderate tannin and slightly rustic/spiced, faintly oxidative-adjacent profile is the textbook shape that invites an automatic "Pinot Noir" call. **Do not make that collapse.** Inside an all-France F2a flight, a pale high-acid red with a savoury, spicy, almost sherry-adjacent top note is Trousseau's (or Poulsard's) signature, not Pinot Noir's — this project's own automated wine-extractor mis-resolved this exact bottling to Savagnin/Chardonnay purely from the cuvée name "Trousseau Singulier," which is itself a warning about how easily this wine misleads on variety even before tasting. Keep Trousseau alive as a live candidate the moment colour reads pale in a French red flight, especially once wines 1 and 2 have already claimed the "obvious" light-red slots (Cabernet Franc, Gamay).

## Universe (F2a reasoning)
Given P2 + "same country, three distinct single varieties" + heavy weight on commercial-position discrimination, the country must field at least three well-known single-varietal reds sitting at genuinely different style/price tiers. France clears this bar comfortably without needing its "big three" showcase wines: Loire Cabernet Franc supplies an accessible, food-driven, co-operative-tier entry wine; Beaujolais cru Gamay supplies a mid-tier, terroir-differentiated, unoaked style; Jura Trousseau supplies a genuine artisanal curveball — biodynamic, low-intervention, premium-priced (£38–48) despite its unfamiliar name and pale appearance. That spread of co-op / cru / grower-icon commercial tiers is exactly what the 33-mark style/commercial block needs to reward.

### Candidate varieties (confidence tiers)
- Cabernet Franc (Loire): **STRONG SIGNAL** — the accessible entry point of the trio; blackcurrant/raspberry fruit, soft tannin, mineral streak, no oak signature
- Gamay (Beaujolais cru): **STRONG SIGNAL** — the mid-tier, terroir-expressive slot; bright cherry/raspberry, floral lift, fine tannin, unoaked, semi-carbonic vinification
- Trousseau (Jura/Arbois): **STRONG SIGNAL but the hardest ID** — pale, high-acid, spice-and-red-berry, biodynamic, partial demi-muid/amphora ageing; the premium/artisanal anchor of the flight
- Pinot Noir: **CURVEBALL rule-out** — the trap variety a candidate will reach for on Wine 3's pale colour; ruled out by the savoury/spice/rustic profile and the all-France "different varieties" constraint (Pinot Noir would be too obvious a headline grape for the examiners' "market fluency" framing here)

- Wine 2: Régnié AOC, Beaujolais cru — Domaine de la Margot, mid-teens-to-twenties €/bottle, unoaked, semi-carbonic, marketed as an accessible entry into the ten crus

### Rule-outs
- Italy/Spain/USA as the country: all plausible in the abstract, but none as cleanly supplies three light-bodied, cool-climate varietal reds at genuinely different commercial tiers as France's non-Bordeaux bench does — see Branch 2.1b
- Bordeaux/Rioja/Chianti Classico-style blends as any of the three wines: ruled out by "different single grape varieties" — these regions are blend-first
- Pinot Noir for Wine 3: ruled out in the glass by savoury/spice/rustic character over pure red-fruit purity; ruled out structurally by the fact Pinot Noir would be a far more "obvious" answer than the market-fluency framing rewards

### IMW's likely intent
Testing market fluency and stylistic breadth within one country's red-wine landscape, not fine-grained origin detective work — the inverted mark order (11/6/8) makes this explicit. The 2026 P2 Q1 wine selection additionally functions as an active pale-red pedagogy question: it wants to know whether the candidate can hold Trousseau (or Poulsard) as a live hypothesis rather than collapsing every pale French red to Pinot Noir.

## What to focus on in the glass
- Colour depth as a first-pass sort: deep ruby (Cabernet Franc) → bright cherry-ruby (Gamay) → pale, luminous crimson (Trousseau) — don't let the pale wine auto-read as Pinot Noir
- Tannin and oak: soft/unoaked (Cabernet Franc, Gamay) vs demi-muid/amphora textural complexity with no vanilla/toast (Trousseau)
- Aromatic profile: blackcurrant-leaf pyrazine (Cabernet Franc) vs kirsch/banana-adjacent carbonic lift (Gamay) vs strawberry-cranberry-white pepper-forest floor-bitter almond (Trousseau) — the last is the decisive tell against Pinot Noir's cleaner red-fruit/earth signature
- Commercial read: co-op simplicity vs cru-Beaujolais accessibility vs biodynamic grower-icon pricing — the 33-mark block rewards naming a specific channel/price band for each, not just "premium" or "commercial"
