### Literal-sea reading (Branch 4.5)
The stem's precise wording is the single most important signal in this question. "Mediterranean Sea" restricts the universe to the actual Mediterranean basin — southern France, Italy (including the islands), Spain's east coast, Greece, Croatia/Slovenia, Cyprus, Lebanon, North Africa, Turkey's Aegean coast — and explicitly excludes Atlantic-facing France/Iberia (Rías Baixas, Vinho Verde), continental Germany/Austria, Marlborough/Central Otago, and the New World "Mediterranean-climate" analogues (California, Chile, South Africa) that would be correct if the stem instead said "Mediterranean climate." Defaulting to climate-type reasoning here is the trap the stem is built to set, and the corpus's one data point confirms the literal reading held exactly: all three wines drawn were from the actual Mediterranean basin.

### White-from-a-red-appellation trap
When a stem or a producer/appellation name points at a region famous chiefly for reds — Bandol, Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Pessac-Léognan, Rioja — a white from that same appellation is a live candidate, not a decoy. Bandol Blanc (Clairette/Bourboulenc/Ugni Blanc) has nothing to do with the appellation's Mourvèdre-led red; an automated first-pass extraction on this very question mis-resolved the wine to Mourvèdre/Grenache by appellation lookup alone, ignoring the "Blanc" designation. This is now a named, evidenced trap in the master tree (Branch 5 / pack Branch 4.5) precisely because of this question.

### Freshness-preservation levers (three different mechanisms)
The winemaking-impact-on-freshness question (30 of 75 marks) is best answered by naming the specific technical lever each wine relies on, since all three sit in a warm-to-hot Mediterranean climate where acid retention cannot be assumed from climate alone:
- Cellar technique (cool must-settling, controlled low-temperature stainless-steel fermentation, no oak, no malolactic emphasis) — Sardinian Vermentino.
- Site and viticulture (ancient bush vines, kouloura training against wind, volcanic/pumice soil, sea/wind moderation, traditional early/night harvest) — Santorini Assyrtiko, freshness preserved almost entirely through terroir rather than cellar intervention despite a severe-drought, high-heat 2024 vintage.
- Altitude and organic viticulture, reinforced by neutral (non-new) wood ageing for texture without masking acidity — Bandol Blanc, from the appellation's highest, coolest, north-facing site.
