# Examiner Confidence Construction Model — How MW Practical Examiner Confidence Is Created, Maintained, and Lost

> **Project 9 · Agent 6 — Examiner Cognition Synthesizer (capstone).** This is the first evidence-based
> *unified* model of the latent judgement an IMW practical examiner forms while reading a written script —
> *"does this candidate understand the wine, or are they guessing?"* — built by synthesising the five
> upstream Project 9 deliverables. The project's hypothesis: the strongest remaining unexplained signal in
> the MW Practical is **examiner confidence**, not raw knowledge. This document tests and largely confirms
> that hypothesis, and formalises the dynamics.
>
> **What this model is.** A *process model* of examiner cognition — not a wine guide, not a frequency study,
> not a composition predictor. It describes the mental act of trust-construction that converts a written
> trace into a verdict, names what credits and debits that trust, and states the laws governing it
> (asymmetry, contamination, confidence≠correctness). It is the deliverable a candidate and the build team
> use to understand *what an examiner is actually grading*.
>
> **Evidence base.** Eight IMW **Practical** examiner reports (2017, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024,
> 2025), five **Chief** examiner reports (2021–2025), and five **Theory** reports (2017, 2019, 2023, 2024,
> 2025). The project target is the **Practical**; Theory evidence is used only to corroborate exam-wide
> cognitive principles and is flagged as such. Built from: `confidence_language_corpus.md` (Agent 1, the
> verbatim quote database), `confidence_building_behaviors.md` (Agent 2), `confidence_destroying_behaviors.md`
> (Agent 3), `plausibility_framework.md` (Agent 4), `sophistication_framework.md` (Agent 5); grounded against
> the pre-Project-9 `examiner_confidence_model.md`, `examiner_objectives.md`, `distinction_candidate_analysis.md`.
>
> **Tier convention (project multi-report rule).**
> - **STRONG SIGNAL** — named/illustrated in **3+ distinct reports** (a report = one year × report-type file).
> - **PLAUSIBLE** — **2** distinct reports.
> - **EXAMPLE-ONLY** — **1** report / a single anecdote; never elevated to doctrine.
>
> Only the unified model's *doctrine* rests on STRONG SIGNAL findings. PLAUSIBLE and EXAMPLE-ONLY material is
> marked as such and not laundered into doctrine. **Practical-evidenced** claims are distinguished from
> **Theory-evidenced** ones throughout.
>
> **Provenance caveat (inherited, standing).** IMW examiner reports are member-gated, not web-public. The
> verbatim quotes are credible because the user holds the reports, but year/paper attributions are *corpus
> claims*, not independently web-verifiable facts. A public IMW document (e.g. the Student Guide on the pass
> standard) overrides any corpus paraphrase on a hard fact.

## 1 · The core model — Examiner Confidence Model v1

### 1.1 The plain-English statement

> **Examiner confidence is a running *trust account* the examiner keeps on the candidate as a
> would-be Master of Wine. It opens neutral and is read off the *written trace alone* — the examiner cannot
> watch the candidate taste, so competence is *inferred*, never observed. The account is *credited* by
> reasoning that runs visibly from the glass to a plausible, committed conclusion (the funnel), by accurate
> structure, by quality calibrated on the real ladder, and — at the top — by a second-order insight that gets
> "under the skin of the wine." It is *debited* by guessing, hedging, shoehorning, and recycled answers, and
> *bankrupted* by a single factual impossibility, which retroactively poisons trust in everything else on the
> page. The final mark tracks the *trust balance*, not the raw count of correct identifications: a wrong but
> well-reasoned answer keeps the account in credit (5–6/8), and a correct but un-argued or contaminated
> answer empties it.**

### 1.2 Why it is a *trust account* and not a *score*

The examiner reads ~36 wines across three papers, usually sub-question by sub-question, and is trying to
answer one latent question that the marks are merely the instrument for:

> *"Is this person a Master of Wine — a trustworthy generalist authority whose written opinion on an unknown
> wine I would stand behind?"* (`examiner_confidence_model.md` §1)

The corpus states the account model in the examiners' own register. Confidence is something candidates
*lack*, *show*, *instil*, *undermine*, or *restore* — all balance-sheet verbs:

- "Often candidates fail because they lack the **confidence** to provide in-depth analysis and **fail to
  persuade the examiner that they truly understand the wine**." (2022 Practical, Intro — corpus A4) **[STRONG]**
- "It was an **insightful observation that instilled much confidence in the eyes of the examiners**." (2025
  Practical, Paper 3, Tokaji — corpus A12/C4) **[the named credit exemplar]**
- "This **totally undermines the confidence** the examiner might have had in the candidate." (2017 Practical,
  Burgundy — corpus A13) **[the named debit exemplar]**
- "credibility in the eyes of the examiners would have been **restored**" by proof-reading (2017 Theory — A27).

### 1.3 The five dynamics — what opens, credits, debits, bankrupts the account, and how the mark relates

**(a) What OPENS the account — *neutral, then earned*.** The account opens at neutral on every script; trust
is built additively, wine by wine. The decisive practical correlate is that **distinction is consistency, not
peak brilliance**: "there were no high pass marks, which demonstrates that one off-day will not ruin one's
chances, but consistency is essential" — in a year with passes, *not a single candidate scored an A*
(`distinction_candidate_analysis.md` §0). The account is built across 12 wines × 3 days, never by one
brilliant flight.

**(b) What CREDITS it (confidence-building).** The behaviours Agent 2 ranks by replication:
1. **The deductive funnel** — read structure first, lay 2–3 evidenced options, narrow, *commit*; "this allows
   the examiner to **see your logic** and award marks **even if your conclusion is incorrect**" (2022, corpus
   D2). **STRONG SIGNAL (≥7 reports).**
2. **Conviction and authority** — the *register* of a pass: "We are **looking for confidence and authority**,
   for answers written **convincingly and with consistency**" (2018, A16). **STRONG (≥7).**
3. **Glass-first description** — report the real structure/aromatics; bank marks even on an un-IDable wine.
   **STRONG (≥6 for the axis).**
4. **Accurate structural reading** (alcohol/acidity/tannin/RS) — the least bluffable, most-trusted evidence and
   the *seed* of the whole account. **STRONG (3+).**
5. **A plausible, well-argued conclusion** — defensible-for-the-style + glass-grounded; "5 or 6 marks … if
   their **reasoning was sound and their conclusion plausible**" (2025, B11). **STRONG (5).**
6. **Quality calibrated on the real ladder, both directions** (name the official tier; don't over- or
   under-call). **STRONG (3+).**
7. **Winemaking tied to the glass** — each technique linked to a tasted attribute; absence treated as a choice.
   **STRONG (3).**
8. **Second-order insight — "under the skin"** — the distinction credit; the Tokaji producer-exceeds-minimum
   inference. **PLAUSIBLE on the Practical, STRONG as an exam-wide value.**

**(c) What DEBITS it (confidence-eroding, recoverable).** Guessing from a single cue; hedging / never
committing ("a wrong answer yields more marks than an answer that is unfinished," 2021, D9); cut-and-paste
across wines ("creates **considerable doubt in the mind of the reader**," 2024, D17); rote commercial
boilerplate; unprofessional language and misspelling ("do not engender confidence," 2018, A14). These erode
the balance but rarely empty it on their own — they are *withdrawals*, not *bankruptcy*.

**(d) What BANKRUPTS it (confidence-destroying, often catastrophic).** Two mechanisms:
- **The theory howler / factual impossibility** — "Amontillado at 14.5%," "Tawny Port aged in a solera," CdR
  called CdP, "Douro, Spain." This is the supreme destroyer because it *contaminates everything else*:
  "**Factual errors undermine confidence in everything a candidate has written**" (2023 Theory, A35;
  near-verbatim 2024 Theory, A38). **STRONG SIGNAL (≥10 reports)** — the single most over-determined finding
  in the corpus.
- **The shoehorn / cascade** — deciding the identity first and then *forcing the structure to fit* (shoehorning)
  or *abandoning the glass and writing the downstream answer for the guess* (the cascade). "There was a lot of
  shoehorning … and ultimately, it led to the **failure of many candidates**" (2025 P2). **STRONG (6 / 5).**

**(e) How the MARK relates to the balance.** The conclusion is cheap to check and deliberately lightly
weighted (ID ≈ 39–46% paper-wide and falling). The examiner reads *for* the trust balance, and the mark tracks
it: a wrong-but-credit answer scores 5–6/8; a correct-but-empty answer scores low; a bankrupting howler caps
the affected conclusion and, **at the borderline, withdraws the benefit of the doubt and resolves the whole
paper to FAIL** — "it is **hard to feel confident about a borderline candidate** when they make obvious theory
mistakes" (2024, A9). The verdict is a threshold on the *minimum* faculty (not the average), evaluated against
an **absolute** standard — an **aggregate 65% across the three papers with a per-paper floor**, not 65% per
paper (`examiner_confidence_model.md` §7; `distinction_candidate_analysis.md` §0). This is why a weak P3 is the
classic borderline-decider: it drags the average or breaches the floor.

### 1.4 What this study confirms, revises, and supersedes vs the prior model

- **Confirms.** The pre-Project-9 `examiner_confidence_model.md` core — competence is *inferred not observed*,
  the trust judgement is *asymmetric* (slow to build, fast to collapse), it is the *minimum across faculties*,
  and it is the *tiebreaker at the borderline*. Project 9's quote-mining over 18 reports vindicates all four.
- **Strengthens.** The contamination law moves from "supported" to the **best-attested finding in the corpus**
  (≥10 distinct reports), and the confidence≠correctness claim is now backed by *named exemplars in both
  directions* (§5). The plausibility sub-component is decomposed into ten testable components (Agent 4) and the
  sophistication sub-component into six (Agent 5), giving the model operational teeth it previously lacked.
- **Revises / sharpens.** "Reasoning > correctness" is **conditional**, not absolute: it rescues a wrong call
  *only* when the structural read is sound, the call is stylistically adjacent, the glass is described (not the
  guess), and there is no attached howler (Agent 4 §3). Latitude is **generous on curveballs, strict on
  bankers**. The "single howler flips borderline → fail" rule remains corpus-supported but publicly UNVERIFIED —
  encode as a strong tendency, not an iron law.
- **Does not supersede** the prior model's standing corrections (the **65%-aggregate-with-floor** pass standard,
  the funnel-when-uncertain / commit-when-certain calibration, the provenance note). Those carry forward intact.

## 2 · The Required Questions, answered with cited evidence and tiers

### 2.1 What *increases* examiner confidence?
Visible, glass-anchored reasoning that commits. **STRONG SIGNAL.** The funnel — "see your logic … even if your
conclusion is incorrect" (2022, D2; ≥7 reports); accurate structure (2025, E5 inverse; 3+); a plausible
well-argued conclusion ("5 or 6 marks … if their reasoning was sound and their conclusion plausible," 2025,
B11; 5 reports); quality calibrated on the real ladder (2024, full-scale discrimination; 3+); winemaking tied
to the glass ("**convincingly linked** … production with how the wine tastes," 2019, B26; 3); and at the top,
second-order insight (Tokaji, 2025, A12; PLAUSIBLE practical / STRONG exam-wide).

### 2.2 What *destroys* it?
A factual impossibility / theory howler, which **contaminates the whole script** — "Factual errors undermine
confidence in **everything** a candidate has written" (2023 Theory A35; 2024 Theory A38; ≥10 reports).
**STRONG SIGNAL — the strongest in the corpus.** Then shoehorning (6 reports) and the cascade — writing the
answer for the guess, not the glass (5 reports). **STRONG.**

### 2.3 What *creates doubt*?
Behaviours that prove the candidate stopped reasoning from the glass. **STRONG / PLAUSIBLE.** Cut-and-paste
across wines — "creates **considerable doubt in the mind of the reader**" (2024, D17; 3 reports); the snap
first-impression call with no alternatives weighed — "trust your first impression was proven wrong with this
wine" (2022, E3; 3 reports); hedging / never committing — "bullet points … **unlikely to inspire confidence**"
(2019, A19; 2 reports); second-guessing the examiner instead of the glass (2018/2019/2023; 3 reports).

### 2.4 What *creates trust*?
The same axis as 2.1, viewed as the accumulating balance: **consistency across all wines** (the no-A-passes
finding, `distinction_candidate_analysis.md` §0), calibration (commit when sure, funnel when unsure — 2023,
A7/D6), and **honest engagement with the unknown** — "a lot of points could have been gained from simply
**describing what was in the glass** even if the region was unknown" (2024, D18). **STRONG.**

### 2.5 What *creates credibility*?
Factual flawlessness and professional register — the *defensive* floor. **STRONG (4 reports, minor/cumulative
force).** "Misused terminology **undermines the credibility** of your response" (2017 Theory A26); correct
spelling of regions/grapes/producers "**restores** credibility" (2017 Theory A27); slang ("stonking," "icon")
"**do not engender confidence**" (2018 A14). Credibility is *neutral when present, corrosive when absent* — it
removes friction rather than building strong positive belief.

### 2.6 What makes reasoning *persuasive*?
The plausibility components (Agent 4), all **STRONG** unless noted: the conclusion is **derived, not asserted**
(P1, 4+ reports); **internally consistent** with the candidate's own numbers (P2, 3+); built as a **funnel**
that terminates in a commitment (P3 STRONG / P5 PLAUSIBLE); the near-misses are **visibly ruled out** (P4, 3);
it is **anchored to the glass throughout** (P7, 3+); and it lands on a **stylistically adjacent** endpoint (P8,
3+). *"Plausibility is reasoning-soundness × glass-anchoring × stylistic adjacency, with theory-consistency as
a gate and difficulty as a latitude multiplier."* (Agent 4 §3)

### 2.7 What makes reasoning *sophisticated*?
A **second move** beyond competence (Agent 5). **STRONG overall, PLAUSIBLE/EXAMPLE-anchored on the Practical
specifically.** Causal/second-order linking — connect winemaking-and-climate *to the glass*, don't catalogue
(S1, 5+); **selective, not exhaustive** — "an **overabundance of examples can sometimes mask a lack of
fundamental understanding**" (2018; S4, 4); **original not rote** (S5, 5); and at the apex, **reconcile
conflicting evidence with one higher-order inference** (S3 — the Tokaji move). *"The middle band shows the
examiner what it knows; the top band shows the examiner how it thinks."* (Agent 5 §4)

### 2.8 What makes conclusions *feel plausible*?
A believable **link from the reported glass to the committed conclusion**, such that a competent reader holding
the key can see *why a sensible taster would land there* — even if it is the wrong house. Plausibility is a
property of the **link, not the endpoint** (Agent 4 §2). **STRONG SIGNAL.** The endpoint still matters only as
a *latitude multiplier* (P8/adjacency): USA→Australia got credit; USA→Italy "few marks" (2021, B5).

### 2.9 What distinguishes *confidence* from *correctness*?
Correctness is *what the wine is*; confidence is *whether the examiner believes the candidate reasons like a
Master*. The exam grades the **trustworthiness of the visible cognitive process**, with the conclusion as cheap
corroboration. The decisive corpus proof: "over half did not get a single origin exactly right, however many
still managed **5 or 6 marks out of the allotted 8 if their reasoning was sound and their conclusion
plausible**" (2025 P1Q2, Agent 4 §3). **STRONG SIGNAL.** A majority got *zero* origins right and still earned
the bulk of the marks — the marks cannot be coming from correctness.

### 2.10 Can a *wrong answer* still create confidence? — **YES. STRONG SIGNAL (5 reports).**
This is one of the best-attested findings in the entire corpus. A wrong identification *retains* examiner
confidence — and 5–6/8 — when delivered by transparent, glass-grounded, stylistically-adjacent reasoning.
- **The clearest single exemplar:** 2025 Practical, Paper 1 Q2 — "over half did not get a single origin exactly
  right, however many still managed **5 or 6 marks** … if their reasoning was sound and their conclusion
  plausible."
- 2025 P2: "A few candidates managed to earn good points with **convincing arguments for Cabernet Sauvignon or
  Merlot, placing the third wine in Bordeaux instead of Tuscany**" — a *wrong* origin that earned confidence
  because the argument held and the call was adjacent (B12).
- 2023 P2 (Grenache W8): "**any plausible origin could get good marks if well argued**" (B6).
- The stated principle: examiners weigh "whether the candidate shows **convincing logic even if wrong**" (2023
  Intro, B7); "**A wrong answer yields more marks than an answer that is unfinished, so whatever you do: Make a
  choice**" (2021, D9).
- The register itself: on a near-impossible wine, "**taste like a detective; argue like a lawyer**" (2019 P3Q3,
  D14) — the examiner names the *deductive register*, not the unreachable right answer, as the thing that earns
  the marks.

**Conditions** (Agent 4 §3): the structural read must be sound, the call stylistically adjacent, the downstream
answer must describe the glass not the guess, the funnel must terminate, and there must be no attached howler.
On a banker, latitude shrinks to "is it correct?"

### 2.11 Can a *correct answer* still destroy confidence? — **YES. Documented in multiple reports.**
A correct identification *loses* confidence when the process is invisible, the reasoning absent, or a dependent
judgement mis-calibrated.
- **The cleanest exemplar:** 2024 Practical, Paper 1 — the *same correct ID (Pinot Grigio)* earned good marks or
  lost many marks **purely on the argument**: "many candidates went straight for Pinot Grigio **without proper
  arguments and lost many marks**" vs "some got good pass marks for Pinot Grigio … **with arguments … that made
  the choice more plausible**." Correctness held constant; confidence varied entirely with reasoning. (Agent 3
  §4; Agent 4 P1.)
- 2018 P (Chilean Cabernet): origin *correctly* identified as Chile, yet "hardly anyone was sufficiently
  effusive about the quality" — a correct ID coupled with an **origin-biased, mis-calibrated** quality judgement
  still read as a weak answer.
- 2023 P2 (Grenache): the correct-ish crowd showed "**little demonstration of knowledge of where Grenache wine
  is made** … a feeling of trying to second guess the examiners' choices" — variety reached, confidence lost on
  the absence of real reasoning.
- 2021 P2Q1 (Saint-Julien): candidates correctly in Bordeaux still failed by "completely neglect[ing] the
  second half" (quality) — a correct region, marks and confidence lost on the neglected dimension.

> A bare correct label transmits *no reasoning*, so there is nothing for the examiner to assent to; an
> un-argued right answer is, to the trust account, almost indistinguishable from a lucky guess.

### 2.12 What behaviours separate *pass-level* from *distinction-level* candidates?
The pass→distinction line is **insight on top of knowledge**, expressed **selectively and decisively**.
- Pass shows the examiner *what it knows* (correct facts, complete BLICCA coverage, glass-grounded reasoning);
  distinction adds *how it thinks* — one **second-order move**: a causal link from process to glass, a
  producer-/site-level inference, or the **resolution of a conflict in the evidence** (Agent 5 §4). The named
  exemplar is the Tokaji answer; the examiners' own word for their reaction was "**confidence**."
- "There was **obvious knowledge, but not enough insight**" (2023 Theory) is the purest statement of the gap;
  "they frequently lack confidence and play it safe by **not truly getting 'under the skin of the wine'**, which
  would show mastery" (2022 Practical, C1) is the practical correlate.
- The distinction move is also *consistency*, not brilliance: distinction is "the ability to taste consistently
  well across 12 wines × 3 days, free from theoretical inaccuracy" (`distinction_candidate_analysis.md` §0).
- **Honesty caveat:** the *explicit* "insight/sophisticated" lexicon is ~60–65% Theory-sourced. On the Practical
  it rests on two phrases ("under the skin," "selective in their arguments") plus the one Tokaji exemplar —
  directionally STRONG but practical-specifically EXAMPLE-anchored. Insight separates *pass from distinction*,
  not *fail from pass*.

## 3 · The asymmetry / contamination law

**The law (STRONG SIGNAL · ≥10 distinct reports — the best-attested finding in the corpus).**

> **A single demonstrable error poisons the examiner's trust in the *entire rest* of the script. Confidence is
> slow to build and fast to collapse; one factual impossibility re-prices every other answer on the page.**

This is *not* "wrong answers lose marks." It is reputational: a howler reclassifies the candidate in the
examiner's mind from "knows the field" to "has gaps a Master shouldn't," and that reclassification is applied
**retroactively** to claims the examiner had already been willing to trust.

**The near-fixed examiner formula** (the closest the corpus has to a verbatim rule), in both formats:
- **2023 Theory:** "**Factual errors undermine confidence in everything a candidate has written.**"
- **2024 Theory:** "such mistakes **undermine confidence in everything a candidate has written**."
- **2018 Practical:** one misplaced producer "will **undermine credibility of the rest of the paper's
  discussion**."
- **2017 Practical — the proof of propagation:** "This **totally undermines the confidence** the examiner might
  have had in the candidate" — and the parenthetical that proves trust tracks *across questions*: "(Further
  **shattered** by reading … 'Douro, Spain' for Vintage Port in the **next question**)."
- 2019 Practical: "It always puts **serious doubts in the mind of the examiner** when theory knowledge is
  lacking." 2024 Practical: at the borderline, "**hard to feel confident** … when they make obvious theory
  mistakes."

Distinct-report support: Practical 2017, 2018, 2019, 2024, 2025 + Theory 2017, 2019, 2023, 2024, 2025 + 2021
Practical → **≥10**. Stated for **both** theory and practical → a **general examiner-cognition principle**, not
a format quirk.

**The asymmetry between a survivable miss and a bankrupting error.** The law has a hard structure that the naïve
"reasoning > correctness" reading misses:

| Error type | Effect on the trust account | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| A wrong **origin/variety** on top of a *sound structural read*, plausibly argued, glass-anchored, adjacent | **Survivable** — keeps 5–6/8; account stays in credit | STRONG (5 reports) |
| A wrong **structural read** (alcohol/acidity/tannin/RS) | **Cascades fatally** — detonates the seed of the whole answer; quality/style/commercial all inherit the wrong premise | STRONG (Agent 4 §3 cond. 1) |
| A **theory howler / factual impossibility** | **Bankrupts** — contaminates the whole script; decisive at the borderline | STRONG (≥10) |
| An **internal contradiction** (structure can't coexist with the named wine, e.g. "a VDN at 20%") | **Self-defeating** — illogical on its face, earns no points even before the wine is known | STRONG (3+) |

> The funnel **rescues a wrong guess; it cannot rescue a howler.** Correctness of the *ID* is negotiable;
> correctness of the *facts and structure* is the gate. A flawless route to an impossible neighbourhood
> re-asserts implausibility at the end of the chain.

## 4 · Confidence ≠ Correctness — the 2×2

The project's crux, with documented exemplars in each cell. Rows = the *trust the examiner extends*; columns =
*whether the ID is right*.

|                          | **ID CORRECT**                                                                                                                                                              | **ID WRONG**                                                                                                                                                                       |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **CONFIDENCE HIGH** (account in credit) | **Right *and* trusted** — the ideal. Correct ID *with* a visible glass-grounded argument and calibrated quality. 2024 P1: Pinot Grigio "with arguments … that made the choice **more plausible**" (B9). The bare correct label is *not* here — it needs the route. | **Wrong but trusted** — the signature finding. 2025 P1Q2: zero origins right, "**5 or 6 marks … if reasoning was sound and conclusion plausible**" (B11). 2025 P2: Bordeaux-for-Tuscany, "**convincing arguments**" (B12). 2019 P3Q3: "**taste like a detective; argue like a lawyer**" (D14). **STRONG (5 reports).** |
| **CONFIDENCE LOW** (account empty / overdrawn) | **Right but doubted** — the cleanest confidence≠correctness proof. 2024 P1: *same* Pinot Grigio "went straight … **without proper arguments and lost many marks**" (E12). 2018 Chilean Cab: correct origin, quality "**not sufficiently effusive**." 2023 Grenache: variety reached, "**little demonstration of knowledge**." 2021 Saint-Julien: correct region, **neglected the quality half** → failed. | **Wrong and distrusted** — the failure mode. Shoehorned / glass-detached / internally contradictory / howler-bearing. 2025 P2 shoehorning "led to the **failure of many candidates**"; "a VDN at 20% **cannot earn points and is illogical**" (2022). **STRONG.** |

**Reading the 2×2.** The two diagonals lay people expect (right→trusted, wrong→distrusted) are *both present but
not dominant*. The two off-diagonals — **wrong-but-trusted** and **right-but-doubted** — are exactly where the
examiner's *confidence* judgement, distinct from correctness, lives, and both are documented in multiple
reports. Confidence is decoupled from correctness **in both directions**: the examiner is grading the
trustworthiness of the cognitive process, with the conclusion as cheap-to-check corroboration.

## 5 · Implications for the study system (criteria for the prompt audit)

A grader, feedback engine, or model-answer generator that aims to model **examiner confidence** — not merely
technical correctness — must demonstrably do the following. These are framed as **test questions** the prompt
auditor can score the live prompts against (the detailed audit is another worker's task; this is the spec).

**Credit the reasoning, not the label:**
1. **Does the grader credit a wrong-but-well-reasoned answer?** A wrong ID with a sound structural read, a
   terminated funnel, glass-anchoring, and a stylistically-adjacent endpoint must score in the 5–6/8 band — not
   be zeroed for the wrong label. *(Tests §2.10 / cell wrong-but-trusted.)*
2. **Does it *penalise* a correct ID with absent reasoning?** A bare correct label with no visible derivation
   must score *below* a well-argued wrong call. *(Tests §2.11 / cell right-but-doubted — the Pinot Grigio
   parity test.)*

**Model the contamination law:**
3. **Does a theory howler / factual impossibility contaminate the surrounding answer**, and at the borderline
   withhold the benefit of the doubt — rather than docking only the local sub-mark? *(Tests §3.)*
4. **Does it distinguish a *survivable wrong origin* from a *fatal structural misread*?** A wrong-origin/
   sound-structure answer must survive; a wrong-structure answer must cascade. *(Tests §3 asymmetry table.)*
5. **Does it catch internal contradiction on the answer's own terms** (structure that can't coexist with the
   named wine) independent of knowing the wine? *(Tests P2 / "VDN at 20%".)*

**Operationalise plausibility (currently judged "by vibes"):**
6. **Does it apply the stylistic-adjacency gradient (P8)?** Wrong-but-adjacent earns credit; wrong-and-distant
   earns little; **on a banker, latitude shrinks to correct/near-correct.** This requires a per-wine plausible-
   neighbourhood map (`plausibility_grading_gap_analysis.md`), which the prose grader is *told about but not
   given*. *(Tests Agent 4 P8 + the curveball-vs-banker latitude conditional.)*
7. **Does it reward the terminated funnel and penalise the un-terminated one** ("make a choice")? *(Tests P3/P5.)*

**Reward sophistication without rewarding volume:**
8. **Does it reward a second-order / under-the-skin inference** (causal link, producer/site inference, conflict
   resolution) as the pass→distinction lift — and *not* reward completeness-for-its-own-sake? An
   "overabundance of examples" must not out-score a selective, causal answer. *(Tests Agent 5 S1/S3/S4/§3.)*
9. **Does it penalise cut-and-paste / undifferentiated answers across wines** as a doubt signal, not treat each
   wine in isolation? *(Tests §2.3.)*

**Honour the structure of the verdict:**
10. **Does it anchor verdicts to the *minimum faculty* and the aggregate-65%-with-floor standard** — not a
    per-paper 65%, and not the average of faculties (a spike must not rescue a hole)? *(Tests
    `examiner_confidence_model.md` §1.2 / §7 row 1.)*

A grader that passes 1–10 is modelling examiner *confidence*; one that only checks the ID against a key and sums
sub-marks is modelling *correctness* and will systematically misgrade both off-diagonal cells of the §4 2×2.
