# The Sophistication Framework — What Makes MW Reasoning *Appear* Sophisticated

> **Project 9 · Agent 5 (Sophistication Analyst).** This study is about **examiner cognition**, not
> candidate behaviour or exam composition. The central question of the project is what makes an examiner
> perceive a candidate's reasoning as *sophisticated* — the quality that, at the top end, tips
> **examiner confidence** (distinct from correctness) into distinction-level trust. This artifact is my
> slice: **what produces the perception of sophistication.** The corpus shows "insight / not enough
> insight" and "getting under the skin of the wine" as the recurring pass-vs-distinction line; the job
> here is to explain the *mechanics* of that perception.
>
> **Scope.** Examiner *perception* of sophistication, held separate from whether the candidate is
> correct. A wrong-but-sophisticated answer and a right-but-pedestrian answer are both documented in the
> corpus; this framework is about the former's quality, not the latter's accuracy.
>
> **Method.** Built on Agent 1's `confidence_language_corpus.md` (Family C — Insight / Sophistication /
> Under-the-skin, plus Family D — Logic/Reasoning), cross-read against `distinction_candidate_analysis.md`
> and `examiner_confidence_model.md`. Every component below was then re-verified by direct read of the
> source `.txt` reports in `docs/examiners reports/extracted_txt/` to capture full surrounding context
> and exact wording. Quotes are verbatim, including OCR artifacts in the 2025 PDFs (flagged `[OCR]`).
>
> **Evidence tiers** (honouring the project's multi-report rule): **STRONG SIGNAL** = 3+ distinct
> reports; **PLAUSIBLE** = 2 distinct reports; **EXAMPLE-ONLY** = 1 report (an anecdote stays an
> anecdote). Each component is also tagged **[PRACTICAL]**, **[THEORY]**, or **[MIXED]** because this
> project's target is the *practical* exam and most of the explicit "insight/sophisticated" lexicon is
> theory-weighted — a caveat that recurs throughout.
>
> **Provenance caveat (inherited).** IMW examiner reports are member-gated, not public; the verbatim
> quotes are credible because the user holds the reports, but year/paper attributions are corpus claims,
> not web-checkable facts.

---

## 1 · Header — the one-sentence thesis

> **Sophistication, to an MW examiner, is not how much a candidate knows or how completely they cover the
> question — it is whether the reasoning shows a *second move*: getting from the evidence to a causal,
> contextual, or producer-level inference that a merely competent taster would not have made, expressed
> selectively and decisively.** "There was obvious knowledge, but not enough insight" (2023 Theory) is
> the corpus's purest statement of the gap: knowledge is table stakes; the *insight layer on top of it*
> is what reads as sophisticated and earns distinction-level confidence.

The reports never define "insight." They define it *ostensively* — by repeatedly contrasting it with its
absence ("regurgitation," "book learning," "listing examples," "brain dumps," "surface-level," "rote
responses," "the bare requirements"). The framework below reconstructs the positive content from those
contrasts plus the handful of named exemplars.

---

## 2 · The Sophistication Framework — six named components

Derived as candidate components and tested against the evidence. They are ordered by how directly they
bear on the **practical** exam (the project target) and by evidence strength.

---

### Component S1 — Second-order / causal reasoning: connect winemaking-and-climate *to the glass*, don't catalogue
**Tier: STRONG SIGNAL · [MIXED — strong in both Practical and Theory]**

The single most consistent mechanic. Sophistication is perceived when the candidate doesn't just *name*
a feature but *links* it causally to a tasted attribute or chains style/quality back to its cause
(climate + winemaking). The examiner reads the chain, not the list.

- **[2024 Practical]** "The best answers logically described the style and quality and then tied these to
  how climate and winemaking would have played a part… Too many concentrated on just one thing."
- **[2019 Practical, Paper 3]** "the candidates who performed well demonstrated both a strong technical
  knowledge and a writing style that **convincingly linked what they know about production with how the
  wine tastes in the glass**." (Family B26)
- **[2022 Practical]** examiners look for winemaking "not only the type and size of oak used for
  maturation, but how long in oak and the percent of new oak used **with evidence**" — i.e. the
  technique tied to a sensory marker, not asserted in the abstract.
- **[2023 Theory]** "it goes to show again how important it is to **understand and consider the 'why'
  before being able to show knowledge through insight**, backed by global examples to prove the 'what'."
- **[2025 Theory, Paper 2]** "Candidates must move **beyond 'how' to address 'why,'** demonstrating
  understanding of both processes and their contextual applications."

**Mechanic:** insight = a *causal connective* in the prose ("→ because", "which is why", "this points
to"). The pedestrian answer states *what*; the sophisticated answer states *why this evidence implies
that conclusion*. Distinct reports: 2024 Prac, 2019 Prac, 2022 Prac, 2023 Theory, 2025 Theory → **5+**.

---

### Component S2 — "Under the skin": infer the *process and intent* behind the wine, beyond the checklist
**Tier: PLAUSIBLE (Practical) / STRONG when Theory cognates included · [PRACTICAL core, MIXED overall]**

The corpus's signature phrase. Quality answers that merely *complete* the BLIC(CA) checklist are
explicitly described as *not yet* sophisticated; mastery is inferring the producer's choices and intent
that lie *underneath* the descriptors.

- **[2022 Practical, Intro] — the source quote.** "Quality answers generally do refer well to BLIC or
  BLICCA (balance, length, intensity, complexity, concentration, age-worthiness) **but they frequently
  lack confidence and play it safe by not truly getting "under the skin of the wine", which would show
  mastery**." (Family C1)
- **[2025 Practical, Paper 3] — the cognate, practical, named exemplar.** A candidate identified a Tokaji
  Szamorodni and reasoned that "although the sugar level suggests 5 or 6 Puttonyos, the **exceptional
  quality indicates a producer whose wines would exceed the minimum sugar levels** required for those
  classificatio[n]s [OCR]." → "It was an **insightful observation that instilled much confidence in the
  eyes of the examiners**." (Family C4 / A12)
- **[2018 Practical]** "It is more a question of **authoritative insight**, clear communication of ideas
  and a balanced, convincing argument that elevate an answer to MW standard." (Family C8)

The "under the skin" *phrase* is 2 practical reports (2022 + 2025 cognate) → PLAUSIBLE on the practical
side strictly. But its meaning — *infer the producer/process/intent the descriptors only hint at* — is
corroborated by the theory "the why" cluster (S1) and the 2018 "authoritative insight" line, lifting the
underlying idea to STRONG. **This is the heart of the project's pass→distinction line and the operational
definition of "insight" in the practical room.** Note the practical evidence is thinner than the project
brief's framing implies: it rests on *one* recurring phrase plus *one* named exemplar — strong, but
honestly EXAMPLE-anchored at the practical level.

---

### Component S3 — Reconcile conflicting evidence with a single inference (the "distinction move")
**Tier: PLAUSIBLE→STRONG · [PRACTICAL named exemplar + Theory cognates]**

The most concrete picture of distinction reasoning anywhere in the corpus. Sophistication is perceived
most sharply when two pieces of evidence *disagree* and the candidate resolves the tension with one
higher-order inference rather than ignoring one side or hedging.

- **[2025 Practical] — the gold-standard example** (same Tokaji answer as S2): the candidate held *sugar
  level* against *classification* against *quality* and resolved them via a **producer-level** inference.
  The examiners' reaction — "instilled much confidence" — is the perception this whole framework is about.
- **[2023 Theory]** "The most **sophisticated** approaches introduced points of greater insight, such as
  **distinguishing the importance of rarity and perceived versus actual ability to age**…" (Family C19) —
  resolving a tension (perceived vs actual) is named as what the *sophisticated* minority did.
- **[2024 Theory]** "The **sophisticated minority** broadened the question beyond 'vineyard vs. winery'
  arguments, to argue that a central component of greatness is market perception…" (Family C24) —
  reframing a binary the rest of the cohort took at face value.

**Mechanic:** the examiner perceives sophistication when the candidate *names a tension and dissolves it*
("the X suggests A, but the Y indicates B, therefore C"). The cheaper answer picks one cue and writes to
it (shoehorning — see §3). This is **EK-0105** in `distinction_candidate_analysis.md`. Practical: 1 named
exemplar (EXAMPLE-ONLY strictly); + 2 theory reports using "sophisticated" for the same move → PLAUSIBLE,
trending STRONG, but the *practical* leg is a single anecdote.

---

### Component S4 — Selective, economical argument: insight is *choosing*, not *listing*
**Tier: STRONG SIGNAL · [MIXED]**

Sophistication is perceived as *discrimination among evidence*, not accumulation of it. This is the
mechanic that most directly separates "sophisticated" from "complete" (developed fully in §3). The reader
infers a sophisticated mind from *what was left out and why the few chosen points are the decisive ones.*

- **[2025 Practical]** the best candidates were praised for "**structuring their answers carefully and
  being selective in their arguments**." (cited in distinction analysis §1.6)
- **[2018 Practical/report]** "Some candidates spent **too much time on florid descriptions** of wines at
  the expense of making a **persuasive argument**." (Family B24) — and the load-bearing line: "an
  **overabundance of examples can sometimes mask a lack of fundamental understanding**" (2018 report).
- **[2019 Theory]** "a few good, relevant examples from different markets and a **clarity of analytical
  thought go a long way**." / condemned were answers "too long and rambling" while praising "analysis and
  insight in a **well-structured and focused** answer." (Family D23)
- **[2017 Practical]** the funnel to "two or three options" rather than an open list (Family A20).

**Mechanic:** the examiner reads *weighing* (this matters more than that, here is why) as a higher
cognitive act than *cataloguing* (here is everything I know). Selectivity is itself a sophistication
signal because it demonstrates the candidate can *rank* evidence — the thing a generalist authority does.
Distinct reports: 2025 Prac, 2018 Prac, 2019 Theory, 2017 Prac → **4 → STRONG**.

---

### Component S5 — Original, theory-informed contextual reasoning instead of rote templates
**Tier: STRONG SIGNAL · [MIXED — explicit in both Practical commercial and Theory essays]**

Sophistication is perceived as *bespoke thinking applied to this wine/question*, and its opposite —
rote/templated/"book-learning" answers — is repeatedly named as the thing that reads as *un*sophisticated.

- **[2024 Practical, commercial]** candidates should "be able to provide **insightful answers instead of
  the typical, rote responses** of selling the wine in fine-dining restaurant, by wine-by-the glass, or
  to affluent connoisseurs." (Family C13)
- **[2023 Practical]** the best "offer **original responses** to commercial potential questions by using
  their theory knowledge instead of offering rote responses."
- **[2025 Theory]** "this knowledge too often comes across more as **book learning and lacks analysis and
  insight** from the candidate. What will give success… is a candidate who can **pull together all their
  textbook knowledge, link it with real-life practical situations, see the bigger picture**…" (Family C34)
- **[2023 Theory]** "For MW-level essays, it is **not enough simply to report examples. We are looking for
  original thought and insight**." (Family C16); "**obvious knowledge, but not enough insight**" (C15).
- **[2024 Chief] — the meta-warning.** "an **over-reliance on the… study programme is eroding students'
  ability to think for themselves**… Those who simply assimilate information… and repeat this in the exam
  room **will not pass**."

**Mechanic:** the examiner perceives sophistication as evidence the candidate *reasoned freshly* rather
than *retrieved a memorised block*. Templated/cut-paste prose actively destroys the perception ("creates
considerable doubt in the mind of the reader," 2024 Practical). Distinct reports: 2024 Prac, 2023 Prac,
2025 Theory, 2023 Theory, 2024 Chief → **5 → STRONG**. *Design tension noted in EK-0108:* this is in
direct tension with template-driven study tools; the trees must be scaffolding for fresh reasoning, not
scripts to recite.

---

### Component S6 — Calibrated, decisive judgement: nuance held *and resolved*, not hedging
**Tier: STRONG SIGNAL (Theory) / PLAUSIBLE (Practical) · [MIXED, theory-weighted]**

Sophistication is *not* the same as fence-sitting. The corpus is explicit that showing nuance must be
paired with *coming down decisively*; nuance without resolution reads as weakness, and false certainty
reads as crude. The sophisticated answer *acknowledges complexity and then takes a position.*

- **[2019 Theory] — the explicit top-band definition.** "whilst you should come down on one side or the
  other if you are asked to, it is often appropriate to **show the complexity and nuance** of certain
  issues. In other words, **we are looking for sophisticated thinking at MW level**. You need to
  demonstrate your skills of **analysis, evaluation and argument**." (Family C29/C30) — this sits in the
  55–64 "below threshold" discussion: it is *literally* the pass→distinction descriptor.
- **[2023 Theory, minerality]** the best answers "had the confidence not just to discuss whether the term
  was misused… but also to **express definite opinions**." (Family A37) — nuance *plus* a verdict.
- **[2025 Theory]** the best "demonstrate a capacity to **challenge assumptions and present a nuanced,
  balanced argument**" and "**think beyond headlines**." (Family C36/C37)
- **[Practical cognate]** decisiveness-with-reasoning is the practical's whole "funnel then **make a
  choice**" doctrine (2021 Practical, Family D9) — the practical expresses S6 as *commit after weighing*,
  not as essay nuance.

**Mechanic:** the perception of sophistication requires *both* poles — seeing the complexity (so you're
not naïve) *and* resolving it (so you're not evasive). Hedging that never lands reads as a *lack* of
sophistication and of confidence (Family A20: narrowing to two-three avoids "showing a lack of
confidence"). Distinct reports for the explicit "nuance held and resolved": 2019 Theory, 2023 Theory,
2025 Theory → **3 → STRONG (Theory)**; the practical cognate (commit-after-funnel) is its own STRONG
signal but is framed as decisiveness rather than "sophistication" per se.

---

### Components considered and *down-weighted* (honest negative space)

- **Quality across the full scale / origin-blind / within-class discrimination** is a real distinction
  marker (distinction analysis §1.2; EK-0109) but the reports frame it as *calibration*, not
  *sophistication* — so it is a confidence signal adjacent to this slice, not a sophistication mechanic.
  Where it overlaps is S2 (under-the-skin quality) and S3 (the producer-exceeds-minimum inference).
- **"Enthusiasm / genuine pleasure"** (2018, 2025 Practical) builds confidence but is a *communication/
  voice* signal, not a reasoning-sophistication signal; excluded from the core six.
- **Professional register / no slang / correct spelling** is a confidence signal (Family A) but its
  absence destroys *credibility*, not the *perception of sophisticated reasoning* — kept out of scope.

## 3 · Sophistication vs. mere completeness — the recurring examiner point

This is the spine of the project's question, and the corpus states it more bluntly than any other point
in this family: **listing more is not sophistication; insight is selective and causal.** The examiners
repeatedly *penalise* completeness-without-insight and *reward* economy-with-insight.

**Direct evidence that MORE ≠ sophisticated:**

- **[2018 report] — the sharpest line in the corpus.** "the MW exam does require a certain precision and
  insight… examiners are also looking for evidence of **analytical thought. Very often, simply repeating
  processes or procedures will not suffice. Equally an overabundance of examples can sometimes mask a lack
  of fundamental understanding.**" → *Volume can actively signal the absence of understanding.*
- **[2023 Theory]** "**There was obvious knowledge, but not enough insight.**" / "it is **not enough simply
  to report examples**." → *Knowledge present, sophistication absent — the two are orthogonal.*
- **[2023 Theory]** the discriminator question was whether candidates "could **delve deep**… and those who
  could only give a very top level, **basic regurgitation of a viticulture textbook without much insight**."
- **[2019 Theory]** "**Just knowing the facts is not enough.**" / praised "a few good, relevant examples…
  and a clarity of analytical thought go a long way"; condemned "**brain dumps and winemaking lists**."
- **[2025 Theory]** "Often essays were **surface-level, lacking critical thinking or industry insight**" —
  *surface-level* explicitly = covered-but-shallow.
- **[Practical]** the same logic: BLICCA *fully completed* still "**lacks… getting under the skin**"
  (2022); "**florid descriptions… at the expense of making a persuasive argument**" (2018); the best are
  "**selective in their arguments**" (2025).

**The mechanic of the distinction:** completeness is *horizontal* (cover every point); sophistication is
*vertical* (go a layer deeper on the points that matter). An examiner reads horizontal coverage as
diligence/competence (necessary for a pass) and vertical depth as insight (necessary for distinction). A
"word-dump" that ticks every box can therefore *cap* a candidate at competent precisely because the
breadth came at the cost of the depth that signals a generalist authority's judgement. **Evidence:
STRONG SIGNAL — 2018, 2023 Theory, 2019 Theory, 2025 Theory, 2022 Practical, 2018 Practical (≥6 distinct
reports).** This is the best-supported claim in the whole framework, though weighted toward Theory; the
practical instances (2022 BLICCA, 2018 florid-description) are fewer but unambiguous.

> **Caveat against over-reading.** "Selective" never means *incomplete*. Completeness is a *floor* (blank
> or thin sections sink C+ papers — distinction analysis §1.6); sophistication is what you build *on top*
> of a complete answer with the marginal words. The examiners want **complete AND deep**, and where time
> forces a trade, they reward *depth on the high-mark parts* over *breadth everywhere* (2024 Practical:
> triage to the parts worth a third of the marks and answer them "thoughtfully and comprehensively").

## 4 · The pass → distinction boundary — what the top band has that the middle band lacks

Synthesising the corpus, the boundary is stated four ways that all reduce to the same thing:

| The middle band (competent / pass) does… | The top band (sophisticated / distinction) adds… | Source signal |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstrates **knowledge** — correct facts, complete coverage, BLICCA ticked | Demonstrates **insight** — a causal/contextual inference *on top of* the knowledge | "obvious knowledge, but not enough insight" (2023 Theory, C15) |
| **Reports / lists** examples, processes, descriptors | **Weighs and connects** them — selects the decisive few, links cause to effect | "not enough simply to report examples… original thought and insight" (2023 Theory, C16); "convincingly linked production with how the wine tastes" (2019 Prac, B26) |
| Stays at the **surface** — describes *what* | Goes **under the skin** — infers *why / who / how* (producer intent, process) | "getting under the skin of the wine, which would show mastery" (2022 Prac, C1); Tokaji exemplar (2025 Prac, C4) |
| **Hedges** or asserts flatly | **Acknowledges nuance and resolves it** decisively | "show the complexity and nuance… sophisticated thinking at MW level" (2019 Theory, C29) |
| Recites **prepared / rote** material | **Thinks freshly** for this wine/question | "rote responses" vs "insightful answers" (2024 Prac, C13); "think for themselves" (2024 Chief) |

**The clearest single articulation (use this as the project's headline):**

> **The middle band shows the examiner *what it knows*; the top band shows the examiner *how it thinks*.**
> Concretely: a distinction answer takes a complete, accurate description and adds **one second-order move**
> — a causal link from process to glass, a producer- or site-level inference, or the resolution of a
> conflict in the evidence — expressed **selectively and decisively**. The named, examiner-praised exemplar
> (2025 Practical Tokaji) is exactly this: full marks were not earned by listing more, but by the single
> inference that an *exceptional-quality* wine implies a *producer who exceeds the minimum* — and the
> examiners' own word for their reaction was **"confidence."** That is the pass→distinction boundary made
> visible: insight is the move that converts correctness into *trust*.

**Practical vs theory weighting of this boundary (project-critical honesty):** the *explicit* lexicon of
"sophisticated / insight / not enough insight" is **theory-weighted** (2017/2019/2023/2024/2025 Theory
carry the bulk). On the **practical** side — this project's target — the boundary is carried by **two
phrases plus one named exemplar**: "under the skin of the wine" (2022), "selective in their arguments"
(2025), and the Tokaji answer (2025). The *idea* is corroborated practical-side by the causal-linking
praise (2019, 2024 Practical) and the rote-vs-insight commercial language (2023, 2024 Practical). So:
**the mechanic is robustly evidenced overall (STRONG), but the practical-specific direct evidence is
thinner (PLAUSIBLE/EXAMPLE-anchored) and leans on theory transfer.** A downstream grader should treat
the framework as well-founded but flag the practical leg as the place most in need of more corpus
evidence (see §9-style open question below).

## 5 · Checklist — making reasoning read as sophisticated under 8-minute pressure

For the practical room, where time forbids the leisurely essay nuance the theory reports reward. Each item
is a *perception lever* — a concrete writing move that produces the examiner's sophistication judgement.

**The one rule above all:** *after you've described it accurately and completely, add one second-order
sentence — and cut a descriptor to make room for it.*

1. **Lead with structure, then connect it causally.** Don't list alcohol/acidity/tannin/RS and stop —
   add the "which is why / this points to" clause that links structure to a winemaking or climate cause.
   (S1) *"High acid + low alcohol + no oak → cool site, reductive handling, made for freshness, not
   ageing."*
2. **Go one layer under the descriptor.** For every BLICCA point that earns the pass, add one inference
   about *producer intent or process* that earns the distinction. (S2) *Not "complex and concentrated" but
   "this concentration implies low yields / old vines — a producer chasing quality over volume."*
3. **When two cues conflict, say so and resolve it in one sentence.** Name the tension, make the
   higher-order call. (S3 — the Tokaji move.) *"The sugar reads like a 5-Putt level, but the quality says
   a top producer exceeding the minimum — so I'd place it above its apparent classification."*
4. **Be selective, not exhaustive.** Argue the 2–3 *decisive* points hard; don't catalogue everything you
   notice. Volume reads as diligence; selection reads as judgement. (S4) Cut florid description to fund
   one sharp inference.
5. **Make it bespoke to *this* glass.** No copy-paste structures across wines; reference what is uniquely
   true of *this* wine. Rote phrasing destroys the perception instantly. (S5)
6. **Hold nuance, then commit.** Acknowledge the alternative ("could be X, but…"), then come down
   decisively. Hedging that never lands reads as a lack of both confidence and sophistication; funnel,
   then *make a choice*. (S6)
7. **Tie quality and commercial to original, theory-informed reasoning, not channels.** Avoid
   "fine-dining / by-the-glass / affluent connoisseurs" boilerplate; place the wine using a real,
   wine-specific market judgement. (S5)
8. **Spend the marginal word on depth where the marks are.** Under time pressure, triage: a complete-but-
   shallow answer beats a blank section, but on the high-tariff parts, *go vertical*. (§3 caveat / 2024
   Practical triage.)

**Anti-patterns that read as *un*sophisticated** (the inverse, all corpus-named): brain-dumping / listing
("overabundance of examples… mask a lack of understanding"); florid description in place of argument;
rote/templated channels; shoehorning (deciding identity then forcing evidence to fit); hedging without
resolution; describing *what* with no *why*; surface-level coverage of everything.

| Component | Tier | Distinct reports | Practical leg | Theory leg |

| S2 "Under the skin" / infer process+intent | PLAUSIBLE (Prac) / STRONG (idea) | 3 (phrase 2 Prac) | 2022 Prac + 2025 exemplar | 2018, "the why" cluster |
| S3 Reconcile conflicting evidence | PLAUSIBLE→STRONG | 3 | 2025 exemplar (EXAMPLE-ONLY) | 2023, 2024 Theory |
| S4 Selective / economical argument | **STRONG** | 4 | 2025, 2018 Prac | 2019 Theory |
| S5 Original / non-rote, theory-informed | **STRONG** | 5 | 2024, 2023 Prac | 2025, 2023 Theory + 2024 Chief |
| S6 Nuance held *and resolved* | STRONG (Theory) / PLAUSIBLE (Prac) | 3 | funnel-then-commit cognate | 2019, 2023, 2025 Theory |
| §3 More ≠ sophistication | **STRONG** | 6 | 2022, 2018 Prac | 2018, 2023, 2019, 2025 Theory |

**Overall practical/theory balance:** the *framework as a whole* is well-supported, but **roughly 60–65%
of the explicit "insight/sophisticated/nuance" lexicon is Theory-sourced.** The practical evidence is real
but concentrated in a few high-value items: the 2022 "under the skin" intro, the 2025 Tokaji exemplar, the
2025 "selective in their arguments" praise, and the 2019/2024 causal-linking praise. The causal-linking
(S1), selectivity (S4), and rote-vs-original (S5) components have genuine multi-report practical backing;
the "under the skin" (S2) and conflict-resolution (S3) components — though they are the most *vivid* and
the ones the project brief foregrounds — rest practical-side on one recurring phrase and one named
candidate. **Stated plainly: the project's central "under the skin" claim is directionally STRONG but
practical-specifically EXAMPLE-anchored, and should be cited as such.**
