# Confidence-Building Behaviors — What Makes an MW Examiner *Trust* a Candidate

> **Project 9 · Agent 2 — Distinction Signal Analyst.**
>
> **Scope.** This is a study of **examiner cognition**, not exam composition or candidate technique for
> its own sake. The object is the latent judgement an examiner forms while reading a written trace —
> *"does this candidate understand the wine, or are they guessing?"* — and the specific, repeatable
> answer-characteristics that **build** that confidence. The companion negative pole (what *destroys*
> confidence) is catalogued in `examiner_confidence_model.md` §3 and is referenced here only where a
> building behavior is best defined as the inverse of a destroyer.
>
> **Method.** Primary input is `outputs/research/confidence_language_corpus.md` (Agent 1) — the
> citation-grounded database of every confidence-/trust-/plausibility-/insight-/reasoning-related
> phrase in the IMW Practical, Chief, and Theory reports 2017–2025. Each candidate behavior below was
> built by (a) isolating the **PRAISE** and **NEUTRAL-CRITERION** rows that describe a thing a candidate
> *did* to earn examiner belief, (b) counting **distinct (year × report-type) reports** that support it
> (the project's multi-report rule), and (c) cross-checking the full passage in
> `docs/examiners reports/extracted_txt/` where a quote is load-bearing. Corroborating prior research:
> `examiner_confidence_model.md`, `distinction_candidate_analysis.md`, `examiner_objectives.md`,
> `evidence_audit.md`.
>
> **Evidence tiers (project standard).**
> - **STRONG SIGNAL** — supported by **3+ distinct reports**.
> - **PLAUSIBLE** — supported by **2 distinct reports**.
> - **EXAMPLE-ONLY** — **1 report / single anecdote**. Not elevated to doctrine.
>
> **Provenance caveat (inherited).** IMW examiner reports are member-gated, not web-public. The verbatim
> quotes are credible because the user holds the reports, but year/paper attributions are corpus claims,
> not independently web-verifiable facts (per `examiner_confidence_model.md` §7).
>
> **A note on polarity skew.** The corpus is ~33 PRAISE vs ~55 DOUBT vs ~65 NEUTRAL-CRITERION quotes
> (corpus §2). Examiner reports are written to *diagnose failure* and *state the standard*, so
> confidence language appears far more often as a thing candidates **lost** than as praise of what they
> did right. Several behaviors below are therefore strongest-evidenced in their **inverse** (the named
> confidence-destroyer); where that is the case it is flagged, because the examiner reaction — trust
> gained vs trust withheld — is symmetric on the same axis.

## 1 · Ranked catalogue of confidence-building behaviors

Ranked by **strength of evidence** (tier, then distinct-report count, then directness of the examiner's
positive reaction). Each entry: **what it looks like in an answer → verbatim support (with citation) →
distinct-report support count → tier.**

---

### CB-1 · Show the deductive funnel — make your reasoning visible, then commit
**STRONG SIGNAL · ≥7 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Read the hard structural evidence first; lay 2–3 genuinely plausible options on
the table with evidence for/against each; commit to the broad anchor early; narrow to a decisive call.
The candidate writes the *argument*, not just the name — so the examiner can watch the logic and award
marks even when the conclusion is wrong.

**Verbatim support.**
- "using deductive reasoning (**funneling**) to consider a few options. This allows the examiner to **see
  your logic** and award marks even if your conclusion is incorrect." (2022 Practical, Intro — corpus D2)
- "the **argument is as important as the conclusion** and is the key to passing identification questions."
  (2022 Practical, Paper 1 — D3)
- "**A wrong answer yields more marks than an answer that is unfinished, so whatever you do: Make a
  choice.**" (2021 Practical, advice — D9)
- "This was a classic wine to **funnel and demonstrate reasoning** and tasting ability and the few that
  did this scored well." (2021 Practical, Paper 1 Q4, Pinot Gris — D11)
- "argued **logically** even if their conclusion was wrong instead of trying to shoehorn their answer."
  (2023 Practical, Intro — D6)
- "many still managed 5 or 6 marks out of the allotted 8 if their **reasoning was sound and their
  conclusion plausible**." (2025 Practical, Paper 1 Q2 — B11)
- "taste like a detective; **argue like a lawyer**." (2019 Practical, Paper 3 Q3 — D14)
- "A candidate who reads the wine correctly and **makes a logical deduction** about its origin or variety
  can still achieve a pass." (2025 Practical, Intro — D8)

**Support count.** 2022, 2021, 2023, 2025, 2019, 2018, 2024 Practical → **≥7 distinct reports** (corpus
recurrence note #2). The single most prescriptively repeated confidence-building behavior in the corpus.

### CB-2 · Write with conviction and authority — the *register* of a Master of Wine
**STRONG SIGNAL · ≥7 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Decisive, authoritative prose addressed MW-to-MW. The candidate commits to a
position and defends it; the writing reads as a working professional's opinion, not a hedging student's.
This is a *tone* that examiners explicitly name as the thing they are looking for.

**Verbatim support.**
- "We are **looking for confidence and authority**, for answers written **convincingly and with
  consistency**." (2018 Practical, Intro — A16)
- "your scripts … showed the necessary accuracy and authority to **convince us** of your excellent
  tasting." (2017 Practical, Intro — B20)
- "justify your answers **convincingly and with confidence** to demonstrate you are a Master of Wine."
  (2023 Practical, Intro — A6/B8)
- "When they recognize a wine, they demonstrate **confidence** by efficiently using relevant and correct
  evidence to prove their point." (2023 Practical, Intro, re borderline passes — A7)
- "They gave us well-informed and articulate answers **written with confidence and conviction**." (2022
  Chief — A23)
- "really excellent papers written with **authority, clarity and conviction**." (2019 Theory, Intro —
  A34/B33); "well-prepared candidates writing with **authority and conviction** … achieved some excellent
  marks." (2017 Theory, Intro — A30/B28)

**Support count.** 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023 Practical + 2022 Chief + 2017, 2019 Theory → **≥7
distinct reports** (corpus recurrence note #4). **Calibration boundary:** authority is rewarded *when
earned*. When genuinely uncertain, the funnel (CB-1) is the right mode; when the candidate genuinely
recognises the wine, a confident commitment is rewarded (see §2 and `examiner_confidence_model.md`
EK-NEW-B). Authority is not bravado — it is conviction backed by correct evidence.

---

### CB-3 · Read the glass faithfully *first* — describe what is there, even when you can't ID it
**STRONG SIGNAL · ≥4 distinct reports (behavior), ≥9 reports for the inverse destroyer**

**What it looks like.** The candidate reports the actual structure and aromatics — alcohol, acidity,
tannin, RS, oak, development — and lets the identity *float* until the evidence supports it. On an
unidentifiable wine they bank marks by describing the glass honestly rather than forcing it into a
familiar box. This is the behavioral inverse of **shoehorning** (the corpus's most-named destroyer).

**Verbatim support.**
- "a lot of points could have been gained from simply **describing what was in the glass** even if the
  region was unknown." (2024 Practical, Paper 3 Q2, Jura — D18)
- "Even if candidates don't know the wine, they should **trust their palates**." / "Many invented
  attributes based on what they had decided the wine was, **rather than simply describing what was in the
  glass**." (2021 Practical, Paper 2 Q4 — D12)
- "we saw some successful answers that managed not to mention any origin…" (i.e. communicated quality
  from the glass alone) (2025 Practical, Paper 2 — A11)
- Inverse (shoehorning, the destroyer that defines the behavior): "Often a candidate will **latch onto a
  single feature** … and then justify that initial assumption by **forcing the wine's components or
  structure to fit it. We call this shoehorning**…" (2025 Practical, Intro — D19); also 2024 (D18, E11),
  2023 (D6), 2022 first-impression (E1), 2019 (E9).

**Support count.** Glass-first *as a rewarded behavior*: 2024, 2021, 2025 Practical → 3 reports
(PLAUSIBLE→STRONG borderline). As the **inverse of the named destroyer** it is corroborated across 2019,
2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 Practical (≥6 reports for the behavior, corpus recurrence note #3) — so the
*axis* (glass-first builds trust / shoehorning destroys it) is **STRONG SIGNAL**.

### CB-4 · Get the structural reading right (alcohol, acidity, tannin, RS)
**STRONG SIGNAL · 3+ distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Accurate, decisive calls on the measurable structure of the wine — the least
bluffable, most diagnostic evidence. Examiners trust structure over the flavour profile, and a correct
structural read is the foundation every downstream judgement (ID, quality, maturity, commercial) is
built on.

**Verbatim support.**
- "they could not reliably identify the wine's components — alcohol, acidity, tannin and residual sugar."
  (named the #1 failure theme — the inverse) (2025 Practical, Intro — E5)
- "the acidity across the wines was too high for this to be **plausible**." (structure used as the
  decisive evidence) (2021 Practical, Paper 1 Q2 — B4)
- "candidates relying too much on their noses … leading them to force … tannin, body, alcohol, acidity,
  or oak use into a mismatched identity." (structure-over-aroma; 2025 Practical, Paper 2 — distinction
  analysis §1.1)
- Cross-corpus: "hard evidence like alcohol and sugar are often more reliable than the flavour profile"
  (2025 Practical, via `examiner_confidence_model.md` §2.3 / EK-0013).

**Support count.** 2021, 2022 (the "VDN at 20%" internal-consistency rule, B-family), 2025 Practical →
3+ reports. The behavior is the **seed** of trust: a correct structural read prevents the within-wine
cascade (`examiner_confidence_model.md` §5A); a wrong one detonates it.

---

### CB-5 · Plausible, well-argued conclusion — even a *wrong* one earns the marks
**STRONG SIGNAL · 5+ distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** The candidate lands on a conclusion that is *defensible for a wine of that
style* and argues it from the glass. Examiners explicitly extend "latitude" to wrong-but-plausible calls
and award good or full marks for them. The same identification can earn marks or lose them purely on
whether the supporting argument made it plausible.

**Verbatim support.**
- "many wrote **convincing answers** to all four wines…" (2017 Practical, Q2, Italy — B15)
- "Many students went for Grenache on W8, and **any plausible origin** could get good marks if well
  argued." (2023 Practical, Paper 2 — B6)
- "some candidates got good pass marks for Pinot Grigio, but with arguments in winemaking and origin that
  made the choice **more plausible**." (2024 Practical, Paper 1 — B9)
- "The best … referred to each of the wines drawing together a **convincing argument** with several
  candidates achieving full marks on this part." (2024 Practical, Paper 2, Pinot Noir — B10)
- "A few candidates managed to earn good points with **convincing arguments** for Cabernet Sauvignon or
  Merlot, placing the third wine in Bordeaux instead of Tuscany." (2025 Practical, Paper 2 — B12)
- "many still managed 5 or 6 marks … if their **reasoning was sound and their conclusion plausible**."
  (2025 Practical, Paper 1 Q2 — B11)

**Support count.** 2017, 2023, 2024, 2025 Practical (and the 2021 "USA→Australia got credit, USA→Italy
did not" plausibility gradient, B5) → **5 distinct reports**. **Boundary (corrects naïve readings):**
latitude is *wine-dependent*. On bankers/paramount classic regions only the correct call gets full ID
marks (distinction analysis §5.3 / EK-0107); and an *implausible* wrong call earns little
(`examiner_objectives.md` A3). Plausibility + sound structure is the gate, not "any wrong answer."

### CB-6 · Cross-reference the wines in a flight — use one to unlock the others
**STRONG SIGNAL · 3 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** The candidate treats the flight as a connected set, using the most expressive
wine as the key, and weaves a single coherent argument across all the wines rather than writing
isolated, repeated notes.

**Verbatim support.**
- "where you have more than one wine to help you, one of them will show enough character to **open the
  door** … which many good students spotted." (2017 Practical — distinction analysis §1.1)
- "The best … referred to **each of the wines** drawing together a **convincing argument** …" (2024
  Practical, Paper 2 — B10)
- "the best candidates **remembered to draw on all four wines**." (2025 Practical — `examiner_confidence_
  model.md` §2.5)

**Support count.** 2017, 2024, 2025 Practical → **3 distinct reports**.

---

### CB-7 · Contextualise quality on a real ladder — name the official tier, calibrate both ways
**STRONG SIGNAL · 3+ distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Quality positioned against the wine's true peers, with the official tier named
(Grand Cru Classé, DOCG, VORS, Prädikat, Reserva) even when unprompted, corroborated by a realistic
price. The candidate discriminates *within* a classification and is willing to be effusive about
greatness and unsentimental about flaws — calibrating in both directions.

**Verbatim support.**
- "we do expect an **official quality level** if there is one and it is relevant" (Beerenauslese,
  Reserva, VORS volunteered unprompted) (2025 Practical — distinction analysis §1.2)
- "Many candidates successfully identified the different levels of quality… using the **full scale of
  quality levels** from Grand Cru to high-volume entry-level wine." (2024 Practical, Paper 1 — distinction
  analysis §1.2)
- "many students stopped at 'Grand Cru Classé quality' although there is a rather large span between the
  top and bottom performers within that group." (discriminate *within* a class) (2023 Practical —
  distinction analysis §1.2)
- Inverse (over-calling destroys trust): "calling a Côtes du Rhône a Châteauneuf-du-Pape, or describing
  the Ruby Port … as a vintage Port, **undermines the examiner's confidence**." (2025 Practical, Intro —
  A10)

**Support count.** 2023, 2024, 2025 Practical (+ 2018 origin-bias, both-directions) → **3+ distinct
reports**. Quality is >20% of marks in 2025 and a top-2 named cause of failure, so calibrated quality
judgement is a high-leverage trust signal.

---

### CB-8 · Tie winemaking to the glass — explain the *how*, treat absence as a choice
**STRONG SIGNAL · 3 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Not a list of techniques but each inferred technique connected to a tasted
attribute, walked chronologically (reception → ferment → maturation → bottling), with the *mechanism*
explained (how the sparkle/RS/colour/fortification was achieved) and the *absence* of MLF/lees treated
as a deliberate decision.

**Verbatim support.**
- "the candidates who performed well demonstrated both a strong technical knowledge and a writing style
  that **convincingly linked** what they know about production with how the wine tastes in the glass."
  (2019 Practical, Paper 3 — B26)
- "the best candidates were thorough in their winemaking observations and **tied these back to what they
  could taste**." (2018 Practical — `examiner_objectives.md` Obj-5)
- "their **absence is as much of a winemaking choice as their presence**." (MLF/lees) (2022 Practical —
  distinction analysis §1.5)

**Support count.** 2018, 2019, 2022 Practical → **3 distinct reports**. Note the production-method
fusion: on P3 fortified/sweet/sparkling, knowing *how* the wine is made is non-negotiable, and a howler
here is the strongest single destroyer (CB inverse → §3 / `examiner_objectives.md` Obj-5).

### CB-9 · Convey genuine, evidenced enthusiasm for great wine
**PLAUSIBLE · 2 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** When the wine is genuinely fine, the candidate's pleasure and engagement come
through the prose — not florid adjectives, but felt, evidenced appreciation. Examiners report this
enthusiasm as something they *share* when reading, and it registers positively.

**Verbatim support.**
- "It's essential that **enthusiasm and a genuine love of great wines comes through** … the best
  candidates clearly enjoyed those wines, despite the pressure." (2018 Practical — distinction analysis
  §1.6)
- "When candidates can convey the **genuine pleasure** they experience in tasting a 40-year-old Tawny,
  that enthusiasm is shared by those reading the paper." (2025 Practical, Paper 3 — verified verbatim,
  `Practical-Examiners-Report-2025.txt` ll.240–241)

**Support count.** 2018, 2025 Practical → **2 distinct reports**. Tier PLAUSIBLE (not 3 reports).
*Caution:* enthusiasm without evidence reads as the florid-description trap ("Some candidates spent too
much time on florid descriptions … at the expense of making a persuasive argument," 2018, B24). The
rewarded version is enthusiasm *anchored to the glass*.

### CB-10 · Second-order insight — get "under the skin of the wine"
**PLAUSIBLE · 2 distinct Practical reports (STRONG if Theory cognate is admitted)**

**What it looks like.** Reasoning a step beyond the obvious — reconciling conflicting evidence with a
producer- or site-level inference; saying something true about the wine that a checklist would miss.
This is the named top-band differentiator and the single clearest picture of *distinction* reasoning in
the corpus.

**Verbatim support.**
- "they frequently lack confidence and play it safe by not truly **getting 'under the skin of the wine'**,
  which would show mastery." (2022 Practical, Intro — C1)
- THE NAMED GOLD-STANDARD EXAMPLE — verified verbatim (2025 Practical, Paper 3): a candidate identified a
  Tokaji Szamorodni and reasoned that *"although the sugar level suggests 5 or 6 Puttonyos, the
  exceptional quality indicates a producer whose wines would exceed the minimum sugar levels required for
  those classifications."* → "It was an **insightful observation that instilled much confidence in the
  eyes of the examiners**." (`Practical-Examiners-Report-2025.txt` ll.242–245; corpus C4/A12)

**Support count.** 2022 + 2025 Practical = **2 distinct Practical reports** → PLAUSIBLE on the Practical
alone. The "insight, not just knowledge" cognate is *heavily* attested in the Theory reports (C15–C19,
C24, C29, C32–C34: "obvious knowledge, but not enough insight," 2023; "sophisticated thinking at MW
level," 2019) — **≥6 distinct reports across Practical+Theory** (corpus recurrence note #5), which would
make the *general* principle STRONG SIGNAL. Held at PLAUSIBLE *for the Practical tasting context*
specifically; STRONG SIGNAL as an exam-wide examiner value. (Matches `examiner_objectives.md` §B2
recommendation to upgrade EK-0094.)

### CB-11 · Compose under pressure with the unknown — narrow, don't hedge; don't panic
**PLAUSIBLE · 2 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Faced with an unfamiliar wine, the candidate stays composed, narrows to two or
three options, and commits — rather than panicking, freezing, or leaving the field wide open. Decisive
narrowing reads as confidence; an unfinished/open answer reads as its lack.

**Verbatim support.**
- "try to **narrow the field to two or three options** to avoid wasting precious time and **showing a
  lack of confidence**." (2019 Practical, advice — A20)
- "when faced with the unknown, **some panicked**." (the inverse) (2025 Practical, Paper 2 — E6); "many
  candidates failed to consider alternative possibilities and **left themselves little room to earn
  marks**." (2025 Practical, Intro — E8)

**Support count.** 2019 + 2025 Practical → **2 distinct reports**. Closely coupled to CB-1 (the funnel
is *how* you narrow) and CB-2 (composure reads as authority).

---

### CB-12 · Thoughtful, original commercial/market reasoning (not rote channels)
**PLAUSIBLE · 2 distinct reports**

**What it looks like.** Commercial placement that is wine-specific and theory-informed — channel,
geography, retail tier, opportunities *and* challenges — tied to the glass, rather than the rote
"steakhouse / by-the-glass / affluent connoisseur" template.

**Verbatim support.**
- "Market position questions were generally answered much more **thoughtfully** this year…" (2022
  Practical, Intro — C2)
- "be able to provide **insightful answers** instead of the typical, rote responses…" (2024 Practical,
  Paper 1 — C13)

**Support count.** 2022 + 2024 Practical → **2 distinct reports**. (Commercial is the *lowest-weighted*
competency, ~9% of marks per 2022 — `examiner_objectives.md` Obj-8 — so this builds trust cheaply but is
not where papers are won.)

---

### CB-13 · Professional, accurate language and correct spelling
**PLAUSIBLE→STRONG borderline · 2–3 distinct reports (named almost entirely via its inverse)**

**What it looks like.** Precise, professional register; correctly spelled appellations, grapes, and
winemaking terms; no slang. The reward is *neutral* (it doesn't lose trust); the corpus evidence is
almost entirely the **penalty** for getting it wrong, which makes the building behavior real but
defensive.

**Verbatim support (inverse-dominant).**
- "qualitative terms such as 'stonking' or 'icon', **do not engender confidence** …" (2018 Practical,
  Intro — A14)
- "If a candidate cannot spell wine regions, winemaking terms etc., it **does not imbue much confidence
  in the examiner**." (2017 Theory — A28)
- "These errors can cost marks and **do not encourage the examiners to have confidence** in a candidate's
  abilities." (misspelt regions/grapes; 2021 Practical — A3); "credibility … would have been **restored**"
  by proof-reading (2017 Theory — A27).

**Support count.** 2017 Theory, 2018 Practical, 2021 Practical → 3 reports (corpus recurrence note #6,
"borderline 3"). Listed lower because it is **cumulative and minor** — it removes friction rather than
building strong positive belief.

## 2 · The confidence-vs-correctness distinction (the project's crux)

The central, repeatedly-stated finding of this corpus is that **examiner confidence tracks the coherence
and glass-grounding of the reasoning, not the correctness of the conclusion.** The examiner holds the
answer key; the bare ID is cheap to check and deliberately lightly weighted. What they read *for* is
whether the candidate's thinking hangs together. Several behaviors above build trust **independent of
getting the wine right**:

- **CB-1 (the funnel)** is the purest example: "This allows the examiner to **see your logic** and award
  marks **even if your conclusion is incorrect**" (2022, D2). The trust is in the *visible process*.
- **CB-5 (plausible wrong call)**: "many still managed 5 or 6 marks … if their **reasoning was sound and
  their conclusion plausible**" (2025, B11) — and the *same* ID (Pinot Grigio, 2024) earned or lost marks
  *purely on the argument* (B9, E12). Correctness held constant; confidence varied with reasoning.
- **CB-2 (authority/conviction)** is a register, not a result — "answers written **convincingly and with
  consistency**" (2018, A16) is praised independent of whether the call is right.
- **CB-3 (describe the glass)** explicitly banks marks *when the wine is never identified at all*:
  "successful answers that managed **not to mention any origin**" (2025, A11); "a lot of points could
  have been gained from simply **describing what was in the glass even if the region was unknown**"
  (2024, D18).

> **The single clearest example of confidence built *without* correctness.** Paper 2, an
> impossible-to-place wine, where the examiner names the *deductive register itself* as the gold
> standard: **"taste like a detective; argue like a lawyer"** (2019 Practical, Paper 3 Q3 — corpus D14).
> The wine could not reliably be identified; the examiner's instruction is to *reason visibly and
> commit*, and that reasoning — not the (unreachable) right answer — is what earns the marks and the
> trust. The structural counterpart in the rules is explicit: **"A wrong answer yields more marks than an
> answer that is unfinished, so whatever you do: Make a choice"** (2021, D9). An unfinished, hedged
> answer — even one that might have been *correct* — scores *below* a committed, well-argued *wrong* one.
> Confidence is in the commitment and the trace, not the conclusion.

**The asymmetry that completes the picture.** Confidence is *slow to build, fast to collapse*
(`examiner_confidence_model.md` §1.3). The behaviors above accumulate trust answer by answer; a single
**theory howler** ("Amontillado at 14.5%," "Tawny aged in a solera," CdR called CdP) can puncture it —
"This **totally undermines the confidence** the examiner might have had in the candidate" (2017, A13);
"**Factual errors undermine confidence in everything a candidate has written**" (2023 Theory, A35;
near-verbatim 2024 Theory, A38). So the confidence-vs-correctness rule has a hard floor: **correctness of
the underlying *facts/structure* matters even when correctness of the *ID* does not.** A wrong *origin*
on a sound structural read is survivable; a wrong *structural read* or a factual impossibility is not
(`examiner_objectives.md` EK-0108). The funnel rescues a wrong guess; it cannot rescue a howler.

## 3 · Synthesis — the behaviors that most reliably make an examiner trust a candidate

The 5–8 behaviors with the strongest, most-replicated link to examiner *belief*:

1. **Funnel and commit (CB-1, ≥7 reports).** Read the structure, weigh 2–3 evidenced options, narrow,
   and *make a choice*. The most-repeated prescription in the corpus and the core mechanism of
   confidence-built-without-correctness. *"See your logic … even if your conclusion is incorrect."*

2. **Write with conviction and authority (CB-2, ≥7 reports).** The named *register* of a passing answer —
   "confidence and authority … convincingly and with consistency." Commit decisively when sure; funnel
   when not (calibration is itself a competence signal).

3. **Describe the glass before deciding the wine (CB-3, ≥6 reports for the axis).** Report the real
   structure/aromatics and let identity float — the exact inverse of shoehorning, the corpus's
   most-named destroyer. Banks marks even on un-IDable wines.

4. **Get the structure right (CB-4, 3+ reports).** Accurate alcohol/acidity/tannin/RS is the least
   bluffable, most-trusted evidence and the seed of the whole answer; misreading it triggers the fatal
   within-wine cascade.

5. **Land a plausible, well-argued conclusion (CB-5, 5 reports).** A defensible, glass-grounded call —
   right or wrong — scores 5–6/8; the *argument* sets the band, not the label. (Boundary: bankers get no
   latitude; implausible calls earn little.)

6. **Calibrate quality on a real ladder, both directions (CB-7, 3+ reports).** Name the official tier
   unprompted, discriminate within a classification, and neither over-call (CdR→CdP) nor under-call
   (New-World penalty, subtle-classic under-rating). High-leverage: quality is >20% of 2025 marks.

7. **Tie winemaking to the glass and explain the *how* (CB-8, 3 reports).** Each technique linked to a
   tasted attribute, chronological, mechanism-explained, absence-as-choice — and, on P3, *factually
   flawless* about how the wine is actually made.

8. **Deliver second-order insight — "under the skin" (CB-10, distinction differentiator).** Reconcile
   conflicting evidence with a producer-/site-level inference (the Tokaji exemplar that *"instilled much
   confidence in the eyes of the examiners"*). PLAUSIBLE in the Practical alone; STRONG as an exam-wide
   examiner value. This is what separates a *pass* from a *distinction*, not a fail from a pass.

**The one-line model.** Examiners trust the candidate who **reads the glass accurately, reasons visibly
from that evidence toward a decisive and plausible call, calibrates quality on the real ladder, ties
every claim back to what's in the glass, and writes it with professional conviction — staying factually
flawless throughout.** Correctness of the *conclusion* is secondary; correctness of the *facts and
structure*, plus the visible coherence of the reasoning, is what builds — and a single impossibility is
what collapses — examiner confidence.
