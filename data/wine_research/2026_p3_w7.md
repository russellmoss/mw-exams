# 20-Year-Old Tawny Port, Kopke, NV

## Quick tasting profile
Delicate orange-green rim over a pale tawny-amber core — visibly paler and more evolved than a Ruby or LBV. Nose is a complex marriage of dried fruit, spice and fine wood (walnut, fig, cinnamon) built by extended oxidative cask ageing rather than fresh fruit. Palate is rich and concentrated with high, well-integrated 20% alcohol, real sweetness, moderate acid lift and a long, velvety, nutty finish — the archetypal "oxidative" half of a Port pair, contrasted against a Ruby/LBV's primary-fruit, "reductive" profile (source: producer technical sheet, PDF below).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Traditional Douro red varieties (field blend; common cultivars include Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz) (source: Sogevinus/Kopke technical sheet, kopke1638.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/KOPKE_20YOTawny.pdf)
- Vineyard: Douro Valley vineyards up to 600m; rows traditionally planted across the slope in horizontal terraces, more recently up-slope ("vinha ao alto") (source: same technical sheet)
- Soils: Schist-sandstone (greywacke), with granite in places (source: same technical sheet)
- Vinification: Hand-picked, destemmed, crushed; maceration/fermentation in lagares at controlled 28-30°C with constant churning until the target baumé (sweetness) is reached, at which point grape brandy (aguardente, ~77% ABV) is added to arrest fermentation and fortify the wine — standard Port practice, brandy added at roughly half of natural sugar converted (source: Sogevinus/Kopke sheet; www.taylor.pt/en/what-is-port-wine/how-is-port-made for the generic fortification mechanics)
- Elevage: A blend of many different harvests, each matured separately in oak casks in Vila Nova de Gaia for varied periods; the "20 Years Old" on the label is the **average age of the blend**, not a single vintage or minimum age — merchant listings indicate the blend spans roughly 15-35 years of component ages to average to 20 (source: Kopke technical sheet; Decanter IVDP tawny-category explainer)
- ABV / RS / TA: 20.00% / 121.00 g/dm3 / 4.78 g/dm3, pH 3.54 (source: official Sogevinus/Kopke technical sheet — a second retailer-hosted copy of the same sheet gives slightly different lab values of 109.40 g/dm3 RS and 4.37 g/dm3 TA; the 121 g/dm3 figure is treated here as the more current producer-published number)

## Vintage character (NV / category character)
Non-vintage: the age statement (20 years) is a blender's average, governed by IVDP category rules for aged tawny (10/20/30/40+ year bands), not a single harvest's weather. The style is defined by slow, controlled oxidative ageing in seasoned wood — gradual evaporation, oxygen ingress through the stave and bung, and gentle esterification build the dried-fruit, nut and spice complexity, in contrast to the fresh, tannic, fruit-driven profile preserved by the largely anaerobic, vat-aged Ruby/LBV styles (source: Decanter, "Understanding tawny Port").

## Producer style
Kopke, founded in 1638, markets itself as the oldest Port house and is now part of the Sogevinus group (based in Vila Nova de Gaia). The house specialises heavily in aged, age-indicated tawnies and rare Colheitas rather than Vintage Port, and positions its 20-, 30- and 40-Year tawnies as a graduated premium ladder built on patient cask reserves and blending skill (source: Kopke technical sheet letterhead, sogevinus.com; cf. sibling brief 2025_p3_w12 for the 40-Year expression from the same house).

## Why it's in this exam
2026 Paper 3, Question 2, wines 7-8: this Kopke 20-Year Tawny is paired with a Taylor's LBV 2020 from the same country/region (Douro, Portugal) as a deliberate oxidative-versus-reductive style contrast within Port. The candidate must state ABV and RS precisely for both (this wine: 20% / ~121 g/L) and explain how the *same regulatory fortification method* (mid-fermentation aguardente addition) produces two very different finished styles purely through the choice and duration of cask ageing — long average-age oxidative ageing here versus short, filtered wood ageing for the LBV.
