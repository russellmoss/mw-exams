# Cava Brut Nature Reserva de la Familia, Juvé y Camps, 2017

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold with a fine, persistent mousse; blind, this should read as a serious traditional-method sparkling wine with real textural weight rather than a simple aperitif Cava. Expect ripe apple and pear, toasted almond, brioche and a faintly saline, savory edge from extended lees contact, on a creamy, well-structured palate with integrated but present acidity, zero perceptible sweetness (Brut Nature, RS <3 g/L), and a long, dry finish (source: https://vintus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Juve-Camps-RDF-2017-Tech-Sheet.pdf).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 55% Xarel·lo, 35% Macabeo, 10% Parellada (source: https://vintus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Juve-Camps-RDF-2017-Tech-Sheet.pdf; corroborated by https://b2bwebstorage.blob.core.windows.net/web/SAP/International_Brands/Juve_%26_Camps/Tasting_Notes/Brut_Nature.pdf)
- Vineyard: Estate-grown, organically certified fruit from the family's own Penedès holdings — Espiells, La Cuscona and Mediona — on loamy calcareous clay soils (source: https://vintus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Juve-Camps-RDF-2017-Tech-Sheet.pdf)
- Vinification: Gentle whole-cluster pressing, free-run juice only, first fermentation in temperature-controlled stainless steel; malolactic fermentation used for the base wine (source: https://b2bwebstorage.blob.core.windows.net/web/SAP/International_Brands/Juve_%26_Camps/Tasting_Notes/Brut_Nature.pdf)
- Elevage: Traditional method (second fermentation in bottle); minimum-published lees ageing for this cuvée is 36 months in the estate's underground caves, with manual riddling — well beyond the Cava DO's own 30-month floor for the Gran Reserva/"Guarda Superior" category (source: https://vintus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Juve-Camps-RDF-2017-Tech-Sheet.pdf; DO minimums confirmed at https://cava.wine/documents/289/Resumen_normas_Segmentacion_Zonificacion.pdf)
- Dosage / RS: Brut Nature — no liqueur d'expédition added at disgorgement; Cava DO regulation caps Brut Nature at <3 g/L residual sugar (source: https://cava.wine/documents/289/Resumen_normas_Segmentacion_Zonificacion.pdf)
- ABV / RS / TA: 12.0% / <3 g/L / Source needed (2018 vintage tech sheet lists 5.6 g/L tartaric as a reference point only, not the 2017 bottling — https://juveycamps.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Reserva-de-la-Familia-Juve-y-Camps-Cava-Gran-Reserva-Brut-Nature.pdf)

Under the Cava DO's Pliego de Condiciones, Gran Reserva Cava must be vintage-dated (mandatory on label), limited to Brut Nature/Extra Brut/Brut dryness levels, aged a minimum of 30 months from tirage to disgorgement in the same bottle at the same winery, and — since the 2015 harvest — sourced from fully organic-certified fruit; Reserva de la Familia meets and exceeds all of these thresholds (source: https://cava.wine/documents/289/Resumen_normas_Segmentacion_Zonificacion.pdf; organic certification from 2015 vintage confirmed at https://juveycamps.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/180921-NP-Juve%CC%81-Camps-100-organic-wines-and-cavas-English.pdf).

## Vintage character (2017 Penedès)
2017 was a warmer- and drier-than-average year in Alt Penedès (annual mean ~14.7°C vs. a ~14.3°C historical norm; rainfall around 474 mm against a ~516 mm long-term average), with a notable June heat spike and a dry spring that reduced yields at many estates while concentrating fruit. DO-level Cava output nonetheless rose about 3% versus 2016, with organic fruit already accounting for nearly half of DO production that year — consistent with Juvé y Camps' own fully organic estate profile (source: https://cava.wine/documents/207/DO_CAVA_globalreport2017_eng.pdf; climate data via research synthesis citing Alt Penedès meteorological records). For the exam, the practical takeaway is a warm, low-yield year that should support ripe, concentrated base wine fruit rather than a lean, high-acid vintage.

## Producer style
Juvé y Camps is a family-owned, multi-generation Penedès grower founded as a sparkling-wine house in 1921, now led by fourth-generation CEO Meritxell Juvé, farming roughly 250+ estate hectares across Espiells, La Cuscona and Mediona (source: https://the-buyer.net/tasting/wine/juve-and-camps-cava; https://vintus.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Juve-Camps-All-Estates-Map.pdf). The house style is estate-driven, dry (Brut Nature-led) and built on long lees ageing rather than dosage sweetness, positioning Reserva de la Familia as the flagship benchmark beneath even longer-aged prestige cuvées like La Capella (100% Xarel·lo) and La Síbèria (100% Pinot Noir) (source: https://wineanorak.com/2023/11/08/cava-discovery-10-juve-y-camps). Note for context: in April 2026 Juvé y Camps left DO Cava's regulatory framework and joined the stricter estate-based Corpinnat collective, a significant recent repositioning of the brand toward terroir-focused, single-estate sparkling wine — though the 2017 Reserva de la Familia predates that move and is labeled under Cava DO (source: https://thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/04/juve-camps-joins-corpinnat; https://hudin.com/juve-camps-leaves-do-cava-for-corpinnat).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 1 opens 2023 Paper 3 Question 1, where wines 1-4 are traditional-method sparkling wines from four different countries, none from Champagne, and candidates must identify origin, grape variety/ies, comment on quality in context, and state residual sugar. This Cava sits as the Spanish anchor of the set and tests whether candidates can distinguish estate-driven, long-lees-aged Gran Reserva Cava (creamy, structured, bone-dry) from both cheaper commodity Cava and from Champagne itself, while correctly reading the RS as Brut Nature (<3 g/L) rather than standard Brut.
