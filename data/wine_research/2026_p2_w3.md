# Trousseau Singulier, Benedicte et Stephane Tissot, 2023

## Quick tasting profile
Pale ruby to light crimson, luminous and translucent — noticeably lighter than most reds a candidate will taste blind. Nose of wild strawberry, blood orange, candied/brandied cherry and cranberry over forest-floor, white pepper, dried herb and a faint bitter-almond edge; the 2023 vintage specifically shows extra floral lift from a brief carbonic maceration step. Palate is medium-bodied with bright acidity, fine but insistent tannins, and a limestone-driven mineral streak; long, peppery, mineral finish. Reads as an "electric," lighter, fresher expression relative to the more structured 2019/2020 vintages of the same cuvee (source: Wine Society tasting note; Tavily-aggregated critic notes incl. Jancis Robinson).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Trousseau
- Vineyard: Blend of the estate's top Trousseau parcels above Arbois (cited sources name Les Bruyeres, La Mailloche, En Barberon, Curon among others), 250-450m altitude, vine age 20-65 years including old vines dating to the 1930s; massal-selection replanting, no clonal material (source: vinoalvino.ca; hartandcru.com)
- Soils: Limestone scree over Lias (Liassic) marl/clay, with some fractured-limestone and gravelly-limestone sites (source: vinoalvino.ca)
- Vinification: Hand-harvested, fully destemmed; spontaneous/native-yeast fermentation, roughly 70% of the volume in open-top wooden fermenters over ~4 months, the remainder in large open-top tanks over ~1 month; no punch-downs for the first two weeks (gentle extraction); total maceration around 4-5 weeks before pressing. The 2023 vintage specifically incorporated a modest carbonic-maceration component to lift the floral perfume and lower perceived weight versus prior vintages (source: vinoalvino.ca; SommSelect 2023 product note)
- Elevage: 12 months in 600L demi-muids, roughly 10% new oak with the balance neutral/older barrels; a portion of the 2023 blend (roughly one-third) aged in amphora. Unfined, unfiltered (source: vinoalvino.ca; buvonswine.com per Tavily aggregation)
- ABV / RS / TA: 13.5% (matches exam full_text); dry, low-intervention, low-SO2 style; acidity reported as bright/fresh (~6 g/L cited in one aggregated source, treat as indicative rather than confirmed lab figure — Source needed for a verified TA)

## Vintage character (2023 Jura / Arbois)
The 2023 Trousseau Singulier is explicitly described by the producer's importers/retailers as lighter, "more electric," and lower in alcohol than the 2022 and earlier vintages, attributed to both the season and a winemaking adjustment (introduction of a brief carbonic step, reduced new-oak proportion). Earlier vintages (2019, 2020) are described as richer and more structured, with deeper blackberry and spice notes by comparison (source: thewinesociety.com; vinoalvino.ca). ## Producer style
Domaine Andre et Mireille Tissot was founded in 1962 in Montigny-les-Arsures near Arbois; Stephane Tissot took over young (first vintage 1989) and converted the vineyards to organic farming from the early 1990s, achieving organic certification in 1999 and Demeter biodynamic certification in 2004. Bénédicte Tissot now co-runs the ~43-45 ha estate, farmed entirely organically/biodynamically, with a philosophy centred on soil health and minimal cellar intervention (native yeasts, no fining/filtration, low SO2). The domaine is widely regarded as having led the modern rehabilitation of serious Jura wine and Trousseau in particular; Jancis Robinson scores recent vintages of this cuvee in the high-teens (18/20), and Wine Advocate scored the 2022 at 94 points. Production of Trousseau Singulier is limited (roughly 30,000 bottles/vintage across the range per aggregated retail data) and it is priced at the premium end for Jura reds — cited retail figures cluster around GBP 38-48 / USD 50-57 / EUR 38-45 per bottle depending on market, positioning it well above generic Arbois rouge and squarely as the domaine's flagship Trousseau bottling (source: jancisrobinson.com; hartandcru.com; thewinesociety.com and multiple retailer listings aggregated via Tavily).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 3 of a three-wine "same country, different variety" question (2026 Paper 2, Q1) — the Jura curveball completing the French light-red trio alongside a Loire Cabernet Franc and a Beaujolais Gamay. Its pale colour, high-toned oxidative/savoury-spice nose and distinctive limestone-Lias mineral streak are designed to test whether candidates recognise Trousseau (rather than defaulting to Pinot Noir on the pale colour, or to Gamay/Cabernet Franc by elimination) — reinforced by the automated wine-extractor in this project mis-resolving the wine to Savagnin/Chardonnay purely from the name "Trousseau Singulier," underscoring how easily this bottling misleads on variety.
