# Amarone della Valpolicella Riserva Vigna Garzon, Pieropan, 2019

## Quick tasting profile
Deep, opaque ruby moving toward garnet at the rim given the 5-6 years of age. Nose is dried/concentrated dark fruit (black cherry, blackberry, plum) layered with bitter chocolate, coffee, leather, black pepper and clove, plus a balsamic lift. Palate is powerful and rounded, high alcohol (16%) is buffered by fresh acidity and a firm but ripe tannic frame; long, spiced, faintly bitter-chocolate finish typical of appassimento-made wine (producer note, [1]).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 60% Corvina Veronese, 30% Corvinone, 5% Rondinella, 5% Croatina Veronese (2019 blend) (source: producer tech sheet [1])
- Vineyard: single-vineyard "Vigna Garzon" on Monte Garzon, Cellore d'Illasi (Verona); pink limestone soils rich in clay and chalk; 300-500m altitude; vines planted 2000 (source: [1][2])
- Appassimento: producer states ~60 days natural drying with occasional fan assistance, losing roughly two-thirds of grape weight; some independent trade sources instead describe a longer ~4-month drying to February — this is a direct conflict in the record and is not resolved (source: [1] vs. [3] grapengrain.com)
- Vinification: indigenous-yeast fermentation at 23-24C for ~30 days with daily pump-overs/punch-downs; malolactic reported complete in barrel by one merchant sheet (source: [1][4])
- Elevage: reported as ~36 months in 500L French oak barrels (some merchant sheets cite ~20% new oak, unconfirmed by producer), followed by ~12 months in vitrified concrete, then minimum 12 months in bottle before release (source: [4], cf. producer minimum-bottle-age statement [1])
- ABV / RS / TA: 16.0% (one merchant lists 16.5%, minor discrepancy) / RS reported as either 8 g/L (SAQ) or 3.5 g/L (Liberty Wine) — two different published values, not reconciled / TA 6.95 g/L, pH 3.44 (source: [4][5])
*(RS and % new oak both carry source conflicts — flagged rather than resolved.)*

## Vintage character (2019 Valpolicella)
2019 opened with an unusually cool, wet spring (coolest April-May in three decades) that delayed flowering, followed by a hot summer with heat spikes to ~38C and strong diurnal swings; harvest ran about a week later than 2018, in mid-September. The combination produced grapes with good acid retention despite summer heat, translating into Amarones widely scored in the low-to-high 90s (Wine Spectator/Decanter regional scores for peers such as Tommasi, Venturini, Allegrini ranged 92-94 points) and described as balancing richness with freshness — a "power plus elegance" vintage rather than a purely brawny one (source: vintage synthesis, multiple regional producer notes).

## Producer style
Pieropan is a historic (1880) Soave Classico family estate that only entered Amarone production after acquiring Cellore d'Illasi vineyards in 1999; it was the first Soave producer to farm organically (certified since 2008) and applies that same low-intervention, traditional ethos to its Amarone program. The house style favours precision and freshness over sheer weight relative to some Valpolicella peers, though the Vigna Garzon Riserva is still a powerful, long-aging wine given its single-vineyard, extended-elevage positioning (source: [6][7]).

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 4 of Paper 2, Q2 (wines 4-6, "three different countries," all powerful high-alcohol reds). Pieropan's Amarone is the appassimento benchmark in a flight built around concentration achieved by different routes — dried-grape sweetness/power for Italy versus New World ripeness and oak for the Barossa Shiraz and Napa Cabernet blend that follow. Part (b) (method of production, 5 marks) specifically rewards identifying and explaining appassimento.
