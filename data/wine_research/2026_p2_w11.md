# Château Giscours, 2017

## Quick tasting profile
A Cabernet-dominant Margaux from a low-yielding, frost-reduced vintage: expect a medium-to-deep garnet-purple colour (already showing some development at 8-9 years old), plum preserve, cassis and violet on the nose with a touch of graphite and spice, and a medium-bodied, elegant, fairly plush palate with fine, polished tannin rather than the density of a top vintage. This should read as approachable and already drinking well rather than needing extended cellaring.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 71% Cabernet Sauvignon, 24% Merlot, 5% Petit Verdot — the estate's official 2017 blend, notably the highest Cabernet Sauvignon proportion recorded at the property, reflecting the vineyard-restructuring programme begun in the mid-1990s
- Vineyard: Estate vineyards on the Giscours plateau, Labarde/Cantenac, Margaux — deep Günzian gravel croupes
- Soils: Gravel over clay-limestone subsoil, typical of the top Margaux gravel mounds
- Vinification: Estate technical note; separate plot/varietal vinification is standard practice at the property (source needed for 2017-specific fermentation detail)
- Elevage: 15–18 months in French oak barriques, 50% new oak
- ABV / RS / TA: 13% (13.0-13.2% reported across sources) / dry / Source needed
- Critic reception: Wine Advocate 94/100 ("refined and delicate Margaux, with juicy fruit and nicely-coated tannins"); Decanter 94/100 ("medium-to-deep garnet-purple... pretty plum preserves, warm cassis and wilted roses... medium-bodied, elegant and plush")
- Drinking window: commercial listings suggest a broad window of roughly 2020–2038/2040, i.e. already accessible now and not requiring long cellaring

## Vintage character (2017 Bordeaux / Margaux)
2017 was defined by the most severe April frost in a generation (night temperatures down to about -4°C on 27 April), which struck the Left Bank hard, though Margaux's core gravel-plateau vineyards were comparatively less affected than peripheral plots and the Right Bank. The frost was followed by a dry, warm summer, giving low yields (Bordeaux-wide, the lowest in 25+ years) but reasonably healthy, evenly ripened fruit. Trade consensus (Decanter, Jancis Robinson) is that 2017 produces wines that are earlier-drinking and lighter/more refined in structure than 2016 — commonly framed as ready in roughly 6-8 years versus 10-12 years for 2016 — with real quality in the top Margaux estates but noticeably less power, concentration and long-haul ageing potential than the outstanding, structured 2016 vintage.

## Producer style
Château Giscours is a Third Growth (3ème Cru Classé, 1855) Margaux estate that underwent a significant quality renaissance from the 1990s onward under new ownership (the Albada Jelgersma family), including a shift toward a higher Cabernet Sauvignon proportion, tighter selection for the grand vin, and a more precise, terroir-driven winemaking approach. House style favours perfumed, elegant, classically structured Margaux rather than raw power, positioning the estate among the more consistently improving Third Growths of the appellation.
