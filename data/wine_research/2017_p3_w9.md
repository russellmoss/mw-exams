## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Pinot Noir (the sole variety in Côte de Nuits red AOCs)
- Vineyard/village: David Clark's base was Morey-Saint-Denis, where he began with a 1.5-acre parcel in 2004 and expanded to just under 5 acres (source: vinouslyspeaking.com; corroborated independently by wideworldofwine.co). Note that Morey-Saint-Denis itself is a distinct village AOC, not part of Côte de Nuits Villages (which covers Fixin/Brochon in the north and Corgoloin/Comblanchien/Prémeaux in the south) — Clark evidently produced multiple bottlings (Bourgogne, Morey-Saint-Denis, Côte de Nuits Villages, Vosne-Romanée) from different parcels/purchased fruit, but the exact vineyard source for the CdNV specifically is Source needed.
- Winemaking: No chaptalisation; hand-bottled by hand from barrel (source: wideworldofwine.co)
- Elevage: Aged exclusively in old (non-new) oak across the range (source: wideworldofwine.co)
- ABV: 13.0% per exam label

## Vintage character (2012 Côte de Nuits / Burgundy)
2012 was a difficult, low-yield vintage in the Côte de Nuits: a cold, wet spring delayed flowering and raised disease pressure, and a hailstorm on 1 August caused localised damage, though the Côte de Nuits escaped the worst of the hail that hit the Côte de Beaune on 6 June (source: decanter.com/2012-vintage-guide-for-red-bungundy). Red yields in the Côte de Nuits fell roughly 30% versus a normal year (less than the up-to-50% losses in parts of the Côte de Beaune). Small berries with a high skin-to-juice ratio gave the surviving fruit notable concentration; Wine Spectator's preliminary barrel assessment scored the Côte de Nuits reds 95, calling them "dense, concentrated reds, with pure fruit, elegance and freshness" (source: winespectator.com/vintage-charts/region/burgundy-cotes-de-nuits-reds). At village level, this should translate into more depth and structure than a typical CdNV vintage, tempered by the appellation's inherent lighter weight relative to named-village or Premier Cru sites.

## Producer style
Domaine David Clark was a micro-domaine (a few thousand bottles a year) run essentially as a one-man operation by David Clark, an American raised and educated in Scotland who worked as a software/pit-stop-strategy engineer for the Williams Formula One team before training at a Burgundy wine school (Beaune) and buying his first vines in 2004 (source: vinouslyspeaking.com). His stated practice was minimal intervention: no chaptalisation, exclusively old-oak ageing, and hand-bottling from the barrel (source: wideworldofwine.co). Multiple independent sources place his final vintage as 2012, after which he stopped producing under his own label; no confirmed information on a subsequent sale of the domaine or its buyer was located — treat this as reported but not documentarily confirmed.
