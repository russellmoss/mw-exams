# Riesling Trocken Niederhäuser Hermannshöhle, Jakob Schneider, 2023

## Quick tasting profile
A bone-dry, unoaked, site-driven Nahe Riesling: pale lemon-green, with a smoky/slate-flint mineral core layered over ripe citrus and stone fruit, herb/spice nuance, and firm, racy acidity carrying a long, saline-mineral finish. Medium weight rather than austere — the site's volcanic/slate soils and pure south exposure give more concentration and grip than a simple estate Riesling — but it should read as classically dry and precise rather than powerful or reductive.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Riesling, hand-harvested (one retailer tech sheet cites picking on 4 October 2023) [3]
- Vineyard: Niederhäuser Hermannshöhle, a VDP.GROSSE LAGE Einzellage on a steep, south-facing bend of the Nahe; Jakob Schneider holds an estate-described ~8 ha there (other sources cite a smaller ~2 ha parcel in the northeast corner, planted 1939) — a documented conflict in the public record on exact holdings [1][5]
- Soils: black-grey weathered Devonian slate interspersed with porphyry/melaphyr volcanic rock and limestone pockets — highly heterogeneous over short distances on the slope [4][5]
- Vinification: no oak reported for this standard Lagenwein bottling (the estate's separate, pricier "Magnus" Hermannshöhle trocken bottling does show dosed oak-barrel aromas; this is not that wine) [wine list]
- ABV / RS / TA: 12.5% / ~2.6-3.8 g/L (two retailer listings) / ~6.5 g/L [2][3]

## Vintage character (2023 Nahe)
2023 was a demanding but ultimately excellent vintage for dry Nahe Riesling. The German Wine Institute called it "just right": warm, dry days offset by cool nights during harvest, with high selection pressure but no need for widespread night-picking. Ripeness was more classical/moderate than the riper, lower-acid 2022, with regional must weights broadly 70-80° Oechsle (Vinous reports ~91° Oechsle and ~30% lower yields for the higher-end producers it tracks). Vinous found excellent acidity, high flavour intensity, moderate alcohol (~12-12.5%) and no need for chaptalisation — several critics flag 2023 as a potential breakthrough vintage specifically for dry Riesling, with strong scores reported for Hermannshöhle bottlings in particular. [winesofgermany.com; v1.vinous.com]

## Producer style
Weingut Jakob Schneider has made wine in Niederhausen since 1575 and is run today by the Jakob Schneider family (18-24 ha under vine, ~85% Riesling), based in the village at the foot of Hermannshöhle. House style favours precision, minerality and restraint: roughly half the portfolio ferments spontaneously with native yeast, and Vinum named the estate "Winzer des Jahres" (Winemaker of the Year) for the Nahe, praising a collection where "Präzision, Mineralität und Charakter" run through every wine. Schneider bottlings from Hermannshöhle sit at the affordable end of Nahe Grand-Cru pricing relative to the site's most famous holder, Dönnhoff, while drawing consistently strong critical scores (Jancis Robinson has reviewed the same bottling from other vintages). [skurnik.com/producer/schneider; schneider-wein.com price list]

## Why it's in this exam
Paper 3, Question 2 pairs Wines 11-12 as same country/region (Nahe, Germany) from a single grower — this wine is the dry half of a deliberate trocken-vs-Spätlese contrast from Jakob Schneider, testing whether candidates can read bone-dry table wine against a distinctly sweet, low-alcohol Prädikat wine from the same producer and correctly infer both styles from must-weight/fermentation logic, and then compare the two Einzellagen (Hermannshöhle vs Kirschheck) for quality within the Nahe.
