# Cabernet Sauvignon TD-9, Shafer, 2021

## Quick tasting profile
Deep, dense purple-black. Nose is supersaturated dark fruit — black cherry, blackberry, black olive — with lead-pencil, dried flowers and toasty new-oak spice. Palate is full-bodied and firmly structured, with grippy but integrated tannins and a long graphite-spice finish; built for cellaring but already showing polish (source: James Suckling, Wine Enthusiast, Jeb Dunnuck notes aggregated [2][3][4]).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: despite the varietal-sounding "Cabernet Sauvignon TD-9" label wording used in this exam's full_text, TD-9 is a proprietary BLEND — 2021 composition is 76% Cabernet Sauvignon, 11% Merlot, 10% Petit Verdot, 3% Malbec (source: official Shafer 2021 TD-9 technical sheet [1])
- Vineyard: estate vineyards in Napa Valley's Southern Foothills, just south of the Stags Leap District, plus select additional Napa sites; harvest Brix 23.5-25.5 (source: [1])
- Vinification: not fully detailed for 2021 in published materials; Shafer's general house practice for reds includes whole-cluster component and malolactic conversion, but the exact 2021 protocol (maceration length, vessel) is not published — "Source needed"
- Elevage: 20 months in 100% new French oak barrels (60-gallon/225L), staves from the Allier and Tronçais forests (source: [1])
- ABV / RS / TA: 15.3% / RS not published (dry table wine) / TA not published

## Vintage character (2021 Napa Valley)
2021 was a long, sunny, low-rainfall growing season across Napa (second consecutive drought year), producing small berries, concentrated colour and clean, pure aromatics per Shafer's own vintage note; low yields generally intensified fruit and tannin concentration valley-wide, consistent with the wine's power and density (source: Shafer 2021 TD-9 tech sheet [1]).

## Producer style
Founded 1972 by John Shafer in the Stags Leap District (first Cabernet planted 1973-74, first release 1978); winemaking since the 1980s under Doug Shafer and, from 1994, chief winemaker Elias Fernandez. House style favours ripe, concentrated, oak-forward but polished Napa reds; flagship is Hillside Select Cabernet Sauvignon, with TD-9 (named for the Toll Daughters property crossroad marker) positioned as a more approachable, blend-driven "second wine"-adjacent bottling that still carries substantial new-oak treatment. Long-standing sustainability program: cover crops, sheep grazing in place of herbicide, solar power, and a 2022 regenerative-viticulture vineyard acquisition (source: [5], Shafer estate history/sustainability materials).

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 6 of Paper 2, Q2 (wines 4-6, "three different countries," a flight of powerful, high-alcohol, heavily oaked reds). TD-9 is the Napa benchmark and is a deliberate trap on the label wording — the exam's own full_text calls it "Cabernet Sauvignon TD-9," but the wine is a Cabernet-led Bordeaux-style blend (Merlot, Petit Verdot, Malbec) matured entirely in new French oak, so part (a) (variety ID, 10 marks) rewards candidates who correctly identify it as a blend rather than a varietal wine, and part (b) (method of production) rewards identifying the 100% new French oak regime as the structural signature completing the three-wine "concentration by different routes" flight (appassimento / warm-climate old-vine / new-oak Bordeaux blend).
