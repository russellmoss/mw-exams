# Brut, Clément Klur, NV

## Quick tasting profile
Light yellow-green with a fine, lively mousse. Blind, this should read as cool-climate, non-Champagne traditional-method sparkling wine: citrus and orchard fruit (apple, pear), light brioche/shortbread autolytic notes, and a saline, slightly bitter finish, with fresh, precise acidity and only moderate weight — leaner and less overtly toasty than a long-aged Cava Gran Reserva or vintage English fizz (source: https://lebendigeweine.de/en/clement-klur-cremant-dalsace-brut-ungeschwefelt-lcns21-nv; corroborated by https://vinselectionpro.com/products/cremant-d-alsace-brut-lcas19-nv-clement-klur-elsass).

- Elevage: Crémant d'Alsace AOC requires a minimum 9 months on lees before disgorgement; Klur/Heitzmann cuvées are reported at 12 months or more on lees, consistent with or exceeding the appellation floor (source: https://extranet.inao.gouv.fr/fichier/pno-cdc-Cr%C3%A9mantdAlsace-cn240911.pdf; producer practice via https://wineroute.alsace/230106788-winery-leon-heitzmann-clement-klur)

## Vintage character (NV / house character)
Non-vintage, so base-wine year matters less than farming and cellar consistency. Klur/Heitzmann farms biodynamically (Domaine Léon Heitzmann converted to biodynamic viticulture in 2008; the original Klur estate was biodynamic from its 1999 founding), which the producer and importers frame as the throughline across the range — purity of fruit, low intervention, and in several cuvées zero or minimal added sulfur (source: https://klur.net/en/a-family; https://bacchusglobal.co.th/blog/preserving-a-legacy-in-alsace). For exam purposes, the relevant point is that this is a small-grower, farming-led Crémant rather than a large négociant blend, which should translate into more textural precision and site character than a commodity Crémant.

## Producer style
The estate has an unusual, well-documented ownership history relevant to placing this in context: Clément and Francine Klur founded Vignoble Klur in Katzenthal in 1999 on organic/biodynamic principles, one of the earliest biodynamic conversions in Alsace, tracing family winegrowing in the village back to the 17th century (source: https://klur.net/en/a-family). In 2017 the Klur vineyards, brand and stock passed to neighboring biodynamic grower Léon Heitzmann of Ammerschwihr (whose own family domaine dates to 1804 and converted to biodynamics in 2008), with the wines now produced as "Domaine Léon Heitzmann et Clément Klur" while retaining the Clément Klur label for export markets (source: https://dna.fr/edition-de-colmar/2018/03/09/seconde-jeunesse-pour-leon-heitzmann; https://vinsklur.com/clement-klur/une-histoire-de-familles). The house makes several distinct Crémant cuvées (standard Brut, a "Sans Soufre"/unsulphured bottling, and the named Cuvée Manekineko), all built around traditional-method production, hand harvest and minimal cellar intervention (source: https://wineanorak.com/Alsace/Clementl_Klur.htm).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 2 sits in 2023 Paper 3 Question 1, where wines 1-4 are traditional-method sparkling wines from four different countries, none from Champagne, testing origin, grape variety/ies, quality-in-context and stated residual sugar. As the French but non-Champagne entry, this Crémant d'Alsace tests whether candidates can place a lighter-framed, Pinot Blanc/Auxerrois-driven blend against the richer, longer-aged Cava and vintage English wines in the same flight, and correctly identify Alsace rather than defaulting to Champagne on "French traditional method."
