# Meursault Les Narvaux, Domaine Latour-Giraud, 2023

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold; a fresh, citrus-and-stone-fruit nose (lemon, white peach, green apple) with a saline, flinty minerality rather than overt buttery richness. Palate is bright and juicy with a creamy but restrained texture, fine tension from Meursault's limestone-driven acidity, and a long, layered finish; oak influence reads as integrated rather than dominant, consistent with the domaine's understated approach in this vintage (source: domaine-hosted Jasper Morris tasting note, JASPER MORRIS MILL.23.pdf).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Chardonnay
- Vineyard: Les Narvaux, a well-regarded Meursault lieu-dit on a sloping, stony site with abundant limestone/chalk and east exposure, base soils of clay-limestone with siliceous "chaillots" (source: Vineyard Brands tech sheet). Sources conflict on vine age for this specific 2023 bottling: the importer tech sheet describes the Narvaux climat generally as c. 40-year-old vines, while a domaine-associated tasting note states the cuvée comes from a young parcel first fruited in 2020 (no 2021 production) — flagged as an unresolved discrepancy rather than resolved fact.
- Vinification: sources conflict on fermentation vessel split — one domaine-linked note states two-thirds fermented in tank and one-third in barrel with wild/indigenous yeast on free-run juice; a separate technical statement for the cuvée cites barrel fermentation with 20% new oak. Both are cited here as reported, not reconciled (source needed to confirm which applies to 2023).
- Elevage: sur lie ageing is consistently reported; fining/filtration practice is disputed across sources (one says fined-but-unfiltered, another says neither fining nor filtration used).
- ABV / RS / TA: 13.5% (per data/wines.json and one merchant listing; a separate tech sheet shows 13.0% — discrepancy noted) / RS not published (dry) / TA not published. Bottled late August 2023.

## Vintage character (2023 Burgundy)
2023 in Burgundy was a generous, relatively early, sunny vintage following a warm growing season, producing wines generally noted for ripe fruit, moderate-to-good acidity retention, and good volumes after several short vintages (2021-2022) — a welcome return to abundance for growers. For Meursault specifically this reads through as riper citrus/stone-fruit character with a saline lift rather than austere green-apple tension (source: producer/merchant tasting notes above; general 2023 Burgundy vintage character - source needed for a dedicated vintage report).

## Producer style
Domaine Latour-Giraud is a roughly 10-hectare, largely white-wine-focused Meursault domaine (Jean-Pierre Latour) holding several parcels across Meursault including Narvaux, farmed organically per some merchant/critic sources. The house style favours freshness and site transparency over heavy new oak — reported oak percentages (c. 20% new, when barrel-fermented) and an emphasis on sur lie ageing without excessive battonage point to a restrained, mineral-driven Meursault style rather than a rich, buttery, heavily-worked one (source: Vineyard Brands and Regal Wine producer pages).

## Why it's in this exam
Wines 1-6 of 2026 Paper 1 Question 1 are all Chardonnay from six different countries; this Meursault Les Narvaux is the Old World, Burgundian benchmark — a village-level, single-vineyard white Burgundy establishing the "restrained, terroir-driven, sur lie" reference point against which the New World Chardonnays in the same flight (Marlborough, Margaret River, and others) are judged for winemaking style and commercial tier.
