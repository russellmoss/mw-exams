# Paso Las Mañas, Artuke, 2021

## Quick tasting profile
Bright, fairly deep ruby, youthful rim. Nose is site-driven and fresher than a classic Reserva — red and dark berry fruit, floral lift, chalky/stony minerality, restrained oak (no obvious vanilla/coconut). Palate is lively, almost effervescent with freshness, chalky and saline on the finish, with dry, fine tannin rather than the plush, oak-derived structure of a traditional aged blend. Reads as modern, terroir-focused, single-site Tempranillo rather than a long-aged commercial blend (source: tedwardwines.com).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Tempranillo (source: vinovico.com; tedwardwines.com)
- Vineyard: single parcel "El Chorro" in Samaniego (municipality Baños de Ebro), Rioja Alavesa, ~600-700 m, north-south orientation, clay-limestone soils; vines planted 1999-2013 (one independent database gives 1990/2013); certified organic, traditionally head-trained/bush vines, some requiring special training due to wind exposure (source: vinovico.com; wwxplorer.com; tedwardwines.com)
- Vinification: hand-harvested, destemmed (not whole-cluster) fruit fermented in 12,000-L stainless steel tanks; full malolactic fermentation completed in stainless steel (one independent source instead reports indigenous-yeast fermentation) (source: vinovico.com; wwxplorer.com)
- Elevage: ~15% new oak, remainder neutral; aged 12-16 months in large-format French oak foudre — sources disagree on exact vessel size (500 L vs. a 3,500-L/35-hL foudre); bottled after soft filtration, no cold stabilization (source: vinovico.com; wwxplorer.com; vinovico.com/products/artuke-el-escolladero for house foudre practice)
- ABV / RS / TA: 14% / not published / not published. One independent database reports pH 3.4 and ABV 13.6% against the 14.0% reported on producer/retail pages — treat retail/producer figure as primary. Production reported as either ~620 cases or ~8,000 bottles, an unresolved discrepancy (source: vinovico.com; wwxplorer.com)
- Drinking window: not published in any source found — "Source needed." Given fresher, less oxidative, minority-new-oak elevage than a Reserva, likely built for earlier-to-mid-term drinking (roughly first 5-8 years) rather than the multi-decade profile of a classic Reserva, but this is inference, not a cited figure.

## Vintage character (2021 Rioja Alavesa)
The Consejo Regulador de Rioja rates the 2021 vintage "Very good" region-wide, but no Alavesa-specific weather, harvest-date or yield data were found in the sources consulted — a genuine evidence gap (source: riojawine.com/en-gb/the-designation/vintages). Region-wide, 2021 followed a normal, non-extreme growing season without the acute stress of 2019's flowering problems; no sources here document a materially difficult 2021 for Rioja.

## Producer style
Artuke is a Rioja Alavesa family estate (brothers Arturo and Kike de Miguel, running the winery since the mid-2000s) at the forefront of Rioja's "vinos de pueblo"/village-and-parcel movement (associated with the Rioja'n'Roll and Futuro Vinador collectives), explicitly critical of the traditional Crianza/Reserva ageing-based classification in favour of site expression. The house range runs from a carbonic-maceration village Tempranillo through named single-parcel bottlings (Paso Las Mañas, La Condenada, Finca de los Locos, El Escolladero) fermented on indigenous yeasts and aged in large-format French oak with minimal new wood, prioritising freshness, minerality and site transparency over overt oak or extraction. Reception is strong: Tim Atkin and others have given the range's top single-parcel wines very high scores (source: vinovico.com/blogs/producers/bodegasartuke; spanishwinelover.com).

## Why it's in this exam
Paper 2, Question 3: wines 7-8 are the Rioja pair, deliberately structured as a traditional-versus-modern contrast within the same country and region. Against La Rioja Alta's long-aged, American-oak, blended Viña Ardanza Reserva (2026_p2_w7), Artuke's Paso Las Mañas is the modern, single-parcel, French-oak, minimal-Crianza-category counterpoint — 100% Tempranillo from one named site rather than a Tempranillo-Garnacha blend, large-format French oak with only ~15% new rather than extensive time in American barrels, and no formal Reserva ageing designation. Candidates are asked to identify origin (a), compare style/quality in the context of the region (b) — the traditional-vs-modern axis is the intended discriminator — and compare maturity/likely vintage (c), where Artuke's fresher 2021 and lighter oak regime should read as the younger, less evolved wine against Ardanza's 2019 Reserva.
