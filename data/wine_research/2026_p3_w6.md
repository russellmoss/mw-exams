# Brut, Delacourt, NV

## Quick tasting profile
A standard commercial Brut NV Champagne: creamy texture with brioche and toasty-nut character, apple/peach fruit, rounded and approachable rather than austere or mineral-driven, with fine bubbles - a mainstream, supermarket-facing style rather than a grower's or prestige-house expression (source: Decanter tasting note; M&S product listing - via Tavily research).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Blend of Chardonnay, Pinot Meunier and Pinot Noir - M&S's own product data for the 75cl bottling lists approximately 29% Chardonnay / 48% Pinot Meunier / 23% Pinot Noir; other trade sources (IWSC, Decanter) show slightly different rounded percentages for the same blend
- Vinification: Not published in located sources (fermentation vessel, malolactic regime, lees-ageing duration all "Source needed")
- Dosage: Not published in g/L in any located source - the wine is labelled "Brut" (i.e. up to 12 g/L dosage under EU rules), but no producer or retailer figure was found; treat as "Source needed" for the exact number
- ABV: Sources conflict - M&S's own product page lists 12% ABV for the 75cl format, while Decanter and an IWSC competition entry list 12.5%; the exam's stated 12.5% is used here as authoritative for this brief
- Producer/negociant: Decanter reports the range is produced for M&S by the Reims-based house Castelnau; an IWSC competition entry lists "M&S" as the submitting producer, and Famille Ducourt (a separate negociant) advertises private-label/bespoke bottling services that reference Delacourt - the exact contractual bottler is genuinely ambiguous across sources
(source: Decanter supermarket-wines coverage; M&S product page; IWSC entry - via Tavily research)

## Vintage character (NV)
Non-vintage; built from a blend of years/reserve wines for consistency of house style rather than expressing a single vintage. *(Source needed for base-vintage composition or reserve-wine percentage.)*

## Producer style
Delacourt is Marks & Spencer's own-label Champagne range, sold in multiple SKUs (Brut NV, Blanc de Blancs, Grand Cru, Rosé, Medium Dry) at UK retail prices around GBP 25 for the standard Brut NV - firmly in the commodity/supermarket tier of Champagne, positioned for volume and accessibility rather than terroir expression or grower identity. This is a private-label / negociant-sourced bottling, not an independent grower's wine - the opposite pole, commercially and stylistically, from the growers'-cooperative, zero-dosage Chavost paired alongside it (source: Decanter, M&S, Wine-Searcher listings - via Tavily research).

## Why it's in this exam
Paired with 2026_p3_w5 (Chavost Blanc de Chardonnay Brut Nature) as the Champagne pair in Paper 3 Q2. The pairing's evident pedagogical point is a dosage and quality-tier contrast within the same region: a bone-dry (0 g/L), minimal-intervention grower's cuvee (Chavost) versus a standard-dosage, commercial supermarket own-label Brut (Delacourt) - testing whether candidates can read winemaking/commercial signals (roundness, dosage-driven sweetness, less textural precision) to place this wine at the accessible, larger-volume end of Champagne rather than assuming all Champagne tastes the same.
