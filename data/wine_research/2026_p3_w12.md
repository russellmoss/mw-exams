# Riesling Spätlese Norheimer Kirschheck, Jakob Schneider, 2022

## Quick tasting profile
A low-alcohol, distinctly sweet Prädikat Riesling: pale gold, with ripe white-peach and orchard-blossom aromatics and a flowery, spicy character on the palate. The defining feature is the interplay between substantial residual sugar and a gripping, high acid structure that keeps the wine fresh rather than cloying, with notable length and a mineral, slate-driven finish. The 8% ABV is itself diagnostic: fermentation was arrested well before dryness, leaving a wine with concentrated natural sugar rather than one built through late-harvest desiccation or added sweetening.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Riesling, hand-picked and gently pressed [thewinebarn.co.uk]
- Vineyard: Norheimer Kirschheck, a south-facing Einzellage in Norheim (adjacent village to Niederhausen on the Nahe) on grey slate mixed with sandstone/porphyry soils; among the oldest recorded vineyard sites on the Nahe (name derives from "Kirsche"/cherry, referencing wild cherry trees once interplanted with vines) [skurnik.com]
- Vinification: natural/wild-yeast fermentation, arrested well short of dryness; one retailer technical note describes a 50% old-oak-barrel / 50% stainless-steel-vat elevage split (large neutral casks, not new oak) — bottled under screw cap, bottling reported March 2023 [thewinebarn.co.uk; skurnik.com]
- ABV / RS / TA: 8.0% (matches exam full_text; one importer listing rounds to 8.5%) / ~72.7-73.6 g/L / ~8.6-8.8 g/L [skurnik.com; thewinebarn.co.uk]

## Vintage character (2022 Nahe)
Direct regional reporting on 2022 Nahe is thin in available sources; the clearest comparative data point (from Weingut Keller's 2023 harvest report) states that 2023 saw lower must weights and acidity 2-3 g/L higher than in 2022 — implying 2022 was the riper, lower-acid year of the two, consistent with favourable conditions for accumulating natural sugar at Spätlese/Auslese level. Regional botrytis incidence, harvest timing and Prädikat-level style specifics for Nahe 2022 were not found in cited sources and are flagged as a gap; the 73 g/L RS at just 8% ABV in this wine indicates a healthy, physiologically ripe Spätlese picking rather than heavily botrytised fruit. ## Producer style
See companion brief `2026_p3_w11.md` for full estate background (Weingut Jakob Schneider, Niederhausen, since 1575). For Prädikatswein specifically, the estate's 2026 price list shows a consistent house pattern: Spätlese-level wines from both Kirschheck and Hermannshöhle finish at 8.0-8.5% ABV with pronounced residual sweetness, positioned as fresher, more everyday-luxury bottlings below the estate's higher Prädikat tiers (Auslese, Auslese "Junior," Beerenauslese) from Hermannshöhle. Kirschheck is used by the estate exclusively at Spätlese level (not as a dry Lagenwein), signalling that the site is valued for classic sweet, high-acid Nahe Riesling rather than for VDP.GROSSE GEWÄCHS-style dry wines. [schneider-wein.com Weinkarte 2026; skurnik.com]

## Why it's in this exam
Paper 3, Question 2 pairs Wines 11-12 as same country/region (Nahe, Germany), same producer (Jakob Schneider) — this is the deliberately sweet half of the trocken-vs-Spätlese pair, built specifically to test candidates' ability to state alcohol and residual sugar precisely (both wines carry 3-mark sub-questions on ABV and RS) and, in part (c), to compare quality/site reputation: Hermannshöhle is the Nahe's most celebrated VDP.GROSSE LAGE, while Kirschheck, though historic, sits a tier below it in regional reputation — a within-region quality/hierarchy contrast that mirrors the Coutet/Delmond Sauternes-Barsac pair earlier in the same question.
