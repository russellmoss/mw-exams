# Maury, Thunevin-Calvet, 1998

## Quick tasting profile
Deep mahogany/tawny-garnet colour consistent with 25+ years of oxidative cask ageing. Expect a classic rancio nose of raisin, fig, dried orange peel, caramel, Christmas pudding spice, dark chocolate and smoky/toasted wood, with a full, sweet, high-alcohol palate carrying dried-fruit concentration and a long, warming, nutty finish (source: https://theotherbordeaux.com/shop/uncategorised/thunevin-calvet-maury-1998). At 25 years old the wine should show essentially no primary fruit — this is a tertiary, oxidative style, closer in character to an old tawny Port or aged Banyuls than to a still red.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Grenache Noir, near-monovarietal; 1998-era Maury AOC rules required a minimum 70% Grenache Noir (raised to 75% in later regulation), with up to 10% Syrah/Carignan/Cinsault permitted — in practice most Maury producers, including small artisanal estates, use 90-100% Grenache Noir (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_AOC)
- Vineyard: Thunevin-Calvet farms across two contrasting Maury-area terroirs — old-vine (40-100+ year) Grenache on poor, black schistous marl in the Maury heartland, plus higher-altitude granite parcels (added c.2007, after this vintage) for freshness (source: https://vinsetmillesimes.com/en/9764-thunevin-calvet)
- Soils: Black schist and marl, the defining Maury AOC soil type, with the wider appellation also showing granite (near Lesquerde) and clay-limestone (toward Tautavel) (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_AOC)
- Vinification: Mutage (fortification with ~95% grape spirit) part-way through fermentation, arresting yeast activity and leaving a partially-fermented must (roughly 6-12% alcohol from fermentation plus 5-10% from the added spirit) — a later mutage point than Port, retaining natural grape sugar and fresh fruit character at the moment of fortification (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_AOC)
- Elevage: Extended oxidative maturation in oak vats/barrels to bottling; classic Maury "rancio" ageing further exposes wine in glass demijohns/bonbonnes to summer heat and light to deliberately oxidize it, precipitating colour/tannin compounds and building the tertiary rancio character this bottling shows at 25 years of age (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_AOC; https://theotherbordeaux.com/shop/uncategorised/thunevin-calvet-maury-1998)

## Vintage character (1998 Roussillon)
Source needed for a dedicated 1998 Roussillon vintage report; comparative evidence from another 1998 Maury (La Coume du Roy — Grenache-dominant, ~16% ABV) supports the general pattern of hot, dry, Mediterranean ripening in the Agly Valley in the late 1990s producing physiologically mature, high-sugar Grenache well suited to mutage and long ageing (source: https://thinking-drinking.com/blog/la-coume-du-roy-maury-grenache-1998-france). At 25+ years, vintage weather is now secondary to the multi-decade oxidative elevage in shaping the glass; the exam-relevant point is that this wine should taste unmistakably mature and rancio rather than fruit-driven.

## Producer style
Domaine/Château Thunevin-Calvet is a small, modern Maury estate founded in 2001 by Jean-Luc Thunevin (of Château Valandraud, Saint-Émilion garagiste fame) in partnership with Maury native Jean-Roger Calvet; the partnership began with garage-scale vinification before an ecological cellar was built in 2008 (source: https://thunevin-calvet.fr). Reported vineyard holdings vary across sources (50 ha per the estate's own site, 60-65 ha per merchant/catalogue summaries), farmed across old-vine schist-marl Maury parcels and later granite additions (source: https://thunevin-calvet.fr; https://vinsetmillesimes.com/en/9764-thunevin-calvet). Merchant profiles describe the estate's Maury VDN cuvées, including this 1998, as concentrated, artisanal, micro-lot bottlings positioned alongside other serious small Maury producers (e.g. Mas Amiel, La Coume du Roy) rather than the region's larger cooperative blends (source: https://theotherbordeaux.com/shop/uncategorised/thunevin-calvet-maury-1998).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 10 opens 2023 Paper 3 Question 4, where wines 10-12 are stated to be from three different countries but the same single grape variety (Grenache/Garnacha) — France, Spain and the USA. This 1998 Maury is the extreme, oxidative, fortified, sweet expression of Grenache against which the dry, unfortified Montsant Garnacha (w11) and Napa Grenache (w12) must be differentiated; candidates need to identify the grape variety across three radically different production styles and place each origin precisely.
