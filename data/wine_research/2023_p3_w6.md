# Les Clans, Chateau d'Esclans, 2021

## Quick tasting profile
Pale rosé in color but unusually full-bodied and textured for the category. Blind, this should show white peach, nectarine, red berries and subtle oak spice on the nose, with a creamy, weighty palate (from barrel fermentation and lees contact) balanced by fresh acidity and a long finish (source: https://rose-wine.com/blogs/the-rose-wine-blog/exploring-chateau-d-esclans-rose-wines-from-provence). The combination of pale color with genuine mid-palate weight and oak-derived spice is the key signal distinguishing this from commodity Provence rosé.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Grenache, Vermentino (Rolle) and Syrah; exact 2021 percentages not published, though a 2020 listing for the same wine gives 81% Grenache / 19% Vermentino as an indicator of typical proportions (source: https://plummarket.com/store/2020-chateau-d-esclans-les-clans-rose-cotes-de-provence.html)
- Vineyard: estate-grown fruit from older parcels on the Château d'Esclans property near La Motte, on gravel-calcareous, clay and limestone soils; estate vineyards total around 44 ha with an average vine age near 35 years (source: https://millesima-usa.com/chateau-d-esclans-les-clans-2021.html)
- Vinification: hand-harvested early morning to preserve freshness, optical sorting, closed-circuit/inert handling, must cooled to c. 7-8°C; approximately 90% free-run juice with c. 10% from a light first pressing; fermented in 600-litre demi-muids (a mix of new and one-year-old oak) with individual barrel temperature control (source: https://rose-wine.com/blogs/the-rose-wine-blog/exploring-chateau-d-esclans-rose-wines-from-provence)
- Elevage: extended barrel ageing on lees, roughly 10-12 months, with bâtonnage (lees stirring) around twice weekly (source: https://rose-wine.com/blogs/the-rose-wine-blog/exploring-chateau-d-esclans-rose-wines-from-provence)

## Vintage character (2021 Côtes de Provence)
No château-published vintage note for 2021 was located; the wine is critically well regarded for the vintage (James Suckling 94, Robert Parker/Wine Advocate 93, Wine Enthusiast 92, Wine Spectator 90), described as having a serious, oak-spiced nose with fresh plum and red-berry fruit and a dense phenolic structure for the category, suggesting a vintage that supported ripeness and structure without loss of freshness (source: https://enotecacollova.com/en/les-clans-2021-p9714). Critics note it may benefit from a further year or two in bottle rather than demanding immediate drinking, unusual for Provence rosé.

## Producer style
Château d'Esclans, under Sacha Lichine (with winemaking guidance historically linked to Patrick Léon), pioneered the "serious" premium Provence rosé category — pale, dry, structured and built for ageing rather than simple immediate consumption. The estate's range runs from the entry Whispering Angel through Château d'Esclans, Les Clans and the flagship Garrus (100% barrel-fermented old-vine Grenache), with Les Clans occupying the upper-mid tier: barrel-fermented, textured and positioned well above commodity Provence pink, priced roughly £60-70 / $85-100 at retail (source: https://wine-searcher.com/find/d+esclans+les+clan+rose+cote+de+provence+france/1/usa).
