# Vermentino di Sardegna Costamolino, Argiolas, 2024

## Quick tasting profile
Pale lemon-green, youthful; a fresh, purity-driven nose of white peach, lemon zest and green apple lifted by a distinct Mediterranean herb/pine-mint note. The palate is dry, medium-bodied and racy, with a silky lees texture, a saline-mineral edge and a persistent, savoury finish — no oak influence, built entirely around freshness rather than weight.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Vermentino
- Vineyard: Argiolas estate sites in Serdiana, Trexenta hills; silty-loam soils, Guyot-trained, SE–S exposure, ~190 m elevation, vines planted 1980–1990, yield ~3.2 tons/acre
- Harvest: reported as September (a secondary retail note cites Aug–Sept); no hand-vs-machine or night-picking detail published (Source needed)
- Vinification: only free-run must used, natural cold decantation/settling at 10–12°C, then temperature-controlled fermentation (16–18°C per one vendor account; importer sheet states 59°F/~15°C over 20–30 days) entirely in stainless steel — no oak
- Elevage: 3–4 months on fine lees in stainless steel, 1–3 months bottle ageing before release
- ABV / RS / TA: importer technical sheet reports 13.7% / 1.09 g/L RS / 5.3 g/L TA / pH 3.33 (a separate retail listing gives divergent figures — 14.5% / 1.59 g/L / 5.79 g/L / pH 3.36 — flagged as a discrepancy between sources; this exam's stated 13.5% ABV sits between the two and is treated as the vintage's bottled figure)
*(Night/cool-harvest timing and hand- vs machine-picking are not confirmed in any cited source — Source needed for the freshness-preservation angle beyond cool must-settling and low fermentation temperature.)*

## Vintage character (2024 Sardinia/Italy)
2024 was Italy's hottest vintage of the century to date on aggregate, following a mild, dry winter and warm spring; national volumes were nonetheless up (~8%) year-on-year. For Sardinian Vermentino specifically, producers report bright, "coastal" freshness with zesty citrus/herbal aromatics preserved through stainless-steel fermentation, and a mineral, saline character (seaweed/iodine notes flagged by some critics) — i.e. a warm year where cellar technique (cool settling, controlled ferment temperature, no malolactic emphasis, no oak) was the primary lever protecting acidity rather than the growing season itself.

## Producer style
Cantine Argiolas is Sardinia's benchmark quality producer, family-run from Serdiana since the 1930s and central to Sardinia's modern reputation (led for decades by the late Antonio Argiolas, now Valentina Argiolas). The house style favours purity and site expression over oak: native varieties (Vermentino, Nasco, Nuragus for whites; Cannonau, Carignano, Bovale for reds) made with precise, cool, stainless-steel-led winemaking for the "classic" range (Costamolino sits here), reserving oak and longer ageing for prestige bottlings like Turriga. The estate promotes vineyard sustainability initiatives (biodiversity plots, subsurface irrigation, beneficial-insect release) though no formal organic/biodynamic certification is documented for Costamolino specifically.

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 10 of Paper 1, Question 3 ("wines 10–12 are from regions influenced by the Mediterranean Sea"): Costamolino represents an unoaked, stainless-steel, high-volume Mediterranean island white where freshness is preserved almost entirely through low-temperature, reductive cellar technique in a hot climate — a clean contrast case against Santorini Assyrtiko (naturally acid-retentive despite extreme heat) and white Bandol (barrel-textured, organic, high-elevation) in the same three-wine set.
