# Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Domaine du Vieux Lazaret, 2013. Rhône Valley, France

## Quick tasting profile
Deep ruby with a browning rim developing by the time of the exam sitting. Reviewers describe the 2013 as warm and complex on the nose — black raspberry, plum, garrigue herbs, leather and a touch of vanilla/cassis — with a full-bodied, plush palate, firm-but-integrating tannin and a long, slightly smoky finish (Wine Advocate 92, Wine Enthusiast 92, Natalie MacLean 92) (source: nataliemaclean.com). The 2013 vintage overall is described by Jancis Robinson as darker and more austere/concentrated than the softer, more opulent recent vintages, with kirsch, blackberry, pepper and crushed-rock notes (source: jancisrobinson.com).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Grenache-dominant Southern Rhône blend; one retailer review cites a specific 2013 blend of roughly 60% Grenache, 25% Syrah, 10% Mourvèdre, 5% Cinsault (source: nataliemaclean.com) — treat this as indicative rather than a confirmed estate tech sheet, as Vieux Lazaret's own published blend proportions were not independently located.
- Vineyard: Domaine du Vieux Lazaret is the largest estate in the Châteauneuf-du-Pape appellation — c. 100 ha (250+ acres) across 35 parcels, owned by the Quiot family, who acquired the property (a former 18th-century Lazarist hospice site) from the commune in the 19th century (source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domaine_du_Vieux_Lazaret)
- Soils: Classic CdP terroir diversity across the estate's many parcels, including the famous galets roulés over sandy-clay subsoil typical of the appellation (source: Wikipedia; exact parcel-level soil breakdown for the 2013 blend — Source needed)
- Vinification: Fermentation in concrete (cement) vats, 15–21 days maceration with twice-daily pump-overs, indigenous yeast (source: famillequiot.com)
- Elevage: c. 18 months, split between cement tanks and large old oak foudres, with only a small proportion (approx. 15%) new oak (source: famillequiot.com)
- ABV: 14.0% per exam label (some vintages/cuvées from the estate are listed elsewhere at 14.5% — Source needed to confirm exact 2013 "Tradition" cuvée figure)

## Vintage character (2013 Châteauneuf-du-Pape / Southern Rhône)
2013 was a low-yield, atypical vintage in the Southern Rhône: cooler and later than the abundant, higher-alcohol vintages of the 2000s, producing wines with retained natural acidity and moderate alcohol rather than jammy ripeness. Jancis Robinson characterises the reds as darker and more austere/concentrated, with pronounced kirsch, blackberry, pepper and crushed-rock character, and recommends focusing on producers' standard "Tradition" bottlings over prestige cuvées in this year (source: jancisrobinson.com/articles/rhone-2013-chateauneufdupape). The appellation's own vintage page confirms the low-yield, moderate-alcohol, good-acidity profile (source: en.chateauneuf.com).

## Producer style
Domaine du Vieux Lazaret is owned by the Quiot family, who also control other Rhône estates, and is the largest single landholder in Châteauneuf-du-Pape (source: Wikipedia). It is a traditional, large-volume, widely distributed producer — a reliable, commercially available benchmark CdP rather than a cult/garage estate. Elevage leans toward large, mostly neutral vessels (cement and old foudres) rather than new small oak, consistent with a traditional Southern Rhône house style (source: famillequiot.com).

## Why it's in this exam
Wines 7-8 in 2017 Paper 3 Q4 share a dominant grape variety: wine 7 is Torbreck "Cuvée Juveniles" 2014, Barossa Valley (14.5%) — a New World Grenache-based blend (per `data/wines.json`). Vieux Lazaret's CdP is the Old World Grenache-dominant benchmark in the pair, testing candidates' ability to identify origin via garrigue/herbal character, moderate-alcohol restraint, and traditional large-vessel elevage against the riper, more overtly fruit-forward Barossa GSM, and to compare market positioning (large-volume traditional AOC producer vs. boutique New World cult blend).
