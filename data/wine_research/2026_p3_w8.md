# Late Bottled Vintage Port, Taylor's, 2020

## Quick tasting profile
Deep, glossy purple-black with a vivid ruby rim — far darker and more youthful in colour than the tawny alongside it. Nose of black cherry, cassis, plum and dark chocolate with rose-petal and orange-peel lift; palate is voluptuous, sweet and full-bodied with firm, "tense, linear" tannins and a long finish (source: German importer technical sheet, weinquelle.com/datenblaetter/s30311.pdf; Taylor's own LBV 2020 page). Unlike an aged tawny, no bottle sediment forms and the wine is filtered and ready to drink without decanting — the fresh, tannic, "reductively" wood-aged counterpart to a long, oxidatively cask-aged tawny.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Traditional Douro red field blend — Tinta Amarela, Tinta Barroca, Tinta Roriz, Touriga Franca, Touriga Nacional, Tinto Cão named on the German market technical sheet (source: weinquelle.com/datenblaetter/s30311.pdf)
- Vineyard: Taylor's Douro estate quintas (Vargellas, Terra Feita and others in the Cima Corgo/Douro Superior); single-harvest (2020) selection of the house's best, most full-bodied red Ports (source: Taylor's LBV product page)
- Vinification: Grapes fortified with 77%-ABV grape spirit at roughly the midpoint of fermentation (when about half the natural sugar has converted), the same mid-fermentation arrest used for all red Port styles including Vintage Port and the tawnies (source: www.taylor.pt/en/what-is-port-wine/how-is-port-made)
- Elevage: Bottled after four to six years in wood — markedly shorter, more oxygen-limiting cask ageing than an age-indicated tawny; Taylor's LBV is a **filtered** style (the "traditional/unfiltered LBV" category exists at other houses but not, per available evidence, from Taylor's), so the deposit stays in the cask rather than the bottle and the wine needs no decanting (source: weinquelle.com sheet; Taylor's LBV page)
- ABV / RS / TA: 19% (confirmed by the producer's own site and independently by the German technical sheet, though two other merchant listings show 20% — treated as the less reliable outlier) / residual sugar not published for the 2020 release specifically, but Taylor's house LBV consistently reports 99-103 g/dm3 across recent vintages (2018 LBV: 99 g/dm3; 2019 LBV: 99-103 g/dm3 across two importer sheets) — the 2020 figure is very likely in this same band; treat the exact 2020 number as **source needed** / total acidity ~4.49 g/L (Wine-Searcher aggregate, unverified against a primary sheet)

## Vintage character (2020 Douro)
2020 was an extreme, early, low-yield vintage: record summer heat (hottest July since 1931 at some Douro stations), scarce rainfall through June-July relieved by a late-August rain event, high mildew pressure from a wet spring, and vine phenology roughly two-to-three weeks ahead of normal — the smallest Douro crop in roughly 40 years. Despite the stress, concentration and phenolic ripeness were high; critics rated the vintage "Outstanding," and several houses (Graham's, Quinta do Noval, Churchill's) even declared Vintage Port. Taylor's drew on this small, concentrated 2020 harvest for its LBV, which shows dark fruit, licorice/spice depth and firm tannin consistent with the vintage's concentration (source: symington.com/post/grit-and-reward-2020-douro-harvest-report; Wine Spectator vintage-chart commentary cited via research synthesis).

## Producer style
Taylor's (Taylor, Fladgate & Yeatman, founded 1692, part of the Fladgate Partnership alongside Fonseca, Croft and Krohn) is widely regarded as the most prestigious Vintage Port house and, notably, is credited as the inventor/pioneer of the LBV category in the 1970s under Alistair Robertson, created to give a high-quality, ready-to-drink alternative to Vintage Port for the trade. Taylor's positions itself as "the international leading LBV producer," and the LBV line trades on that pioneering reputation as a more affordable, no-decanting entry point into the house's Vintage Port style (source: www.taylor.pt/en/port-wine/late-bottled-vintage; weinquelle.com sheet).
