# Vouvray Brut, Château Moncontour, NV

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold with a fine, persistent mousse from traditional-method second fermentation and extended lees ageing. Nose combines classic Chenin orchard/stone fruit (apple, quince, white fruit) with biscuity/pastry autolytic notes. Palate has good structure and a round, creamy entry with well-integrated carbonation and a lingering finish — dosage sits near the top of the legal Brut band, so expect a touch more perceived roundness/generosity than a bone-dry Champagne or Crémant, without tipping into off-dry (source: haywines.co.uk product description; theliquorbarn.com).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Chenin Blanc, hand-harvested from Vouvray parcels including Rochecorbon, on the tuffeau limestone ridge above the Loire (source: haywines.co.uk; vouvray.com)
- Vinification: **traditional method (méthode traditionnelle)** — secondary fermentation in sealed bottle, Champagne-style (source: vouvray.com, official appellation product listing for this exact wine)
- Elevage: **~14 months on lees** post second fermentation before riddling and disgorgement (source: morrishandbanham.com merchant tech sheet — producer's own site confirms the method but not the precise duration, so this figure is merchant-sourced, not producer-confirmed)
- ABV / RS / TA: 12.5% / **dosage ≈ 11 g/L RS** — just under the 12 g/L Brut ceiling, so legally Brut but at the generous end of the category (source: theliquorbarn.com merchant spec; not independently confirmed by the producer's own page, flagged as merchant-sourced)
- Malolactic / oak: one synthesized source claimed "malolactic fermentation in barrel" and "regular batonnage" for Moncontour Chenin, but this could not be confirmed as specific to the sparkling Brut base wine rather than the estate's still Vouvray range, and traditional-method base wines are more commonly vinified in stainless steel to preserve acidity for the second fermentation. **Treated as unresolved — oak_signature omitted from frontmatter rather than guessed.**

## Vintage character (NV)
This is a non-vintage, multi-year blend, so there is no single-vintage weather narrative to report; the house style (14 months lees, Brut dosage near the top of the legal range) is designed for consistency across base-wine years rather than to showcase one vintage's character. Source needed for the specific base-wine years blended into the current release.

## Producer style
Château Moncontour is a well-known Vouvray estate (130 ha per merchant sourcing) on the tuffeau limestone slopes near Rochecorbon, producing the full Vouvray spectrum — still (sec/demi-sec/moelleux) and traditional-method sparkling — and positioned as a mid-market, widely-exported, commercially-established Loire Chenin house rather than a boutique grower-producer. The Brut bottling is the entry point into the estate's sparkling range, alongside a "Tête de Cuvée" Brut (source: nicolas.com listing distinguishing the two cuvées) — worth noting to avoid conflating this standard Brut with the estate's higher tier.

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 3 of the four-country sparkling flight (Q1, Paper 3), immediately before the English Blanc de Blancs at Wine 4 — a deliberate pairing that tests whether candidates can distinguish two traditional-method, cool-climate sparklers by base variety and site rather than defaulting both to "similar cool-climate fizz." Chenin Blanc's distinctive quince/wax/honeyed-apple varietal character under Loire limestone, combined with NV blending (vs. the English wine's likely vintage or house-blend framing) and a generous Brut dosage, are the discriminating cues the examiner is testing for in part (c).
