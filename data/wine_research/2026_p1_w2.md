# Chardonnay, Greywacke, 2022

## Quick tasting profile
Bright citrus (lemon, pink grapefruit) and stone/yellow fruit (white nectarine, peach, mango) layered over a flinty, reductive, struck-match minerality typical of wild-ferment Marlborough Chardonnay; nutty, toasty barrel notes (hazelnut, toasted almond, brioche/nougat) add textural weight from partial new-oak fermentation and full malolactic conversion, finishing long, dry and mineral rather than overtly buttery (source: Greywacke tasting-note PDF; producer profile interview, IWC/the-buyer.net).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Chardonnay, principally Mendoza clone with some Clone 95 in most vintages
- Vineyard: fruit sourced across multiple long-term grower partnerships in Wairau Plains, lower Brancott Valley, Fairhall, Renwick and Rapaura; wine is made at Dog Point Vineyard's winery facilities
- Vinification: hand-picked, whole-bunch pressed with very low maceration; spontaneous/indigenous (wild) yeast fermentation; majority of the wine fermented and aged in small French oak barriques (Tonnellerie de Mercurey, heavy toast), capped at roughly 20% new oak; complete (100%) malolactic fermentation for textural roundness; periodic battonage (lees stirring) during elevage
- Elevage: c. 11-12 months in barrel for 2019-onward vintages (reduced from a historical c. 18 months for pre-2019 vintages); bottled under screwcap
- ABV / RS / TA: 14% (per data/wines.json); published figures for nearby vintages: 2023 pH 3.2 / TA 6.2 g/L, 2022 pH 3.35 / TA 7.0 g/L (source: producer/retailer tasting-note PDFs) - RS not published, style is dry

## Vintage character (2022 Marlborough)
2022 followed a cooler, more variable growing season in Marlborough compared to the very warm 2021/2023, generally associated with slightly higher retained acidity and a leaner, more citrus-driven fruit profile in the region's Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc — consistent with the brighter, tenser style noted for Greywacke's cooler-year Chardonnays versus its richer, more tropical warm-vintage releases (general Marlborough vintage character; dedicated 2022 vintage report - source needed).

## Producer style
Greywacke is Kevin Judd's own label (founded 2009 after his long tenure as founding winemaker at Cloudy Bay), made using Dog Point's winery infrastructure with fruit from long-standing grower relationships rather than estate vineyards. Judd is an outspoken advocate for barrel-fermented, wild-yeast Marlborough Chardonnay as a serious, ageworthy style distinct from the region's Sauvignon Blanc-dominated commercial identity; the house consistently uses restrained new-oak levels (~20% ceiling), full malolactic and lees work to build texture while preserving Marlborough's naturally high acidity and flinty/reductive character (source: Decanter interview with Kevin Judd; IWC producer profile). Greywacke sits at a premium boutique tier within Marlborough, priced and positioned above commodity regional Chardonnay.

## Why it's in this exam
Wines 1-6 of 2026 Paper 1 Question 1 are all Chardonnay from six different countries; Greywacke represents the New World, cool-climate/wild-ferment benchmark — its full malolactic, partial new-oak barrel fermentation and extended lees work give a textural, barrel-influenced style that contrasts directly with the Old World restraint of the Meursault (2026_p1_w1) and tests candidates on distinguishing New Zealand's flinty-reductive signature from other New World winemaking approaches in the flight.
