# I Sassi, Chianti Classico DOCG, Melini, 2021

## Quick tasting profile
Bright ruby red. Nose of crunchy red fruit (cherry, raspberry), subtle florals, faint earthy and balsamic hints. Medium-bodied palate balancing softness and tension, fleshy/firm tannin from the estate's stony galestro-derived soils, moderate acidity, and a persistent, slightly spicy, saline finish with lingering wild-berry notes. Reads as a straightforward, fruit-driven, commercially made Chianti Classico normale — accessible and food-friendly rather than concentrated or age-worthy (source: bottleofitaly.com; gruppoitalianovini.it).

## Vintage character (2021 Chianti Classico)
2021 is widely regarded as an excellent, ageworthy Chianti Classico vintage. A cool, wet spring (some localized frost) delayed budbreak and trimmed yields modestly, followed by a dry, sunny summer with only sporadic rain and helpful late-season diurnal swings that preserved acidity alongside good phenolic ripeness. Producer technical sheets from top estates (Tenuta di Arceno, Castello di Brolio) show ~14-14.5% ABV with fresh, retained acidity (TA ~6.1-6.2 g/L). Vinous and Decanter panel tastings both rate 2021 among the best recent Chianti Classico years — bright, aromatic, intensely fruited wines with ripe, polished tannin, freshness and clear ageing potential, generally suggested for 5-8+ years at the top end (source: vinous.com "Chianti Classico: The Magnificent 2021s"; decanter.com panel tasting). Even a commercial-tier bottling like I Sassi should show good ripeness and retained freshness from this vintage, though its own drinking window (5-7 years) is markedly shorter than the top estates' projections.

## Producer style
Melini is one of Chianti's oldest houses, founded in 1705 by the Florentine Melini family and historically significant for early quality moves (a gold medal in 1877 for spreading Tuscan wine abroad; reportedly the first winery to bottle a single-vineyard Chianti Classico, La Selvanella, in 1969). Today it is a brand of Gruppo Italiano Vini (GIV), Italy's largest wine group, based at a modern four-storey winery in Gaggiano between Poggibonsi and Castellina in Chianti, with a 556-hectare estate (136 ha under vine) and annual production estimated at 3.8-4 million bottles across its range. I Sassi sits at the accessible, high-volume "annata" end of Melini's Chianti Classico line (below Riserva bottlings such as its single-vineyard La Selvanella), positioning it as a well-made but clearly commercial-tier, wide-distribution wine rather than an artisanal or limited-production one (source: gruppoitalianovini.it/en/brand/melini/winery/history; vinicum.com; superiore.de).

## Why it's in this exam
Paper 2, Question 3: wines 9-10 are the Chianti Classico pair. Melini I Sassi is the commercial, high-volume, base-tier "annata" foil against Castello di Ama's Gran Selezione San Lorenzo 2021 (2026_p2_w10) — same country, region and vintage, but opposite ends of the Chianti Classico quality/classification pyramid (large GIV-owned négociant-scale annata vs. estate-grown Gran Selezione from a top single cru). Candidates identify origin (a), then must contrast style and quality within the region (b) — GIV/industrial scale and neutral, minimally new oak vs. an estate Gran Selezione's site specificity and likely more ambitious élevage — and compare maturity (c), where I Sassi's short 5-7 year window argues for a wine drinking in its youth versus a Gran Selezione built for longer development.
