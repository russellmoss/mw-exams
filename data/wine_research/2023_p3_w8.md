# Furmint Tokaji, Úrágya, Dubogó, 2019

*Note on spelling: the exam corpus/source material lists the producer as "Dubogó"; the winery's own branding is "Dobogó." This brief treats them as the same estate — Dobogó Winery, Tokaj — and cites Dobogó's own materials throughout.*

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold, textural and mineral-driven: ripe stone fruit (peach, nectarine, dried pineapple), citrus zest and a saline, volcanic minerality, with a whisper of vanilla/toasted-oak spice from partial barrel ageing. Bone-dry (2 g/L RS) but not lean — the palate is medium-bodied with crisp, racy acidity and a long, dry, mineral finish (source: https://www.libertywines.co.uk/our-portfolio/DO105-uragya-tokaji-furmint).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Furmint (source: https://www.dobogo.hu/en/our-wines)
- Vineyard: Úrágya, a single site on the western edge of Mád, on volcanic rhyolite tuff interspersed with pumice and quartz over a thin, moisture-retentive clay-rich topsoil (source: https://media.libertywine.co.uk/Perfion/FactSheet.Report?id=183042)
- Vinification: hand-harvested fully ripe fruit, very fast and gentle destemming and pressing to preserve aromatic freshness; no skin contact reported (source: https://www.dobogo.hu/en/our-wines)
- Elevage: 10-12 months in 300L Hungarian oak casks, 50% new and 50% two-year-old (source: https://www.dobogo.hu/en/our-wines)
- ABV / RS / TA / pH: 13.5% / 2.03 g/L / 6.9 g/L / 3.05 (source: https://media.libertywine.co.uk/Perfion/FactSheet.Report?id=183042)
- Ageing potential: producer states a minimum of 15 years for its dry Furmint program (source: https://www.dobogo.hu/en/our-wines)

## Vintage character (2019 Tokaj, dry wines)
2019 was a warm, early season: heavy spring rain recharged soils after the 2018 drought, then summer turned hot with heat spikes (regionally into the mid-30s°C) that pushed early flowering and an early-September harvest for dry Furmint; intermittent summer rain raised fungal disease pressure and cut yields regionally. The resulting dry Furmints, including benchmark bottlings from Disznókő, Royal Tokaji and Patricius, are widely described as ripe, mineral and textured, with retained acidity (roughly 6.5-7.5 g/L) and ABVs in the 13-14% range — consistent with this wine's own numbers (source: https://news.tokajtoday.com/2020/01/22/vintage-report-2019).

## Producer style
Dobogó is a small, family-run Tokaj estate owned by Izabella Zwack (of the Zwack family behind the Unicum liqueur) alongside winemaker Attila Domokos, who acquired and restored the historic property (with an 1869 cellar) in 1995. The estate farms organically and frames its "green philosophy" as central to the wines, aiming to let Furmint's inherent volcanic minerality show through with only a light oak veneer rather than heavy winemaking (source: https://www.dobogo.hu/en/our-wines). Dobogó is part of the modern wave of quality-focused dry Tokaj producers — alongside names like Royal Tokaji, Patricius, Oremus and Szepsy — that has repositioned Furmint as a serious, ageworthy dry white over the last two decades (source: https://www.jancisrobinson.com/articles/in-a-ferment-about-furmint).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 8 opens 2023 Paper 3, Question 3, paired with Wine 9 (Disznókő Tokaji Aszú 5 Puttonyos) under the instruction that both wines are "from the same region." The pairing tests whether candidates can identify Tokaj/Furmint from both wines while correctly distinguishing the modern dry, minimal-intervention single-vineyard style (this wine) from the classic botrytis-affected, selectively harvested Aszú style (Wine 9) — and can articulate how radically different production choices within one region and one dominant grape variety produce opposite finished wines.
