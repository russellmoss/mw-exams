# Garnacha Old Vines "Cabrida", Celler de Capçanes, 2018

## Quick tasting profile
Deep violet-ruby, full-bodied dry Garnacha built on centenarian bush vines: expect ripe black cherry, blueberry and raspberry-marmalade fruit layered with violet, rose, white pepper, anise and garrigue herbs, framed by toasty French-oak spice from foudre ageing (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf; https://www.alliancewine.com/celler-de-capcanes/cabrida-2021-1734). The palate is concentrated and full yet retains vibrant Mediterranean-altitude acidity and fine, ripe tannin, giving a long, warm, structured finish typical of premium old-vine Montsant Garnacha rather than a rustic co-op blend.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Garnacha (Grenache Noir)
- Vineyard: Blend of roughly fifteen small old-vine plots around the village of Capçanes on the slopes of the Serra de Llaberia, 300-550m altitude, vines aged 60-110+ years, trained as traditional bush vines (gobelet), dry-farmed (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf)
- Soils: Mixed clay-granite, slate/schist and mineral-rich limestone across the constituent plots, reflecting Montsant's heterogeneous geology (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf)
- Vinification: Hand-harvested, sorted, temperature-controlled fermentation (roughly 24-28C) with indigenous yeasts, around 30 days' maceration with a portion vinified with stems for freshness; malolactic fermentation started in stainless steel and finished in French oak (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf)
- Elevage: Approximately 12 months in French oak foudres (large-format, medium/light toast) followed by a short stainless-steel rest before bottling, unfined/soft-filtered only when necessary (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf)
- ABV / RS / TA: 15.0% (exam-stated; recent vintages 14.5-15.0%) / dry, not quantified / Source needed

General regional pattern: Montsant has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers moderated by altitude and diurnal temperature swings on the slopes encircling Priorat, which preserves acidity in old-vine Garnacha even at high ripeness/alcohol (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montsant_DO).

## Producer style
Celler de Capçanes is a cooperative founded in 1933 by five local families, today comprising around 80 member families farming roughly 250 ha across Montsant's mosaic of slate, limestone, clay and sandy soils at 200-700m (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/en/vineyards; https://www.cellercapcanes.com/en/press). The coop transformed from bulk producer to quality-focused winery from the early 1990s, is also known internationally for its kosher range (Flor de Primavera/Peraj Ha'abib, launched 1996), and is regarded as a benchmark cooperative bridging traditional Montsant winemaking with modern, terroir-driven, old-vine cuvees such as Cabrida — its flagship single-vineyard-style Garnacha, positioned at the premium end of the Montsant category and reflecting the schist/llicorella soils the DO shares with neighbouring Priorat (source: https://www.cellercapcanes.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Cabrida-EN.pdf; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montsant_DO).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 11 sits in 2023 Paper 3 Question 4 (wines 10-12, three countries, one grape variety). This dry, old-vine, oak-aged Montsant Garnacha is the Spanish "control" against the French fortified Maury (w10) and the lighter, whole-cluster Napa Grenache (w12) — testing whether candidates can hold Grenache/Garnacha as the common thread while precisely separating Montsant's schist/limestone old-vine style (full, structured, oak-inflected) from Priorat next door and from the other two origins.
