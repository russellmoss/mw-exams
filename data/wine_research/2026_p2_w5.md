# Shiraz The Factor, Torbreck, 2019

## Quick tasting profile
Deep, glossy red-purple, near-opaque. Nose is a dense stack of blackberry, satsuma/blood plum and morello cherry compote over dark chocolate, vanilla, cedar and sweet spice, with savoury tertiary notes (olive tapenade, cured meat, graphite, pipe smoke) emerging with air. Palate is dense and mouth-filling with polished, substantial tannins and enough acid to keep the finish long rather than blowsy; a powerful, oak-framed, old-vine Barossa Shiraz built for the mid-to-long term (source: aggregated critic/trade notes, [1][2][3]).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Shiraz (source: [4], retail/spec listing)
- Vineyard: fruit sourced from Barossa Valley parishes Gomersal, Krondorf, Marananga and Ebenezer (source: [4][1])
- Fermentation: not specified for 2019 in available sources (basket-pressing and separate small-parcel ferments are Torbreck's stated general practice, not confirmed 2019-specific)
- Elevage: 12 months in French oak barriques for the 2019 vintage — shorter than the 24-month regime used in 2020-2023, and the new-oak percentage for 2019 specifically is not disclosed (2018 used ~50% new, 2020-2023 use ~40% new, so 2019's shorter contact likely implies a lower new-oak share, though this is inference, not a stated figure) (source: [4][5])
- ABV / RS / TA: label ABV reported at 15.0-15.84% depending on source (Vivino lists 15.84%, retail spec sheets 15%) / RS not published, dry table wine / TA not published

## Vintage character (2019 Barossa Valley)
2019 was the driest calendar year on record at the Nuriootpa weather station (7% below average annual rainfall, only 60% of average Jan-June rain), with an October frost, strong flowering-period winds, and a severe November-December heatwave (42C on 20 November; eleven days above 35C in December). The stress produced smaller berries with higher skin-to-pulp ratios — deep colour, concentrated dark fruit, firm tannic grip and elevated potential alcohol, with generally lower acidity than cooler vintages; irrigation management was critical to avoiding over-extraction (source: Barossa Grape & Wine Association 2020 vintage report retrospective on 2019 conditions [6]).

## Producer style
Torbreck was founded in 1994 by David Powell in Marananga on the Barossa's Western Ridge, went through receivership in 2002, and has been under Peter Kight's ownership since 2008, with Ian Hongell as Chief Winemaker/GM since 2016. The house is known for powerful, old-vine, Rhone-inspired Barossa reds (RunRig Shiraz-Viognier, The Steading GSM) made with minimal intervention — separate small-lot ferments, large-format French oak, unfined/unfiltered bottling. The Factor sits just below RunRig in the flagship hierarchy: a 100% Shiraz statement wine built for power and longevity (source: [7], Torbreck company history).

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 5 of Paper 2, Q2 (wines 4-6, "three different countries," a deliberate flight of powerful, high-alcohol, oak-shaped reds). The Factor is the New World single-varietal benchmark — old-vine, warm-climate Barossa Shiraz with heavy new-oak influence — set against Italian appassimento power (Amarone) and a Napa Bordeaux-style blend, testing whether the candidate can separate "concentration from ripeness/warm climate" from "concentration from drying" and "concentration from blend/new-oak Napa style."
