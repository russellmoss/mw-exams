# Nuits-Saint-Georges, 1er Cru Clos des Argillières, Domaine Rion, 2011. Burgundy, France

## Quick tasting profile
No tasting note specific to the 2011 vintage of this wine was located; the note below is inferred from the domaine's confirmed site character and house style, not a verified 2011 tasting report — flagged accordingly. Clos des Argillières sits on "poor brown soil on [a] base of pink Premeaux limestone," planted with vines around 50 years old at the time of this vintage, on the slope directly opposite the domaine (source: truenorthwines.com/patrice-rion, the domaine's US importer page). The house does not pursue maximal extraction, aiming instead to preserve "the freshness and minerality of Burgundian Pinot Noir" (same source) — consistent with a medium-bodied, limestone-driven, structured but not heavy style typical of southern Nuits-Saint-Georges 1er Cru. ## Producer and label disambiguation
The exam label reads simply "Domaine Rion." Several related but legally distinct Rion domaines exist in Prémeaux-Prissey/Nuits-Saint-Georges (Daniel Rion et Fils; Patrice Rion; Michèle & Patrice Rion), but the family estate was not split into these separate legal entities until a settlement concluded in summer 2022, when Daniel Rion's children (Pascale, Patrice, Christophe, Olivier, and Olivier's son Jean-Charles) each took over their own parcels (source: insideburgundy.com/overview/domaine-daniel-rion). Since this wine is from the 2011 vintage — well before that 2022 split — it should be attributed to the unified, pre-split **Domaine Daniel Rion (et Fils)**, based in Nuits-Saint-Georges, rather than to the later independent Patrice Rion or Michèle & Patrice Rion labels. Clos des Argillières was historically the family's flagship 1er Cru bottling under the Daniel Rion name (older vintages — 1990 rated 93 pts Wine Spectator, 1991 rated 92 pts Burghound — were released under this same producer name).

## Vintage character (2011 Côte de Nuits / Burgundy)
2011 is generally regarded as a "very good," elegant, fruit-forward vintage in the Côte de Nuits, positioned between the rain-affected, less consistent 2010 and the smaller, more concentrated/powerful 2012. Decanter's vintage guide notes the Côte de Nuits "had the edge" over the Côte de Beaune that year, having largely escaped a damaging 12 July hailstorm that struck the southern Côte de Beaune and Côte Chalonnaise, and describes wines with "ample structure and persistence" that are "enjoyable and accessible" while reflecting their terroirs well (source: decanter.com/2011-vintage-guide-for-red-bungundy). Jasper Morris's retrospective characterises 2011 as lighter-styled than the great vintages, with the best wines fully approachable now but capable of ageing gracefully for a decade or more, particularly in strong vintages/sites (source: insideburgundy.com/report/2011-an-encouraging-retrospective). For a southern Nuits-Saint-Georges 1er Cru like Clos des Argillières, this points to a medium-weight, already-approachable wine at six years old (2017 tasting), rather than a dense, closed, long-term-cellaring style.

## Why it's in this exam
2017 Paper 3 Q5 pairs wines 9-10 as being from the same country and region, asking candidates to identify origin, deduce the likely vintage, and comment on quality and further ageing potential. This Nuits-Saint-Georges 1er Cru sits at the top of the pairing's quality ladder against the Côte de Nuits Villages Vieilles Vignes (w9), one vintage apart (2011 vs 2012) — testing the candidate's ability to place Burgundy Pinot Noir correctly within the region's hierarchy (village vs Premier Cru) and to use structural/textural differences (site-driven concentration and length, the Clos des Argillières limestone signature) alongside vintage character to differentiate the two wines and their remaining ageing potential.
