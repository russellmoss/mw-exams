# Blanc de Blancs, Harrow & Hope, 2020

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold with a fine, persistent mousse. Nose of lemon curd, orange blossom, baked apple and brioche, with toasted-nut/digestive-biscuit and a whisper of vanilla from the barrel-fermented fraction. Palate is bright but creamy-textured, ripe (apple, nectarine, clementine), underpinned by chalky minerality, finishing long, nutty and faintly saline (source: IWSC tasting note; The Real Review, via Tavily research).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Chardonnay
- Vineyard: Estate vineyards on the Chiltern Hills chalk escarpment, Marlow, Buckinghamshire (Thames & Chilterns)
- Vinification: 50% fermented in 500-litre oak puncheons, 50% in stainless steel; spontaneous/wild-yeast fermentation; malolactic conducted
- Elevage: ~34-35 months on lees before disgorgement (batch bottled 11 May 2021, disgorged 4 June 2024); closed with technical cork
- Dosage: 8.1 g/L (published) - places the wine in the Brut category
- ABV / RS / TA: 12% / dosage 8.1 g/L (exact post-ferment RS not separately published) / ~8.5 g/L
- Certified organic viticulture; low-intervention, minimal-sulphur winemaking; vegan
(source: IWSC technical entry; Bound by Wine product page - via Tavily research)

## Vintage character (2020 England)
2020 was a warm, relatively early and generous English vintage after several difficult years, giving riper fruit and good yields for many Sussex/Kent/Chiltern producers, which supports the ripe-fruit, less austerely acidic profile described in tasting notes relative to cooler English vintages. *(Source needed for a producer-specific vintage statement; general English 2020 vintage character inferred from trade commentary, flagged here as directional rather than firmly cited.)*

## Producer style
Harrow & Hope is a small, family-run estate (Henry and Kaye Laithwaite) in Marlow, Buckinghamshire, on chalk soils comparable to Champagne's Côte des Blancs - part of the "Thames & Chilterns" English wine region rather than the more established Sussex/Hampshire chalk. The house favours barrel-fermented components, extended lees ageing (34+ months), wild-yeast ferments and organic viticulture, positioning itself at the premium, terroir-driven end of English sparkling wine, distinct from larger-volume Champagne-owned or corporate English producers. This Blanc de Blancs won Blanc de Blancs of the Year (Great Britain) recognition for the 2020 vintage (source: The Real Review, via Tavily research).
