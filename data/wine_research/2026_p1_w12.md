# Château de Pibarnon Blanc, 2024

**IMPORTANT DATA CORRECTION:** This wine is Château de Pibarnon's **white Bandol** ("Le Blanc"), not the estate's red Bandol (which is a Mourvèdre-dominant blend with Grenache, aged 18+ months in foudre). The two wines share a producer and appellation but are entirely different grapes, colours and vinifications. An earlier automated extraction incorrectly resolved this slot to Mourvèdre/Grenache via a red-Bandol appellation lookup that did not account for the "Blanc" designation — that resolution is wrong and should not be used for this wine.

## Quick tasting profile
Pale gold; a clean, refined nose of peach, pear, honeysuckle and orange blossom with a floral lift. The palate is dry, medium-plus in body and smoothly textured — "richly textured, creamy white fruit" per Decanter — carried by saline minerality and elegant bitter (almond/citrus-pith) notes on a long finish. Freshness here reads as structural (saline, bitter, long) rather than overtly high-acid or green.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Bandol Blanc is a blend of Clairette, Bourboulenc and (in smaller measure) Ugni Blanc/Marsanne. Two independent retailer sources (Skurnik, Wine Solutions) give the estate's standing blend as **50% Clairette, 30% Bourboulenc, 20% Marsanne and Ugni Blanc combined**; the producer's own site for the specific 2024 vintage lists **55% Clairette, 30% Bourboulenc, 15% other varieties** — the small variance is consistent with normal vintage-to-vintage blend adjustment rather than a data error, but is flagged as a discrepancy between the estate's own current-vintage page and general retailer descriptions of the wine.
- Vineyard: single north-facing terraced site at 300+ metres — the highest-elevation vineyard in the Bandol appellation — on clay-limestone soil with a high proportion of white chalk; certified organic viticulture.
- Vinification: fermentation with indigenous/wild yeast in stainless steel.
- Elevage: aged in a mix of stainless steel and large, older (non-new) barrels — i.e. neutral wood for textural gain without overt oak flavour or new-oak spice.
- ABV / RS / TA: exam-stated 13.5% ABV; retailer tasting notes describe "medium acidity and medium-plus body" — no published numeric RS/TA/pH found (Source needed).

## Vintage character (2024 Provence/Bandol)
2024 is being reported as an excellent, high-scoring Provence vintage: a cooler, wetter spring slowed early development and kept volumes moderate, followed by steadily rising heat toward harvest that allowed gradual, even phenolic ripening; regional vintage summaries single out Provence (and southern Languedoc) as standout areas in an otherwise mixed, rain-pressured French 2024. The combination of a wet winter/spring (rebuilding water reserves after difficult 2022-23 vintages) and a warm, dry run to harvest is consistent with balanced acidity and full ripeness in Bandol's white blend grapes.

## Producer style
Château de Pibarnon is one of Bandol's grand names, re-established from 1977 by the Comte Henri de Saint-Victor on the appellation's highest and coolest terraces; now run by the family's next generation, the estate is a benchmark for structured, ageworthy Bandol Mourvèdre-based reds as well as this Clairette/Bourboulenc-led white. The house is certified organic, favours indigenous-yeast fermentation and neutral/large-format wood over new oak across the range, and positions its wines at the premium end of Bandol — sold in both magnum and standard bottle formats, reflecting a cellar-worthy, fine-wine market position rather than a everyday-drinking one.

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 12 of Paper 1, Question 3 ("wines 10-12 are from regions influenced by the Mediterranean Sea"): the white Pibarnon is the altitude/organic-viticulture case for freshness in a warm Mediterranean appellation — its single highest, north-facing, 300m+ site is the primary freshness lever, reinforced by neutral (not new) wood ageing that adds texture without masking acidity — set against Costamolino (cellar-technique freshness) and Thalassitis (ancient-vine/volcanic-soil freshness) in the same three-wine Mediterranean set. Because Bandol is overwhelmingly known for its red Mourvèdre, this white bottling is also a likely candidate to catch out candidates who default to "Bandol = Mourvèdre" without registering the "Blanc" cue in the stem/label.
