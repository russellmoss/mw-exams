# Cuvée Rosé Brut, Laurent-Perrier, NV

## Quick tasting profile
Salmon-pink with fuchsia reflections and a fine, persistent mousse. Blind, this should show vivid, primary red-fruit aromatics — raspberry, redcurrant, strawberry, black cherry — with a fresh, perfumed, moderately full palate carried by balanced acidity and a long finish (source: https://cuveerose.com/data/files/2019/09/laurent-perrier-fiche-cuvee-rose-fr.pdf). The intensity and precision of red-fruit color and aroma (rather than a diluted, blended-in pink hue) are the key clue that this is a maceration rosé, not a rosé d'assemblage.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Pinot Noir (source: https://laurent-perrier.com/en/magazine/expertise/cuvee-rose-the-pinot-noir-experience)
- Vineyard: fruit sourced from ten selected crus in the Montagne de Reims, commonly citing Grand Cru villages such as Ambonnay, Bouzy, Louvois and Tours-sur-Marne (source: https://media.libertywine.co.uk/Perfion/FactSheet.Report?id=70546)
- Vinification: this is a genuine maceration (saignée-style) rosé, not a blend of red and white wine — Laurent-Perrier is one of the few Champagne houses to produce its rosé this way. Whole bunches are sorted and destemmed, then given controlled skin maceration in stainless steel for approximately 48-72 hours, with the marc pumped over roughly every eight hours; free-run juice is separated from the pressed fraction and the two are vinified separately (source: https://laurent-perrier.com/en/magazine/expertise/cuvee-rose-the-pinot-noir-experience)
- Elevage: extended cellar ageing before disgorgement; house materials cite a minimum four years in some technical literature and five years in other house communications (an unreconciled discrepancy in the published sources) (source: https://cuveerose.com/data/files/2019/09/laurent-perrier-fiche-cuvee-rose-fr.pdf)
- Dosage: reported in the 9-10 g/L range across trade sources, consistent with Brut (source: https://champagne411.com/laurent-perrier/cuvee-rose.html)

## Vintage character (NV / house style)
Non-vintage, blended across multiple years and crus to preserve house consistency; the technique of maceration rosé was introduced by Laurent-Perrier in 1968 and the house has refined a proprietary, controlled maceration protocol since (source: https://laurent-perrier.com/en/magazine/expertise/cuvee-rose-the-pinot-noir-experience). The exam-relevant point is method rather than harvest: extracting color and aromatic intensity from short, controlled Pinot Noir skin contact rather than blending in still red wine yields a paler, more precisely fruited, more aromatically primary rosé than a typical rosé d'assemblage.

## Producer style
Laurent-Perrier is a large, family-controlled Grand Marque house particularly associated with elegance, freshness and a lighter dosage/lower-oxidative house style across its range (Ultra Brut, Brut NV, Grand Siecle). Cuvée Rosé is the house's signature and most celebrated wine, widely regarded as a benchmark for maceration-method Champagne rosé and frequently used in blind tastings as the reference point for that production style, distinct from Grand Marques that blend red and white base wines for their rosé (source: https://decanter.com/premium/champagne-laurent-perrier-rose-perfectionists-562851).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 5 opens Question 2 of 2023 Paper 3, where wines 5-7 are made using different methods of production and candidates must identify origin/variety, discuss quality and commercial potential, and identify the key production techniques shaping style. Laurent-Perrier Cuvée Rosé is the textbook case of a maceration/saignée-style Champagne rosé, testing whether candidates can distinguish this production route from the far more common blending method used by most pink Champagne.
