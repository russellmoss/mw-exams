# Maury "Grande Reserve", Domaine Pouderoux, NV. Roussillon, France

## Quick tasting profile
Deep garnet/mahogany colour consistent with extended oxidative ageing. Retail tasting notes for this exact cuvee (Grande Reserve / "6 ans") describe "complex aromas of gingerbread, toffee, orange, vanilla and hints of wax" on the nose, with "toasted brioche, tobacco, leather, mocha with baked fruit" on the palate (source: Fourth & Church listing) — classic Maury rancio/oxidative character: dried fruit, coffee, chocolate, spice. Full-bodied and sweet, with the alcohol (15.5-16%) and residual sugar balanced by acidity and the drying, nutty rancio finish typical of long-cask-aged Grenache VDN.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: Grenache Noir (multiple independent retail listings and the domaine's own materials confirm Grenache as the variety for this cuvee)
- Vineyard: Domaine Pouderoux's Maury parcels, Agly Valley, Pyrenees-Orientales, on black schist and marl-schist soils; the domaine reports vines commonly 30-50 years old (source: Planete Vin listing; domaine site)
- Vinification: Mutage (fortification with neutral grape spirit) arresting fermentation while substantial natural sugar remains — the domaine's standard young "Vendange" cuvee is stopped at roughly 8-10% natural alcohol, per an independent review of the domaine's NV Maury (source: amanwithaglass.wordpress.com)
- Elevage: The "Grande Reserve"/"6 ans" cuvee is aged oxidatively for an extended period before bottling — one UK retailer's product description states "two years in barrel followed by a year outside in glass demijohns before spending a further three years in cask" (source: Fourth & Church), consistent with the "6 ans" (6-year) designation used by other retailers (e.g. Planete Vin)
- ABV: 15.5% (per this exam's stated ABV and multiple retail listings); some current retail listings of the same cuvee show 16% ABV, a minor discrepancy likely reflecting vintage/bottling variation
- RS: Not found as a precise g/L figure for the Grande Reserve cuvee specifically. Maury AOC regulation sets a minimum of 45 g/L residual sugar for VDN (source: Maury AOC cahier des charges PDF); given the early mutage point described for the domaine's young cuvee, the Grande Reserve's actual RS is very likely well above this floor, but an exact number could not be confirmed. Source needed.
- Certification: The domaine is reported as organically certified since 2011, with biodynamic-syndicate involvement noted from 2021 (source: biodyvin.com domaine presentation PDF); a UK retailer's Grande Reserve listing also flags the wine as organic.

## Vintage character (NV)
Non-vintage; the "Grande Reserve"/"6 ans" designation reflects a minimum ageing period (roughly six years across barrel, demijohn and cask) rather than a single harvest's weather character. The oxidative, multi-vessel ageing regime (large old barrels, glass demijohns exposed to temperature swings, and cask) is the primary driver of the wine's rancio, dried-fruit, and nutty tertiary profile, more so than any specific vintage.

## Producer style
Domaine Pouderoux is a family-run Maury estate reporting six generations of history, tracing to 19th-century roots in the appellation. Ownership transitioned from Robert Pouderoux to his cousin Louise Tanguy (with vigneron/manager Leo Hemmer Bury) in a process completed around 2023. The domaine farms roughly 20 hectares and produces around 350 hl (about 45,000 bottles) annually across some 12 cuvees — roughly three-quarters dry wines and one-quarter Vin Doux Naturel (source: biodyvin.com domaine presentation PDF). The estate runs a deliberate two-track VDN program: an early-released, reductively-handled young "Vendange"/grenat style that preserves fresh cherry fruit, and long, oxidatively-aged cuvees (including this Grande Reserve and a Hors d'Age bottling) built on extended barrel/demijohn/cask ageing for rancio complexity.

## Why it's in this exam
Used in 2017 Paper 3 Q6 (wines 11-12, both fortified) testing origin identification, method of production in relation to quality, and target market. As a Grenache-based, extensively oxidatively-aged French VDN, the Maury Grande Reserve is the discriminator against the paired wine (Valdespino Palo Cortado, w11) on: mutage timing (Maury arrests fermentation early to retain natural grape sugar, versus Sherry's fully-fermented-then-fortified, biologically/oxidatively aged system), red-grape/sweet identity versus Palomino-based dry Sherry, and Roussillon/French origin versus Jerez/Spain. The long multi-vessel ageing (documented here at around six years across barrel, demijohn and cask) is directly relevant to the "method of production with reference to quality" part of the question.
