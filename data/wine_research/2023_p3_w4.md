# Riesling Extra Brut Marcobrunn, Schloss Reinhartshausen, NV

## Quick tasting profile
Pale lemon with fine, persistent perlage. Blind, this should signal traditional-method German Riesling Sekt: green apple, lemon, stone fruit (Mirabelle plum, peach) and mineral/flinty aromatics rather than autolytic brioche weight, with a taut, high-acid, light-bodied palate and a very dry, precise finish (source: https://vinello.de/marcobrunn-riesling-sekt-extra-brut-grosse-lage-schloss-reinhartshausen). Its acid spine and site-driven minerality, rather than dosage sweetness, are the giveaways for still-wine-quality fruit converted to sparkling.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Riesling (source: https://vinello.de/marcobrunn-riesling-sekt-extra-brut-grosse-lage-schloss-reinhartshausen)
- Vineyard: single-vineyard fruit from Erbacher Marcobrunn, a VDP Grosse Lage of c. 7.1 ha on the Rhine's right bank between Erbach and Hattenheim, south-facing at 90-100m (source: https://vdp.de/en/the-wines/vineyardonline/lage/7127-marcobrunn)
- Soils: calcareous tertiary marl (Mergel) with loess-rich topsoils (source: https://vdp.de/en/the-wines/vineyardonline/lage/7127-marcobrunn)
- Vinification: base wine fermented in stainless steel at 18-22°C, undergoes malolactic fermentation; staged lees ageing pre-tirage (c. 4 months full lees, then 5 months fine lees, both in stainless steel, no oak); traditional-method (bottle) second fermentation, tirage recorded July 2020, first disgorgement September 2022 (source: https://vineyardbrands.com/content/Tech%20Sheets/0169/0169-25-02-15-14-07-51-204.pdf)
- Elevage: minimum 24 months on lees before disgorgement per the trade technical sheet (a retailer page separately states 48 months, an unreconciled discrepancy); dosage 2 g/L (Extra Brut) (source: https://vineyardbrands.com/content/Tech%20Sheets/0169/0169-25-02-15-14-07-51-204.pdf)
- ABV / RS / TA: 13.0% / c. 5.8 g/L / c. 7.3 g/L (NV listing figures; note a separate 2017 vintage listing shows 12.0% / 2.9 g/L / 7.0 g/L, illustrating vintage-to-vintage variation) (source: https://vinello.de/marcobrunn-riesling-sekt-extra-brut-grosse-lage-schloss-reinhartshausen)

## Vintage character (NV / category character)
Non-vintage, so the exam-relevant point is category and site rather than a single harvest: Marcobrunn is one of the Rheingau's oldest documented sites (references from the 13th century) and a benchmark VDP Grosse Lage normally associated with concentrated, ageworthy dry and sweet still Riesling (source: https://vdp.de/en/the-wines/vineyardonline/lage/7127-marcobrunn). Converting Grosse Lage fruit to a traditional-method Sekt is a deliberate quality statement — German quality Sekt (Winzersekt/estate Sekt from top sites) is a small, prestige-driven category compared to the country's still Riesling output, and this bottling trades on the vineyard's pedigree rather than volume commerciality.

## Producer style
Schloss Reinhartshausen is a historic Rheingau estate (now under the Lergenmüller family) based in Erbach/Eltville with holdings across top sites including Erbacher Marcobrunn and Erbacher Hohenrain (source: https://schloss-reinhartshausen.de/en). The estate produces a full range from dry GG-tier still Rieslings and Prädikat sweet wines through to a dedicated Sekt programme built on single-vineyard fruit — Marcobrunn Extra Brut sits at the top of that Sekt range, positioned as a serious, food-friendly, site-expressive sparkling wine rather than an entry-level fizz.

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 4 opens Question 1 of 2023 Paper 3, where wines 1-4 are traditional-method sparkling wines from four different countries, none from Champagne. Marcobrunn Extra Brut is the curveball among classic sparkling regions: candidates must recognize German Sekt as a category, identify Riesling from its lifted, mineral, non-autolytic aromatics, and place it as a premium, site-specific outlier rather than a bulk Sekt.
