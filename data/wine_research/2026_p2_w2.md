# Regnie, Domaine de la Margot, 2024

## Quick tasting profile
Bright cherry/ruby colour with a raspberry-toned core. Nose of red fruit (raspberry, red berries) with delicate violet and peony floral lift. Palate is juicy, fresh and supple with a firm core and delicate, fine-grained tannins — a classic light-bodied, unoaked, early-drinking cru Beaujolais built around primary fruit rather than structure (source: The Wine Society product page).

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Gamay (Regnie is one of the ten Beaujolais crus)
- Vineyard: The Wine Society's listing cites a 3-hectare plot in Regnie; no further parcel/lieu-dit detail published (Source needed)
- Soils: Regnie appellation as a whole sits on granite (approx. 64%) with piedmont/alluvial deposits (approx. 34%), at elevations around 350m, spanning the communes of Regnie-Durette and Lantignie (source: beaujolais.com)
- Vinification: No producer-specific technical sheet was located for this domaine/cuvee. Best-supported inference from regional practice: Beaujolais cru producers, including Regnie, typically use semi-carbonic maceration (roughly 10-14 days at 18-20degC) in the traditional Beaujolais cuve, producing the kirsch/red-berry aromatics and soft, easy tannic structure seen here — flagged explicitly as an inference, not a confirmed fact for Domaine de la Margot (Source needed for confirmation)
- Elevage: No oak indicated in any source reviewed; treat as stainless/concrete, unoaked, released for early drinking (Source needed)
- ABV / RS / TA: 12.7% (matches exam full_text); RS/TA not published (Source needed)

## Producer style
CellarTracker records attribute this cuvee to "Vignerons de Bel Air" as producer of record for Domaine de la Margot's 2024 Regnie — suggesting the domaine's fruit is vinified/bottled through a grower group rather than being a fully independent estate winery, though no separate winemaker statement or house-style dossier was found. The wine is positioned as an accessible cru Beaujolais: a French retail listing shows prices in the mid-teens to mid-twenties of euros (14.50-24.10 EUR, two figures found on the same listing without reconciliation), and The Wine Society (UK) lists it within an "all the crus" mixed case, i.e. marketed as an entry point into the ten Beaujolais crus rather than a cult/grower-prestige bottling (source: twil.fr; thewinesociety.com).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 2 of a three-wine "same country, different variety" question (2026 Paper 2, Q1) — the Gamay anchor of the set, positioned between a Loire Cabernet Franc and a Jura Trousseau; all three are light-to-medium, cool-climate, unoaked-or-lightly-oaked French reds, testing the candidate's ability to distinguish Gamay's semi-carbonic, kirsch-fruited freshness from Cabernet Franc's leafier/blackcurrant profile and Trousseau's paler, spicier, more savoury character.
