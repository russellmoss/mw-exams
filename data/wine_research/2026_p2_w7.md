# Viña Ardanza, La Rioja Alta S.A., Reserva, 2019

## Quick tasting profile
Deep, bright ruby with only moderate development at this age. Nose of spiced black cherry, plum, clove/vanilla and cedar with balsamic and tobacco lift — a classically oak-driven, "old-school" Rioja profile rather than a fruit-forward one. Palate is medium-to-full bodied, opulent yet lifted by good acidity, with fine, polished, well-integrated tannin and a long, spicy, toasted finish. Reads as a traditional, barrel-aged Reserva blend, not a single-site modernist wine.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 80% Tempranillo, 20% Garnacha (official ficha técnica; note one retailer, The Wine Society, lists it simply as "100% Tempranillo" — the winery's own blend figure is the authoritative source) (source: riojalta.com PDF; thewinesociety.com)
- Vineyard: Tempranillo from ~30-year-old estate parcels La Cuesta (Fuenmayor) and Montecillo (Cenicero), Rioja Alta; Garnacha (~17 years old) from La Pedriza, Tudelilla (Rioja Oriental/Baja), ~550 m, deep rocky soils (source: riojalta.com; klwines.com)
- Vinification: destemmed fruit, primary fermentation ~17 days in stainless steel, natural malolactic fermentation (source: riojalta.com)
- Elevage: Tempranillo and Garnacha aged separately in the winery's own handcrafted American oak barrels — Tempranillo ~36 months, Garnacha ~30 months, with only a minority of new oak (most barrels several years old, from La Rioja Alta's in-house cooperage); five manual rackings by candlelight; components blended before bottling; minimal/no aggressive filtration (source: riojalta.com PDF; millesima.com)
- ABV / RS / TA: 14.5% / 1.7 g/L / 5.5 g/L (pH 3.54); one merchant source gives pH 3.57/TA 5.7 g/L — minor discrepancy, winery figure treated as authoritative (source: riojalta.com PDF)
- Drinking window: "Now to 2032" per The Wine Society; producer's own head winemaker states the wine "will see the greatest evolution in the coming years," implying substantial further cellaring upside (source: thewinesociety.com; riojalta.com blog)

## Vintage character (2019 Rioja)
2019 followed a cool, uneven spring with irregular flowering that cut yields roughly 20% below average, then a warm, dry summer that ripened fruit cleanly; overall vine health was excellent into harvest. The Consejo Regulador and trade press rate 2019 among the strongest recent Rioja vintages — concentrated, powerful, well-balanced wines with round tannins, good freshness and strong long-term ageing potential, comparable to benchmark years. La Rioja Alta's own winemaker compares 2019 to 2001, 2010 and 2015 (source: laprensadelrioja.com; decanter.com premium vintage guide; riojalta.com blog).

## Producer style
La Rioja Alta S.A. (founded 1890) is Rioja's benchmark classicist house: estate-focused, long barrel- and bottle-aged Reservas and Gran Reservas matured almost exclusively in American oak from its own on-site cooperage, with traditional manual rackings rather than modern micro-oxygenation. Viña Ardanza is the house's flagship Reserva (first made 1942) and sits between the everyday Viña Alberdi/Viña Arana and the top-tier Gran Reserva 890/904 in the range — full-bodied, structured, deliberately traditional in style, positioned as an ageworthy, mid-to-upper-premium benchmark rather than a modern single-vineyard wine (source: riojalta.com).

## Why it's in this exam
Paper 2, Question 3: wines 7-8 are the Rioja pair, a deliberate traditional-versus-modern contrast — Viña Ardanza is the long-aged, American-oak, blended Reserva benchmark set against Artuke's modern, French-oak, single-parcel Paso Las Mañas (2026_p2_w8). Candidates are asked to identify origin (a), compare style/quality in context of region (b), and compare maturity/likely vintage (c) — Ardanza's extended American-oak Reserva regime and 2019 vintage character are the key discriminators against Artuke.
