# Riesling Cuvée Frédéric Emile, Trimbach, 2017

## Quick tasting profile
Pale-to-medium gold; a precise, powerful nose of lemon, lime, bergamot and green apple over a chalky, compact stone-fruit minerality. The palate is dense and racy — bright, penetrating acidity (TA ~7.6 g/L, pH ~3.08) framing concentrated citrus and white-peach fruit, finishing bone-dry and long with ginger-spiced minerality (source: Vintageandvine tech data; critic consensus via Vinous/Wine Enthusiast/James Suckling). Some bottles are already showing incipient petrol development (source: CellarTracker), but the wine reads as compact and still youthful for its age.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Riesling
- Vineyard: Blend of two Grand Cru parcels on the south/southeast-facing slopes of Ribeauvillé — Geisberg and Osterberg — with old vines reported at roughly 50-55 years in several estate/retail descriptions; the wine is deliberately bottled under Alsace AOC rather than Grand Cru, per Trimbach's longstanding house policy of not using the Grand Cru designation (source: Vintageandvine, Rare Wine Co.)
- Soils: Grand Cru Geisberg and Osterberg — calcareous/marl slopes typical of Ribeauvillé's Grand Cru amphitheatre (Source needed for parcel-specific detail)
- Vinification: Hand-harvested; stainless-steel, temperature-controlled fermentation; matured on fine lees; no new oak in the modern vinification (source: estate/retail descriptions - exact fermentation vessel and lees-contact duration not published, "Source needed")
- Elevage / bottling date: Not specified in available sources ("Source needed")
- ABV / RS / TA: 13.5% (most-cited figure across retail listings; one Vivino consumer listing gives 12.5%, treated as less reliable) / RS reported 2.4 g/L for 2017 (Vintageandvine); a 2016 tech sheet shows 4.3 g/L, not directly transferable to 2017 / TA ~7.6 g/L, pH ~3.08 (Vintageandvine)

## Vintage character (2017 Alsace)
No dedicated BIVB/regional 2017 Alsace climate report was found in the available sources ("Source needed" for frost, yield and harvest-date data at the regional level). Critics who reviewed this specific bottling describe the wine as "ripe yet brightly acidic," concentrated and built for long ageing, implying a vintage that delivered good ripeness while retaining structural acidity (source: Vinous, Wine Enthusiast reviews of the 2017 CFE). Drinking windows given by critics — Vinous 2026-2039, Wine Enthusiast 2025-2045, James Suckling "best from 2028" — indicate a vintage of real longevity for Trimbach's top cuvées.

## Producer style
Maison Trimbach, family-run in Ribeauvillé since 1626 (13 generations), is Alsace's benchmark for a rigorously dry, high-acid house style, in deliberate contrast to producers who leave noticeable residual sugar. Cuvée Frédéric Emile sits just below Clos Sainte-Hune as the house's second prestige Riesling, sourced from Grand Cru fruit but bottled as Alsace AOC — Trimbach's policy is to reserve the "Grand Cru" label for wines it considers to fully express that status. It is widely regarded as one of Alsace's most age-worthy dry Rieslings, routinely scoring in the mid-90s to high-90s from major critics.

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 8 of Q2's three-wine Riesling flight spanning three countries. At 9 years old in 2026 — right at the start of critics' quoted drinking windows (2025/26 onward) despite being built for decades-long ageing — this is the "deceptively youthful, high-quality Old World benchmark" of the trio, testing candidates' ability to distinguish genuine long-ageing structure (concentration, acid, dry extract) from simple bottle age, and to place Trimbach's atypically bone-dry Alsace style against the sweeter reputation Riesling often carries. See also related Trimbach wines in the same corpus: Gewurztraminer 2012 (2015_p1_w10) and Muscat Réserve 2014 (2017_p1_w8), both demonstrating the same house's dry-leaning philosophy across other varieties.
