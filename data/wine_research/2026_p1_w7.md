# Riesling Federspiel Ried Bruck, Domäne Wachau, 2020

## Quick tasting profile
Pale lemon-gold; a cool, taut nose of white peach, apricot and apple with a smoky, stony minerality typical of the Bruck site. The palate is racy and firmly structured — crystalline, saline acidity carrying ripe stone fruit and a phenolic grip, finishing long and mineral rather than fruit-sweet (source: Domäne Wachau 2020 tech sheet; Robert Parker/Wine Advocate 93pts). At 12.5% ABV and near-zero RS this reads as a classic, bone-dry, mid-weight Wachau Riesling rather than the riper, higher-alcohol Smaragd style.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Riesling
- Vineyard: Ried Bruck, a steep, south-facing terraced single vineyard at the edge of the Wachau growing zone; Domäne Wachau farms roughly 5 ha of the site (2.5 ha strictly selected for this bottling); average yield ~2,500 kg/ha (source: Domäne Wachau site profile / 2019 tech sheet)
- Soils: Terraced primary rock (gneiss/migmatite) typical of the western Wachau, imparting the site's "cool tautness" and minerality (source: Domäne Wachau producer notes)
- Vinification: Hand-harvested (Federspiel category requires hand harvest at 17-18.5° KMW; chaptalisation strictly forbidden); maceration then temperature-controlled fermentation in stainless steel (source: Domäne Wachau 2019/2020 tech sheets)
- Elevage: Matured in stainless steel before bottling, no oak (source: Domäne Wachau tech sheet)
- ABV / RS / TA: 12.5% / 1.6 g/L / 8.5 g/L (2020 official Domäne Wachau tech sheet). Note Federspiel is a Vinea Wachau trademark category defined by harvest ripeness (17-18.5° KMW) and finished alcohol (11.5-12.5%), not a formal RS/dryness rule — but the analytical RS here is well within bone-dry territory.

## Vintage character (2020 Wachau)
Domäne Wachau's own 2020 tech sheet flags a severe late-August hailstorm in the Wachau that year, yet the Bruck bottling still shows strong concentration and structure — Wine Advocate called it "pure, precise, refined... firmly structured, phenolic, driven by crystalline acidity" (93 points). The evidence available doesn't include a full regional (Vinea Wachau/Austrian Wine) climate report for 2020, so broader yield/weather statements beyond the hail event are "Source needed."

## Producer style
Domäne Wachau (formerly Freie Weingärtner Wachau) is the region's dominant growers' cooperative, farming roughly a fifth of all Wachau vineyard, including many of its most celebrated terraced sites. Its single-vineyard Federspiel and Smaragd bottlings are considered benchmarks for site-transparent, non-interventionist Wachau winemaking — stainless steel, no new oak, an emphasis on terroir over ripeness for its Federspiel tier (in contrast to the riper, higher-alcohol Smaragd wines from the same vineyards). Bruck specifically is marketed as one of the coolest, steepest, most mineral sites in the portfolio (source: Domäne Wachau website, single-vineyard pages).

## Why it's in this exam
Wine 7 of Q2's three-wine single-variety flight (Riesling from three different countries). As the youngest-drinking, lowest-ABV, most acid-driven of the three at the 2026 sitting (6 years old, Federspiel = Wachau's "classic" dry mid-weight tier rather than the riper Smaragd), it anchors the Austrian end of the style spectrum and tests the candidate's ability to place Wachau Riesling's bone-dry, mineral, high-acid signature against the off-dry stereotype many candidates default to for Austrian/German Riesling, plus a maturity call on a 6-year-old wine still likely in primary-to-early-secondary phase.
