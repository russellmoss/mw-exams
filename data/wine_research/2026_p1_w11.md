# Assyrtiko Thalassitis, Gaia, 2024

## Quick tasting profile
Pale lemon with a bright topaz rim; a taut, mineral-driven nose of citrus, orchard fruit and, in riper vintages, subtle tropical/floral lift. The palate is bone-dry, high-acid and saline with integrated (rather than hot) alcohol and a phenolic, grapefruit-pith bitterness on the finish — the archetypal racy, volcanic-mineral Santorini Assyrtiko, made entirely without oak to preserve its cutting freshness.

## Technical specs
- Grape variety: 100% Assyrtiko
- Vineyard: old, ungrafted, own-rooted bush vines (reported around 70-80+ years old) on volcanic/pumice soils across Episkopi, Akrotiri and Pyrgos on Santorini; vines trained in the traditional low "kouloura" basket form, which shields grapes from the Aegean meltemi winds and buffers daytime heat
- Vinification: fermented and matured entirely in stainless steel, no oak, no malolactic emphasis — a fully reductive, protective approach designed to retain primary fruit and acidity (Gaia also makes an oak-fermented "Wild Ferment" Assyrtiko as a separate, richer bottling — Thalassitis is deliberately kept in the unoaked, high-acid style)
- Elevage: short stainless-steel ageing pre-release; Gaia separately bottles a barrel-aged "Thalassitis Cellar Aged" version, confirming the standard Thalassitis is the fresh, unoaked expression
- ABV / RS / TA: 13% (this bottling); island-wide 2024 vintage data (Vassaltis, same appellation) show harvest pH as low as 3.1-3.2 and "very high" retained acidity despite extreme heat — precise TA/pH for this specific Thalassitis lot not published (Source needed)
*(Harvest timing for Assyrtiko on Santorini is traditionally very early-morning/night-picked, or picked at first light, to avoid daytime heat; this specific practice is well documented for the appellation generally — e.g. the local "Nykteri" style is literally named for night-harvested fruit — but is not explicitly confirmed as Gaia's own protocol in the sources cited; treat as appellation-level, not producer-confirmed.)*

## Vintage character (2024 Santorini)
2024 was a severe-drought, high-heat year across the Cyclades — the third consecutive drought year, with rainfall roughly 60% of the long-term average and heatwaves from spring through midsummer, plus wind-driven fruit loss and sunburn pressure. Yields island-wide fell 30-40% (a neighbouring Santorini producer, Vassaltis, reports -40% vs. 2023 with total harvest under 33 tonnes). Despite the heat, growers report unusually high retained acidity and low pH (3.1-3.2) at harvest, clean, healthy fruit and intense concentration — the volcanic soil, altitude-free but wind- and sea-moderated site, and the ancient bush-vine root systems are credited with preserving Assyrtiko's signature freshness even in an extreme year. Overall a very small but very high-quality Santorini vintage.

## Producer style
Gaia Wines was founded in 1994 by winemakers Yiannis Paraskevopoulos and Leon Karatsalos, and is widely credited as one of the producers that put modern Greek fine wine on the international map. The estate operates on two fronts — Nemea in the Peloponnese (Agiorgitiko, and the Wild Ferment Moschofilero) and a striking beachfront winery on Santorini for Assyrtiko — with a consistent house signature of precise, cool, protective winemaking aimed at showcasing site and variety rather than winemaking artifice. Thalassitis (the flagship, unoaked Santorini bottling) sits alongside the richer, barrel-fermented "Wild Ferment" and cellar-aged tiers, giving the estate a clear stylistic ladder from bone-dry/racy to textured/ageworthy within the same variety.

## Why it's in this exam
Slot 11 of Paper 1, Question 3 ("wines 10-12 are from regions influenced by the Mediterranean Sea"): Thalassitis is the benchmark case of a variety that retains freshness almost entirely through site and viticulture (ancient bush vines, kouloura training, volcanic soil, sea/wind moderation, early/cool harvest) even in an extreme-heat, drought vintage, paired against Costamolino (freshness via cellar technique in a warm but less extreme climate) and white Bandol (freshness via altitude and barrel texture) in the same Mediterranean-set trio.
